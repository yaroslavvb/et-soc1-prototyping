# E149: the GPU sanity check of the ET-SoC-1 power model, on Modal A100-40GB boards under the cap (registration)

Registered Mon 2 Nov 2026 (week 45), evening PST, on the Intel, **before the first measured call**. Q138, in the owner's
words that evening: *"all cards are available, feel free to settle any unanswered questions regarding horace's power
model using mordast, runs on aifoundry and modal for sanity checks on GPUs (don't exceed $20 on modal)"*, with Q137's
goal, *"to be able to predict the power requirement and heat generation of various kinds of numeric computations as
accurately as possible"*. It answers, on a GPU, the kinds page's
[section 11](https://spacesheep.dev/@yaroslavvb/horace-kinds-of-numbers#11-what-is-still-open) **item 6, "a power model
across kinds"**, and the part of **item 2, "a GPU measured the same way"**, that E135 left: E135 measured the A100 at
its cap, where power reads the cap; here the GPU runs under it, where power reads the data.

## The question

The ET-SoC-1's power model (Q137(m)'s M6, `docs/reports/data/2026-11-02-q137m-model/README.md`) prices a multiply-add
by the operand bits that change between consecutive operands (by field and side), a gate term for zeros, and a sign
term. **On an A100 under its 400 W cap, does power follow the operand bits the way that model says (zero fraction,
bit-position Hamming ladders, signs, order), and does a toggle model of that form, fitted on ladders, predict held-out
kinds of numbers blind?** And for heat: **the A100's die rise per board watt and its time constants at a held power,
predicted for the held-out kinds.**

## Stage 1: the fit set, measured and fitted first (92 kinds)

All kinds are built on the GPU from their own seeded generators (`build` in `e149_app.py`; the same bytes in every
container, by sha256). "Both" means A and B are drawn the same way, independently.

- **(a) zeros**, bf16, on one shared randn source: 0 (randn), 25, 50, 75, 90 and 100% zeros, **scattered** (each element
  zero with that probability) and **in blocks** (runs of 64 along K); 50% scattered on A only and on B only. fp16: 50
  and 75% scattered, 50% in blocks. int8 (on random −128…127): 25, 50, 75, 90% scattered, 50% in blocks.
- **(b) bit-position Hamming ladders** from the fixed base 1.0 (bf16 0x3F80, fp16 0x3C00): **k random flips per element
  in one field**, the k positions drawn afresh for every element: bf16 mantissa (7 bits) k = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7; bf16
  exponent, its low 5 bits (so every value stays in 2⁻³¹…1, never 0, subnormal, Inf or NaN) k = 1, 2, 3, 5; fp16
  mantissa (10 bits) k = 1, 3, 5, 7, 10; fp16 exponent, its low 3 bits (2⁻⁷…1) k = 1, 2, 3; **the sign** as a
  probability ladder (a 1-bit field has no k ladder): bf16 p = 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, fp16 p = 0.25, 0.50. Single positions,
  each flipped with probability 0.5: bf16 mantissa bits 0, 3, 6 and exponent bits 0, 4 (of the low five); fp16 mantissa
  bits 0, 5, 9. One-sided (the other operand the base): bf16 mantissa k = 3 and exponent k = 2 on A only and on B only,
  fp16 mantissa k = 5 and the sign (p = 0.5) on A only. int8 from the base 1, flips in bits 1–7 (bit 0 stays set, so no
  value is 0): k = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7; bits 1–3, bits 4–6 and bit 7 each flipped with probability 0.5 (both sides), and bit 7
  on A only.
- **(c) signs**, bf16, constant magnitude 1.5: fixed + on both; A fixed −, B fixed +; random signs on both, on A only, on
  B only; and randn's magnitudes with a fixed + sign against the same magnitudes with random signs.
- **(d) order**, bf16, one randn source: sorted along K (each row ascending) against **the same values shuffled along
  K**; the magnitudes sorted along K with random signs; sorted along M (A) and N (B), K order random; sorted along K and
  each row rolled by a random offset. **Square waves along K** between 1.0 and 1.9921875 (7 mantissa bits apart, both
  positive) in blocks of b = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64 elements, a random phase per row: at a K-lag s the share of pairs that
  differ is a triangle in s with period 2b, so these six separate which lag carries the flips.
- **(e) int8** as listed in (a) and (b), plus random −128…127 on A only and on B only.
- Anchors: random bf16, fp16 and int8 (randn, randn, −128…127) and each format's zeros.

## Stage 2: the held-out kinds, measured only after the freeze (21 kinds, 17 scored blind)

**bf16**: Horace He's nine (zeros, randn, rand, sparse75, ternary, onebit, pi, twos, checker; E135's constructions),
post-GELU activations (A) against LLM-like weights (B: Student-t, 5 dof, × 0.02), a power law (Pareto magnitudes,
α = 1.5, random signs, at most 10⁴), low rank (rank 16, unit variance), uniform values sorted along K, and the gist's
`sparse_neg`. **fp16**: zeros, rand, post-GELU × weights. **int8**: zeros, int8-quantized post-GELU activations (per
tensor) against int8-quantized LLM-like weights (per row), ternary, twos. zeros are the anchors (0 by definition) and
`bf16.randn` is a stage-1 kind, so it is reported but not blind: **17 kinds are scored blind.** Their operands are
built in stage 1's containers **after the last stage-1 measurement, with the NVML sampler stopped**, only to compute
their features (no window runs on them there); stage 2's containers rebuild them and check the sha256 of every operand
against the predictions' (a mismatch stops the scoring).

## How a window is read

A **pass** is a reference window (bf16 randn, 3 s), then for each kind a **cell window (5 s)** followed by a reference
window (3 s). Each format's zeros also run at the start, middle and end of a pass. Unit A runs pass 1 forward and pass 2
reversed, unit B the opposite; the order is one fixed shuffle. Each window is back-to-back calls with a CUDA event
every ~20 ms of work. NVML is read every 100 ms in its own process (E135's sampler). A window's power is **the mean of
the power readings** whose midpoint lies after its settle time (cell 1.5 s, reference 1.0 s) and before its end; the
energy counter's slope is recorded beside it. **d** = cell power − the mean of its two bracketing reference windows;
**y** = d − the mean d of that format's zeros windows in the same pass: **watts over the same GEMM on zeros, at
1410 MHz, at the reference's thermal state.** A kind's y is the mean over its valid replicates (2 passes × 2 boards);
its SE is their sd / √n.

**Stage 1 also holds two loads for heat**: after a 30 s idle, the reference (bf16 randn) for 180 s, then bf16 zeros for
90 s, then the passes. **Stage 2** runs its two passes, then for each held-out kind (and randn) a **hold**: bf16 zeros
for 15 s, then the kind for 30 s.

## The model

Features come from the operand bits alone (`side_feats`, `cross_feats` in `e149_app.py`), in flips per multiply-add:
for a side (A, or B as stored), the share of operand pairs (k, k + s) in one row whose bit p differs, by bit position
p, at lags s = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32; the same between consecutive rows at one k (the row direction); the share of ones
per position; and from both sides together: `nzprod`, the share of multiply-adds with both operands nonzero (the gate
term: a multiply-add with a zero operand is gated); `gtr`, the share of (i, j, k) whose gate state changes between k and
k + s (M6's `gtr`); `psgn`, the share whose product sign changes between k and k + s (M6's `psgn`); `negprod`, the share
of negative products; and `acc`, the flips of the fp32 (int32) running sum between consecutive 16-k blocks, over 4,096
sampled (i, j) pairs, per multiply-add. Fields: bf16 sign 15, exponent 7–14, mantissa 0–6; fp16 sign 15, exponent
10–14, mantissa 0–9; int8 sign 7, high 4–6, low 0–3.

**The primary model, G1, in the ET-SoC-1 model's form** (lag 1 = consecutive operands along K), fitted per format by
least squares on stage-1 kinds' y (one row per kind, zeros excluded; no intercept, because zeros are 0 by
construction):

- bf16: `nzprod`, `gtr`, flips of the sign, exponent and mantissa fields (A + B), the exponent and mantissa flips
  weighted by position within the field (a linear trend in bit position, centred), A − B over all bits, `psgn`;
- fp16: the same without the exponent's position term (no fp16 arm moves one exponent position);
- int8: `nzprod`, `gtr`, flips of the sign, high and low fields, A − B, `psgn`.

So "per bit position" is a field price plus a linear trend in position inside the field, which the single-position
arms identify.

**Registered comparisons, fitted and frozen the same way and scored beside it:** **G0**, `nzprod` alone (power as
density); **S1**, static bits: `nzprod`, the share of ones per field (A + B), `negprod`, A − B ones (values, not
changes); **G1s**, G1 at the K-lag s chosen from {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32} by the lowest pooled nested error over the three
formats (chosen on stage 1, before the freeze); **G2**, G1 plus `acc` (the result path); **G3**, G1 plus row-direction
flips per field.

**Scoring on stage 1**: leave-one-kind-out (LOKO), per format. **The frozen band** for a held-out kind is ± 1.645 × G1's
LOKO rms of its format (a 90% band). Everything — coefficients, LOKO errors, the chosen lag, the bands, the heat model
— is written by `analyze_e149.py fit` into `model-e149.json` and **committed and pushed before stage 2's first call**;
the predictions (`analyze_e149.py predict`, mechanical, from the frozen file and stage-1's feature records) are
committed with it.

**Heat.** One two-pole linear model of the die temperature driven by board power, T(t) = T₀ + r₁x₁ + r₂x₂ with
x′ᵢ = (P − xᵢ)/τᵢ, fitted on both stage-1 sessions (shared τ₁, τ₂, r₁, r₂; per unit an intercept and an initial-transient
term; τ by grid, the rest by least squares; `fit_heat`). **The die rise per board W** is r₁ + r₂ and its fraction
reached in 30 s is F(30). Leakage: the slope of power on die temperature at the held reference load (from 10 s into the
180 s hold). For a stage-2 hold, the blind prediction drives the frozen model with the measured power up to the hold,
then with **the pre-hold's measured zeros power + the format's frozen zeros offset + the kind's frozen G1 prediction +
the frozen leakage slope × the simulated rise**; the rise is the mean over the hold's last second minus the pre-hold's
last second, measured and predicted alike. Its frozen band is ± 1.645 × √(σ_x² + (rise-per-W at 30 s × G1's LOKO
rms)²), σ_x = the rms error of 30 s rises when the model fitted on one stage-1 board predicts the other's session.

## Rules, which can fail

**Validity.** **V0**: ≥ 90% of stage-1 kinds keep all their windows valid on each board; else the size did not keep the
data off the cap and the run is reported partial. Invalid windows are dropped (named per kind).

**Stage 1, the ET-SoC-1 model's structure on the A100** (`stage1_rules`; "resolved" = difference > 2 SE with ≥ 3
replicates on each side):
- **R1a** (bf16, int8): along the scattered zero ladder 0 → 25 → 50 → 75 → 90 → 100%, no step rises resolved.
- **R1b**: blocks of zeros cost less than scattered zeros at 50% and at 75% (bf16), both resolved (the ET-SoC-1 form:
  scattered zeros flip operand bits and the gate state; blocks do neither).
- **R2** (bf16 mantissa, bf16 exponent, fp16 mantissa, int8 bits 1–7): the ladder follows flips, not ones: the middle
  rung (k = 3, 3, 5, 3) rises over the base, resolved, and the all-flipped constant (k = 7, 5, 10, 7) comes back below
  half that rise.
- **R3a**: random signs at constant magnitude cost more than a fixed sign, resolved. **R3b**: a fixed negative sign
  costs nothing extra: |y(A −, B +) − y(fixed +)| ≤ max(2 SE, 10% of R3a's difference).
- **R4a**: the values sorted along K cost less than the same values shuffled, resolved.
- **R4b**: the ET-SoC-1 form's lag: the period-2 square wave (b = 1) costs more than the period-128 one (b = 64),
  resolved. (If the A100's operand wires see elements 16 or 32 apart along K in turn, as an MMA's K-step suggests, R4b
  fails and the lag scan says where.)
- **B1**: the two boards agree: the slope of board B's y on board A's within 0.9–1.1 and their rms difference ≤ 5% of
  S, where **S = stage 1's y of random bf16** (the bf16 span from zeros to random).

**Stage 2, blind** (`do_score`; over the 17 blind kinds, each kind's y the mean of its replicates; error = measured −
predicted):
- **P1**: G1's rms ≤ 0.10 × S. **P2**: G1's |bias| ≤ 0.05 × S. **P3**: ≥ 75% of the blind kinds (13 of 17) inside G1's
  frozen 90% band. **P4**: G1's rms ≤ 0.7 × G0's (the toggle form beats density alone by 30%). **P5**: G1's rms < S1's
  (changes beat static bits).
- **H1**: the blind hold rise (G1's power through the frozen heat model) rms ≤ 0.8 °C. **H2**: the same with the
  measured power, rms ≤ 0.6 °C. **H3**: ≥ 75% of the blind kinds' rises inside the frozen heat band. **H4**: the two
  stage-1 boards' die rise per W within a ratio of 0.8–1.25.
- Reported beside them, not scored: every model's rms, bias and worst error by format, per board as well; fJ per flip
  at the measured multiply-add rate; the lag scan; every held-out kind's steady die rise predicted at r₁ + r₂.

[`selftest_e149.py`](selftest_e149.py) builds synthetic logs in the harness's format (a known G1, a known two-pole heat
path, leakage, noise, one capped window) and runs reduce → fit → predict → score: the chain recovers the coefficients
and the heat path, drops the capped window, and every rule holds on a reduction made to hold and fails on one made to
fail ([`selftest.txt`](selftest.txt)).

## The smoke (S0), declared

Unit `S`, one container: static reads, 8 stage-1 kinds built with their features (bf16 randn, zeros, mantissa k = 3,
square wave b = 1, sorted along K; fp16 mantissa k = 5; int8 random and zeros), 15 s idle, a 30 s reference hold, one
pass over the 8, two 5 s + 8 s holds. Plumbing only (the windows, the NVML columns, the validity bits at 2048³, the
features, the reducer on real logs). Its numbers are not scored and not used to fit or predict.

## What this does not do

- It sets nothing on the GPU: no power limit, no clocks.
- It touches no lab card, posts nothing in `#community-lab`, and reads nothing from the archive.
- The model is a predictor in the ET-SoC-1 model's form, not the A100's circuit: the GPU's dataflow (registers, shared
  memory, the tensor cores' K-steps) decides which operand pairs meet on a wire, and the lag and row-direction
  comparisons only ask which pairing predicts best.
- One size (2048³), square, K-contiguous operands, one GEMM library (cuBLAS through torch 2.5.1), one GPU model.

## Notes after verification (dated; not amendments)

*Mon 2 Nov 2026, about 21:40 PST, after both stages had run and been scored. The text above is unchanged, and these
notes change no rule, prediction, band, number or verdict: every rule was scored as registered. The evidence is in the
data README's "Corrections after verification" (C1–C14), from `posthoc_e149.py` beside the data
(`docs/reports/data/2026-11-02-e149-gpu-model-check/`).*

- **N1, what y is.** "How a window is read" calls y "watts over the same GEMM on zeros, at 1410 MHz, at the reference's
  thermal state". The heat fit's fast pole (τ₁ ≤ 0.2 s) settles inside a cell's 1.5 s settle, so each cell's die sits at
  its own fast-pole temperature (a zeros cell 9.3 °C below its references). y therefore carries its window's own
  leakage, about λr₁ = 0.11–0.16 of it (0.131 pooled), and the per-flip prices fitted on it are upper bounds on switching
  energy by that share.
- **N2, the blind heat path counts that leakage twice.** "Heat" drives the frozen model with the pre-hold's zeros, the
  format's zeros offset, G1's y and the leakage slope × the simulated rise; the fast pole's part of that rise is already
  inside y. H1 was scored as registered (it FAILS). A registration that reuses this path (E150) should state this and
  score a corrected arm beside the registered one: y × (1 − λr₁) in the path, or the leakage term driven by the slow
  pole's rise only.
- **N3, the timeouts' ceiling.** "Budget" was written before A1 added the smoke S2 and its 15-minute timeout; with S2 the
  timeouts cap the spend at $8.66 (the $8.08 above is $8.09 by the same arithmetic). The metered cost was $4.48.
- **N4, R4b's parenthesis** ("If the A100's operand wires see elements 16 or 32 apart along K in turn ... R4b fails and the
  lag scan says where") is the registration's hypothesis, not a finding. R4b failed and stage 1's lag scan chose 32, but
  blind the lag-32 form G1s predicted no better than lag 1; which pairing the A100 pays for is open.
