Live

Each machine streams a reading every second (tools/lab/live/live-collector.py): CPU per thread, load, memory, disk, temperatures (CPU package, NVMe drive, network chip and each ET card's die), its cards' PCIe links and locks, and the busiest processes by name. A card's temperature is read through its management node, which admits one process at a time, so the collector reads it (in about 4 ms) only while nobody holds a card on that machine; while someone does, the page shows the last reading. The small line beside each card is its last 48 hours (dashed: 95 °C).

People

Everyone seen on a lab machine: logged in (a login session, or an open terminal such as a tmux pane), with processes running, or holding a card; an account's own background services (which linger keeps running for someone who is not there) are not counted. Active means a terminal used in the last 30 minutes, or a session with no terminal (an IDE's, say). "Doing" sorts their processes into coarse kinds on the machine itself (on a card, AI agent, building, simulator, Python, editor; else shell only, or other processes): no command, file or process name leaves the machine, except the name of a program holding a card, which et-who and et-usage show every user. A person seen only on machines that are not answering is "unknown".

About this page

A collector on aifoundry2 reads the three machines every 10 minutes, read-only and at low priority: /proc, the card driver's counters in sysfs, et-who, the lab's health check (et-lab-health, rev 3, sent over ssh), et-lab-manifest, the card-use log (et-usage), nodewatch's logs, and on aifoundry2 whether Tailscale sees each machine online. It bakes the result into this page and republishes it when something changed, and at least every 60 minutes; an open tab reloads itself on each new version. It never opens a card's device node; a live card sample is off unless someone switches it on, and then waits for a free card.

It names the lab's users. For each person it shows a login name, session and process counts, idle time, coarse kinds of activity, card holds and card use with the programs' names; never commands, files, addresses or where anyone connects from. The collector's own account is listed like everyone else's.

Something wrong or stale on this page? Tell the maintainer, who runs the collector.

For the maintainer (the maintainer): updating and checking it
  • On aifoundry2, in the checkout: tools/lab/dashboard/update.sh now collects, rebuilds and republishes at once; open tabs update within seconds. update.sh now --card-sample also takes a card telemetry sample, behind every rule of the card etiquette. update.sh status shows the last runs and deploys, a halt, and the space's visibility.
  • From another machine: ssh aifoundry2 claude/et-soc1-prototyping/tools/lab/dashboard/update.sh now; or ask Claude in the aifoundry2 session to refresh the lab dashboard.
  • A machine that shows "approval needed": run ssh aifoundry1 true (or aifoundry3) on aifoundry2, approve the address it prints, then update.sh now.
  • Silence a known alert: update.sh ack <alert id> [days] [note] (the id is in each alert's detail).
  • After a halt (the private mode only: the space was, or may have been, readable without signing in): check the space, then update.sh resume; nothing resumes deploying by itself.
