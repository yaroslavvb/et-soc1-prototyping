## A1-3. What KE-D and KE′ can detect, stated exactly

**The words.** E163.md § Rank 14 (lines 247-248) reads "The flat 0.05 W is large against steps of 0.03-0.16 W: KE-D tests that the steps
are not lost or doubled, and KE′ (Welch) tests their size."

**Why that is not exact.** A lost step reads about 0 and a doubled one about 2s. Either way the reading departs from E151's by the step s
itself, so KE-D catches either only where s is above its 0.05 W line.

- That holds for `b1_1` … `b1_5`, whose steps are 0.088-0.164 W.
- It does not hold for `b1_0`, whose step is 0.0321 W (`rerun-ref.json` › `e151`; `predictions-e163.json` › `spec` › `e151_block` ›
  `ref`). A lost `b1_0` and a doubled `b1_0` both stay within 0.05 W and pass KE-D.
- KE′ does no better on `b1_0`. Suppose E163's kept blocks scatter as E151's eight did. Then Welch's 95% half-width on `b1_0` is about
  0.042 W at 3 kept blocks and 0.097 W at 2, both above its step.
- On the other five the half-width is 0.023-0.042 W at 3 blocks and 0.053-0.098 W at 2 (`logs/selftest-notes.txt` § 3; planned, not
  measured).

**The words now read:** KE-D tests that the five steps above 0.05 W (`b1_1` … `b1_5`) are neither lost nor doubled, and KE′ tests their
size to about ±0.02-0.04 W at three kept blocks. **For `b1_0` neither rule can detect a lost or a doubled step.** KE-D and KE′ still
score it, as registered, and its reading is reported with them. The rules, the line and the scorer are unchanged.
