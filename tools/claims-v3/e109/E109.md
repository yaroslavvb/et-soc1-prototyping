# E109: int8 zeros' extra 0.61 W - reads, per-op work or the lanes (Q133)

Sun 1 Nov 2026 (week 44), Q133, lane a3-e109-int8-zeros. Registered before the claim and the first launch. It answers [section 11 of the kinds page](https://spacesheep.dev/@yaroslavvb/horace-kinds-of-numbers#11-what-is-still-open), **item 11, "int8 zeros' extra 0.61 W"**, as it reads at `1fe8377a18af`: *"Open; part of old item 12. The fixed bench's count points the wrong way, post hoc (section 6, Q132(b) §14). A card run that changes the read rate on zeros could separate reads from ops."* It adds an arm for **item 15**'s *"Why a live zero on A costs 1.72 W more than priced"* (E93 R5).

## The question

On all-zero tiles every slot is gated in both formats, yet int8 zeros sit 0.612 ± 0.053 W above fp32 zeros on this card (E78 Result 5; +0.41 to +0.62 W over 13 sessions on two cards, Q132(b) §14). Q132(b) §14 counted the fixed RTL bench on zeros: by its register bits int8 should cost **less** (it says int8 − fp32 = −0.51 W at M4's constant), and what points the right way lies outside the bench: int8 runs **1.72×** fp32's TensorFMA ops a second and makes **1.34×** its scratchpad reads. It left two one-number readings, each fitted to E78's two cells and so each exact there: **reads**, S = r × reads (r = 5.93 pJ a read, with clocked bits nearly free), and **ops**, S = c + e × ops (e = 770 pJ an op, c = 1.12 W). One number cannot separate them. E109 changes the op rate, the read rate and the lanes' work on zeros, one at a time, on the card.

## What the RTL says each knob does (external/core-et, read for this registration)

- **The row mask** (`tensor_mask` with the MSK bit: `--row-mask`). A masked row's micro-op is not issued: `conv_skip` drops `s0_fma_en_conv` (`vpu_tensorfma.v:649-650`; not on a launch's first op, which writes zeros, nor int8's last copy-out). The FSM still steps every (row, column), and **the A and B scratchpad requests have no row-mask term** (`:323-340`), so the reads, the cycles and the op rate stay. The lane clocks are enabled by the issued micro-op, before the zero gate: `clock_en_txfma` from `ex_txfma_clock_valid` (`vpu_lane.v:191-194`), `clock_en_tima = ex_tima_valid_unqual | delay` (`:212-214`). So **mask 0x0000 (`m0`) removes the lanes' work and keeps the scratchpad's.**
- **K, the A columns** (ACOLS). The FSM reads A for each (row, column) and B's two half-lines for each column (`:487-561`), so K = 8 (`k8`) halves the reads and the micro-ops an op, and the op takes about half the cycles: **the op rate nearly doubles at the same reads, micro-ops and busy cycles a second.** Only per-op work moves.
- **The A rows** (AROWS). 8 rows (`r8`) halve A's reads and the micro-ops an op but keep all 16 B lines (32 half-line reads; B's prefetch has no row term, `:557`): as `k8`, with **B's reads a second nearly doubled** (B-line reuse halved). 4 rows are not used: the sparsity README's errata note (type D, TensorFMA16A32/32 with 4 A rows) rules them out for the float types.
- **int8's A stream.** int8 reads A 64 bits at a time on even rows of even columns and skips the odd column's read there (`:338-350`): 192 A reads and 32 B half-lines an op against fp32's 256 and 32. That is where the bench's 224 against 288 comes from.
- **Not used:** `bcols` masks lanes but never removes a micro-op or a read (`:654-673`); TensorLoadB streaming (`--b-stream`) changes three things at once (B's scratchpad reads go, a 16-line load an op arrives, and the op time moves: ABLB's bands, 265-300 cycles for int8 against 318), so it cannot separate reads from ops.

## The registered quantities (per kept pass, paired; mean and 95% interval over passes)

D is `reduce_e78.py`'s: the item's power over its format's zeros, interpolated in time between the pass's three zeros anchors. Rates are the reducer's measured ops a second.

- **U(f) = −D(m0.f.zeros)**: what issuing the micro-ops costs on zeros (the lanes).
- **e_op(f) = [D(m0k8) − D(m0)] / [rate(m0k8) − rate(m0)]**: the price of an op with no lanes.
- **G = S(int8.zeros) − S(fp32.zeros)**, this session's gap; **G_lane = U(int8) − U(fp32)**; **G_op = e_op(int8) × O(int8) − e_op(fp32) × O(fp32)** (O: each format's op rate on zeros); **G_rest = G − G_lane − G_op**, the per-time side: the A-word stream and B's reads, the FSM's busy clocks, and anything else that neither the op rate nor the lanes move.
- **r_B** (fp32) = {[D(r8) − D(k8)] + e_op × [rate(k8) − rate(r8)]} / [B/s(r8) − B/s(k8)]: the price of a B half-line read.
- Item 15: **T₊ = D(m0.a_zeros_b_randn) − D(m0.zeros)** and **T₋ = D(m0.a_nz_b_randn) − D(m0.zeros)**: B's random bytes moving to the register files with no micro-op; **L_live = D(a_nz_b_randn) − D(m0.a_nz_b_randn)**: the live lanes.

## Protocol

**aifoundry3 card 0, its pinned 600 MHz** (boot-time TDP 0 / 65 °C), `ettelem config` read under the card lock with `timeout 10` at the start and end of the session and of every pass; nothing set: no governor, TDP, threshold or clock set, no `--reset-ms`, no PMIC command. **Frame: E78's.** `block_e109.sh` is `block_e107.sh` with E109's items (each change marked); `run_e78.py`, `e78_common.py`, `reduce_e78.py`, `kinds.json`, E93's `e93.json` and tile, and `lib.sh` are used byte-identical, their sha256 checked against `predictions-e109.json` › `harness` before any run or reduction. A pass: the built-in π tie first and last, E78's six anchors (each format's zeros and random) in each of three segments, E109's 12 items dealt round-robin (seed `E109|aifoundry3|<pass>`): 32 registered runs and E78's two burn-in runs of π, about 330 s. Launch at a 58 °C die reading after a 59 °C preheat, 2 s quiet, 4 s FMA runs, all 1,024 minions, cap 78 °C, STOP file: E78's.

1. `V3_DRY=1` of one pass and the smoke on the box before this commit; this registration committed and pushed before the claim.
2. The claim in `#community-lab` (one line, `for 0.5 h, until ~HH:MM PST`), then the session's first config read.
3. **Smoke** (pass 10901, 8 items, 2 s runs, no preheat): every new mode once. It gates only on launch records, `ok` and `tensor_errors` 0 (`session_e109.sh` checks every smoke record and starts the queue only if all pass); its cycles are not scored.
4. **Passes 10910-10913** (`queue_e109.sh`, detached on the box by `session_e109.sh`, which also runs the smoke and both session config reads): it stops before a pass that would end past 23 min from its start, on the STOP file, or after a failed pass. **At least 3 kept passes** (E78's pass gates, `reduce_e78.py`) are needed to score; fewer is reported as not scored.
5. The closing config read; the release by editing the same post.

**Budget: at most 25 card-minutes** from the first config read to the last; expected about 24 (E107's 9.3 s a device run and 12 s a pass on this card today). If the passes run slower, the deadline drops pass 4 and three are scored. No new card session starts after 18:00 PST.

**What a result changes.** If R1 passes, the gap is per-op work: an int8 op on zeros costs what an fp32 op costs, and int8 runs more of them. If R3 passes and R1 fails, it sits on the per-time side, where the scratchpad is, and R4 says whether a read is priced. If R2 reads LANES-OPPOSE, the bench's direction holds for the lanes and the gap is elsewhere. Item 15: R7 and R8 test that with no micro-op A's sign is invisible and that a gated lane's moving B costs only its transport; R9 splits a live −0 lane's watts into transport and lanes on this card. Departures, if any, are dated amendments written before the data they touch.
