## 1. The question, and the answer frozen here

**Do MM1-v1's frozen forecasts of a matmul's clock cycles, seconds and joules hold on shapes, formats, data kinds and shire masks that never ran,
and does MM1-v1 beat today's models on them?** "Hold" is registered as: per case (time) or per point (energy), the measured value within a band
of twice MM1-v1's own held-out error, at 90% of cases (section 7); "beat" as: closer to the measurement than F1 (cycles) or F2 (joules over
idle) in more cases than not, by an exact sign test at 5%. **F1** is E190's GEMM route widened by E206 (seconds from the line-cost law at a
point's lines; watts by E136's power law; E37's card factor), **F2** is ledger v3's tensor terms (the lab's energy ledger, with F1's seconds); both
were run unchanged by MM1-v1's lane on these points with the masked map's lines, and frozen beside MM1-v1. **MM1-v1** predicts each point's
cycles from its lines by place (the L2, the L3, fresh from DRAM; a closed-form map with a logistic L3 retention), the stride and contention terms,
and its energy from a resident price per op, per-line prices and an awake power (its README, sections 2 and 3). His question - how well the timing
simulation matches reality - is read beside every point: the measured cycles over the simulator's instruction count and over the tensor unit's
floor (section 7, reading e).

**How far apart the models are** (desk, from the frozen files): MM1-v1's wall cycles an op against F1's differ by 17.5% rms in
ln over the 47 cases (MM1-v1 lower by 15.3% on average; 45 of 47
cases more than 3% apart); its counter's mean against F1, 21.2%. In J over idle MM1-v1 and F2 differ by 39.8% rms
(49 points both price; all 49 more than 0.5% apart), MM1-v1 and F1 by 17.9% (50 points).
The bands do not separate the models (MM1-v1 and F1 can both lie within T1's 13% of one measurement at 45 of 47 cases; MM1-v1 and F2
within E2's 16% at 28 of 49 points); the head-to-head rules do.

**The pins the kit gates on:** the kernel ELF `aace138caf8071cc7679134211045a89fb09b65cc9a170bfe33409fd36b4fda0` (E136's build record; aifoundry2's and aifoundry1's builds
byte-identical) and its `.text` `61315bf6a449c2823fbffc33834b4c0a68aa19e8436a29c1124b15c04114b763`. aifoundry3's RISC-V GCC prints `(g1b306039a) 15.1.0` where aifoundry1's prints
`() 15.1.0`, and the string lands in the ELF, so on aifoundry3 the kernel is gated on its `.text` (E191's kit fix; E206's kit).

**The arm used: `gemmL<MB>x<NB>x<KB>`** (`SP_GEMM_LARGE`): C (MB x NB blocks of 1,024 x 512) = A (MB x KB blocks) x B (KB x NB blocks), each
block the pattern's 64 x 32 x 32 tiles; every minion owns two row tiles and one column tile of every C block and runs each C tile's whole K
(KB x 32 ops) before its one TensorStore. M is a multiple of 1,024, N of 512, K of 512 (fp32), 1,024 (fp16) or 2,048 (int8). Ops a minion a GEMM:
MB x NB x 2 x KB x 32. **Shire subsets** (`--shires <mask> --per-shire 32`): shire s computes rows 128 (s >> 2) .. +128 and columns 128 (s & 3)
.. +128 of every C block, so the day's masks compute `0x3` 128 x 256 of each block (2 shires: one row group, two column groups), `0xfff`
384 x 512 (12), `0xf0f0f0f` 512 x 512 in alternate 128-row bands (16; MM1-v1 spells it 0x0f0f0f0f), `0xffffff` 768 x 512 (24). Each minion's
work is the full-chip GEMM's; the host checks the participating minions' tiles of C, so every subset point is checked.

**The data** (E101's `--values <arm>:<gen>:<files>` grammar, `--seed 1`): random (`randn`; fp32 normal(0, 1), fp16 the same rounded to half,
int8 uniform -128..127 from `int8/full.bin`), **e37** (the host's +-1 / +-2 generator, E37's fill; never in a GEMM before), **blind4's real
tiles** (two committed real tensors a format, never in a GEMM), **blind3's designed tile** (constant-column A against random B). Each point's
exact host arguments are in `points-e207.json` > `host_args`.

**The operations a matmul contains**, as this kernel runs them: tensor multiply-adds (4,096 MACs an op in fp32, 8,192 in fp16, 16,384 in int8),
loads of A and B tiles (an op asks 64 A lines and 128 B lines; where they come from is the line map), and one TensorStore of C per C tile row.
**No epilogue** (bias, activation, requantization).

## 7. The scoring (`score_e207.py`; the numbers in `rules-e207.json`; a point or case with no run is NO DATA, never a failure)

**A run** (E206's form): its die gate, 10 s at rest (p_rest = the median of its last 5 s), one host process `--seconds 6 --budget 7.5` under a
plain `timeout 10`, a 4 s tail. **Measured, per run** (the kit's reducer, E206's run form; the scorer's docstring has it exactly): counted
launches are launch >= 0, ok, `wall_s` >= 0.1 s and the kernel clock in 590-610 MHz; **t_gemm** = sum of wall / sum of passes; **cycles an op,
the wall** = the median of cycles_max / passes over ops a minion a GEMM (the slowest minion); **cycles an op, the counter's mean** = the median of
the host's `cycles_per_op`; **p_work** = the median board W of the 10 Hz samples inside the counted launches; **W over idle** = p_work - p_rest;
**J over idle** = (p_work - p_rest) t_gemm; **J with the card at rest** = p_work t_gemm. A point is the mean of its runs; a run whose host record
differs from its point (arm, type, minions, or the shire mask read as a number) is set aside and flagged. A case is the mean of its points.

**MM1-v1's rules.** Each case or point passes when |measured / MM1-v1 - 1| is within its band; **a rule's verdict: PASS if at least 90% of its
scored cases or points pass, FAIL otherwise, TOO FEW under 10, NO DATA with none.** The band is **twice the larger of MM1-v1's two held-out rms
errors** for that quantity - E206 held out (`compare_e206.json`, the E206 fold: every constant refitted without E206; the same card and clock as
this day) and leave one experiment out over all its points (`holdout_v1.json`, where recorded) - rounded up to a whole percent. If MM1-v1's blind
errors are normal with an sd equal to that held-out rms, about 95% of cases fall inside and a rule passes with the probability in the last column;
at 1.25 or 1.5 times that error (MM1-v0 ran 1.3-1.6 times its held-out numbers blind on E206) it mostly does not:

## 8. What E207 cannot say (stated before the data)

- **It tests rgm136 on its grid only**: the `gemmL` arm, M a multiple of 1,024, N of 512, K of one block per format. MM1-v1's "from sizes and
  operations" holds for this kernel's tile order; another kernel or a library GEMM needs its own line map (MM1-v1's README, mm-critic F4).
- **The transistor switches are estimated, not measured**: no card counts switches. MM1-v1's switch estimates sit beside every point (REPORTED,
  reading i) and are never scored; the day tests the cycles, seconds and joules they are built on.
- **One card, one clock**: aifoundry3 card 0 at its pinned 600 MHz. MM1-v1's awake power is per card (with a card held out, its W over idle was off by 14-42% rms), so a
  pass here does not carry to card 1 or aifoundry2 (E206 arm B and E208 read those).
- **No epilogue**; **a small GEMM's joules are a warm, repeated GEMM's** (a launch repeats the same A and B, so a footprint the L3 holds is served
  from it after the first pass).
- **The subsets** are 8 points on 4 masks of one shape and 2 of another; 0x3 holds 2 of 4 column groups, a layout MM1-v1's row-group term never saw.
- **The kinds** are 6 points: e37 at two shapes, blind4's real tiles at one, blind3's designed tile at one; MM1-v1's resident prices for blind4 and
  blind3 are earlier frozen predictions, not measured anchors.
- **No RSW second forecast**: the day does not decide MAXAB against RSW (MM1-v1's README, mm-critic F8).
- **The head-to-head says which model is closer more often, not by how much** (the bootstrap interval, REPORTED, says how much); and the bands
  of section 7 hold both MM1-v1 and F1 at most cases, so a PASS of T1 does not by itself favour either.
- **The three points under 3 W** test the meter's noise as much as the model (0.215 W is about 2 sd of a run).
- **The meter's absolute scale**: one fixed gain per board, never set beside an outside meter.
- **One session, two runs a point**; no interval between sessions.
