## 2. The GEMM and the binary

**The binary is E136's `rgm136`, unchanged** (`tools/claims-v3/e136/rgm136/`; no new source tree). Its `SP_GEMM_LARGE` arm `gemmL<MB>x<NB>x<KB>`
computes C (MB × NB blocks of 1,024 × 512) = A (MB × KB blocks) × B (KB × NB blocks), each block the vendor's 64 × 32 × 32 tiles, B packed by
column, each C tile accumulated over its whole K before its one TensorStore. A tile row holds 16 fp32, 32 fp16 or 64 int8 elements, so
**M = N = K = 8,192 in every format** is `gemmL8x16x16` (fp32), `gemmL8x16x8` (fp16) and `gemmL8x16x4` (int8): 131,072 / 65,536 / 32,768 tensor ops
a minion, 1,024 minions, 2 × 8,192³ = 1.0995 × 10¹² operations in each. *(E136 ran one arm, `gemmL4x8x8`, in all three formats, so its fp16 and
int8 had K = 8,192 and 16,384; E190's fp16 and int8 are true 8,192³.)* A, B and C take 256 + 256 + 256 MB in fp32 (768 MB), 128 + 128 + 256 in
fp16, 64 + 64 + 256 in int8 (C is 32-bit).

**Why no change is needed (desk check of code, `host/main.cpp` and `kernel/sparsity.c` at `f9632ee3`):** the arm parser reads each count with
`strtoull` and refuses only a zero or a malformed tail; every size and offset is `size_t` on the host and `uint64_t` in the kernel (the largest
offset, 256 MB, is far below 2³¹ anyway); the args struct carries MB, NB and KB in 64-bit fields. With 131,072 ops a pass the host's calibration
launch is one GEMM (`max(1, 64000 / opsPerPass)`), so every later fp32 launch holds one GEMM (fp16 about 3, int8 about 6), and its 6 s kernel
timeout is about 13 times one fp32 GEMM at its band's slowest (0.44 s at 600 MHz).

**The simulator check** ([`desk/sim-check-e190.txt`](desk/sim-check-e190.txt); aifoundry2, in a sandbox with no `/dev/et*` and an empty
`/run/lock`, under a 4 GB cap; never the full size): `rgm136` built unchanged at `f9632ee3`; **its kernel ELF is byte-identical to
E136's build record** (`aace138c...`); the unchanged host ran `gemmL2x2x16` fp32, `gemmL2x2x8` fp16 and `gemmL2x2x4` int8 on one shire (32 minions;
E190's three K tilings, a two-digit count, 2 × 2 C blocks, A up to 64 MB), rc 0 each, and its check of C read **approx, approx and exact**
(8,192 of 8,192 words within tolerance in each). Not checked: the full size, the runtime's 256 MB allocations and DMAs on silicon,
aifoundry1's own build. The kit's smoke checks C on the card before any run counts (R0).

**The process time** (desk, [`desk/timing_e136.py`](desk/timing_e136.py) → `timing_e136.txt`, from E136's committed launch records): E136's
4,096³ processes spent 0.25-0.29 s before their first launch with 128 MB of A and B and 0.12-0.23 s after the check launch. Scaled by bytes, an
8,192³ process with `--seconds 6 --budget 7.5` under `timeout 10` takes about 7.9 s in fp32 at the slow end (7.2 fp16, 6.9 int8): about 2 s inside
the timeout. E136's sustained processes of `--seconds 8` would not fit, so E190's sustained runs are twelve 6 s processes.

**The data: random, E136's kind.** fp32 and fp16 `--values gemmL…:randn:tools/claims-v3/kinds/tiles/<format>/randn.bin`, int8
`:randn:tools/claims-v3/kinds/tiles/int8/full.bin`, `--seed 1`: rgm105's generator, `std::mt19937_64` seeded 1, fp32 `normal(0, 1)` floats, fp16 the same
rounded to half, int8 uniform over −128..127; the pools' first tile pair is the file's; at most 8,192 distinct 1 KB tiles a pool, repeated (an
8 MB period, which E81 found does not change the power). **How it compares with the A100 report's data cannot be told here**: that report is a
private page this lane cannot read. If it used fresh normal draws, E190's random differs in that its tiles repeat with an 8 MB period.

## 4. How the forecasts were made (`forecast_e190.py`; every input's sha256 in `forecast-e190.json` › `inputs_sha256`)

1. **Seconds.** t = ops a minion × T, T the time per op. T is a measured large GEMM's of the same block shape, scaled by E136's line-cost law
   re-footed on E126's `c16s` (E136's post hoc footing, −1.5% on its own fp32): T(f) = c1(f) + n_L3/128 (c8s(f) − c1(f)) + n_fresh/128 (c16s(f) −
   c1(f)), E119's and E126's aifoundry2 fits. **Anchors:** fp32 and fp16 on E136's `g4k` (T800 2.1726 and 2.1746 µs, T600 2.3661 and 2.3640;
   E146's replicate D8 within 0.2%), int8 on E146's `D4` (`gemmL4x8x4`, KB 4 as E190's int8; T800 2.1033, T600 2.2703). **How the larger size
   changes reuse and DRAM traffic** ([`desk/reuse_e190.py`](desk/reuse_e190.py) → `reuse_e190.txt`: E136's desk model imported unchanged; its g4k
   numbers re-derived to the last digit first): per C block the chip streams an A row panel of 64 × KB × 32 KB and a B column panel of 32 × KB ×
   32 KB. At K = 8,192 in fp32 (KB 16) these are 32 and 16 MB, so A comes back after 50 MB of other traffic and B's second row tile after 33 MB:
   past the 32 MB L3 (E107: 26 MB served, 40 MB not), and **strict LRU keeps nothing: 192 of 192 lines a shire-op are fresh** (E136's g4k: 85.9),
   8.86 GB from DRAM a GEMM (64 KB of distinct A and B tiles an op step; about 23 GB/s at the point, under the energy manual's catalogue rate of
76 GB/s that the ledger uses). fp16 (KB 8) has
   g4k's panels exactly (82.9 fresh at 32 MB); int8 (KB 4) halves them (A 8 MB, 68 fresh, 0.87 GB a GEMM). T = T_anchor × law(E190's lines) /
   law(the anchor's), and the **two-sided band** runs from min(1, every alternative) × 0.97 to max(1, every alternative) × 1.03 over the c16s and c16
   footings at a 32, 28 and 24 MB L3 (1 = the extra fresh lines cost nothing, which the band's fast end keeps open: E136's g4k ran beyond the law's
   all-A-held placement). Strict LRU is the point because it matched the power-of-two-stride shapes D4 and D8 within 2.2% where E158's retention
   curve missed by 8.5-11.9% (Q140(q) `stride-sm.txt` S2), and all three E190 shapes have power-of-two panel strides (NB 16).
2. **Watts, arm A.** W over idle = the anchor's measured bursts less the ledger v2's idle law at each run's starting die (fp32 30.48 and 30.80 W in
   E136 and E146; fp16 28.50, 28.86; int8 18.29), moved to E190's shape and clock by **E136's frozen power law** (`make_predictions_e136.py`,
   parametrized by the lines; it reproduces E136's frozen c600 and P(f) to the last digit), its switching coefficient re-read from the anchor's
   measured burst: c_E190 = c_meas × c_law(E190) / c_law(anchor); P(f) = B(f) + c s(f) 600 T(600) / (f T(f)). fp32's slower op lowers its watts
   (25.8 against g4k's 30.6 W). **Sustained:** the governor's clock by E119's dither at G2's refitted cap (E136's better footing: 800 MHz in all
   three) plus the anchor's measured sustained heating (E136's g4k: 3.6 / 2.9 / 0.9 W), scaled by W. Band: W at the seconds band's two ends, at
   "no change" and at the 28 and 24 MB lines, ± 2 W.
3. **Watts, arm B.** The same switching at the same clock: W_B = κ × W_A(600), **κ = 1.0094**, card 1 over aifoundry2 in E37's DRAM-streamed fp32
   matmul at 600 MHz (8.865 against 8.783 W over the pre-launch idle; aifoundry3 8.355: the ledger's `scores-v2.json`); band W_A(600)'s × [0.95, 1.05].
   The seconds are arm A's T600 (E37's cycles an op on three cards agreed within 0.9%). Sustained on card 1: + its leakage slope (0.48 W a °C at
   58 °C) × E136's sustained shape (0.621) × 0.67 °C a W (aifoundry2's steady coefficient, carried by hand).
4. **The ledger v2 arm (REPORTED, not scored for a verdict):** the load ledger v2 as frozen (`06d60fa2`; `tools/ledger/ledger_v2.py`), card
   `aifoundry1-c1`, 600 MHz, 58 °C, the frozen seconds, tensor ops by format and kind (its card-1 rows), bytes by E136's convention (lines a
   shire-op × 64 B / 32 minions, `l3_load` and `dram_load`): **fp32 10.63 J over idle with heat [9.23, 10.82], fp16 3.34, int8 0.99; with the card
   at rest 24.27 / 8.42 / 3.43 J**, about twice the primary over idle. *Expectation stated before the data:* that convention counts every shire's
   request (4 per A tile, 8 per B tile), about six times the distinct bytes here, and its DRAM row read high on E37 and E101's DRAM arm.
5. **The die.** Arm A: E136's frozen burst law die_max = die_start + 0.1445 (P_burst − 21.5) (0.82 °C rms in E136, 0.98 in E146) and its
   sustained law; arm B: the burst rise at M10's 0.12 °C a W [0.09, 0.20] (card 1's own fit), the sustained die REPORTED only.

## 5. The given-seconds formulas (`forecast_e190.given()`; the scorer calls its `W_at()`)

For each cell, at its **measured** seconds t, rest P_rest and mean clock f: **J over idle = W(f) t; J with the card at rest = (P_rest + W(f)) t**,
W(f) the frozen W over idle at the measured clock (arm A: E136's `p_at` between the frozen levels at 600, 700 and 800 MHz; arm B: its 600 MHz point).
Self-check of code: `given()` at every cell's own point reproduces its frozen joules (worst 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ J).

## 6. Rules (`score_e190.py`; scored on the bursts unless named; a cell with no run is NO DATA, never a failure)

The measured joules of a run: over idle (p_work − p_rest) t_gemm, with the card at rest p_work t_gemm; t_gemm = Σ wall / Σ passes over the run's
counted launches (arm A: kernel clock at or above 790 MHz; arm B: 590-610 MHz; sustained: inside the last 40 s); p_work the median board W of the
10 Hz samples inside them; p_rest the median of the last 5 s at rest before the run. A cell is the mean of its runs.

| rule | what | registered threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **R0** the ties | every check of C exact or approx; the smoke's cycles an op within 50-250% of the point | a FAIL labels every other verdict |
| **S1** seconds | the cell's t_gemm inside its frozen two-sided band | per cell; PASS if every scored cell passes |
| **S2** J over idle, given the measured seconds | \|measured / given − 1\| | ≤ 10% |
| **S3** J with the card at rest, given the measured seconds and rest | \|measured / given − 1\| | ≤ 5% |
| **S4** J over idle, the whole forecast | the measured value inside its frozen band (bursts and sustained) | per cell |
| **S5** the die, arm A bursts | die_max within 3 °C of E136's law at the measured start, rest and the frozen W | per cell |
| **S6** the clock | arm A bursts at or above 790 MHz; arm A sustained within 30 MHz of the frozen 800; arm B every counted sample in 590-610 MHz | per cell |
| **S7** arm B against arm A scaled by the clock | per format: t_B / (t_A(800) X) within ±4%, X arm A's own T600 / T800 (else the frozen X); J_over,B / (κ J_over,A(800) j) within ±15%, j the frozen 600-to-800 J ratio | THE SAME SCALED if both hold in at least 2 of 3 formats with at most one miss; else DIFFERS |

## 7. The plan (`plan_e190.py` → `plan-e190.json`, `plan-e190.txt`)

## 9. What this cannot say (stated before the data)

- **fp32's seconds rest on an extrapolation**: the law was calibrated up to 128 fresh lines a shire-op (`c16s`), and E190's fp32 has 192. The
  band keeps "no cost" open at its fast end; S8 says which end the card is at.
- **Arm B's watts are a transfer**: aifoundry2's switching at 600 MHz, which E136 never measured directly (its law, re-read at 800 MHz), times
  E37's card ratio. The ledger arm disagrees by about a factor of two; one of the two is badly wrong for this load, and E190 says which.
- **One session per arm, no interval between sessions**; E146's replicate of E136 (0.2-0.4% in seconds, ±0.6% in W over idle) is the only
  evidence of the spread between sessions, on one card.
- **The governor and the room:** arm A's sustained clock (800 MHz frozen) holds only while the die stays under the 65 °C trip; a warm room
  moves it (E146's `F6` held at 745 MHz).
- **Not the vendor's binary**, not a library GEMM: the vendor's pattern built from public sources (E136). Not the A100 comparison itself.
