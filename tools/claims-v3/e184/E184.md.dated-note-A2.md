## A2-1. E184-C: an intercept, and the reference level set aside (the verifier's fault 2)

**What was wrong.** The scan's reference, n = 144 on M_s shires, sits at about 33.59 W, where code 1024 falls for a 12.0 V input. The
registered statistic, y = b (n - 144) + d ([n > k] - [144 > k]) with no intercept, pins the line at the reference. Under the registration's
own dither model (one code's transition displaced by a code, dithered by the board's 2.12-code spread: the mean reading moves by at most
0.0061 W around the carry, which section 5.2 calls harmless), the reference's reading error appears in every other level's contrast, and the
registered statistic reads SEEN in **0.519 and 0.528** of 1,000 draws (both signs, the carry at 12.0 V; base noise), and **0.587 and 0.998**
for a uniform 5 and 10 mW at every level but the reference. Section 7 would read each as "a step near code 1024".

**The change.** E184-C is read by `scan_fit_a2` (this note's `power_e184_a2.py`, run by the scorer from that file's text): the quads at the
reference level (A and B the same image) are set aside, since they carry no step, and are read as the scan's own null (their mean and 95% t
interval, reported); over the other 24 levels, for each break k between consecutive levels, OLS of **y = c + b (n - 144) + d [n > k]**, T_k =
d / se(d) on N - 3 degrees of freedom, T = max |T_k|. The intercept c takes the reference reading's own error, whatever its source. **SEEN if
T exceeds t\*_A2 = 3.004** (the 95th percentile of T with no step, 4,000 draws; T is free of the noise's scale; the registered statistic's
2.946 recomputes at 2.925 on 4,000 draws). A SEEN whose break lies inside the carry band widened by one scan level (32.78 W less 12 b to
34.46 W plus 12 b) reads "a step near code 1024"; outside it, "a step at <W> W, outside the carry band". The registered statistic is
reported beside, never a verdict.

## A2-2. Which R governs E184-L and E184-P (the verifier's minor 1)

**What was open.** R is the ratio of slopes through the origin. A protocol offset o in every quad moves it by dR/do = **8.03 per watt** for
E184-L and **7.64** for E184-P (at a linear meter), and E184-N's 95% half-width is 10 mW at base noise, so an offset it cannot see moves R by
up to about 0.08. At base noise E184-L's registered R reads HIGH in 0.244, 0.830 and 1.000 of draws at a +5, +10 and +20 mW protocol offset.
The null-corrected R (each slope through the null level's mean) does not move with o, but its interval must carry the null mean's own
uncertainty (s / 6 at 36 quads, about 5 mW, against a 25 mW smallest step): `delta_ratio_nc`, the delta method with that variance in both
slopes and their covariance, resolves LINEAR in only **0.166** of linear draws (E184-L, base) and 0.245 (E184-P). So it cannot govern always.
(The verifier's 0.774 for the null-corrected R is not reproduced: an interval that treats the null's mean as known is narrower, and too narrow.)

**The rule.** **The registered R governs E184-L's and E184-P's verdict words, unless the part's own null level reads OFFSET by E184-N's rule**
(E184-N itself for E184-L; part P's 24 quads at n = 0 for E184-P, reported as E184-N-P); then the null-corrected R, read by `delta_ratio_nc`
against the same band, governs, and E184-P's METER EXCLUDED is read on that interval's lower end. Both readings are always reported, with
the part's own null and a flag where they disagree (one LINEAR and the other LOW or HIGH, or LOW against HIGH).

| E184-L, base noise (2,000 draws) | registered R | A2-2's governing reading |
|---|---|---|
| linear, no offset | LINEAR 0.987 | LINEAR 0.953 (the null reads OFFSET by chance 0.035) |
| protocol offset +5 / +10 / +20 mW | HIGH 0.244 / 0.830 / 1.000 | HIGH 0.223 / 0.504 / 0.044 |
| small steps read 0.8 / 0.9 | LOW 1.000 / 0.973 | LOW 0.993 / 0.961 |
| +5 mW at the switched levels only (not in the null) | HIGH 0.234 | HIGH 0.240: an effect of the switched levels, read as the board's response |

E184-P (base): linear LINEAR 0.997 -> 0.952; +20 mW HIGH 1.000 -> 0.118; small steps at 0.8 LOW 1.000 -> 0.987. Under twice the noise the
null rarely reads OFFSET and the two columns nearly agree (`power-e184-a2.json` > R). Offsets of 5-10 mW stay below what E184-N resolves;
the design's guard against them is the quad's symmetry (A B B A against B A A B, the same protocol in the null quads), as registered.

## A2-3. The words of E184-L and E184-P, and the bound of METER EXCLUDED (the verifier's minor 2)

E184-L and E184-P read **the board's response to the number of minions switched**. At 32 shires the levels 3-19 switch one minion in each of
3-19 shires and 30-121 switch one to four in a shire (`e184_common.selected_minions`, round robin); at part P's ~16 shires the levels 3-13 put
one in a shire and 19 and 121 more. So a power effect per shire (the first switched minion of a shire costing more or less) reads LOW or
HIGH exactly as a meter nonlinearity would. **A LOW or HIGH names the meter only when the minion rail's own R** (the registered statistic on
the rail's quad contrasts, E184-RAIL) **reads LINEAR**; the rail reading the same way names the power, not the meter; otherwise the words
are "not separated: the meter, or power not proportional to the minion count". The rail is not expected to resolve that ratio (its 1.06 s
filter spans a quad's 1.6 s blocks: on the kit's synthetic sessions its interval runs from -0.5 to 2.6, kit note 1's self-test V2), so a LOW
or HIGH will most likely read "not separated". **METER EXCLUDED excludes a static compression** of fadd.ps steps of 3-19 minions at E178's
operating point; a compression that depends on the power's waveform (TensorFMA's differs from fadd.ps's) is not excluded, so section 7's
"E178's int8 pair is the card's answer, not the meter's" holds against a static compression only.

## A2-5. A refresh rule for uncaught partial dips (the verifier's minor 4)

**The rule.** Inside every block, after E82 amendment A2's dip rule and the 0.35 s discard (the registered reader, unchanged), **a kept
refresh more than 0.5 W from the median of that block's kept refreshes is set aside** (the board's reading; the rail's block reads the same
refreshes). A block still needs 4 refreshes. 0.5 W is 7 sd of the zeros bursts' refresh noise and 4.8 sd of random's; a block's operands do
not change inside it.

**Checked on committed data** (`a2-check-e184.json` > `e180_fadd_bursts`): over E180's 162 fadd.ps bursts read as 324 E184 blocks, the rule
sets aside **5 of 2,649** block refreshes: the four partial dips the verifier counted (1.30, 1.74, 1.955 and 2.34 W below the block's median)
and one more 0.72 W low; none in a zeros burst; no block falls under 4 refreshes.

**What it saves.** With that rate (5.2% of quads carry one such refresh, which moves a contrast by 0.05-0.17 W), the registered reader's powers
fall: E184-N NULL 0.94 -> **0.63**, E184-L LINEAR 0.99 -> **0.81**, E184-P LINEAR 1.00 -> 0.95; E184-J's and E184-C's false alarms barely
move (`power-e184-a2.json` > O, base noise). With A2-5 the dips are set aside and the base model's powers stand.
