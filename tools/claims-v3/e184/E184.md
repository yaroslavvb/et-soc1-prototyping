## 2. The design

### 2.1 Arm S, aifoundry3 card 0 at 600 MHz pinned (its boot service's TDP 0; nothing set): the staircase

- **The workload.** The energy catalogue's fadd.ps loop (`workloads/enercat`, mode 160: `RUN(8 x "fadd.ps f(8+i), f(i), f((i+1)%8)")`)
  on both harts of every minion of the active shires, `--window 240000000` (0.4 s launches at 600 MHz). **The base:** every hart's eight
  source registers hold zeros. **A step:** n minions' two harts each get their own random [0.5, 2) words (the catalogue's `random`
  range), the rest stay zero. The issue rate, the clock and every instruction stay the same; only the operands change, and only on the
  host (the 256 B block per hart the kernel loads at each launch). No new kernel, no new kernel class, no new device command.
- **The host-only change** (the kit's, built on the box into its own tree; nothing committed is edited): the host takes a schedule file
  (`<label> <launches> <image>` per segment) and an operand image of 2,048 x 256 B (one block per hart), copies each segment's image to
  the card before its launches, and checks a stop file before every launch. Everything else is the catalogue's host.
- **Which minions** ([`e184_common.py`](e184_common.py)): the active shires are the first M of a farthest-point order over the mesh
  (`shire_order()`: 0, 31, 10, 20, 13, 5, 11, 15, ...), so every mask spreads over the die. The n switched minions go round robin over the
  mask's shires, inside a shire in one fixed slot order (`random.Random(18401)`). Levels nest: level n's minions are the first n of
  level n + 1's, each hart with its own words (`random.Random(18402 x 4096 + hart)`). Every image arm S can use, 467 of them, is
  made by rule ([`make_images_e184.py`](make_images_e184.py)) and its sha256 frozen in [`images-e184.sha256`](images-e184.sha256).
- **One quad = one host process**, under `timeout 10` (the lab's rule: no device held over 10 s): a settle segment S (the A image, 2
  launches), then four blocks of 4 launches (1.6 s each), **A B B A or B A A B**, the orientation alternating from quad to quad (balanced
  inside every level and visit). The base load runs through the whole process: there is no idle between A and B, only the launch gaps of
  about 2 ms. About 8.3 s a quad with the process's open and close.
- **The reading of a block:** the mean of the board's distinct SP refreshes (`board_w`) sampled from 0.35 s after the block's first
  launch to the end of its last, after E82 amendment A2's dip rule; at least 4 kept refreshes, else the quad is set aside with the
  reason. **The quad's contrast:** (B1 + B2 - A1 - A2) / 2. A linear drift inside the process cancels in every quad; the drift's curvature
  cancels between the two orientations.
- **The parts, in order** ([`plan_e184.py`](plan_e184.py), [`plan-e184.json`](plan-e184.json)):

| part | passes | mask | A / B | levels n (minions), quads | what it reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L, the main ladder | 18410-18412: visits up, down, up | 32 shires (base about 35.5 W) | zeros / level n | the null n = 0 (12 a visit); 3, 5, 8, 12, 19 (16 each a visit); 30, 48, 76, 121 (4 each a visit) | E184-N, E184-L, E184-J |
| Cal | 18413 | 16 and 24 shires | zeros / zeros | one null quad each | the base line, for M_op and M_s |
| P, the int8 pair's operating point | 18414-18416: up, down, up | M_op (about 16 shires) | zeros / level n | 0 (8 a visit); 3, 6, 13, 19 (16 each); 121 (8) | E184-P |
| C, the scan across the 1024 carry | 18417-18418: up, down | M_s (about 23 shires, base about 32.4 W) | the reference n = 144 / level n | 0, 12, ..., 288 (25 levels, 6 quads each a sweep) | E184-C |

  - **The ladder's levels** are a geometric series, round(3 x (121/3)^(k/8)), k = 0..8: from 3 minions (about +0.025 W, under one code
    of the board's current reading) to 121 (about +1.0 W); "small" are the steps under 0.2 W. **Part P's steps** are 0.5, 1, 2 and 3
    times E178's int8 pair as measured (+0.0532 W) in minions at E180's 8.256 mW a minion, with the ladder's top step as its large step.
  - **The calibration rule** (written before any datum): a, z are the least-squares line of the base reading (the A blocks' mean) on the
    mask's shire count through Cal's two quads and the last ladder visit's A blocks at 32 shires; **M_op = round((30.132 - a) / z)**,
    clipped to 10-24 (30.132 W: E178's int8 4s and 5s board level); **M_s = round((32.4 - a) / z)**, clipped to 16-30 (32.4 W: 0.38 W
    under the carry band's low end). The runner writes `cal.json` and makes the chosen masks' images, each checked against the frozen
    list, before part P.
  - **Why this order:** the ladder (the main question) first; the operating point (the int8 question) second; the scan last, so a
    budget stop cuts the scan.

### 2.4 The board meter, from the public firmware

`board_w` is the service processor's copy of the PMIC's `POWER_IN` register, refreshed once per SP pass (`thermal_pwr_mgmt.c` at
`836a4ab60`, as `docs/energy-manual/09-method.md.dated-note-1.md` reads it). In the public PMIC firmware (et-boards `e5e0cf2`):
- `adcIMON12VInterruptCallback` (`power_manager_event.c` lines 199-210): `imon12vMA = ((result << 9) x 1000 + den/2) / den`, with den the
  float `IMON_DENOMINATOR` (`adc.h` lines 19-35: 1.0 V full scale over 4,096 counts, a 1 mOhm sense, 6.67 uA/mV, 12.4 kOhm, the fixed
  `CUR_ADJ_FACTOR` 1.0787) times 512, cast to 187,101: **2.73649 mA a code**; then `instmW = (imon12vMA x vmon12vMV + 500) / 1000` and
  `POWER_IN = instmW / 10`. One code is **32.84 mW at 12.0 V**.
- `system_init.c` lines 251-259: the internal 1.0 V reference, 64 conversions accumulated with ADJRES 4, so each IMON result is a
  64-conversion average on the 12-bit scale the driver's 4,096 assumes. The ADC interrupts come every 6-10 ms (line 156).
- **Code 1024** (bit 10 of the 12-bit reading) is **2,802 mA**: 32.78, 33.62 or 34.46 W at a 12 V input of 11.7, 12.0 or 12.3 V. No stock
  READ returns the 12 V input (the SP reads `PMIC_I2C_INPUT_VOLTAGE` only in an error handler, `pmic_controller.c` line 785), so the band
  32.8-34.5 W is the carry's location. Part P (30.1-31.1 W) lies under the band, part C (32.4-34.8 W) across it, the ladder (35.5-36.6 W)
  above it.
- *Version caveat:* the cards run PMIC 1.5.0 (aifoundry3) and 1.3.0 (card 1); only runtime 1.7.0 is public. E184 reads the running
  meter's behaviour on the card; the constants are a forecast's inputs, not a claim about the running build.

## 3. The regulators' phase shedding: no stock READ returns it

The question: a multiphase regulator that adds or drops a phase as its load changes would change the board's 12 V draw for the same rail
power, and could make a small step read wrong. Read first, if a stock READ offers it.
- **The DM command table** (et-platform `836a4ab60`, `device-api/include/management-api/device_mgmt_api_spec.h` lines 41-129; the lab's
  classification of all 92 commands in `tools/claims-v3/dmcmd_table.json`): no command returns a regulator's phase count or phase-shedding
  setting. The READs that reach the PMIC are the voltage reads (`GET_MODULE_VOLTAGE`, `GET_ASIC_VOLTAGE`), the board power and its average,
  the PMBus statistics (each rail's current, voltage and power as average, minimum and maximum), the temperature, the thresholds, the board
  type and the firmware hash.
- **The SP's PMIC register map** (`device-bootloaders/src/ServiceProcessorBL2/include/pmic_hal.h`, registers 0x01-0x33): firmware hash,
  board type, input voltage, input power, average power, system temperature, the power and temperature alarms, interrupts, faults,
  watchdog, reset, GPIO, the PMBus statistics port, firmware management, each rail's and each minion group's voltage, FRU. **None is a
  phase register.** The SP's raw PMIC register reads (`I2C_PMIC_Read` in `pmic_controller.c`, and the test framework's own
  `SPIO_I2C_PMIC_Read_Word_Cmd_Handler`) are reached by no DM command.
- **The public PMIC firmware** (et-boards `e5e0cf2`, `TPS53681.c` lines 22-33): the minion and NoC controller's initialisation writes
  `CLEAR_FAULT`, `ON_OFF_CONFIG`, `PHASE 0x80` (all phases selected for the commands that follow), `OT_FAULT_RESPONSE`, `OPERATION` and the
  boot voltages; nothing reads or sets phase shedding. Whatever the regulators do is their own stored configuration, which no stock READ
  reaches.

**So E184 does without it:** the zeros base runs through every quad (the minion rail well above light load, the board at or above 30 W in
every part), so A and B differ by at most +1.0 W at a fixed load; the null step reads the protocol with nothing changed; and the ladder is
read up, down and up, where a load-triggered phase change would show as a jump between levels (E184-J) or as a difference between the up
and down visits (reported).

### 5.1 The size of a step

- **One minion switched** adds **8.256 mW** on the board (E180's fadd.ps random less zeros over 1,024 minions, se 0.043 mW), band
  **8.090-8.669 mW** (E180 +- 1.96 se, widened 1% below and above to the larger of the catalogue's 8.492 mW and E180 with the most of
  its own leakage a step carries, +3.97%). On the minion rail 6.716 mW (E180), 0.813 of the board.
- **The levels** (watts above the base, P): the ladder 0.0248, 0.0413, 0.0661, 0.0991, 0.1569, 0.2477, 0.3963, 0.6275, 0.9990; part P
  0.0248, 0.0495, 0.1073, 0.1569 and 0.9990; the scan -1.189 to +1.189 W about its reference, 0.0991 W a level.
- **The rail in a quad:** its 1.06 s filter, one pass late, keeps **0.380** of a step across the quad's blocks, so 2.55 mW a minion.
- **The operating points:** part P's base about 30.13 W (M_op about 16 by the straight line from E180's idle card, 24.4 W, to its zeros
  base on 32 shires, 35.5 W); the scan's base about 32.4 W (M_s about 23). The calibration decides on the day.

**The forecasts:** E184-L **LINEAR**; E184-P **LINEAR** and **METER EXCLUDED**; E184-N **NULL**; E184-J **NONE SEEN**; E184-C **NONE SEEN**.
Why NONE SEEN at the carry: each reading averages 64 conversions, and the board's own spread between refreshes is 2.1 codes (0.0696 W
over 32.84 mW); a single code's transition displaced by a whole code then moves the mean reading by at most **0.187 of a code** (6.1 mW)
under Gaussian dither, half a code 0.094 (`forecast-e184.json` > `carry.dither_bound_lsb`). E184-C is built to catch the worse case the
dither model would miss: a code-sized step in the mean reading.

**What E184-P answers:** E178 read its int8 pair at 0.29 of M10's frozen forecast. If the board read small steps at that point at 0.29 of
their size, E184-P's interval would lie near 0.29 and the meter would stand as the cause; METER EXCLUDED says it cannot be.

## 7. What it would change

- **E184-L and E184-P LINEAR, METER EXCLUDED:** the board reads steps from 0.025 W up in proportion at both operating points, so E178's
  int8 pair (+0.053 W against +0.186 W) is the card's answer, not the meter's; the one-number registrations (E182) can trust the board's
  small differences. LOW at P: the meter compresses small steps where E178 read its pair, and E178's size must be re-read with that gain.
- **E184-C SEEN:** the board's current reading has a step near code 1024; every pair that straddles 32.8-34.5 W carries it, and the kits
  should keep pairs on one side.
- **E184-J SEEN, or a difference between up and down visits:** a load-dependent change in the board's 12 V draw (a phase change or the
  meter) at that level; it bounds which load ranges a small difference may span.
- **E184-M SUPPORTED:** card 1's SRAM rail carries about 1.43 times its reading; the energy manual's card-1 SRAM rows, the off-rail fit's
  card-1 coefficient and the anatomy's card-1 SRAM lines need a dated note, in a records round. REFUTED: the two-phase reading of Q150 is
  wrong and its 1.4x correction is withdrawn.
