## A1-1. E184-T is read with E58's filter model

**What the kit's self-test found.** The registration reads each B2 burst with "a first-order step one SP pass (0.16 s) late" from the
launch edge, with a level and a size and nothing else (E184.md section 5.3). [`a1_check_e184.py`](a1_check_e184.py) ran that reader on
E58's committed card-1 bursts at the 100 ms sampler, the only committed bursts of this rail (`logs/a1-check-e184.txt`): on the twelve
SRAM-dominated D bursts it read the SRAM rail's tau at a median **0.625 s** on the rise and **0.62 s** on the fall, against E58's committed
**0.525 s** (by kind D; 0.54 and 0.52 s rise and fall), and on the twelve H and M bursts the minion rail's at 1.11 and 1.17 s against
E58's 1.08 s: about 0.1 s long, so a rail at the forecast's own 0.54 s would read 0.62-0.63 s, within 0.02 s of the SRAM band's top, and
a true forecast would often read NOT RESOLVED or OUTSIDE. Three things the registered form leaves out make the difference, each in E58's model: a
pass's time is half a sampling interval before the first sample that shows it (`deconv.passes`); the rails are published one SP pass after
the board, so a reading describes the previous pass's time, less E58's fitted d (`deconv.eff_times`); and a rail drifts with the die's
leakage through a burst, which E58 fits as a line (`taulib.fit`: y = c + e (te - t_on) + A sq).

**The change.** E184-T reads each burst with E58's model, in pure Python ([`tau_a1_e184.py`](tau_a1_e184.py) `fit_burst`): the passes
and effective times as `deconv` makes them, with E58's own d for card 1's rails from its committed `report.json` (SRAM +0.03 s, minion
-0.02, NoC -0.03); c, e and A by least squares; the shared tau on a 0.01 s grid from 0.10 to 3.00 s, then the rise and the fall split as
`taulib.split_fit` splits them (the fall starting from the level the rise reached), each on a 0.01 s grid within 0.25 s of the shared
tau; the window E58's (2.5 s before t_on to 8 s after t_off, or 0.05 s before the next burst). **Checked on the committed data**
(`logs/a1-check-e184.txt`, and the self-test's R2): E58's twelve D bursts read the SRAM rail at a median **0.525 s** (rise 0.525, fall
0.52; E58 0.525 by kind, 0.54 and 0.52 rise and fall), and its twelve H and M bursts read the minion rail at **1.07 s** (rise 1.06, fall
1.07; E58 1.08, and 1.06-1.07 by kind).

**Unchanged:** the six B2 bursts, the mean over them with its 95% t interval, the bands (SRAM 0.44-0.64 s; minion 0.90-1.35 s; NoC
0.90-1.25 s), the words PASS, OUTSIDE and NOT RESOLVED, and the forecast (SRAM 0.54 s).

## A1-2. E184-T's verdicts are the SRAM rail's

B2's load, `tstore/scp/random` on 32 shires, moves card 1's minion rail by about **1.86 W** and its NoC rail by **0.12 W** (the
catalogue's card-1 row; SRAM 6.17 W). A filter's time constant is not read on so small a step: on the kit's synthetic sessions, with
noiseless rails and a true 1.08 s, the minion and NoC rises read 1.15-1.35 s, where E58's drift term and a 2 s burst's rise trade off.
So **E184-T is the SRAM rail's rise and fall**, the rail H-MIX is about; the minion and NoC rails are read the same way and reported beside
with their bands, never a verdict.
