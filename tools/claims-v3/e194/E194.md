## 1. The question

**Does the frozen ledger transfer to a third card, under its own governor?** The same four programs and six configurations as E189
(a copy through DRAM on zeros and on random bytes, SHA-256 of 2,048 messages, a merge sort on sorted and on shuffled input, and the
sparse-parity instance (544, 4, 0.39, m 1,792, seed 3)), forecast before the card by the load ledger v2 as frozen, on aifoundry2's
card 0 as its governor runs them: at 800 MHz, its top point.

The ledger's card `aifoundry2` has its own idle law, its own byte, instruction and tensor prices in `frozen-v2.json` and its own
column in the energy catalogue. The ledger's form scales every per-op, per-instruction and awake price by `v2 = (V/V600)^2`, and
`frozen-v2.json` carries aifoundry2's volts at 600 and 800 MHz. So E194 is two tests in one:
- the ledger on a third card;
- the first blind test of the ledger v2 at 800 MHz on a whole program. The ledger's only 800 MHz rows so far (E136's) were
  differences of two loads, "so the card at rest and the clock's static part cancel; v2 has no validated 800 MHz static term"
  (`tools/ledger/loads_ledger_v2.py`). Here nothing cancels.

Each configuration is scored twice, as E189's: given its measured seconds (score 1: the prices and the counts) and with its seconds
forecast too (score 2: the whole ledger). Both footings are always reported: joules over idle, and joules with the card at rest.

### 3.1 The clock (frozen here, before any datum)

**The forecast is at 800 MHz, the governor's top point, the clock every counted launch of E190's arm A read on this card.** The
governor's rule gives a running kernel that point while the shires' mean is at most 65 C and the board's ~1 s average is under
65 W. Every E194 configuration's predicted board power is under 50 W at its point (section 11), and E194's die gate keeps
each start at or below 57 C. What the clock changes, by the frozen ledger's own form and nothing else:

| what | at 600 MHz (E189, E191) | at 800 MHz (E194) |
|---|---|---|
| the prices the ledger's form scales: the tensor term, the instruction classes (sparse parity's vector-integer class; the catalogue rows of the plain programs and of v2c, all measured at 600 MHz) and the awake term | x 1 | x v2(800) = (0.6174109375 / 0.5174)^2 = **1.42395** |
| the byte paths (scratchpad, L2, DRAM, the copy's write-back) | as frozen | as frozen: bytes carry no v2 in the ledger's form |
| seconds made of cycles: SHA-256's and the sorts' point, fast end and slow end; sparse parity's `model_s` and `est_s` | cycles / 600 MHz | the same cycles / 800 MHz (x 0.75) |
| the copies' seconds (27 GB/s stores, 76 GB/s loads) and the L2's 27.4 G lines/s | byte and line rates | the same: no minion clock is in them |
| the awake hart-cycles from a measured t_k | N x 600 MHz x t_k | N x 800 MHz x t_k (`spp_eval()` reads the load's own clock) |
| cycle counts, instruction and byte counts, the int8 loop's cycles an op, the probes' bytes a cycle | counted | the same counts |
| the card at rest | the card's idle law | aifoundry2's idle law: its idle is at 600 MHz, the governor's idle point, so the law stands |
| the clock's own base at 800 MHz | - | **no term**: the frozen ledger has none (below) |

**What the clock does not change and the ledger cannot see.** The card draws more at 800 MHz than its per-instruction prices times
v2 say if it has a base of its own at that clock (the higher voltage's leakage, and whatever runs with the clock and no work). E136's
relation on this card (`tools/claims-v3/e136/predictions-e136.json` > `relation.B`: E101's relation through E119) puts its busy base at
25.456 W at 600 MHz and 33.610 W at 800 MHz, **8.154 W more**. Over each configuration's forecast seconds that is 3.1-5.5 J a launch:
as much as or more than the whole forecast J over idle of SHA-256 and the sorts. If that base is real for these loads and no part of it is
inside the v2-scaled prices, the measured J over idle will read far above the primary forecast, and E194-S1, S1T and S2T will fail
on most configurations. This is stated before the data; the reading "with E136's base" is REPORTED beside the primary (section 6) and
decides nothing.

**The governor's climb.** At each process's start the card is at its 600 MHz idle point. E190 arm A's 84 processes on this card
reached 800 MHz 0.014-1.325 s after their first launch began (median 0.852 s; `desk/climb-e190.txt`), and in its 12 burst processes
no sample left 800 MHz after that. The forecast prices every launch at 800 MHz and does not model the climb. By desk arithmetic
(REPORTED, `forecast-e194.json` > `readings_reported`), a 0.852 s climb at 600 MHz at the start of a burst of E189's K launches does
13.5-18.4% of the burst's work at 600 MHz. That lowers the instruction-bound programs' J over idle a launch by 2.9-4.9% (those
instructions are without v2) and the copies' by under 0.01%. It adds 0.024-0.030 s to each launch's wall seconds. Score 1's rest term
uses the measured t_w, so it follows the climb. The pass rule allows the climb (section 8.2), and t_k comes from the burst's last
launch, after it.

**The interpreter is named:** `/usr/bin/python3` 3.12.3 wrote `forecast-e194.json` on aifoundry2, the only python3 on that box;
`--check` passes under it. Every float in the frozen files is rounded to 6 significant digits, as E189's and E191's are. No other
interpreter was run: under any other it is NOT RUN.

### 5.2 The given-seconds forecast, frozen as a formula (score 1 evaluates it; nothing is refitted)

`forecast_e194.given()` at each pass's **measured** t_k (the kernel's seconds: the slowest minion's cycles over **800 MHz**, from the
burst's last launch, which section 8.2 requires to have run at 800 MHz), t_w (the wall seconds a launch: the burst's span over its
launches, `1 / solves_per_s`) and T0 (`die_c.before`), as E189.md 5.2 defines them otherwise.
- **The plain programs:** J over idle = the frozen point (copy 5.14484 / 6.92768 J, SHA-256 3.49970, sort 3.82300 / 4.67683): counts
  times aifoundry2's prices times v2(800), with no seconds in it (the awake rule leaves no waiting hart).
- **Sparse parity:** J over idle (t_k) = 7.88580 J + 2.75798 pJ x [max(0, 8.192e11 t_k - 1.90201e11) + max(0, 8.192e11 t_k -
  1.20260e11)] (hart-cycles at 800 MHz; 2.75798 pJ is the awake price 1.93685 pJ times v2). Above t_k = 0.2322 s this is 7.02955 +

### 8.2 A pass counts if

- the reducer's hard checks pass (its windows filled, no foreign device process, its host's own status), **except its clock line,
  which THE CLOCK RULE reads instead**;
- **THE CLOCK RULE:** every busy sample of the reducer's window [lo + 0.5 s, hi] **at or after lo + 1.5 s** reads **800 MHz** (lo
  and hi are the burst's first launch's start and its last launch's end; the 1.5 s allow the governor's climb from its idle point,
  which took at most 1.325 s in E190 arm A's 84 processes); **and** the burst's last launch ran at 800 MHz by its own count: its
  slowest minion's cycles over its wall seconds read 780-810 MHz (by the same count E189's 24 last launches on aifoundry3, pinned at
  600 MHz, read 599.5-599.8 MHz: 0.999 of the clock, so a launch at 800 MHz reads about 799 and one at 600 or 700 MHz falls
  outside). The reducer's hard line "minion clock in busy samples: [...] MHz (the governor moved it)" is read by this rule, not as a
  failure;
- the program's own checks pass;
- the runner did not set it aside.

A pass that fails the clock rule is set aside (the clock), never a failed prediction. A burst the host's budget cut short is scored
on the launches it ran and flagged SHORT. A configuration with fewer than 2 good passes reads **NO DATA**, never a failed prediction.

### 8.3 The rules, with their words and thresholds (E189's, renamed)

For each configuration c, e_c = (the mean over its passes of the forecast) / (the mean over its passes of the measured value) - 1;
the pass is the unit of replication.

| rule | words | threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **E194-S1** | score 1, given the measured seconds: J over idle a launch | within 10% on at least 5 of the 6 configurations, **and** the rms of the six errors at most 10% |
| **E194-S1T** | score 1, given the measured seconds: J with the card at rest a launch | within 5% on at least 5 of 6 |
| **E194-S2** | score 2, with the seconds forecast: the measured kernel seconds (the mean of the passes' t_k) | inside the frozen two-sided band **and** 0.85 <= mean t_k / point <= 1.15, on at least 5 of 6 |
| **E194-S2T** | score 2, with the seconds forecast: J with the card at rest a launch, at the forecast seconds and the forecast die (the frozen point) | within 15% on at least 5 of 6 |

- **Verdicts** follow E189.md 8.3. PASS if at least 5 configurations meet the rule (and, for S1, the rms over those scored is at
  most 10%). FAIL if 2 or more miss it, or, for S1, if at least 5 are scored and their rms exceeds 10%. Otherwise NOT DECIDED. NO
  DATA counts neither way. A FAIL is reported as FAIL.
- **S2's ratio** is mean t_k / point, as the scorer tests it (`ok_S2`). The `e_t` reported beside it does not decide S2 (E189.md 8.3).

## 9. Replication and order

Four independent passes a configuration, one host process each: 24 scored passes. The order is four rounds, each holding every
configuration once, shuffled by `random.Random(194)` and written out in `plan-e194.json` (the runner reads it and never re-draws it):
1. sparse parity, SHA-256, sort sorted, copy random, copy zeros, sort shuffled;
2. copy zeros, copy random, sparse parity, sort shuffled, sort sorted, SHA-256;
3. sparse parity, copy zeros, sort shuffled, copy random, SHA-256, sort sorted;
4. sort shuffled, SHA-256, copy zeros, sort sorted, copy random, sparse parity.

Each pass is energy.sh's form, as E189.md 9:
- 8 s of idle;
- ONE host process, a burst of K launches;
- 10 s of idle;
- the sampler at 10 Hz throughout (`ettelem sample --every-ms 100`, no statistics reset);
- then the die gate before the next pass.

## 14. What this cannot say (stated before the data)
