## 1. The question, and the answer frozen here

How many joules does one pass over the 60,000-image MNIST training split in int8 cost on an ET-SoC-1, **over idle and
with the card at rest**, per pass, per image and per multiply-add, and how many **seconds** does it take, on aifoundry3
card 0 at its pinned 600 MHz?

cost 3.58% more than trained ones: the activations they produce are less sparse (54-57% zeros against 55-68%), and the zero gate turns off
fewer gate decisions (section 4.2).

## 2. The program (by path and sha256; `workloads/mnistmlp/`, as landed with E202's program commit)

16 in layer 5 with zero weights; **11,153,408 multiply-adds an image issued, 11,056,640 real**; a group of 16 images is
10,892 TensorIMA8A32 operations (16 x 64 x 16, uint8 activations x int8 weights -> int32; UA = 1), 449 chunks (a chunk:

6.692 x 10^11 multiply-adds issued**. Every working minion's hart 0 takes one group at a time through all five layers

activations written back with whole 32-byte `fswg.ps` stores; the output layer's first-maximum argmax in scalar code;
one 64-byte label line a group (16 labels, the group, the epoch, a checksum of the 160 logits). The program's README
gives the rest.

### 4.2 The tensor-data price: THE KINDS RULE (the design's M10 route is not reachable here)

M10's frozen predictor (`docs/reports/data/2026-11-04-q142m-m10/predict_m10.py`) needs, for a new tile pair, its
features from the lab's RTL toggle bench (`rtl-sim/fma_toggle_v2`), whose recorded build is not on this box. So the D
(M10's data-dependent price) of E202's operands is set by a **registered rule from committed kinds**, the ledger v2's own
precedent for a kind M10 has no row of (frozen-v2.json's `fp32.small_int`: "the mean of M10's held-out predictions for
its three nearest kinds, their spread as part of the band"):

1. **The tile pairs E202 issues** ([`desk/tiles_e202.py`](desk/tiles_e202.py) -> `desk/tiles-e202.json`; measured on the
   desk, `/usr/bin/python3` 3.12.3, numpy 1.26.4): the model's own functions build every (A, B) pair exactly as the
   kernel issues them, for 8 groups chosen by a rule (g = floor(i x 3,750 / 8), i = 0..7): 87,136 pairs a configuration.
   Per pair, the lab's two tile numbers (`tools/claims-v3/kinds/make_kinds.py` `two_numbers`, equal to this script's
   vectorised form on every 97th pair, 899 of 899): **g**, the share of the op's 2,048 gate decisions the int8 zero gate
   turns off (an all-zero A word, or eight zero bytes of a B column pair), and **b**, the input-bus bits flipped an op.
2. **Per layer**, the mean (g, b); the three committed aifoundry3 int8 kinds (all 97 with M10 rows) nearest by
   d = sqrt((g - g_k)^2 + ((b - b_k) / 69,312)^2), 69,312 = `int8.full`'s b; **D = the mean of their M10 held-out
   predictions** (`freeze_ledger_v2.kind_entry`, unchanged).
3. **The band:** [their least - h, their largest + h], clipped at 0, h = max(M10's int8 half-width 0.310 W, M10's int8
   band_k 1.829 x the rule's own leave-one-out rms) = **1.20 W**. The rule's leave-one-out rms over the 97 committed kinds
   is **0.657 W** (bias +0.04 W, worst 2.63 W), against M10's own held-out rms of 0.175 W on the same kinds: the rule is
   about four times cruder than M10, and its band says so.

## 5. The seconds, forecast (a cycle model of the busiest shire's rounds; two-sided by rule)

The cycle counter is exact at 600 MHz, so **the seconds are measured first** and scored as their own number (S3);
the joules are scored given them (S1, S1T) and with them forecast (S2, S2T). A launch's kernel seconds follow its busiest
shire: t_k(R) = busiest(R) x (cycles a round) / 600 MHz + (scp: the resident copy and two barriers, 89,119 cycles); a
round is its 32 minions in lockstep through the 449 chunks:

| part of a round (cycles) | point | fast end | slow end |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| the tile loop: 10,892 ops x c_op | 4,224,210 (387.8 an op) | 3,475,390 (319.1) | 6,412,460 (588.7) |
| the rest of the counted instructions | 543,070 | 282,752 | 759,189 |
| 449 shire barriers | 239,317 (533 each) | 104,617 (233) | 374,017 (833) |
| the round's input copy | 107,617 | 0 | 116,841 |
| stack and table lines waited at the L2 | 0 | 0 | 151,890 |
| **a round** | **5,114,210** | **3,862,760** | **7,814,400** |

- **c_op, the point:** sparse parity's fitted streamed-A loop (`workloads/sparseparity/tools/cycle_model.py` FITTED,
  C_OP_str 378.2 x (1 + KAPPA_T 0.02297 x U)), the loop E202's is (A private and streamed, B shared through TenB), and
  the model E189 scored within 0.3% on a never-run instance (sparse parity: 0.5131 s measured against 0.5147 s,
  REPORTED); U, the shire's demand over its 128 B a cycle (2 KiB an op for 32 minions, the chunk and input copies
  landing), solved with the round: **U = 1.1081**, beyond the fit's range (an extrapolation of a small term). **Fast:**
  the int8 tensor unit's own rate (E78: 1,024 x 600 MHz / 1.9255 x 10^9 ops/s = 319.1). **Slow:** E37's private int8
  tiles, both operands private (511.94 cycles an op, 4.00 B a minion-cycle), x 1.15 for 32 minions on one staged tile
  and the copies.
- **The rest of the instructions:** point at each class's catalogue rate x 1.559 (the lone-hart factor: add issues 0.581
  a cycle alone, 0.3727 with both harts; hart 1 returns at once), taken branches and jumps at their both-harts rate (E189
  5.1's rule), at most one a cycle; fast: one a cycle, taken branches and jumps x 1.559; slow: every class at its
  catalogue rate (E189's point, which read scalar code about 30% long on its blind day), plus every stack and table line
  waited at the L2's 27.4 G lines a second (E48).
- **Barriers:** 233 cycles (the 32-minion fast-local-barrier and credit barrier) plus skew 300 [0, 600]. **Input:** 13 KiB
  a minion from DRAM, all 1,024 minions at once, at 76 GB/s [hidden, 70].

**Frozen (per pass; R = 96):** **0.03126 s [0.02361, 0.04776]**; a launch of 96 passes **3.0005 s [2.2662, 4.5846]**;
one pass alone (R = 1, 4 rounds on the busiest shire) about 34 ms, REPORTED only. Every launch lies above the meter's
0.5 s and inside the host's 8 s launch timeout and the 10 s process rule.

## 6. The given-seconds formula (`forecast_e202.Model.given()`; the scorer calls it)

For a burst of one launch of R passes with **measured** t_k (the slowest minion's `hart0_cycles.max` / 600 MHz), t_w (the
wall seconds of the launch, `1 / solves_per_s`) and T0 (the starting die, `die_c.before`): the ledger's `predict()` on
the load of R passes, with **awake hart-cycles = max(0, 1,024 x 600 MHz x t_k - the frozen busy cycles)** (the busy
cycles: every group slot's tile loop and rest of the instructions at the point rates, plus the scp pre-launch copy), the
card at rest over t_w at T0's idle law, and the rise k_leak(T0) x k_rise x the joules over idle. **J over idle a pass =
J_over / R; J with the card at rest a pass = J_total / R.** Everything else in the load is counts times frozen prices, so
the measured seconds move only the awake term and the card at rest. Self-check of code: `given()` at each configuration's
point reproduces the frozen point exactly (difference 0.0).

## 7. Scoring (`score_e202.py`; frozen; self-tested before any card)

### 7.1 The data's form (the kit's runner writes it; the scorer reads only this)

`DATA/smoke/<arm>/host-raw.json`: the host's own `--out` file for each smoke process (T-scp, Z-scp, T-dram, Z-dram, ACC).
`DATA/bursts/<config>/b<k>/` (config `trained-scp`, `random-scp`, `trained-dram`, `random-dram`): `energy.json` (the
reducer's output for the burst: `ok`, `hard`, `flags`, `solves` 1, `solves_per_s`, `board.j_per_solve` (J over idle a
launch: the board average over the busy window above the before bracket, less the leakage law on the measured die),
`board.j_per_solve_total` (J with the card at rest), `die_c.before`), `host-raw.json` (the host's own `--out` file),
`burst.json` (the runner's marks: config, burst k, order index, R, `set_aside`). The host's result is read from its own
file, never from stdout (E191's first session).

### 7.2 What counts

- **The gate (E202-R0):** an arm's smoke passes when its host result reads `core_status` PASS (every record, label line,
  checksum, discard line and operation count, `tensor_error` 0). A `logits_status` FAIL with `core_status` PASS is a
  defect of the debug path (64-byte TensorStore rows, never run on silicon), recorded, not a gate failure. **The scored
  pair is scp** when T-scp and Z-scp both pass, **else dram** when T-dram and Z-dram both pass (the registered fallback),
  else no burst counts: no data, never a failed forecast.
- **A burst counts** if the reducer's hard checks pass, the host reads `status` and `core_status` PASS with groups =
  3,750 R, no padding and ops = 3,750 R x 10,892, R is an allowed R, and the runner did not set it aside. **A
  configuration with fewer than 2 good bursts reads NO DATA**, never a failed prediction.

### 7.3 The rules (each scored configuration: trained and random of the scored pair)

For each configuration, e = (the mean over its bursts of the forecast) / (the mean over its bursts of the measured value) - 1.

| rule | words | threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **E202-S1** | score 1, given the measured seconds: J over idle a pass | \|e\| <= 10% |
| **E202-S1T** | score 1, given the measured seconds: J with the card at rest a pass | \|e\| <= 10% (within 5% REPORTED beside it) |
| **E202-S2** | score 2, with the seconds forecast too: J over idle a pass at the frozen point | \|e\| <= 15% |
| **E202-S2T** | score 2, with the seconds forecast too: J with the card at rest a pass at the frozen point (forecast seconds and die) | \|e\| <= 15% |
| **E202-S3** | the seconds as their own score: the mean measured t_k a pass | inside the frozen two-sided band **and** 0.85 <= mean / point <= 1.15 |

**Verdicts:** a rule reads PASS if every scored configuration with data meets it (at least one has data), FAIL if any
misses it, NO DATA if none has data; a configuration with NO DATA counts neither way, and a rule scored on one
configuration says so. A FAIL is reported as FAIL. The forecast and the band at a burst's R are the frozen model's at
that R (they differ from R = 96's by under 0.6%).

**Reported, never a verdict:** both numbers per pass, per image and per multiply-add (issued and real), measured beside
the forecasts; every burst's errors and their spread; the frozen bands' coverage; the dram pair given its seconds when
the scp pair is scored; the arms v2.1 and epi-only; the split's terms beside the rails where one maps (the scratchpad
terms beside the SRAM rail); the on-card test accuracy (it must equal the model's 98.49%, since its labels are checked).

### 7.4 The scorer's self-test (desk check of code; `selftest-stub-e202.json`)

Stand-in days made from the frozen forecasts by `given()`, each scenario's verdicts stated before the run; **PASS: 8 of 8
as expected**, FAIL and NO DATA verdicts included: **A** as forecast: all five PASS; **B** J over idle 25% high: S1 and S2
FAIL (e -0.2000), S1T and S2T PASS (e -0.0873 trained, -0.0890 random), S3 PASS; **C** 30% slow with the joules
following the given formula: S1, S1T, S2 PASS (S2 e -0.0221, -0.0213), S2T FAIL (-0.1596, -0.1577), S3 FAIL; **D** the scp smoke fails: the dram pair is scored, all PASS;
**E** every smoke fails: all NO DATA; **F** a logits-only smoke failure: the gate passes, all PASS; **G** trained short of
data (three bursts set aside, NO DATA) and random at R = 64, 10% fast with its J over idle 10% high: every rule PASS on
one configuration;
**H** two bursts dropped by failed host checks, random 25% fast: S2T FAIL (+0.1774), S3 FAIL, the rest PASS. The scorer
refuses to run unless `SHA256SUMS-e202-registration` matches and `forecast_e202.py` recomputes the committed

## 8. The plan (`plan_e202.py` -> `plan-e202.json`)

1. **Checks** (the look: `et-who`, `who`, `uptime`, `ps`; the card lock for every device process; `etcfg` and `ettelem
   config` at both ends), the dry listing, the stop-path test, an idle anchor of 2 minutes.
2. **The smoke (the gate, 7.2):** five R = 1 processes: T-scp, Z-scp, T-dram, Z-dram with `--debug-logits` (every label,
   checksum and layer-5 tile checked), and ACC (T-scp on the 10,000-image test split, `--true-labels`: the on-card test
   accuracy). **UA = 1 is a first use on silicon:** a different polarity of bit 21 would fail every smoke check of both
   weight configurations at once, and E202 then stops with no data; the exact UA = 0 fallback (inputs and activations
   XOR 0x80, biases + 128 x the column sums) is not built, and building it would be a new program commit and a dated
   amendment before any datum.
3. **The R rule (per arm, from its own R = 1 smoke):** t_round = `work_cycles.max` / 4 (the busiest shire's rounds at
   R = 1) / 600 MHz; a launch of R passes is predicted at busiest(R) x t_round + `copy_cycles_max` / 600 MHz; **R = 96**
   if that is at most 6 s, else the largest of 16, 32, 48, 64, 80 that is; no burst for the arm if none is. (At the
   forecast: 3.0 s at the point, 4.6 s at the slow end: R = 96.)
4. **The scored bursts:** the scored pair's trained (T) and random (Z), **K = 4 each, in the order T Z Z T T Z Z T**; each
   burst the die gate, 8 s at rest, ONE host process (one launch of R passes, `timeout 10`, never `-k`), 10 s at rest; the
   sampler at 10 Hz throughout, no statistics reset.
5. **REPORTED bursts:** the dram pair, K = 2 each (T Z Z T), only when the scp pair is scored. coop4 is **not built**: the
   design's REPORTED coop4 arm is dropped.
6. An idle anchor of 2 minutes, the closing reads, the release.

**Card time: about 17 card-minutes at the point with no die-gate wait, about 29 at the slow end with a full minute of
die-gate wait before every burst (`plan-e202.json` shows both sums); cap 35:** no burst starts after 33 card-minutes
from the first card command. **The die gate:** a burst
starts only when `minshire[0]` reads 60 C or less (it waits up to 10 minutes, else that burst is skipped: no data); below
52 C does not block (scored given its own T0, labelled so).

## 9. Stops and the predicted peak

**Stops (live samples; aifoundry3's lines as E189 registered them):** board power at or above **65 W**, or `minshire[0]`
at or above **70 C**, ends the queue: the running process finishes (a device process is never killed), nothing further
starts, the closing reads, release. **The clock:** every sample at 600 MHz; the first sample off it sets its burst aside,
a second stops the session. A telemetry line older than 1 s stops the session (watch the live `.raw` file). A record
with an error flag, `tensor_error` other than 0 or a failed label check ends that arm.

**The predicted peak:** board **41.3-42.0 W at the points**; at the bands' tops (the idle law at 60 C plus the over-idle
band's top at the fast seconds and its rise) **54.4 / 54.9 W for the scored scp pair and 61.5 / 62.0 W for the dram pair**,
under the 65 W stop; `plan_e202.py` refuses (exit 3) otherwise. The dram pair's band top is near the stop: it runs after
every scored burst, so a stop there costs no scored datum. The die is forecast to rise about 1.90 C in a burst (M10's 0.12 C a W).

**Commands:** reads only. The kit classifies every command its chain sends with the lab's device-command audit
(`dmcmd_audit_v2`) and needs GO before the first device access; the firmware-revisions read is allowed by the owner's
rulings of Sun 15 Nov 2026 (15:07:01 and 15:09:49 PST) and is recorded if sent; **no statistics reset outside a kit's
allowance, no set of any kind (TDP, threshold, clock, voltage), no reset, no PCIe recovery, no driver reload, no power
cycle, no firmware write.**

## 11. What E202 cannot say (stated before the data), and the risks registered

- **The A100 comparison is not here:** the owner's report and checkpoint are private, and the per-pass, per-image and
  per-multiply-add side-by-side happens on his private pages after the card day. Not a ResNet-50 image nor a
  language-model token (the checkpoint's other headline numbers); not another clock or card.
- **The meter's absolute scale is one fixed gain for every board, never set beside an outside meter** (E184's arm S is the
  open test): every number is against this meter. E202's data README carries this caveat.
- **D rests on a cruder rule than M10** (4.2: leave-one-out rms 0.657 W against M10's 0.175 W), on tile pairs priced as
  if each were repeated (M10's convention): the toggles between successive operations of E202's stream are in no
  committed kind, and **no committed kind ran with UA = 1** (unsigned activations): layer 1's saturated pixels read as
  large positives here and as negatives in every kind. The trained and random configurations bracket the data, and the
  given-seconds score isolates prices and counts from the seconds.
- **Shared operands are priced as private:** all 32 minions of a shire read the same staged B tile; the ledger prices
  each read at the own-scratchpad price (0.17 J a pass, the largest term). Whether a shared line costs less is untested.
- **The seconds extrapolate** sparse parity's fitted loop to a shire demand (U = 1.1081) above its fit's range, and the
  epilogue's issue rates are the catalogue's with a lone-hart factor measured on one instruction; the band is wide by rule
  (-24% / +53%).
- **The stack and table traffic's level is unknown** (1.6 GB a pass): priced at the L2 (0.006 J a pass), the L1 end would
  remove it; the time model waits for it only at the slow end.
- **scp staging at 32 x 32 has never run as a whole** (the simulator ran 32 x 1 and 32 x 2): its smoke is the first; the
  dram fallback stands. **The padding path** runs only in the R = 1 smokes (26 padding groups), checked in the simulator.
- **Hart 1 is parked** (returns at once), priced as in the catalogue's runs, not measured: the checkpoint's awake-time item
  stays open for this program.
- **One session, one card, no interval between sessions.** The seconds are per launch divided by R; one pass alone (R = 1)
  is reported, not scored.
