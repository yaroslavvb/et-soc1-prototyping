## 1. The question (from the post and the proposal's README)

**How many clock cycles, seconds and joules does the lab's large-GEMM binary rgm136 take, arm by arm, as the matrix sizes M, N and K vary
apart, over three element types (fp32, fp16, int8), three data kinds (random, zeros, ones) and four shire counts (32, 16, 4, 1), on three
cards?** (`src/README.md`: "A sweep of E136's large-GEMM binary `rgm136`, unchanged, over the sizes M, N and K apart, three element types
(fp32, fp16, int8) and three data kinds (random, zeros, a constant), on three cards, with E190's session form".) Per run the reduction gives
the time an op (`T_op_us`) and cycles an op (max and mean over minions), the kernel clock, the board's power over the rest before the run
by three baseline rules, the joules an op and a pass over idle and with the card at rest, the pJ a multiply-add, the minion, SRAM and mesh
rails' lifts and electrons an op, the die, and a DRAM-traffic witness (section 7). It serves the owner's matmul focus of Mon 16 Nov 2026
(one model of the matmul's cycles, energy and switches from its sizes and operations); the post opens with the owner's words as the model
session relays them ("There's no usage limit; use all the cards without restriction").

The families (`src/make_schedule.py`, quoted): **K** - K alone at M = 1,024, N = 512 (fp32 random KB 1-32, int8 random KB 1-16, fp32
zeros KB 1-24); **N** - N alone at M = 1,024, K = 2,048 fp32 (NB 1-16; zeros at NB 16); **M** - M alone at N = 512, K = 2,048 fp32 (MB 1-8;
zeros at MB 8); **SQ** - M = N = K (fp32 1,024-4,096; int8 2,048 and 4,096); **D** - type x data kind at three shapes (1 x 1 x 1, 1 x 1 x 4,
4 x 8 x 8, "E136's 4,096-wide product"); **S** - part of the chip (fp32 random at 1 x 1 x 1 and 1 x 1 x 4 and fp32 zeros at 1 x 1 x 1, on 1, 4
and 16 shires; fp16 random at 1 x 1 x 1 on 16 shires). **The anchor**, 4 x 8 x 8 int8 random on all 32 shires ("E136's and E146's arm"), runs
first, between every two blocks and last in every session.

## 6. What is measured, and how (registered: `proposal/src/run_mms.py`, its own steps 1-11, quoted and read)

1. **The box lock** (`run/box.lock`, taken by `start_mms.sh` before its checks and handed to the session: never free between them).
2. **The box and the build:** the hostname is the card's box; `build/BUILD-mms.json` written today by `build_mms.sh` on this box (BUILT;
   on aifoundry1 carrying `ET_DEVICES`); the kernel ELF at E136's pin, the host and the reader at the sha256 the build recorded; every
   `src/` file at `SRC-SHA256SUMS` (`check_mms.py --box`: HARNESS OK); the device-command audit GO; no STOP file. Else no device opened.
3. **The card's lock**, root's, opened read-only, never created, `flock -n`, held for the whole session (one retry after 15 s); then 3 s;
   then **the holders**: et-who (counts by card, never a login) and ps. Before the first card command nothing may block: no holder line of
   another login on the card (on aifoundry2, on any card), no node line of the session's own login (no device of ours open yet), no lock
   line of ours on another card; the session's own lock line on its card is the only line allowed - **et-who empty but for the session's
   own lock**; no CI job, no other device process of ours, on aifoundry2 no other user's device process. One retry after 5 s; else no device.
4. **The first card command:** `timeout 10 bash src/guard_ettelem.sh <card> config`, waited at most 30 s (one re-run after 5 s if it
   fails); it must be the config of section 5 and pass **the identity gate**; the card time counts from it. A start read that fails either
   gate ends the session with no closing read. The runner does not wait for the claim's stated window start: `start_mms.sh` takes only the
   window's **last end** (`--deadline-epoch`), so a session begins when it is started (*desk check of code*; section 12 item 4).
5. **One sampler for the session:** `bash src/guard_ettelem.sh <card> sample --seconds <cap + 600> --every-ms 100` (bounded by its own
   `--seconds`, not by `timeout`), every line to `telemetry.jsonl` and to **the stop lines** as it arrives; a trip touches the hosts' stop
   file at once (a running host ends at its next launch boundary, about 0.5 s) and nothing further starts. Stopped by SIGTERM only; a
   sampler that is not live is restarted only once it has stopped; a sampler of ours that will not stop is never joined by a second.
6. **The stop-path test** (each line with a test threshold every live sample crosses must trip within 5 s, touch its own file and refuse a
   start) and **the identity read** (et-who with the sampler running shows a line of ours on the card's management node and none off the
   card; the sampler's own open files hold the card's management node and no other card's).
7. **The opening rest:** 60 s at rest (the session's rest die and board; the aifoundry1 cards form their die gate from it).
8. **The smoke** (section 4): rc 0, every launch ok, the host's check of C exact or approximate, cycles an op inside 200-4,000, the host on
   its card (stderr or held nodes) and the board 2 W over its rest on at least 3 samples. A failed smoke: no run.
9. **The runs, in the schedule's order:** each waits for the die gate (at most 180 s), reads ps and et-who **before every run** (another
   login on the card, a line of ours off the card, the sampler's line missing or another device process stops the session), then its form
   (section 4). **Each device process is `timeout 10 sparsity_host ... --seconds 6 --budget 7.5`: a 6 s burst under a plain `timeout 10`**
   (no `-k`, no `-s`). Each host is watched every 0.1 s while it runs (`mms_ident.HostWatch`: its stderr's identity block and the card nodes
   its process tree holds): another card named or held stops the session at once. Two failed processes in a row (rc not 0, or the check of
   C not ok) stop the session.
10. **A host past its time:** `timeout 10` sends it SIGTERM at 10 s; alive 30 s after it started, the runner sends SIGTERM to its `timeout`
    wrapper (never SIGKILL); alive 30 s later it is a **LEFTOVER**: the session ends at once, makes no closing read, and holds the card's
    lock until no device process of its login is left (at most 10 minutes), recorded in `queue.json` and `status.json`.
11. **The closing** (bounded by the cap from the first card command): the sampler stopped (SIGTERM); et-who and ps once more; the closing
    config read (not after an identity trip, a stop for another holder, a failed start read or a leftover, nor when that last read shows a
    holder); "session end"; the locks released; `queue.json`; `MANIFEST.sha256` last; `session_mms.sh` then writes `runner.rc`.

**What each run records:** every launch's `SPARSITY` line from the host (cycles max and mean over minions, wall seconds, ops, passes,
start and end stamps in ms, ok, launch index; the sizing launch -1, the check launch -3 and the check of C); every 100 ms sample from the
reader (board W, the service processor's rail averages and millivolts, temperatures, clocks, the on-die monitor voltages, the read's own
duration); the run's marks, gate, rest and tail windows; the identity reads. **The stops armed** are section 5's lines, the identity watch,
the operator's STOP files (`stop_mms.sh` touches them; no process is killed), the card-time rule and cap, and the window's last end.

## 7. The reduction (registered: [`proposal/src/reduce_mms.py`](proposal/src/reduce_mms.py), sha256 `b9e32618020d28a590a615f97e5655c1ea458a6a757bf5658f2517a87fc3e0a5`, with the constants it imports from [`proposal/src/mms_common.py`](proposal/src/mms_common.py), sha256 `c3fc7883544ad73c3e455f7e950b62b9b155fd2ed972cee5e3524d74a378569c`)

Run per session after its data commit, unchanged: `python3 -I -B reduce_mms.py <session dir> --out data-mms.json [--txt reduce-mms.txt]`,
and across sessions `--combine`. Its rules, as its own head states them and its code does them (quoted and read):

- **Launches:** `fma` launches with launch >= 0, ok, wall >= 0.1 s, passes >= 1 (every process of a run: three in `sus3`); each launch's
  kernel clock is cycles max / wall.
- **Views:** `band <b>` (the launches in a clock band; on the governed cards **time only**); `win` (governed cards: the launches starting
  1.0-3.5 s after their own process's launch 0 and in the 800 MHz band, pooled over the run's processes: **the primary view there**);
  `burst` (every launch at the governor's own mix, with its 10 MHz clock histogram and each launch's own record). The primary view is `win`
  on the governed cards and `band600` on card 1; a governed run whose every launch sits in the 600 MHz band is read in `band600`, flagged
  `no_step`. A view needs 3 launches and 10 board samples (the window: 2 and 8), else it is thin and carries no energy.
- **Capped** (governed cards): of the launches starting 1.0 s or more after their process's launch 0, fewer than 80% in the 800 MHz band. A
  capped run's energy is not read nor contrasted; a `time` run is never read for energy.
- **Board:** the 10 Hz samples inside the view's launches, less those the reader took over 100 ms to read; the median, and the mean after
  the dip rule (a sample under median - 4 x 1.4826 x MAD, MAD at least 0.05 W, is left out).
- **Baselines** at the view's centre: `line` (a least-squares line through the board over the rest before the first process: 10 s, 20 s in
  `sus3`), `plain` (the median of the rest's last 5 s), `bracket` (the line between the rest's last 5 s mean and the tail's mean from 3 s
  after the last process). **Primary: `line` in `burst`, `bracket` in `sus3`.** dP = board mean - primary; J an op = dP x T_op / minions over
  idle; pJ a multiply-add; J a pass over idle and with the card at rest (board mean x t_pass); the other rules' dP beside.
- **Rails** (minion, SRAM, mesh): the service processor's running averages undone on each segment's own pass grid with the card's tau,
  one pass late (x_k = (y_k - a_k y_(k-1)) / (1 - a_k)); card 1's spike rule first; a rail's lift is its time-weighted mean over the view's
  launches less the same baseline rule over the rest (and tail), with its millivolts, joules and electrons an op; `rails_late` beside.
- **Die:** start (the rest's last 5 s), window, max, tail. **DDR droop:** the on-die DDR monitor's mean 2.5-0.2 s before the first process
  less its mean over the view from 0.5 s in (a witness of DRAM traffic), the minion monitor's beside.
- **Null runs:** pretend launches of 0.5 s from 0.6 s into the window: every rule's reading at zero signal; per session their mean and sd.
- **Per session:** per arm the mean over its runs of each view; the anchors in order; the nulls; **the die-start correction** dP_corr = dP /
  (1 + s (die_start - die_ref)), die_ref the session's median start die over the runs read for energy, s the anchors' fitted slope when
  their starts span 3 C or more and its standard error is under 0.0015 a degree, else 0.0048 a degree (the lab's 0.48 %/C on aifoundry2).
- **Combine:** per card and arm, the mean over every session's runs of the primary figures, their sd and n, and an error (sd / sqrt(n) with
  n >= 2, else the session anchors' relative sd x the value); the nulls' bias per rule pooled.

**The lab's data lane runs this reducer unchanged** on each released session (its sha256 checked against this line first) and commits its
outputs with the session's files, labelled measured. Nothing in the lab's reduction reads the sealed forecast. The reducer's head says its
rules were "registered before any datum exists (request.md section 8)": **no `request.md` reached the lab** (the post named only `src/`), so
for E208 the registered rules are this file's code and head as committed here (section 12 item 2). Where the head and the code differ,

process in `sus3` and null runs but 2 s after it in `burst` runs (where `bracket` is a side figure); and the anchors' fitted die slope
also needs at least 4 anchors read for energy.
