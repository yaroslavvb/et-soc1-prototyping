## The question

E115 scored the power model M5 (Q133(b)) on a blind pool on this card and found its misses on the float B-lane gate, per tile
and post hoc: two fp32 tiles with lanes 0–3 held off all launch read **0.74–0.76 W below** M5 (`g_blanes_steady` −0.744,
`x_logu_bsteady` −0.761 W), and the tiles whose every B lane switches on and off each cycle read **+0.48 W (fp32) and +0.69 W
(fp16) above** it. The registered fp32 contrast, `g_blanes_alt` − `g_blanes_steady`, read **+2.00 ± 0.09 W against M5's +0.78
[+0.23, +1.34]**. In the bench's own terms the steady tile clocks *more* register bits than the switching one (M5's `clk` term
4.79 against 3.93 W), and the card reads the opposite order of cost. **Clock gating under a steady gate is the first suspect;
it was not tested.** E115's pair also differs in operand activity (the switching tile's B bus moves 73,024 bits an op, the
steady tile's 28,066), so the miss could instead go with the moving data.

E127 asks four things, at equal operand activity:

1. **Does the miss replicate?** E115's own two fp32 tiles, byte for byte, in a new session (R1).
2. **Does it stay when the data are held still?** Ten new tiles carry every A word as one constant and every live B word as a
   one- or two-bit denormal, so every product is 0, C never moves, and the B operand bus moves exactly 4,096 bits an op in every
   one of them; only the gate's pattern differs. If the miss is the gate's own dynamics (clocking), it stays; if it goes with
   the data, it goes (R2 in fp32, R3 in fp16).
3. **Is it each lane's gate flipping, or all eight lanes off together?** In E115's switching tile, the micro-op on h = 0 has
   all eight lanes off and the one on h = 1 all eight live. A checkerboard tile flips every lane every cycle with four live in
   every micro-op, at random data with exactly the switching tile's operand bus and products (R4), and at quiet data (R4q).
4. **On what time scale?** A lane off for 32 cycles, then live for 32 (R5); and does it matter which lanes are held off (R6)?

## The tiles

All 2,048 bytes in the host's `--values file:` layout (A then B, 1,024 bytes each), generated by [`make_e127.py`](make_e127.py)
(`--check` rebuilds them and asserts every rule below on the bytes) into [`tiles/`](tiles/), described in
[`e127-tiles.json`](e127-tiles.json) (rules, seeds, sha256 of each file and half, the design figures), hashes in
[`SHA256SUMS`](SHA256SUMS). The stream is `make_kinds.issue_streams` (the kinds README § 1): for each B line k, for each A row
i, two micro-ops h = 0, 1; A's word is broadcast and lane l carries B[k][8h + l]; a lane is off when its 32-bit B word (or the A
word) is all zero (`make_kinds.gate_mask`; `vpu_ctrl.v:808-817`). The figures are `make_kinds`' (B bus bits: changed bits between
consecutive micro-ops, cyclic, summed over lanes; gate flips: the eight gate bits' changes, as `fit_m5.gate_transitions`).

**Why the quiet tiles are quiet.** Every A word is the constant 1.0 (fp32 `0x3F800000`; fp16 `0x3C003C00`), so A's broadcast
never changes. Every live B word is `0x00000001` or `0x00000002`: not zero, so the lane is live (the gate compares raw words;
E93 R3 on aifoundry1 card 1, E121 on this card), and a denormal, which by the manual and the RTL is flushed before the
multiplier (PRM § 3.4; `txfma_f0.v`), so every product is 0 and every accumulator stays 0. Half of all lane-cycles are live in
every quiet tile, and the B bus moves exactly 4,096 bits an op in each (`make_e127.py --check` asserts it): in `q_steady` four
lanes change two bits a cycle, in `q_alt` and `q_chk` eight lanes change one. What differs is only the gate: never flipping,
flipping every cycle with the micro-ops alternating all-off / all-live, flipping every cycle with four live in every
micro-op, or flipping every 32 cycles. **The random checkerboard** `g_blanes_chk` carries `g_blanes_alt`'s A and its live B
values, with lanes 0–3 live on h = 0 instead of h = 1: each lane carries the same values in the same order, one cycle apart, so
its operand bus bits and its set of products equal `g_blanes_alt`'s exactly (asserted), and only the micro-ops' fullness differs.

## Stops

E115's three passes of these anchors on this card read at most **55.07 W** board power and **63 °C** on the die (its
`telemetry.jsonl.gz`). E127's hottest items are the same anchors. Registered stops, with margin: **a die reading ≥ 70 °C** (or a
PMIC reading ≥ 80 °C) ends the pass (E78's own `--max-c`, default lowered from 78 to 70 in `block_e127.sh`), and **a board
reading ≥ 65 W** at any of E78's pre-launch reads writes the pass's STOP file and ends it with E78's exit 6 (`run_e127.py`
`guard_board_power`, a wrapper on `run_e78.last_sample` that changes nothing else; the self-test checks it fires at 65.0 W and
not at 64.9 W). Either stop ends the queue. The PMIC over-power alarm that aifoundry1's card 0 showed sits at 75 W (E84; Q134(a)); this
card's was never read. E127's highest expected reading is 20 W below that, and nothing in E127 risks a power cycle.

*Note added Mon 2 Nov 2026, after the run, from its verification (nothing above changed): "a PMIC reading ≥ 80 °C" is not a PMIC reading.
`run_e78.py` stops at `temp_c.pmic` ≥ `--max-c` + 10, and that field is `ettelem`'s `pmic_sys`, which `get_module_current_temperature`
(`services/thermal_pwr_mgmt.c` at `836a4ab`) fills from `pvt_get_minion_avg_temperature`, the minion shires' PVT average; in E127's telemetry
it equals `minshire[0]` in 16,149 of 16,155 samples (±1 °C in the other 6). So E127's two temperature stops were both die readings (70 °C and
80 °C), it read no PMIC temperature, and its margin to a PMIC over-temperature rested on board power and die temperature alone (the run's
highest readings: 53.84 W and 62 °C). Later lanes should not count this field as a PMIC guard.*

## Decision rules

All on the primary reduction (E78's, dips removed by E82 A2, leakage `auto`, E78's gates) and the a3 footing (M5's constants are
this card's: no scale). **Fewer than 3 kept passes: every rule NOT SCORED**, and the gates-off arm is then the reading reported,
labelled as that arm. A tile with fewer than 3 kept passes is left out and named. A contrast C = D(plus) − D(minus) is paired
inside each kept pass and averaged; its 95% interval is t(n − 1) × sd / √n over passes. **Its excess e(C) = C − M5(C)**, M5's
frozen point: the part the model does not price, with C's interval. Every verdict is also computed at the arms and on the anchor
footing (each prediction times the session's anchor over M5's predicted anchor); one that moves is marked arm-sensitive or
footing-sensitive. "This session's line" is half of the reference contrast's excess, measured in the same passes.

| rule | what | verdicts |
|---|---|---|
| **E127-R1** | **the replicate**: B1 = `fp32.g_blanes_alt` − `fp32.g_blanes_steady` (E115: +2.00 W; M5 +0.78 [+0.23, +1.34]) | **PASS** if B1's interval lies above M5's upper bound (+1.34); **FAIL** if B1's mean is at or below it; else INCONCLUSIVE |
| **E127-R2** | **the primary: does the miss stay with the data held still?** e(Q1), Q1 = `fp32.q_alt` − `fp32.q_steady`, against L = ½ e(B1) | **GATE** if e(Q1)'s low end ≥ L; **DATA** if its high end ≤ L; **OPPOSITE** if its high end < −L; else INCONCLUSIVE. NOT SCORED unless R1 PASS |

**Why these lines, before the data.** R1 asks only that E115's miss come back: its pair read +2.00 ± 0.09 W over three passes,
1.22 W above M5's point and 0.66 W above its upper bound, so a real replicate clears the bound easily and a FAIL would say E115's
reading was a session's, not the chip's. R2–R5 split a measured excess at half: half of the random-data miss appearing with the
data still is the line between "the gate's own dynamics" and "the moving data", and a reading near the line is INCONCLUSIVE
rather than forced. The reference is measured in the same passes, so the session's own level of miss sets the line, not
E115's. R6's 0.30 W is about three times a quiet contrast's expected half-width (E115's contrasts, ±0.08–0.29 W over three passes;
quiet tiles sit near 4 W, where the meter is quieter).

**Reported, never a verdict** (`scored.json` › `primary`, `results.md`): every tile's D with its interval, M5 [95%], D − M5, M4
and D − M4; every contrast with M5's interval read INSIDE / ABOVE / BELOW and its excess; the ratios e(test) / e(reference) with
a delta-method 95% interval; B2 (`g_blanes_chk` − `g_blanes_alt`, equal operand activity), Q3 (`q_alt` − `q_chk`) and F3 (fp16's
checkerboard); coverage of M5's and M4's intervals over the 13 tiles; E78's anchor cells beside E115's.

**What I expect, frozen so it can be wrong** (written after the bench's counts, before any card data; a first draft, before
the counts, expected PER-LANE and SHORT): R1 PASS, B1 near +2.0 W. **R2 GATE**: the miss stays with the data still. The bench
puts its whole 0.86 W clocked-bit difference between E115's pair on the micro-ops whose eight lanes are all off (M5's `clk` term:
`q_alt` and `q_blk` −0.86 and −0.84 W against `q_steady`, `q_chk` 0.00 W; `g_blanes_chk` keeps `g_blanes_steady`'s 4.78 W), and
no data value moves that. R3 GATE. **R4 ALL-OFF** and R4q ALL-OFF: the miss goes with micro-ops whose lanes are all off at once,
where the bench prices a clocking saving the card may not take, not with each lane's own gate flipping. (E115's A-side gates,
which also turn a whole micro-op off, read inside, so this would be a B-lane-only effect.) R5 LONG, since `q_blk`'s off
stretches are all-off micro-ops too. R6 PASS. S1 PASS.

**The statement the page gets, decided now.** R2 GATE: "the float B-lane gate's miss is the gate's own: with the data held still
and the operand bus equal, switching a lane every cycle against holding it costs X W more than the bench prices". R2 DATA: "it goes
with the data: with the data still, the bench prices the gate's switching to within X W". INCONCLUSIVE is stated as such, with the
excess and its interval. **Only tiles that ran are scored. No tile is dropped, re-drawn or added after the first pass, and no
model, interval or line changes.**

## What the rules cannot say

One card, the card M5's constants are on; one clock. A D says what a tile costs, not where on the die it is spent: "the gate's
own dynamics" means everything that changes with the gate pattern at equal operand bits — clock gating, the gate's enable
logic, the valid pipeline, the write-back mask — and E127 cannot tell those apart. The quiet tiles rest on the denormal flush
(the manual and the RTL; E93's power reading agrees, it does not read the flush): if the silicon multiplied denormals, their
products would be 2^−149 and C would creep, on every quiet tile alike. The bench is the public RTL's later revision, not the
ET-SoC-1's own, and M5 prices merged nets, not wires. E127 tests no A-side gate and no int8 gate (E115 read both inside), and no
other clock, voltage or card.

**At most 35 card-minutes**, from the session-start config read (the smoke's) to the session-end read, the smoke and the heating
gates included. Expected about 26–32: the smoke about 1 min, then four or five passes of about 6 min (35 launches at E115's 9.7–10.4 s
a launch) with 15 s between. `queue_e127.sh` starts a pass only if its projected end (the longest measured pass; 380 s until the
first ends) is inside **1,890 s** of the session start and before **15:50 PST**; a pass in flight is never cut. No session
starts after 15:00 PST; every run ends by 16:00 PST. It starts only with `et-who` clean on card 0, no other aifoundry3 lane of
ours holding the card, and the day's post in `#community-lab` rewritten to carry this reservation (§15 of the brief).
