## The primary, exactly

For each scored tile, **`predict_m9_a2.burst(D, t, anchors, die_starts, D_band_hw)`**, the frozen code imported from its committed directory after its
sha256 and `m9_a2.json`'s are checked (`e165_common.primary`), with:
- **D** = `tiles.<name>.models.M9.D600_a3_footing_w` (M9's frozen D at 600 MHz on aifoundry3's footing), **D_band_hw** = half the width of
  `M9.D600_a3_95` (it enters only the peak, as when the frozen file was written);
- **anchors** = the session's zeros-anchor reads: per format, **every counted burst** of that format's `anchor7_zeros` in the session, `{burst_w,
  burst_mhz, die_start}` as the registered reduction reads them. `burst()` keeps those at 795 MHz or more (E160's fp16 anchor ran a pass at 752 MHz
  under the service processor's mid-burst idle status, C1 there), fills a format with none from the others' mean, and takes the frozen fallback if no
  anchor burst exists at all;
- **die_starts** = the tile's own counted bursts' die starts, one per counted pass (whole-degree readings);
- the scored quantities are **`burst_mhz`, `burst_board_w` and `burst_die_rise_c` as `burst()` returns them** (rounded by the frozen code to 0.01 MHz,
  0.001 W, 0.001 °C), with **its 95% bands** (the frozen half-widths by the recompute's own predicted group: governed ±9.65 MHz / ±1.85 W, pinned
  ±15.35 MHz / ±1.26 W; die rise ±1.41 °C).

**The groups are fixed by the frozen file**: `tiles.<name>.group` = `M9.a2_q140l.group` (governed: M9's part puts the burst below 795 MHz at the
fallback and T_ref): 12 governed, 40 pinned scored. **The zeros anchors are not scored**: they are the primary's inputs. **Beside the primary, never a
verdict:** the frozen block as frozen (`M9_as_frozen`), M8, M7 and M6 as frozen; and three arms that split the primary's inputs: the anchors of pass 1
only (read before any pool tile: every input known before every scored burst), the fallback offset with the die starts, and the session's anchors at
T_ref.

## Decision rules

A run is **complete** if every launch of it ran and was ok and `burst_mhz`, `burst_w`, `die_start`, `die_max` exist; **counted** if complete **and every
SPARSITY line of it (the calibration launch included) has a C record that reads PASS** (bit-exact; ROUNDING, UNMODELLED, FAIL and a missing record are
not checked: Q139(k)'s rule). A tile's measured value is its mean over its counted passes; a tile with fewer than 2 is left out of every rule and
named. **Fewer than 2 counted passes in all: every rule NOT SCORED.** "Bootstrap" = the 2.5–97.5% range of the rms of n errors drawn with replacement
from the reference errors, n the tiles scored (20,000 draws, seeded 165; `e165_common.boot_rms_range`): **PASS** at or below the 97.5% point, **FAIL**
above; below the 2.5% point "better than the reference says". "Coverage" = **PASS** if the number of tiles inside the band is at least the 2.5% point of
Binomial(n, 0.95) (46 of 52). "**Resolved better than M7 as frozen**" = the 95% interval of rms(M9 primary errors) − rms(M7 as frozen errors) over the
set's tiles, paired, drawn with replacement (20,000 draws, seeded 1651; `e165_common.paired_rms_diff`), lies below 0.

**±1.75 / ±1.48 W against the frozen ±1.26 W**); **the peaks**: each tile's highest board sample against M9's and M8's frozen expected and 95% upper
peaks (E160-PK's form), and per burst; **k_T per burst**: the within-tile slope of the pinned tiles' board on the burst's die start over their counted

per class (S, E, L, H, U, R, 0), priority 1, the real tiles, the sets split by the measured clock; the heat law on the measured board W; the kernel's
own clock; every rule over the complete runs, C not required, beside the counted; budget stops; the session's peaks.

**Why these lines, before the data.** R2's lines are the critic's, written before Q140(l)'s fit, so they are the honest target the owner's goal sets;
the resolution test against M7 as frozen is the critic's too, and it keeps a lucky session from passing a model no better than the last one. F1-W2
ask the other question: whether the blind error is the size the best committed estimate says (Q140(l)'s end-to-end held-out errors), so a FAIL there
says the held-out estimate was optimistic. The coverage rules score the frozen bands as frozen: they are not re-sized to the nested widths (A1-3), so
the pinned board band may under-cover, and that is reported beside. The groups are the frozen file's because that is what is known before the run.

## Precision, before the data

At the full plan (12 governed, 40 pinned, 52 in all; [`predictions-e165.txt`](predictions-e165.txt)) the bootstrap lines are **F1 2.45–6.41 MHz**, **F2
0.29–4.04 MHz**, **W1 0.42–1.14 W**, **W2 0.60–1.04 W**, **H1 0.56–0.81 °C**; coverage 46 of 52 (10 of 12 governed, 35 of 40 pinned, reported). **A
forecast, registered (information only):** drawing 12 governed and 40 pinned errors from the end-to-end reference, rank 2's governed clock line (rms ≤ 4
MHz and |mean| ≤ 2 MHz) holds in 35% of draws, the governed board's mean line in 93%, the pinned board's two lines in 95%, all five lines together in 32%
(before the resolution test against M7). So **R2 is more likely to FAIL than to PASS**, on the governed clock, as Q140's critic expected ("the 4 MHz
line will likely be missed: nested 5.1-6.9 MHz"); its board lines are likely to hold. Twelve governed tiles resolve an rms difference against M7 only
if M9's part is clearly better there. **How one pass repeats** (E160's three passes on this card): a tile's passes spread by a median 2.2 MHz and 0.84
W, the largest 41.6 MHz and 6.71 W (one bistable float), so the pass, not the sample, is the unit; three of E160's 150 bursts ran at about 755 MHz under
a mid-burst idle status that no model of the bits predicts.

## What the rules cannot say

- **One card, one firmware build (BL2 0.22.0), one session.** The session's own zeros anchors and die starts are inputs; they never include a scored
  tile's own data, but they make the score conditional on the day's reads, as the model is meant to be used.
- **The die start is the firmware's whole-degree reading**, and there is no inlet thermometer: k_T cannot be split into the die's leakage and the room.
- **The error is end to end**: D is M9's 600 MHz prediction, so a pinned tile's board error carries D's error (Q140(m): 0.18-0.19 W out of kind) with
  the zeros level's and the die term's. The 600 MHz terms are E163's and E164's to test.
- **The pinned tiles test little of the clock term** (at 800 MHz the clock cannot move up); the 12 governed tiles test it, all fp16 or fp32 near the cap.
- **The 82 W admission rests on M8's peak rule, unchanged** (Q140(l): a level term must not loosen a stop validated without it); E165 reports how it
  bounds the peaks, and aifoundry2's PMIC over-power configuration stays unread (it waits on the owner's word).
- **The host is E160's C-logging build, not E80's binary**; the device code is the same. E165 against E160 reads the day's drift with no host change.

## Card budget and time

**About 36 card-minutes, at most 45** (Q140's critic: "about 36-38 ... a 45-minute cap"): 171 bursts at E160's 11.94 s a burst (E160's `s1`: 150 bursts
in 29.85 card-minutes) is **34.0 min**, plus the smoke and the two stop tests (E160: 0.97 min) and the queue's start and end reads, about 2 min. The
session's cap is **40 card-minutes** (`run_e119.py` starts a run only if its planned time fits; it cuts pass 3's tail); the queue's slot is **45 minutes**
from its start, and its hard stop (the stop file 120 s before the slot's end, SIGTERM at 30 s) holds everything inside it. **The registered subset for a
short window**: the first two passes (114 bursts, **22.7 min**): the queue started with `--cap 25` and a 30-minute slot; every rule is scored on what is
counted (at least 2 counted passes). **First on aifoundry2** in the card half (Q140's critic's plan): its zeros anchors give E167's and E168's reported
arms the day's offset, then E167, E168, E169. The day's one `#community-lab` post gets this card's clause, `using aifoundry2 for 1 h, until ~HH:MM PST`,
before the first launch, and loses it at the release (section 15). If the lab approval lapses, the detached queue keeps running and the data is
collected when the box answers again (section 9). An idle login of another user does not block (the owner's ruling of 1 Nov 2026, E111's rule in the
runner); any other user's activity, device process or lock ends the session at once.

## How to run it

From the top of the tree on the Intel: `python3 -B tools/claims-v3/e165/make_predictions_e165.py > tools/claims-v3/e165/predictions-e165.txt` (this
registration), the scorer's self-test `python3 -B tools/claims-v3/e165/selftest_score_e165.py > tools/claims-v3/e165/selftest-score.txt` (ALL PASS), the
kit's `make_protocol_e165.py`, `selftest_e165.py` and the dry queue ([`README.md`](README.md)). On aifoundry2, from `~/claude/et-soc1-prototyping` at
or after the kit's commit, with `build/ccheck` holding the pinned C-logging host: the binaries' sha256 and `et-who` checked, the slot file `SLOT E165:
start <epoch> ... end <epoch> ` written, then `setsid nohup python3 tools/claims-v3/e165/queue_e165.py --slots <slot file> --runs build/e165/runs --cap 40
> build/e165/queue.out 2>&1 < /dev/null &`. Collect `build/e165/runs` to `docs/reports/data/<the run's date>-e165-blind7-aifoundry2/aifoundry2/`, check
C at the desk (`ccheck.py check <session>/c-log.jsonl --out ccheck-<session>.json --jobs 2` per session) and score: `python3 -B
tools/claims-v3/e165/score_e165.py <the sessions> --ccheck <the ccheck files> --out results.json > score.txt`.
