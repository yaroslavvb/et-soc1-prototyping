  ("one device process under `timeout -k 1 10`"). That runner is `tools/claims-v3/e193/proposal/src/run_x04.py`, sha256 `26d08862...`.

  - Its `end_device()`, used when the runner itself is stopped by a signal, relies on the same KILL. Its doc says: "SIGTERM to its

- **The orchestrator's answer, verbatim** (W/out/orch/e198-gap1.txt, 02:56:14 PST): "HOLD ... The orchestrator holds E198's registered
  runner kill: the lab's AGENT.md binds 'Stop tools with Ctrl-C or a plain kill, never kill -9' (a tool killed mid-request poisons the

  and E197 dropped its -k as departure D1. E193's 369 clean processes show the kill never fired, not that it may. No E198 sitting until a
  dated amendment (A1) gives the lab's runner copy 'timeout 10' without -k and an end_device() that never sends SIGKILL (a process that does
  not end is reported and left for the lab admin), verified, landed and re-readied (Q196)."
- **Its standing ruling** (W/out/orch/kill-rule.txt, the same second), for every card lane of every round: "No run of ours sends or arranges

- **The lab's etiquette** (`AGENT.md`, the table "The lab's etiquette, and why"):
  - "Never hold a device for more than 10 s: `timeout 10` on every launch (per device-opening process ...)";
  - "Stop tools with Ctrl-C or a plain `kill`, never `kill -9` | a sampler killed mid-request poisons the card's management queue for the
    next user".
- **The precedent:** E197's departure D1 (E197.md 15) dropped the same `-k 1` from X02's runner, one line, before any datum. **REPORTED:**
  E193 ran 369 device processes under this runner on aifoundry3 on Sun 15 Nov 2026 (234 + 135), all exit 0. So neither the TERM nor the

- **The hunks (the kill path only):**
  - **(1) Each device process under a plain `timeout 10`** (K5): `["timeout", "-k", "1", "10", a.host, ...]` becomes
    `["timeout", "10", a.host, ...]`. TERM comes at 10 s and is never followed by a KILL.
  - **(2) A device process that does not end is reported at once and left for the lab admin; the session stops launching** (K4, K6, K8).
    The runner waits for each device process at most `END_WAIT_S` = 25 s from its start: the 10 s to its timeout's TERM, then the 15 s
    that `end_device()` first waits. If the process is still running then:
    - the runner writes at once "device process still running 25 s after it started (its timeout's TERM came at 10 s; nothing is killed):
      device process not ended: tell the lab admin" and the event `device_not_ended`;
    - it leaves the process running and keeps it as its device process;
    - it makes the registered stop "<arm>: device process not ended 25 s after it started, its timeout's TERM at 10 s (nothing killed: tell
      the lab admin)" (STOPPED, the stop file). No further run is launched.
  - **(3) `end_device()` sends SIGTERM and waits, never SIGKILL** (K7). Its code was already TERM-only; the KILL came from the timeout's
    `-k`, now gone. It sends SIGTERM to the process's `timeout`, waits 15 s, reports and sends SIGTERM again, waits 60 s, reports and sends
    SIGTERM a third time. If the process still runs, it returns "not ended" and leaves it: "device process not ended: the card lock goes
    only when this runner exits". Only its doc changes. **What those TERMs reach** (the dated fix, verify-a1e198's finding 2; GNU
    coreutils 9.4's `timeout`, read on aifoundry2 by the verifier's probe; aifoundry1's coreutils was not read): `timeout` passes on the
    first TERM sent to it and ignores every later one, and once it has sent its own TERM at 10 s it ignores them all. So a host that
    outlived its timeout's TERM receives **one TERM in all**, the timeout's own at 10 s, and `end_device()`'s three TERMs to the timeout
    reach nothing. Only when the runner itself is signalled in a device process's first 10 s does the first of them reach the host, which
    then gets the timeout's own TERM at 10 s as well (two in all).
  - **(4) The doc lines that described the kill** (K1-K3), so that no line of the copy says it kills.
  - **(5) The clean-up after a device process not ended stops the sampler all the same** (K9, the dated fix, verify-a1e198's finding 1).
    E193's clean-up returned before the lock and the sampler when `end_device()` returned "not ended". Under E193's `-k 1` that return was
    all but unreachable: the KILL at 11 s ended a host that ignored its TERM, `end_device()` read it ended and the clean-up stopped the
    sampler. Under A1 such a host is left running, so without K9 the runner's sampler, a device process on card 1's management node, would
    stay until its own `--seconds` (`int(wall x 1.5 + 600)`: about 13 minutes after the smoke's start, 52 in sitting B and 79 in sitting A,
    desk arithmetic), and the lab admin could not use the management node meanwhile. With K9 the clean-up says "E198 A1: the sampler is
    stopped all the same (SIGTERM), so it does not hold the management node" and runs E193's own sampler stop (SIGTERM and a 10 s wait,
    the same signal E193's clean-up sends the sampler at every normal end). The card lock still goes only when the runner exits, as in
    E193. K9 needs no ruling and no step by the card lane; K3 and the clean-up's docstring say it too.
- **How E198 runs it:** the session passes the copy E193's own `arms.tsv`
  (`--arms tools/claims-v3/e193/proposal/src/arms.tsv`). That is the value E193's runner takes by default from its own folder; the copy

## 3. What it changes in behaviour (desk check of code; shown on stand-ins, section 6)

- **Nothing on the normal path.** The host refuses a budget above 9.5 s (`main.cpp` 382-384: "--budget must be in (0, 9.5] seconds") and
  the runner passes none, so a run that opens normally ends in about 5.5 s (4 s of launches, the opening and closing; 2 s in the smoke).
  **The TERM at 10 s can reach only a host that is hung or very slow to open.** The dry listings of the copy equal v1's own E198
  listings (the kit's `desk/dry-e198-{smoke,A,B}.txt`) line for line, except that each device call reads `timeout 10` where v1's reads
  `timeout -k 1 10`; they equal E193's registered listings through `check_e198.py`'s five exact mappings and A1's sixth (`timeout 10` for
  `timeout -k 1 10`), as KIT.md.dated-note-A1.md section 3 says. The smoke has 10 device calls, sitting A 234 and sitting B 135: desk
  check of code. (Dated fix, verify-a1e198's finding 5: this sentence first said the listings equal E193's "line for line" but the timeout
  word, which holds only after those mappings.)
  - One small difference: the runner now learns a device process has ended through a timed wait. That wait polls at most every 50 ms,
    where a blocking wait returns at once. The next run still waits for the idle-down and the registered 5 s gap, so no registered time
    moves.
- **A host that ends on its TERM** exits 124 through `timeout`: the registered stop "killed by timeout (tell the lab admin)", as before.
  - Under `-k 1`, a host that ended more than 1 s after its TERM (the kit's runtime-line guard takes a moment to write its output) was
    KILLed at 11 s with its wrapper. Under A1 it ends by itself. The real-path test's HG2 shows it: a stand-in host that ends 1.5 s after
    its TERM exits 124, with no KILL.
- **A host that does not end on TERM** is the case the KILL was for.
  - Under `-k 1` (desk reading of GNU timeout): the KILL at 11 s would also have ended the `timeout` process itself, so the runner would have
    read exit -9 and stopped with "device process exit -9 (a launch that did not finish: tell the lab admin)".
  - Under A1, the runner reports it at 25 s, makes the same kind of stop ("tell the lab admin"), waits 75 s more in `end_device()`, and leaves
    it running. Nothing is killed. The host has had one TERM, its timeout's at 10 s (section 2 (3)).
  - The clean-up then stops the runner's sampler with SIGTERM all the same (K9, the dated fix), so the sampler does not hold card 1's
    management node. The card lock goes when the runner exits, while the host left running still holds card 1's ops node (its
    `timeout` and the host wrapper wait for it and open no device node): `et-who` shows the host.
  - The kit's session then sends nothing else to the card: its closing config read is skipped. Step 10 reads DEVICE-NOT-ENDED and step 9
    refuses any further E198 sitting before a dated note (KIT.md.dated-note-A1.md).
  - The sitting's files can still change after its manifest, written by the processes left running: the host wrapper's output when the
    host ends. (Since K9 the sampler has stopped before the runner exits, so the telemetry is final before the manifest; the real-path
    test's HG shows it.) Then step 11's exact copy-back refuses, and the collection waits for that dated note. Such a sitting is reported
    at once and decided by a dated note, as E198.md 9 says of a launch that did not finish ("a card reset is never E198's to make").
  - A host that ends by itself inside `end_device()`'s waits (25 to 100 s after its start, the real-path test's HGE) is still the
    registered stop above: the session has already said "A DEVICE PROCESS DID NOT END", skipped its closing read, and step 10 reads
    DEVICE-NOT-ENDED. That is conservative; the runner's own `device_ended` event (exit 124) says when it ended, and the card lane's
    report gives both (CARD-LANE-BRIEF-e198-a1.md 4).
- **A signal to the runner** (the session stopped by hand): `end_device()` sends SIGTERM to the timeout and waits. The timeout passes the
  first TERM on to the host if its own TERM has not gone yet (section 2 (3)); no KILL follows.
