## A1-2. E185-C: its consequence is read on the frozen chain alone (the verifier's fault 5)

E185-C's statistic d_h (the cool-down window's miss less the hold's plateau miss) carries each arm's own scaling. Suppose R follows the
frozen chain exactly, so that d_F = 0. Then d_B = (G_B − 1)(P_F − cool P_F), and d_Rl = (P_Rl − P_F) − (cool P_Rl − cool P_F). That is
a property of each arm's gain, not of the cool-down's shape. At E185's frozen forecasts (50.8 °C, desk computation):

| hold | d_B − d_F | d_Rl − d_F |
|---|---|---|
| H_E | +0.320 | −0.071 |
| L1 | +0.241 | +0.403 |
| L2 | +0.241 | +0.408 |
| H_E2 | +0.320 | −0.056 |

Suppose the chain's shape is exactly right and d's noise is σ_c/2. Then the registered rule reads THE SHAPE HOLDS on F in 73.1% of draws.
On B it reads THE SHAPE MISSES (warmer than the chain) in 100%, and on Rl NOT RESOLVED in 100% (at σ_c: 13.4%, 95.5% and 99.9%). So B's line
would report B's gain, and Rl's line nothing.

The committed windows show the same thing (two-pass; E144's H_H is in-sample for B and Rl, while E181's H_E and L1 are held out):

| Rl | −0.077 / −0.162 / +0.329 | +0.030, 0.263 |

On the one-pass reading: d_F's sd is 0.041 and d_B's mean is +0.208.

**The rule (registered, replacing E185.md § 6's last bullet on E185-C):**
- **E185-C's consequence is read on F alone**, the frozen chain's own cool-down shape.
  - THE SHAPE MISSES on F: the next registration states a chain with a different cool-down (a refit of the poles is not an arm here).
  - THE SHAPE HOLDS on F: the cool-down is not where the frozen chain loses heat over minutes.

## A1-6. The cool-down window: its public derivation, and a disclosure (the verifier's fault 9)

  E181 the next hold's anchor, [b + 240, b + 360), lay inside the window. E185.md § 4 and the design say the rest is the window and then
  the anchor. What they did not say is why the window ends at 360 s; the reason is E181's rests, above.
