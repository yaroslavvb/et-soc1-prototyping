## 1. "A cut connection never did" (note 2 section 2, its line 61): not true of step 6 v4; true of step 6 v5

Note 2 says of its re-run rule: "a build that fails once for a passing reason ... no longer ends the trains for the day. A cut connection never
did: the build is a detached job, and the count is the box's." **Under step 6 v4 that second sentence was not true.**
- k6's `rb_job_wait` returns 14 for three different things: a job that ended non-zero, a lost job, and a wait call that printed none of
  JOB-DONE, JOB-LOST or JOB-RUNNING ("no reading", for example ssh's exit 255 on a dropped link).
- Step 6 v4's wait read every 14 as a failed build. At the day's first build that was harmless: it asked for a start that the start's own
  refusals turn away (a job still running, or a good build today). At the day's SECOND build, during the one re-run, it named the registered
  fallback, and step 6 v4 `--no-trains` had no good-build refusal: once the re-run's good build had ended, `--no-trains` passed beside a good
  train host. One network fault there would have read every train rule NOT SCORED that day (the verifier's cases N2 and N4).
- **Under step 6 v5 the sentence holds.** Its wait reads a build's failure only from the box's own end of the job (a whole JOB-DONE line with
  a non-zero status, or JOB-LOST); any other 14 stops with 14, "no reading: run this step's wait again", and nothing is counted or named.
  Its `--no-trains` is refused (10) after a good build that day, as its start is. A cut read of a check after the build, or of the
  fallback's own checks, is "no reading" too (14), never "a binary or file differs" (16). The v5 desk test shows each beside v4's fault: N1,
  N1b, N2 and N2v, N4 and N4v, N6 and N6v, and R4 (`logs/selftest-rb-e182-v5.txt`).
- A cut call on `start` never ended the trains, under v4 or v5: the brief's note 1 says to run wait first, and the start's refusals stand.

## 3. Which lost job counts (note 2 section 2, its line 45)

Note 2: "A build that failed, ran past its own 500 s bound, or whose job was lost counts." **That holds once the job has written its "started"
line**, which is the job's first act on the box, before the build. A job lost before that line built nothing and is not counted: step 6 v4 and
v5 then say "before the job recorded its start (no build recorded today): run this step's start once more" (v4's line 195; v5 the same). So
the day's builds are, as note 3 section 1 says, the day's "started" lines less the jobs that read TRAIN-HOST-INPUTS-DIFFER.
