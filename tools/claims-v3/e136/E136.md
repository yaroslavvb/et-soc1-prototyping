# E136: a large GEMM on the ET-SoC-1, its power, clock, rate and die predicted first, under aifoundry2's own governor (Q137)

Mon 2 Nov 2026 (week 45), about 15:50 PST. Registered before the claim and the first launch. It answers the kinds page's
[section 11, item 1, "Where a large GEMM falls"](https://spacesheep.dev/@yaroslavvb/horace-kinds-of-numbers#11-what-is-still-open), the part
[E126](../../../docs/reports/data/2026-11-02-e126-gemm-arms/README.md) left: *"Still open: a large GEMM itself, which needs its own reuse per L2-sized
block and its footprint, C included"*. It is Q137's first lane: the owner's words of 2 November 2026, about 15:20 PST, *"The overall goal is to be able
to predict the power requirement and heat generation of various kinds of numeric computations as accurately as possible"*. So the predictions come
first and are the point: E136 freezes the board power, the clock, the rate and the die of a real large GEMM from what E107, E119, E126, E101, E83, E111
and E133 measured, then measures them.

## What a large GEMM is here, and what it is built from

Every source is public: the lab repo's `kernels/` and `tools/claims-v3/rgm*` (E101's port of gp-sdk's generated matmul, `libautogen/matmul_f32_1024_512_512`,
`MatmulCmd_compute.cc`), E105's layouts and E107's `rgm107`. **The vendor's pattern** is one block: C (64 × 32 tiles) = A (64 × 32) × B (32 × 32), 1,024 ×
512 × 512 elements in fp32, every minion owning two row tiles and one column tile of C, the 8 minions of a neighbourhood loading A cooperatively and the
4 minions at one position loading B cooperatively, A load, B load, the finished tile's TensorStore, wait for A, FMA. **E136's large GEMM**
(`rgm136`, `SP_GEMM_LARGE`, [`rgm136/README.md`](rgm136/README.md)) keeps all of that in every C block and scales it up: C (MB × NB blocks) = A (MB × KB
blocks) × B (KB × NB blocks); each C tile accumulates over its whole K, KB × 32 ops, before its one TensorStore, so C is written once; the minion walks
the C blocks mb outer, nb inner. B is packed by column (E105's by-column layout, the one its desk model says fits the L2), tiles packed in blocks.

| GEMM | `--values` arm | M × N × K, fp32 (fp16, int8: K × 2, × 4) | A, B, C | with C | ops a pass a minion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `g4k` | `gemmL4x8x8` | 4,096 × 4,096 × 4,096 | 64, 64, 64 MB | **192 MB**: streams to DRAM | 16,384 |
| `g2k` | `gemmL2x2x2` | 2,048 × 1,024 × 1,024 | 8, 4, 8 MB | **20 MB**: the 32 MB L3 holds it from its second pass | 512 |

**Its own reuse and footprint** (desk, [`desk/reuse_e136.py`](desk/reuse_e136.py) into `reuse_e136.txt` and `.json`; E105's L2 model unchanged, a tile-level
LRU for the L3): per shire, one row pass of a C block streams 4 row tiles' A panel (`g4k`: 1,024 KB) and 8 column tiles' B panel (2,048 KB) through a
512 KB L2, so **no line of `g4k` hits the L2 (192 of 192 lines an op miss) and 176 of 192 miss in `g2k`**. Where the missing lines are served: a column
panel's B is used again by the minion's second row tile while the L3 still holds it (B: half its lines from the L3); A's row panel (16 MB across the chip)
is used once in every C block of its row, and whether the L3 still holds it after a column panel and C have passed through is the one thing the model
cannot settle (A from the L3: 0.658 of its lines at a 32 MB L3, 0.221 at 28 MB, 0 at 24 MB; E107 measured the L3 holding 26 MB, C included, and not 40).
So `g4k` is predicted at two ends: **P1, A held by a 32 MB L3** (106.1 lines an op from the L3, 85.9 fresh), frozen as the point, and **P2, A fresh**
(64 from the L3, 128 fresh). Per pass every A tile is used NB = 8 times, every B tile 2 × MB = 8 times, every C tile stored once.

## What runs

**aifoundry2 card 0 under its own governor** (65 W / 65 °C, read by `ettelem config` at the queue's start and end and after every run). **Nothing is set on the
card**: no TDP, threshold, clock or voltage; no `--reset-ms`, no statistics reset; no firmware-revisions query; no residency read; no governor move.
**Design:** E80's, with E105's, E119's and E126's parameters unchanged: a burst is one 6 s process from a die reading at or below 55 °C; sustained is nine 8 s
processes (about 76 s) from at or below 57 °C, its last 40 s read. Every start is cool. **The runner** is `run_e136.py`: E119's `run_e119.py` imported and run
unchanged (E105's admission rule, E111's idle-login rule under the owner's ruling of 1 November 2026, the residency rewrite, the cap that cuts the tail
between runs), after a preflight that refuses the session unless the three device programs are the registered ones (sha256 in `run_e136.py` and
[`logs/build.txt`](logs/build.txt): rgm136 built on aifoundry2 at 15:37 PST from this commit's source, before this registration; the sampler is E119's and
E126's, unchanged). **The queue** is E126's, detached on the box, with E136's names.

**Kinds**, in fp32, fp16 and int8: **zeros** (the file's tile through the whole pool), **random** (fp32 and fp16 `randn`, int8 `full`, rgm105's generator),
**one sparse** (`sparse50`: half the elements zero, the rest random), **one real model** (E101's registered real set, `kinds/e101_common.py REAL_SET`:
four tiles per format cut from GPT-2 small, BERT and ViT-B/16 by Q132(r), cycled through the pools).

**The order** (`protocol.txt`), most important first; **the cap is 45 card-minutes from the session's start, the smoke apart (about 1.5 min); about 28 are
planned**: SMOKE (the 30 s rest reading, four 2 s launches with their check of C, the cycles per op within 50-250% of P1's); A, fp32 bursts, `g4k` zeros,
random, sparse50, real, `g2k` zeros, random; B, fp32 sustained `g4k` random and zeros; C and D, the same bursts in int8 and fp16; E, sustained `g4k`
int8 full and zeros, fp16 random and zeros; F, sustained `g2k` fp32 random and zeros, `g4k` fp32 real and sparse50; G, sustained `g4k` int8 and fp16 real,
bursts `g2k` fp32 sparse50 and real. **Card budget: at most 50 card-minutes in all, inside the 60 the lane has; the claim is "for 1 h".** No new session
starts after 21:00 PST, and the slot's hard stop ends everything by 21:45 PST.

## The power limit

The highest planned estimate is `g2k` fp32 random's 75.6 W (E119's rule: p800 = B(800) + s(800) × c(600) from the cell's own S on a pinned card, which
over-reads a walk). E119's admission rule, unchanged: **a cell runs only if its estimate is at or below 88 W; after any run whose board reading reached
93 W, no cell with an estimate at or above that run's goes on**. **The session's stops are E126's: one die reading at or above 80 °C, or the board above 93 W
on three samples in a row, ends the session**; E101's registered stop is 97 W, so every stop sits 4 W or more inside it, and the highest estimate 21 W
inside the session stop. Nothing here drives the card toward a PMIC over-power or over-temperature reset: this card's own alarm level is unmeasured (section
11 item 14, E117 not run), E126 read 85.62 W at its highest on it with no event, and the highest planned estimate is 10 W under that.

## Frozen predictions

`predictions-e136.json`, printed in `predictions-e136.txt`, from committed inputs only (`make_predictions_e136.py`). **The speed law:** a line that misses the
L2 costs what it costs in E119's arm that serves it the same way, on this card: T(f) = T_c1(f) + n_L3/128 (T_c8s(f) − T_c1(f)) + n_fresh/128 (T_c16(f) −
T_c1(f)), each arm's T(f) = C/f + M as E119 fitted it on aifoundry2; the kind does not change T. **The power law:** energy per op interpolated the same way
from E107's pinned card (S × T of c1, c8s and c16, per kind), the real kind as zeros + r (random − zeros) with r E95's mean over the four real tiles
(0.995, 0.983, 0.792); then E119's rule for c(600) and its board power at each clock, B(f) + c s(f) (600 T600)/(f T(f)). **The clock:** E119's dither of
E101's relation, footing **G1** (the registered primary: burst cap 66.15 ± 2.75 W, sustained 49.75 ± 1.95 W) and footing **G2** (the sustained cap
refitted, 60.83 W, so that the dither gives E126's five held random fp32 clocks, rms 25.5 MHz; a held cell then predicts E126's mean held board power,
62.06 W). **The die:** a burst's die_max = die_start + 0.1445 °C/W × (P_burst − 21.5 W) (fitted over E119's and E126's 59 bursts); a sustained run's
window mean = min(65.5, 0.379 die_start + 0.621 (44.0 + 0.67 (P_sus − 21.5))), with 0.67 °C per board W the steady coefficient of E111 and E133 and
0.621 fitted over the 25 sustained runs of E119 and E126 whose die stayed under 64 °C. die_start is each run's own.

Every cell (24 of them: two GEMMs × three formats × four kinds) has its frozen S, c, P(600/700/800), admission estimate, burst clock, power and rate,
sustained clock, power and rate on both footings and its die rise in the JSON. **In short: the large GEMM that streams to DRAM runs at 0.29 of a resident
block's rate at 800 MHz (E119's c1) and barely gains from the clock (×1.056 for ×1.333), so random's power stays near or under the caps and zeros lead random by at most 2%
(G1) or not at all (G2); the GEMM the L3 holds runs 1.6 times faster than that and draws 68 W at 800 MHz in fp32 random, so the governor holds it back,
and zeros lead by 6-8% (G1) or 1-2% (G2) in fp32 and fp16.**

## Rules, which can fail

The full text and bars are in `predictions-e136.json` `rules`; `selftest_e136.py` shows each passing on the frozen numbers and failing far off them, R1's
three placements and the preflight refusing (`selftest.txt`).

- **R0, the ties:** every check of C (the host's own reference over the whole K) exact or approx; the smoke's cycles per op within 50-250% of P1's. A FAIL
  labels every other verdict.
- **R1, where `g4k` falls:** h = (T800 − T800(P2)) ÷ (T800(P1) − T800(P2)): A HELD at h ≥ 0.75, A FRESH at ≤ 0.25, BETWEEN otherwise, OUTSIDE below −0.5 or
  above 1.5 (the line-cost law fails). The point: PASS if T800 is within 5% of P1's. Per format; fp32 primary.
- **R2, `g2k`:** T800 within 5% of the law; X within 0.03.
- **R3, burst power:** each admitted cell's first-second board power at 800 MHz within 4 W of the frozen P(800), and at or below its estimate + 1 W.
- **R4, sustained clock:** within 30 MHz of the frozen clock, on G1 and on G2; the footing with the lower rms predicts better.
- **R5, the zeros-against-kind gap:** burst within 0.03 of the frozen gap; sustained within 0.05 of G1's.
- **R6, the die:** a burst's die_max and a sustained run's window mean within 3 °C of the law, with the run's measured start.
- **R7, the kind:** every cell's T at or above 790 MHz within 1% of its zeros'.
- **R8, the rates:** burst within 5% and sustained within 7% of the frozen rate.

The reduction is `reduce_e136.py` (E126's collect(), E119's clock_x). **Every README number of measured against predicted is measured − predicted, per cell
and as an rms per quantity** (`results.json` `errors`): the prediction error the README leads with.

## What this changes for the prediction (Q137)

The kinds page's power model has never been asked about a GEMM whose operands stream. E136 tests two new terms against a card: **a per-line memory term**
(time and energy per missing line, by where it is served, calibrated on E107's and E119's arms and applied to a GEMM it was never fitted on) and **the heat
term** (E111's and E133's 0.67 °C per board W, with a shape fitted on E119 and E126) on a workload neither saw. The README reports, per quantity (burst
power, sustained power, clock, rate, die), the error of these frozen numbers on this card, out of sample, and which footing of the governor predicts better.

## What this does not do

No energy per op at a fixed clock (a governed card has none; E107's pinned-card S is the energy input). No vendor binary: rgm136 is the vendor's pattern
built from public sources, not the vendor's compiled kernel. One card, one session, one run per cell; the unit of replication is the run, so no interval
here is between sessions. No room thermometer: the room is a proxy (the card's idle die and power before the session, E126's C1).
