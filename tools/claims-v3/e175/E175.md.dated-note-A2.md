## A2-1. What was wrong

- On E175's 24 kept tiles, M9's part on M9's D predicts the burst board above M10 carried on 19 tiles, equal on 1 and below on 4. The difference
  delta = M9's part − M10 carried is **+0.0338 W on average** (governed +0.0133, pinned +0.0748; −0.005 to +0.151 W), at the frozen fallback
  ([`m1-power-e175.txt`](m1-power-e175.txt)). On the clock, M10 carried sits 0.72 MHz above M9's part on the governed tiles on average.
- The paired engine compares rms(a) with rms(b), where a = measured − M10 carried and b = measured − M9's part = a − delta, tile by tile. So the
  error the two models share cancels, and exactly: **mean(a²) − mean(b²) = 2 · mean(delta) · (ā_δ − θ)**, with ā_δ = Σ delta·a / Σ delta (the
  session's own board error, weighted by how far the two models differ on each tile) and θ = Σ delta² / (2 Σ delta) = **0.047 W**. M1's point is
  below zero exactly when ā_δ < θ (it held in every one of the 32,000 simulated sessions below). **M1 therefore reads on which side of the two
  predictions the session's board level falls, not whether M10's D is better.** The pinned tiles carry 74% of Σ delta, so M1 is mostly the
  pinned board level, which E175-A2-Bp already scores.
- So the registered meanings misread M1 on these tiles. "BETTER: M10's D … improves the governed bursts through a part fitted on measured D, so
  the 600 MHz gains carry" can be read off a board level alone: a session whose board reads below both predictions gives BETTER whatever D does.
  "NOT RESOLVED: the part's own error (about 0.8 W) hides the D difference" is not the mechanism, since the pairing removes that error; NOT
  RESOLVED means the session's board level is small against the errors' tile-to-tile spread.

**So:**
- E175.md's expectation "M1 NOT RESOLVED" holds in 80.7% and 72.2% of sessions at the two empirical truths.
- Nearly every resolved M1 is BETTER with the board below both predictions: 659 of 739 BETTER sessions at the held-out cells, 1,031 of 1,105 at
  E165's errors.
- With the board 0.5 W low, M1 reads BETTER in 73.6% of sessions.
- With M9's part right, M1 still reads BETTER in 11.7%, more often than WORSE (1.7%).
- **E175 cannot test M10's D against M9's on the board at this design.** The verifier's own estimate (19.6 / 0.6 / 79.8% and 27.2 / 0.3 / 72.5%,
  fresh bootstrap draws) agrees within about one point.
- The session's own reads move delta a little. Zeros-anchor reads 1 W below or above the fallback level change it by at most 0.011 W on any

  bootstrap resolves more often than its nominal 5%. That makes it a gate in the reading, not a test of its own.

## A2-3. The reading: how the records state E175-A2-M1

1. **The verdict** is computed and recorded exactly as registered (E175.md, "Decision rules" and "The paired engine").
2. **Beside it**, the record states:
   - Bg's and Bp's means (M10 carried's errors, governed and pinned);
   - the same groups' means of M9's part's errors;
   - ā_δ and θ;
   - the reported arm E175-A2-M1c.
3. **E175-A2-M1c** (reported, never a verdict) is the same paired engine with its own rule id's seed (80,000 + the character codes of
   "E175-A2-M1c" = 80,656). It runs on each model's board errors less that model's own mean in each frozen group (governed, pinned). Each
   group's level is removed, and what is left compares the two models' tile-to-tile patterns. Its clock form is reported beside the clock.
4. **A resolved M1 with the session's board on its own side is the session's board level, not evidence on D.** That is BETTER with the board
   below both models' predictions in both groups, or WORSE with it above both in both groups (M10 carried's predictions being the lower ones, as
   on these tiles). The record says so, gives the means, and does not write the registration's sentence for that verdict. This is the
   verifier's rule: "BETTER with board errors below the predictions, or WORSE above them, is a session level", with "above" read as above both
   predictions. Otherwise a session in which M9's part is exactly right would be read as a level.
5. **Any other resolved M1** has the board between the predictions, or on different sides of them in the two groups. It is **evidence on D
   only if E175-A2-M1c resolves the same way**. Otherwise it is a group's board level, recorded as in 4. Where it is evidence on D, the record
   adds that the reading is weak at this design (point 8).
6. **NOT RESOLVED** is recorded as "the session's board level is too small against the errors' spread to resolve the two models'
   near-constant difference; it says nothing on D". This supersedes, as the mechanism, the registered "the part's own error (about 0.8 W)
   hides the D difference"; the frozen text stays.
7. **The clock beside M1** is reported, never a verdict, and read the same way. A resolved clock beside, with the session's governed clock level
   on its side, is that level. At the registration's level truth (governed clock +3 MHz) it reads BETTER in 58.0% of sessions, and at −3 MHz
   WORSE in 78.8%.
8. **The reading gives "evidence on D" rarely.** At the two empirical truths it does so in 1.2% and 1.1% of sessions, where the registered
   sentence would say "the 600 MHz gains carry" in 18.5% and 27.6% (every BETTER). Under every truth tried it is at most 1.6%. With M9's
   part right it splits 0.6% BETTER against 0.4% WORSE, so a D reading from E175's M1 points the right way barely more often than the wrong
   way.

## A2-4. At scoring: one desk step after the collector, before the records

After `box/step_e175_v3.sh collect` has written `docs/reports/data/<day>-e175-governed-aifoundry2/results.json` with the registered scorer
(unchanged), the scoring lane runs the following at the desk, as a short local job:

```
/usr/bin/python3 -B tools/claims-v3/e175/m1_reading_e175.py docs/reports/data/<day>-e175-governed-aifoundry2/results.json \
  --out docs/reports/data/<day>-e175-governed-aifoundry2/m1-reading.json > docs/reports/data/<day>-e175-governed-aifoundry2/m1-reading.txt
```

- [`m1_reading_e175.py`](m1_reading_e175.py) re-derives M1 and the clock beside from the scorer's own per-tile blocks with the registered
  engine, and refuses (exit 2) unless both equal the scorer's. It then computes M1c, the means, ā_δ, θ and the identity, and prints A2-3's
  sentence. Its two files go into the data commit with the rest.
- Its self-test, [`selftest_m1_reading_e175.py`](selftest_m1_reading_e175.py) → [`selftest-m1-reading.txt`](selftest-m1-reading.txt), passes
  13 of 13 on made-up sessions scored by the registered scorer itself:
  - measured = M10 carried: BETTER, evidence on D;
  - measured = M9's part: WORSE, evidence on D;
  - 0.5 W below / above both, with noise: BETTER / WORSE, the session's level;
  - noise only: NOT RESOLVED;
  - 9 tiles: NOT SCORED;
  - an altered M1, an altered clock beside, and a foreign file: refused;
  - one pass: NOT SCORED;
  - the identity, in every case.

## A2-6. What this does to predicting power and heat

Nothing measured changes. The note stops E175 from reporting the session's board level as a test of M10's D under the governor. On these tiles
the two models differ by a near-constant 0.034 W on the board. So E175 can score M10 carried's governed clock and board errors blind (G, Bg, Bp,
F1, W1, F2, W2, H1), but it cannot say whether M10's D beats M9's there. That needs tiles on which the two models' D differ by far more than a
session's board level is known to (a later design).
