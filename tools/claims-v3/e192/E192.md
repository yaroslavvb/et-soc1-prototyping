## 1. The question (quoted from the proposal, section 1)

On every minion, a stream of register-to-register scalar integer instructions with no load and no store, in arms that differ in one
thing only. What does an issuing core cost per instruction (the no-op sled), and what does an add add; then, per instruction, the price
of one flipped bit on operand port A as the flipped bits grow (4, 8, 16 in one 16-bit field; 32 over two fields; 64), by field position,
on port B, when the bits move from port A to port B with the result held, when the result is held at zero by an `and`, and the price of
one position of carry propagation, from two carry ladders with different held operands. Each in pJ and in electrons per instruction,
through the core (minion) rail at its measured voltage and through the 12 V input.

The lab has priced such ladders only in the 8-lane vector integer unit (E60, E61) and the scalar instructions only on zeros, one constant
and random data (the energy catalogue). These scalar ladders are new; they build on both: E60/E61's harness style, E78's meter method and
approach, and the catalogue's `nop` and `add` as anchors of scale (section 13: E192 is not blind to any of them).

## 2. The arms (registered; the designed counts are the host's own table, checked cell by cell in section 16)

A **pair** arm alternates `op x5, x6, x7` and `op x5, x28, x29` (128 pairs a loop body); **same** repeats `op x5, x6, x7`; **nop** is the
32-bit `nop`. Every loop body is 256 instructions plus the loop's own `addi`, `csrr`, `bltu` (259 an iteration), all 32-bit encodings,
the head 64-byte aligned. The four sources are fixed per arm and the same on every hart. The background bits are fixed random values
(`std::mt19937_64`, seed 1: X = 0x2245bd5fbb686f68, Y = the second draw with X's ones cleared = 0x00aa020000109006), with the two sources'
ones disjoint, so outside the carry arms an add has no carries and its result equals the xor, and the `and` of the sources is zero
throughout. Fields: f0 = bits 0-15, f1 = 16-31, f2 = 32-47, f3 = 48-63; `add.a.f2.k4` and `.k8` flip bits 32-35 and 32-39; `add.a.f12`
flips bits 16-47. Carry ladders, both from bit 16 and inside bits 16-47 (L = 0, 8, 16, 24, 31): `add.cr.lL` (result held: A = a run of L
ones from bit 16 plus a one at bit 16, carrying L positions; B = a one at bit 16+L plus 0) and `add.crb.lL` (port A held: A holds the run
in both instructions; B alternates 0 and a one at bit 16). `add.ripple` (all ones + 0, then all ones + 1) is a demonstration, reported
alone, outside every fit. Every arm's four source values are in [`desk/arm-table-e192.jsonl`](desk/arm-table-e192.jsonl).

## 4. What is measured, and how (registered)

- **One sampler at 10 Hz for the whole session**, started first and stopped with SIGTERM only, at the end: board power (`board_w`, the
  12 V input), the minion rail's power and voltage (`sp.minion_w[0]`, `sp.minion_mv[0]`), the die reading (`temp_c.minshire[0]`) and the
  minion clock (`mhz.minion`). `SAMPLER=pvt`, the lab's per-sensor sampler (E89's `tools/claims-v3/e89/pvt_sample.py`, which adds the 34
  raw temperature codes at 0.061 °C a code), is preferred where it answers on this card and the kit's audit reads its command as a READ;
  the plain sampler (`ettelem sample --every-ms 100`, no statistics reset) is the fallback. Which one ran is recorded with the data.
- **A run**: one device process, `timeout 10 intbits_host --arm <arm> --seconds 4 --window 240000000 --stop-file OUT/STOP`: launches of
  240,000,000 cycles (0.4 s at 600 MHz) back to back for 4 s. The host starts no launch that could end after its 8.5 s budget (at the
  slowest clock it allows for, 500 MHz, plus 1 s), and none once the stop file exists. Every launch prints one `INTBITS {json}` line: the
  arm, its four sources and designed counts, the loop iterations and body instructions, the neighbourhood counters read before and after
  the loop (thread-0 instructions retired, I-cache requests and misses, L1 data misses), the cycles, the launch's epoch bounds, the
  participant count and `check_bad` (the destination after one more A,B pair, against the host's arithmetic).
- **The approach before every run (E78's):** heater launches (the lab's E78 heater, `--test fma --type fp32 --pattern none --values randn
  --shires 0xffffffff --per-shire 32 --seconds 2 --seed 1`, each under `timeout 10` with the stop file, at most 15) until the die reads 59
  °C or more; then idle until it reads 58 °C or less; then at least 2 s with no device process, the reading in [57.5, 58]; at most 120 s
  of waiting. Every run's record carries its approach (start reading, heats, wait, quiet, a wait that timed out, on protocol or not).

## 5. The pass, the order, the passes and the smoke (registered)

- **Before anything opens a card node:** the card is free: `et-who --check` exits 0, no other user's device process runs, and a probe of
  the card lock succeeds (the existing lock file opened read-only, `flock -n`, never created, never waited for). Otherwise nothing is
  opened.
- **A pass** is one block under the card lock: 10 s idle (marked `before`); two unrecorded burn-in runs of the anchor; the anchor
  (`anchor_first`); **the 32 arms of `arms.txt` in the order `random.Random("<hostname>|<pass>")` gives, the anchor's own arm `add.a.f2`
  among them**; the anchor again (`anchor_last`); 10 s idle (marked `after`): **36 runs a pass.** *A wording difference, read before any
  datum:* request.md section 4 and BUILD.md say "the 31 other arms"; the reference code (`run_block.py` with `arms.txt`), the proposal's
  own count (section 9: "36 runs a pass") and its dry listing (section 10: 327 device calls, 36 a pass) shuffle all 32. E192 registers the
  code. The order for a host named `aifoundry3`, passes 0-8, is written out in
  [`desk/order-e192-aifoundry3.txt`](desk/order-e192-aifoundry3.txt) (registered: the draw depends only on the hostname string, the pass
  and `arms.txt`).
- **Between passes** the lock is released for 30 s, then the holder check is made again (every holder must be this session's own sampler
  on this card's nodes; no other user's device process) before it is taken again; a holder stops the session.
- **Nine passes**, pass 0 a registered warm-up, recorded and set aside by the reducer: **8 measured passes** (the pass is the unit of
  replication).
- **The smoke block first** (pass -1, under the lock, never reduced): with a heater, one heater launch whose highest board reading on this
  card must stay under **70 W** (else the session stops before any pass: the preheat would come too near the 80 W stop); then `nop`,
  `add.a.f2` and `add.cr.l31`, 2 s each, no preheat, no burn-in.

## 6. The reduction (registered: [`proposal/src/reduce_intbits.py`](proposal/src/reduce_intbits.py), sha256 `715fddaa36c1ca81889b20f39a649254479cb1db68d797c8a9876e0c0677d0b7`, run unchanged)

- **Per run** (one device process): t0 = the start of its first launch, t1 = the end of its last. **Board:** the samples in [t0 + 1 s,
  min(t0 + 3 s, t1)]; **dip rule:** a sample more than 2 W below the window's median is dropped (a reading caught between two launches),
  counted per run and per arm; P_board = the mean of the rest. **Rail:** the samples in [t0 + 2.5 s, t1], over idle, divided by the
  fraction a first-order average with tau 1.05 s reaches over that window after a step at t0 (about 0.95 for a 4 s run): (P_rail -
  P_idle) / frac. **Rate:** body instructions over t1 - t0 (the gaps between launches are in the time and in the power window: the lab's
  basis); the device rate (cycles over the clock) beside it. **Temperature:** the window mean of the 34 codes' mean (when more than 90% of
  the session's samples carry them), else of the whole-degree reading.
- **Run gates** (a failed run is reported and dropped): every window sample within 1 MHz of the window's median clock; at least 5 board
  samples left; every launch `ok` with `check_bad` 0; the same participant count on every launch.
- **Per pass:** idle = the board and rail means over the last 7 s of the `before` idle and the first 7 s of the `after` idle; the anchor
  drift (last minus first anchor) is taken out linearly in run order.
- **Temperature (primary):** the pooled slope beta (W per °C) of arm-minus-control power on arm-minus-control temperature, one intercept
  per arm-control pair, over all pairs of all used passes, once per meter; every arm's power is corrected to the temperature at the root
  of its control chain. The uncorrected values are reported beside. The per-arm energy over idle carries instead the lab's leakage
  correction, 23.257/36 x exp((T - 80)/36) W per °C times (T_run - T_idle) (board; the rail x 0.79).
- **Energies:** E = (P - P_idle) / rate per arm, pJ per instruction over idle, on both meters; contrasts E_arm - E_control in the same
  pass; mean and SE over the used passes; a contrast whose 95% interval (mean +- 2 SE) contains zero is reported as a bound. **Electrons**
  per instruction: the contrast's energy over (e x V_rail) on the rail, over (e x 12 V) at the board.
- **Ladders** (per pass, then mean and SE, fJ per bit or per position): the f2 count ladder's slope and quadratic term on K = 0, 4, 8, 16
  (`add.z`, `add.a.f2.k4`, `.k8`, `add.a.f2`); the per-bit field prices f0-f3 ((E(add.a.fN) - E(add.z)) / 16) and a12 ((E(add.a.f12) -
  E(add.z)) / 32); additivity (`add.a.all` - `add.z` - the four fields; `add.a.f12` - `add.z` - (f1 + f2)); the `and` field prices f1-f3
  ((E(and.a.fN) - E(and.z)) / 16) and and12 (the mean of f1 and f2); d12 = a12 - and12; f2 - f3 for `add` and for `and`; the slopes S_cr and
  S_crb on L = 0, 8, 16, 24, 31 and their quadratic terms; **C1 = (S_cr + S_crb - a12) / 2**, which under a model linear in the flips of
  each port, of the result and of the carry vector is the price of one carry position; C2 = ((S_crb - d12) - (S_cr - and12)) / 2; C3 =
  and12 - C2. The field-0 arms and `add.ripple` are reported alone, outside every fit.
- **What E192 publishes:** the reducer's output (`intbits.json`: every run, every arm's energies and contrasts on both meters, corrected
  and uncorrected, the ladders, beta) and the registered checks' outcomes (section 7), in E192's data commit, with the interpreter named.

## 7. The registered checks (request.md section 7, in its words; each reported PASS or FAIL with its numbers, after the data)

1. `check_bad` = 0 and 1,024 participants on every launch; retired / body within 0.1% of 259/256 on every run.
2. Every board sample in a window at 600 MHz; rail voltage within +-3 mV of the pass median; L1 data misses 0.
3. Instruction-cache misses per iteration equal across arms (the 1 KB loop stays resident).
4. Rates equal across arms within 0.5%; a slower arm is reported. Runs whose approach was off protocol are counted.
5. Additivity: `add.a.all - add.z` = the sum of the four field rungs within 2 SE; `add.a.f12` = f1 + f2 within 2 SE.
6. Linearity: the quadratic terms of the f2 count ladder and of both carry ladders within 2 SE of zero.
7. The zero rungs: `xor.z` and `and.z` against `add.z` (the opcode alone).
8. Charge per instruction (energy / V) is what the electron counts use; the rail and board splits are both reported.

The reducer applies part of these as run gates (section 6: `check_bad`, one participant count, the clock within 1 MHz of the window's
median); it does not itself test **1,024** participants, **600** MHz, the rail's +-3 mV, L1 misses, I-cache misses per iteration, the
0.5% rates, or the 2 SE tests of 5 and 6. Those are computed after the data from the reducer's output and the raw records by the kit's
checker, with these thresholds and no other (desk check of code: the reducer's per-run records carry `ret_over_body`, `icache_miss`,
`l1d_miss`, `rate`, `V_rail`, `mhz` and the approach flags, section 16). The proposal attaches no exclusion to a failed check beyond the
reducer's run gates: a failed check is reported as failed, beside the prices it qualifies.

## 8. The proposal's signal budget (quoted from the proposal, section 5; its arithmetic, not re-derived here)

The budget assumes 3.82e11 instructions a second (1,024 minions x 600 MHz x 0.622, the lab's one-hart rate for its short `addi` loop;
with 259-instruction bodies the card's rate may be 0.9-1.0 a cycle, which makes every step larger); the rate is measured per run.
*Desk check of the sources:* the nearest figure in the lab's public record is the catalogue's 0.6214 a cycle for its hand-written `spin`
loop on one hart (as `tools/claims-v3/e180/E180.md` cites it), and E60's public data does not state the floor below, 0.032 W rms per
run, in those words (its standard errors lie near 0.032 W); both are the proposal's, quoted here, not re-derived. At that
rate 1 pJ per instruction is 0.38 W. Floor: E60's 0.032 W rms per run about its line on this card, 8 passes. Expected standard errors
(board, at the budget's rate): a single arm-control contrast 0.016 W (42 fJ per instruction); a per-bit price from one 16-bit field 2.6
fJ, from `add.a.f12` 1.3 fJ; a carry ladder's slope 1.2 fJ per position; the f2 count slope 2.5 fJ per bit; **C1 about 1.1 fJ**; and12
1.9 fJ; f2 minus f3 3.7 fJ per bit. The ladder steps are rungs of a fit, not decisions on their own; the lone contrasts (`add.same - nop`,
`add.z - add.same`, `xor.z`, `and.z`, `xor.a.f2 - xor.z` against `add.a.f2`) are registered as measured with their SE, or as bounds; none
is a decision.

## 9. Stops and commands (registered)

- **Live stops** (`proposal/src/stopwatch.py`, on the session's telemetry; each writes `OUT/STOP` with its reason): board power **above
  80 W on three distinct readings in a row** (a repeated value is the same reading of the board meter, which neither counts nor resets the
  run); a die reading **at or above 70 °C**; the sampler **quiet for 10 s** (or no sample in its first 60 s). The host checks the stop
  file before every 0.4 s launch and the block before every device process, so a stop takes effect within one launch. Nothing is killed.
- **In the block** (`run_block.py`): a die reading at or above 70 °C, or a newest telemetry line older than 5 s, before a device process
  writes the stop file and ends the block.
- **The smoke's heater check:** the heater's highest board reading on this card under 70 W, else no pass runs.
- **A holder** found before the sampler or between passes, or the card lock busy, ends the session; nothing more opens.
- **By hand:** `touch OUT/STOP` (the launch in flight finishes first).
- **Commands:** reads only (section 3); every device process under `timeout 10` with `--stop-file`; one sampler, nothing else on the
  management node; the card lock per pass, never waited for; between passes released for 30 s.

## 11. Fallbacks, and what each means (registered)

| what happens | what it means |

## 14. What this cannot say (stated before the data)

- **The card's rate for this 32-bit loop is unknown until the first run** (the proposal's own open point: the budget assumes 0.622 a
  minion-cycle; 0.9-1.0 would make every step larger). Every energy is per instruction at the measured rate.
- **aifoundry3 is the lab's card for switching power over idle, never absolute watts** (14-card-behaviour.md): one card, one session,
  no second card.
- **C1 is a carry position's price only under a model linear** in the flips of each port, of the result and of the carry vector; the
  ladders' quadratic terms test linearity at their resolution, not every interaction. The field prices assume a bit costs the same inside
  a field; f2 - f3 tests one difference.
- **The pair arms change 6 bits of the instruction encoding** between neighbours (the register fields), the same in every pair arm;
  `add.z - add.same` carries that change together with the second register pair, so it reads the two together.
- **The absolute energies over idle share one bias, the contrasts do not** (desk check of code). The reducer's idle takes the first 7 s
  of the `after` idle, which starts when the last run's process exits; a meter that averages (the rails' and the board's about 1.05 s,
  E58) carries part of the last run's power into those seconds. On a synthetic session whose rail has a 1.05 s first-order filter, every
  arm's rail energy over idle read low by one common amount (7-11% of the injected values) while every contrast and ladder came back
  exact (section 16). On the card its size
  depends on each meter's averaging and on how long the host takes to exit; the per-arm "pJ over idle" (the no-op sled's included) is to
  be read with it, the contrasts and ladders without.
- **The kernel built here differs, as a file, from the one the proposal cites** (a different toolchain; section 16); E192's kernel is the
  one built on aifoundry3 and checked there.
- **The sealed forecasts:** E192's lab files say nothing of them; the model session scores them.
