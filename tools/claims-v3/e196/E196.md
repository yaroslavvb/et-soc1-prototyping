## 1. The question (quoted from the proposal, section 1, shortened)

On the reference card, the lab's resident fp32 TensorFMA loop (hart 0 of all 1,024 minions; A and B in the L1 scratchpad; 16 x 16 x
16, 4,096 multiply-adds an op, 546 cycles) has a measured price: 5.816 pJ a multiply-add on random data, 0.433 on zeros, ones
8.257 W over zeros (E78); a tile whose products all flush to zero costs what ones cost within 0.045 W (E178). "What is not known is
how the clocked part and the data part follow the issue":

1. **The issue ladders:** the same instruction, reads and op rate with 0, 4, 8, 12 or 16 of the 16 A rows issuing micro-ops (the
   row mask: a masked row issues nothing, the sequencer still steps, E109), on the zero tile (every lane gated) and on a held live
   tile whose every element is 1.5 x 2^-64 (every lane live, every product below the smallest normal, so the accumulators stay +0.0
   and no operand or result bit changes); and on random normal values at 0, 8 and 16 rows. The slopes give energy per issued
   micro-op; each ladder's shape says whether energy follows the issued micro-ops alone.
2. **The spacing of the issued rows at equal issue:** on the held tile at 8 rows, the issued rows grouped 8, 4, 2 or 1 at a time
   (masks 0x00FF, 0x0F0F, 0x3333, 0x5555); and on the zero tile, two A B B A trains read spread against packed: 4 rows (0x1111
   against 0x000F) and 8 rows (0x5555 against 0x00FF).
3. **The data part by field:** against a held partner, B's sign or all 8 of B's exponent bits flipping on every micro-op (A =
   1.0), and A's exponent bits on every second micro-op (B = 1.0). B's sign ties to E65's bit-31 price.
4. **Zeros:** what a multiply-add with one zero operand costs against a live one (r), from the random tile with half of A's
   elements zero, half of B's, and both (the lab's sparse50 tile); and the lab's A-zero tile with random B with every row issued
   (the E78 tie) and with none, which separates B's transport through gated micro-ops from the gated multiply-add itself.

Each in board watts over idle and over its reference, pJ per op and per issued multiply-add, and electrons per op (and per flipped
bit) through the core rail at its measured volts and through the 12 V input (quoted).

## 5. What is measured, and how (registered; request.md sections 3 and 4)

- **One sampler at 10 Hz for the whole session** (the lab's choice of sampler: `ettelem sample --every-ms 100`, never `--reset-ms`;
  the per-sensor sampler is not used: the reduction reads the whole-degree die reading), started as the lab's `lib.sh` does (its first
  line within 10 s, else stopped with SIGTERM and started again, up to six times; its own bound the deadline plus 5 minutes) and
  stopped with SIGTERM only. Read: `board_w` (the 12 V input), `sp.minion_w[0]` and `sp.minion_mv[0]` (the core rail and its
  volts), `temp_c.minshire[0]` (the die), `mhz.minion` (the clock).
- **A pass run:** one device process of 4 s of launches of about 0.5 s after a calibration launch. Board watts over seconds 1-3 from
  its first launch; its own idle bracket [-1.2, -0.2) s; the die over both; the core rail over the same window, its 1.05 s first-order
  lag undone and read 0.26 s late; the rail's volts; the clock in every sample (quoted, `reduce_x14.py`'s head).
- **A train launch:** the mean board watts from its start + 170 ms to its end (E182; quoted).
- **Denominators from the device** (quoted): tensor ops completed inside the window (each launch's op count, prorated by overlap),
  never the wall clock; cycles per op from the cycle counter; issued micro-ops, issued multiply-adds and designed flips per op from
  `arms.json`. **The unchanged host's records** carry the op count as `iters` and the participating minions as `minions`; registered:
  a pass run's op count per launch is `iters x minions`, which equals the device's count on every launch that is `ok` (the host's `ok`
  requires every participant to report; *desk check of code*).
- **The approach before every pass run and every train-block anchor** (quoted; E60's and E78's): heater launches (the lab's E78
  heater, section 3) while the die reads below 59 C, at most 15; then idle until it reads 58 C or less; then at least 2 s with no
  device process, the reading in [57.5, 58]; at most 120 s of waiting. A run is on protocol only when that approach was kept; two
  failed heater launches in a row end the block. The train's processes follow the opening anchor's approach without one of their
  own (E182). **Dated note 2** (Q191's lane `fix-e196`, Sun 15 Nov 2026, before any E196 datum and before this registration landed;
  verify-e196's finding 3): the approach's rule is quoted, but **its heater is the lab's choice**, not the request's: request.md
  section 3's default heater is `x14_host --values randn --label heater --seconds 2` (its own host; it notes that the lab's sparsity
  host takes other arguments); E196 runs the lab's E78 heater on the unchanged host (section 3's command), because the passes' frame
  runs on that host, E78 and E192 used this heater on this card, and the lab's host refuses the request's heater arguments (section

## 6. The run: the session, the smoke, the trains, the passes, the order (registered)

**The session, in order** (quoted, with the lab's choices marked):
1. The look and the gates before anything opens a card node (the kit's): no holder (`et-who --check` exit 0), no other user's device
   process, the card lock free on a probe; the 40-minute rest met (section 10); the window open (section 12).
2. **The opening configuration read** (`ettelem config`, read only, under the card lock, `timeout 10`), never before the rest opens
   (a configuration read is a card command: the lesson of E192's day). **It must read TDP 0 W and 600 MHz** (the card as found;
   the lab's choice, E189's form), else no session: a gate, no data.
3. The sampler; the live stops started and seen running (section 9).
4. **The smoke block** under the card lock (pass -1; never reduced).
5. 30 s with the lock released, the holder check, **train T1** (block 101) under the lock; again for **train T2** (block 102).
6. 30 s, the holder check, then **the passes 0-5** (six), and **pass 6 (a seventh)** only if it fits before the deadline, each under
   the lock with 30 s between; a block starts only if it can end before the deadline, by the first pass's measured length.
7. The sampler stopped (SIGTERM); **the closing configuration read**, only when the card is free (after a session that ended because
   another holder was seen, or on a signal, it is not made and `config-end.json` says why; otherwise the holder check must pass again
   first, with our sampler stopped).

**The smoke block** (registered: the request's smoke, with **the lab's choice** of a second set of its four arms so that both hosts
are tried before any pass):
1. The heater check: one heater launch (the lab's E78 heater); it must exit 0 with every launch record `ok` and no tensor error, else
   the gate fails at once and nothing more runs; its highest board reading at most 70 W, else the session ends.
2. `I.H.m00FF`, `I.R.m00FF`, `Z.a0` and `I.R.full` on **the lab's unchanged host**, 2 s each, no preheat, no burn-in.
3. **The premise runs:** the same four arms on **the request's host**, 2 s each, with their read-backs of C (`I.R.m00FF`, a masked
   random arm, is the request's "riskiest premise": its masked rows must stay zero and its issued rows live).
4. The first two processes of train T1 (SFFS, then FSSF) on the request's host.

**The smoke gate** (quoted, applied to both hosts' records): every smoke run and both train processes have launch records; every
launch is `ok` with no tensor error; every read-back of C is `ok`; cycles per op within 0.5 % of 546 (no row mask, or every row
enabled) or 544 (a row masked); the train records follow their order. **A failed gate runs no train and no pass** (no data that
session).

**A train block** (quoted; hosts registered in section 3): 10 s idle; the approach and `K.pi` (`train_anchor_first`) on the
unchanged host; the train's 24 processes back to back on the request's host (between processes the stop file, the die reading and
the telemetry's age are checked; no approach between them); `K.pi` (`train_anchor_last`) with its approach; 10 s idle.

**A pass** (quoted; every run on the unchanged host): 10 s idle; two unrecorded burn-in runs of `K.pi`; `K.pi` (`anchor_first`); the
28 other pass arms in the order `random.Random("<hostname>|<pass>")` gives over `arms.json`'s order (`run_block.py`'s own draw);
`K.pi` (`anchor_last`); 10 s idle: **32 runs a pass**. Every pass counts (each starts with two burn-in runs); results from fewer than
5 kept passes are reported as provisional. **The order for a host named `aifoundry3`, passes 0-6, is written out in
[`desk/order-e196-aifoundry3.txt`](desk/order-e196-aifoundry3.txt)** (registered; the draw depends only on the host name string, the
pass number and `arms.json`; the cut order's in `desk/order-e196-aifoundry3-cut.txt`). The kit reads the box's host name; if it is
not `aifoundry3`, the written order is used.

**The cut order** (quoted, with the lab's rule for when it applies): at the session's start, with M the minutes from the first card
command to the earlier of the window's last end and the cap: M >= 53, every arm and six passes (a seventh if it fits); 43 <= M < 53,
every arm and the passes that fit (five expected, the minimum for a reported result); 40 <= M < 43, `DROP_ARMS=F.Ae,Z.b50,I.H.mFFFF`
(left out of every pass, recorded in each pass's plan mark; the trains stay); M < 40, no session. Inside the window of section 12 M
is 60 unless the day's end cuts it.

## 7. The reduction (registered: [`reduce_e196.py`](reduce_e196.py), sha256 `c58c6690016f18e5dac62ba620086f3cefda812cab9e93d2a929cb1aa207f3f7`, run unchanged)

**`reduce_e196.py` is the proposal's `reduce_x14.py`** (committed byte for byte as `proposal/src/reduce_x14.py`, sha256
`c86892e134b5a037770db99c38201d7cce9bc5eb066884096347241d474eaabc`) **with one change, registered here** (the lab's, made necessary by

`"readback": "none"` is not set aside for the C read-back gate, and the output carries `readback_gate` (the runs it exempted). Every

kit's runner writes `"readback": "none"` into every launch record of every run of the unchanged host (the pass runs and the train
blocks' anchors) and into no other. **Run after the data commit:** `reduce_e196.py OUT --readback-none-ok --json x14-e196.json`, its

code in section 16):

- **The dip rule** (E82's amendment A2), over the session's samples in time order: board sample i is dropped when it is more than
  4 W under both samples i-2 and i+2; nothing is set aside for a dip; sample counts are of kept samples.
- **Per pass run:** S = the board's mean over [t0 + 1, t0 + 3) s less its idle bracket [t0 - 1.2, t0 - 0.2) s less the leakage
  slope times the die difference; Bc = the busy mean less the slope times (die - 58 C); the rail's true mean over each window from its
  first-order filter; the rate from the device's op counts prorated over the window.
- **Run gates** (each a reason to set the run aside, counted): at least 15 kept board samples in the window and 8 in the bracket; no
  gap over 0.5 s between kept window samples; launches covering at least 0.97 of the window; every window sample at the pass's clock;
  rail volts within 3 mV of the pass median; cycles per op within 0.5 % of 546 (no mask, or 0xFFFF) or 544 (a row masked); no tensor
  error and every launch `ok`; both read-backs of C `ok` (except, with the flag, a run of the unchanged host); the approach on
  protocol.
- **A pass is kept** when both `K.pi` runs pass and differ by at most 0.3 W; with only one, kept with its drift unmeasured (flagged);
  otherwise set aside.
- **Per arm** over the kept passes (the unit of replication): S, D over its reference in two forms (the leak-corrected busy means, the
  primary E60 form, and the bracketed S), per op and per issued multiply-add (pJ, board and rail), S/rate - S_ref/rate_ref beside every
  D, electrons per op through the core rail and the 12 V input; means, standard errors and 95 % intervals (t over passes); every
  result also at leakage slopes 0 and 0.3803 W/C.
- **Derived:** the ladders' S and steps; the slopes per issued micro-op over the three middle rungs and in W per row; the held line's
  intercept against the mask-0 arm and the full arm's distance from it; the held ladder's middle-step ratio and the zero ladder's
  first-step ratio; the held over the zero tile per multiply-add at full issue (board and rail); random over held per multiply-add; the
  spread contrasts at 8 rows; the MSK bit alone per op; held, random and A-zero over zeros with nothing issued; B through gated
  micro-ops; per field, fJ per designed operand flip and per all designed flips (operand plus the accumulator estimates), electrons per
  operand flip on the rail; the zero shares and r = (share - (1 - g)) / g, g the arm's designed gated fraction; the ties.
- **Trains** (E182's statistic): a launch's reading is the mean of its kept board samples in [start + 170 ms, end] (at least 2); a quad
  (launches 4q+1 to 4q+4, q = 0, 1, 2; launch 0 settles) is sigma (L2 + L3 - L1 - L4) / 2, sigma +1 in SFFS and -1 in FSSF, so every
  quad reads F - S; a process's value is the mean of its complete quads; a unit is one SFFS and one FSSF process; the estimate is the
  mean over units with its 95 % interval; fewer than 8 units: not scored. A process with a launch not `ok`, a failed read-back, a launch
  off its order, or a stop during it gives no value. Raw board watts; per op over the train's mean ops a second; the anchors' drift.
- **Interpreter:** desk-checked under `/usr/bin/python3` 3.12.3 on aifoundry2 (standard library only); the kit names the interpreter

- **What E196 publishes:** `x14-e196.json` (every run, every set-aside run with its reasons, every pass kept or not and why, every arm's
