## A2-2. Beside A1-1's four-rule reading: what it shows about the accumulator, and D(85) − D(127), reported beside it

A1-1 reads the owner's sentence as measured only when four rules read together:
- E178-H-int8 POSITIVE with SIZE PASS;
- E178-S-int8 POSITIVE;
- E178-R-int8 PASS;
- E178-W-int8 PASS.

Beside it, this limit, written before any datum.

**What the four rules together show:**
- int8 one-number power follows the accumulator, at M10's price for it: a matrix of 5s costs more than a matrix of 4s, by about what P
  forecasts from the accumulator and its buses at M10's price;
- the held operands' 1-bits do not set the order (E178-S-int8: int8 85's A and B words hold half the 1-bits of neg_ones', A1-1);
- the card is not flat (E178-R-int8, E178-W-int8).

**What they do not show:** whether that price is paid for the accumulator's flips, or for the 1-bits the accumulator holds each time
it is read.
- The flips are P's account and (i)'s: the Hamming distance of the accumulator's walk, section 3.
- The held 1-bits would cost energy at a read whose energy rises and falls with the bits held, as a precharged read of the
  accumulator's storage would.
- The open RTL's bench, (ii), counts no such cost, but the silicon's register files are not the open RTL's: section 4 notes the
  register file is full custom and not in the open RTL (Ditzel 2026), and A1-8 gives the RTL caveat. So the desk cannot rule the second
  account out.

*Design arithmetic, written before any datum. It is never a forecast or a verdict.* The second account, set by a mechanical rule, has
two parts:
- the accumulator's held 1-bits: 256 elements read at each of an op's 16 steps, each read rising and falling with the 1-bits of the
  element, so 256 x 16 x 2 x popcount(C) toggles an op;
- TenB's bypass, exactly section 3's `tenb_rz`.

C is the exact int32 walk c_t = t x 4vw mod 2^32, for t = 1 to 16 x `forecast-e178.json`'s int8 launch size (912,355 ops). Its
popcount is averaged over that launch. The figures were derived twice for this note, once with a byte-table popcount and once with
per-bit sums of the same walk. The two agree exactly on all 13 kinds. No code is committed with this note; the method above re-makes
the figures from `forecast-e178.json` in a few lines.

Over E178's int8 kinds the two accounts move together, with one exception (the last row). A walk that flips more bits a step also
spreads its sum over more of the word, so it holds more 1-bits at each read:

| | P's account (P, registered) | the held-1-bit-at-read account (design arithmetic) |
|---|---:|---:|
| order of the 13 int8 kinds | P's, section 4 (P against (i)'s flips: Spearman +0.994) | Spearman **+0.989** against P |
| int8 5s − 4s | +0.1862 W; (i) +13,120 flips an op | +19,499 toggles an op: positive too (+0.277 W at P's own 5s − 4s price a flip) |
| int8 85 − neg_ones (E178-S-int8) | +0.3549 W; (i) +25,246 | +22,362: positive too (+0.317 W at that price) |
| E178-W-int8's slope, with the second account priced to give P's 5s − 4s | 7.48 fJ a flip | 5.47 fJ a flip: about W's half-width (1.95-2.18 fJ) from the line |
| **int8 85 − 127** | **+0.1127 W** (F +0.1132; card 1 +0.1083 / +0.1089); (i) **+8,197** | **−3,083**: −3,072 from TenB, −11 from the held bits; **about −0.044 W** at that price |

So H-int8's and S-int8's SIGN and R-int8 read alike under the two accounts. A truth that pays part of the accumulator's cost per held
bit at read can read all four rules as P's account would. A truth that pays all of it reads them at the edge: priced to give P's
5s − 4s, its W-int8 slope is 5.47 against 7.48 fJ a flip; priced to give P's slope, its 5s − 4s is +0.255 W against P's +0.186 W, inside
a couple rule's half-width of about 0.10 W (A1-5's).

**So the four-rule reading, where records or pages quote it, reads:** "a matrix of 5s costs more than a matrix of 4s, by what the
accumulator and M10's price for it say; int8 power follows the accumulator, not the held operands' 1-bits". It does not say which of
the accumulator's activities carries the price: its flips, or the 1-bits it holds at each read. The scorer's printed sentence (A1-1:
"... and int8 power follows the flips, not the held 1-bits") stays as committed. Its "flips" is read with this limit, and its "held
1-bits" are the operands'.

**The one contrast that splits the two accounts: D(int8.e178_v85) − D(int8.e178_v127), per kept pass. It is reported beside the rules
and is never a verdict.** Why its sign differs:
- Both sums wrap the int32 range many times within a launch (section 7; about 98 and 219 times, at 28,900 and 64,516 a step over
  16 x 912,355 steps). So at each read they hold about the same 1-bits: 14.997 against 14.999 a word over the launch, in the design
  arithmetic above.
- 85's walk flips more of them a step. In (i), the accumulator's classes `c_store`, `c_in` and `c_out` give 38,558 against 27,289
  flips an op.
- 127's B word holds 12 more 1-bits than 85's (28 against 16), which TenB's bypass turns into 3,072 more flips an op (section 3's
  `tenb_rz`: 7,168 against 4,096).
- So P forecasts the contrast positive, and the second account negative, by the bypass alone.

**How it is read, with no change to the scorer:**
- **The per-pass value.** In each kept pass p, x_p = D_p(85) − D_p(127). These are the reduction's per-pass D's (`e178.json`,
  `per_pass[p][kind].D`), the values the scorer reads.
- **The same value from the scorer's output.** To rounding, x_p is the scorer's `scored.json`
  `primary.rules["E178-S-int8"].per_pass[p].x` minus `primary.rules["beside: 127 - neg_ones"].per_pass[p].x`, since neg_ones cancels.
- **The per-kind table.** `primary.reported.kinds` gives each kind's D_w, sd_w and n over the kept passes, and its `c_kept`.
  D_w(85) − D_w(127) is the contrast's mean when both kinds have a D in the same kept passes.
- **Its mean and interval** are section 5's, m ± t(0.975, n − 1) sd/sqrt(n) over the passes that give a value. It is read as the
  scorer reads its own beside contrast, 127 − neg_ones, with no SIZE verdict.
- **When it is reported:** only when E178-C keeps both kinds and each has a D in 2 or more kept passes.

**Its resolution here.** It has E178-S-int8's form, one run of each kind a pass. A1-1 gives that form's half-width as 0.177 W at four
passes. P's +0.113 W is about two-thirds of it, and the second account's −0.044 W about a quarter. So E178 is not expected to resolve
the contrast, and a sign it reads is weak evidence either way. A later registration in the trains' form can separate the two
forecasts: E102, E134 and E147 read each item between reads of its own constant zero, at an se of about 0.015 W a difference
(section 9). +0.113 W and −0.044 W lie about ten of those se apart.
