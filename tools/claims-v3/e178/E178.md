## 0. The answer this registration expects, in one table

A **one-number kind** is a TensorFMA tile whose A matrix holds one value v everywhere and whose B matrix holds one value w
everywhere. The kernel the cards run multiplies A by B and adds the products into an accumulator C that persists across every
instruction of a launch (section 1). So in a one-number kind **no operand bit ever changes**: what switches is the accumulator
walking 0, p, 2p, 3p, ... (p the step it adds), the buses that carry it, and two buses that fall to zero between uses.

| per TensorFMA op, one minion; aifoundry3, 600 MHz, s = 1 | int8 4s | int8 5s | fp32 4s | fp32 5s | fp16 4s | fp16 5s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| (i) flips, the exact model, launch mean | 10,240 | 23,360 | 23,888 | 28,291 | 23,997 | 28,364 |
| of which the accumulator's storage | 8,192 | 18,944 | 8,192 | 11,351 | 8,192 | 11,352 |
| (ii) RTL nets toggled, the bench, above zeros | 11,872 | 24,885 | 39,825 | 44,187 | 39,494 | 46,646 |
| P: M10 frozen on (ii), W over zeros | 1.7066 | 1.8928 | 8.2325 | 8.2424 | 8.2249 | 8.2416 |
| F: P with the accumulator's launch mean, W | 1.7081 | 1.8958 | 8.2342 | 8.2656 | 8.2266 | 8.2648 |
| **5s minus 4s** (P / F) | | **+0.186 / +0.188 W** | | +0.010 / +0.031 W | | +0.017 / +0.038 W |
| chance the card resolves it (4 passes) | | 0.90 | | 0.05-0.10 | | 0.07-0.13 |

In int8, a matrix of 5s flips 2.28 times as many bits per op as a matrix of 4s, nearly all in the accumulator's walk (4 x 4 x 4 = 64 is a power of
two, so the int32 accumulator counts like a binary counter, about 2 bits a step; 4 x 5 x 5 = 100 is 1100100 in binary, 4.6 bits a
step), and M10 prices the difference at +0.19 W, which four passes resolve. In the floats the same contrast is a few hundredths
of a watt, below this protocol's resolution. The cleanest float contrast is **1.0 against 1.5 x 2^-64** (equal popcount, 7; every
lane live and multiplying in both), where the second's products flush to zero so its accumulator never moves: +0.075 W (P) or
+0.131 W (F), resolved with probability 0.31-0.67. The full table is `forecast-e178.txt`.

## 1. The computations, in the lab's existing kernel

**(a) TensorFMA multiply-accumulate of one-number matrices** (fp32 TensorFMA32 K 16; fp16 TensorFMA16A32, fp16 products into an
fp32 C, K 32; int8 TensorIMA8A32, int32 C, K 64) - the kernel every kinds session runs, `workloads/sparsity/kernel/sparsity.c`
`run_fma` (unchanged since 18 Sep), behind Q139(k)'s C-logging wrapper `tools/claims-v3/ccheck/sparsity_ccheck.sh` as E171 ran it:
`sparsity_host --test fma --type <t> --pattern none --values file:<tile> --shires 0xffffffff --per-shire 32 --seconds 4` on hart 0
of all 1,024 minions. What the registration's forecast rests on, read from the kernel, the host and the RTL (and checked on
silicon):
- A and B are TensorLoaded once per launch into scratchpad lines 0-15 and 16-31; every TensorFMA re-reads them from the
  scratchpad (the bench counts 288 reads an op in the floats, 224 in int8).
- **C accumulates across instructions within a launch and restarts at every launch**: op 0's first A column multiplies
  (`vpu_tensorfma.v:726`, `rtl-sim/fma_toggle_v2/accum.c`), every later step and op accumulates, so after n ops C = n x A x B,
  rounded per step for fp32 (round to nearest even, DAZ/FTZ), in fp16's fixed-point window, exactly mod 2^32 for int8. Silicon
  follows this word for word: E104 110/110; every launch E171 C-logged passed its check (3,490 of 3,490; P8-KC 2,550 of 2,550).
- A launch is sized to 0.5 s by `repeatFor`: E171's C log (`docs/reports/data/2026-11-05-e171-blind8-aifoundry3/ccheck/`, pass
  17110) has medians of 539,295 ops (fp32), 539,149 (fp16) and 912,355 (int8) a launch, after a 20,000-op calibration launch.
- In a one-number kind every C element follows the same sequence, one step per k: 16 steps an op. The step adds v x w (fp32), two
  products (fp16) or four byte products (int8) to every element.

**(b) Element-wise add (and fmadd) of one-number arrays on the vector unit: not in E178; it waits for a later registration.** A
committed kernel can run it with no new code (`workloads/enercat`, `enercat_host --pattern fadd_ps|fmadd_ps|fadd_pi --operands
file:<256 B of v>`), but (1) its forecast is null in steady state: the loop rotates eight source registers it never overwrites
and writes sinks it never reads, so every instruction sees the same operands and rewrites the same values - no accumulation, no
memory traffic, no bit to flip by the number; (2) the energy manual's committed rows already measured that null at the board
(`docs/energy-manual/03-instructions.md`: fadd.pi 12.7 pJ constant against 12.7 zeros, fadd.ps 22.9 against 22.4, fmadd.ps 27.2
against 27.1, at about ±1 pJ, i.e. ±0.45 W); (3) it runs under a different kit (`tools/claims-v3/catfull`, V3-CATFULL), not the
600 MHz kinds kit E178 ports. A non-null vector case needs an accumulating add (c += v on eight read-write chains), about 10-20
new lines in `gen_ops.py`, with its own registration and forecast.

## 3. Forecast (i): the exact flip model (`flipmodel_e178.c`)

It steps the accumulator's bit sequence through a whole launch with the lab's committed arithmetic (`accum.c` included
unchanged: the arithmetic ccheck pins), and counts the Hamming distance on every piece of state the dataflow writes, per op,
by class. The structure is the RTL's (core-et `b38a1a3`, the open RTL the toggle bench runs), read from its source and from
one-op VCDs of the bench on these tiles (`vcd_signals_e178.py`, `bench/vcd-signals-e178-1.json` and `-2.json`):

| class | where (RTL) | flips per op |
|---|---|---|
| `c_store` | the accumulator's storage: floats `vpu_64x32_3r2w_vpurf` entries 0-31 of each of 8 lanes; int8 `vpu_tensorc_rf` rows 0-15 of 16 TIMA slots | 256 x HD(c_t, c_t+1), summed over the op's 16 steps |
| `c_in` | the accumulator's read data into the adder: floats `txfma0_f0` opc (8 lanes); int8 `tenc_rd_data` (16 slots) | lanes x HD, once a k-pass (every micro-op of a k-pass reads an element at the same count) |
| `c_out` | int8 `tima_out` (TenC's write data) | 16 x HD a step |
| `wb_rz` | floats: the write-back data `f8_txfma_res` / `f8_wdata` / `vpu_rf` `wr_data[0]`, which is **zero between k-passes** and at the op's end: 17 returns an op | 8 x 2 x popcount(result), each return |
| `byp` | floats: `vpu_rf.v:33-46`, three bypass registers (one a read port) copy `wr_data` every cycle | 3 x `wb_rz` |
| `tenb_rz` | int8: `vpu_lane` `ex_bypass_tenb_rf_rdata`, the B word, **zero once a k-group** in every lane | 8 x 16 x 2 x popcount(B word) |
| `opnd`, `prod` | A and B at the units' inputs (held by enable flops, `tima_top.v` EN_FF on `f1_mul_en`; txfma's opa/opb do not move in the VCDs) and the products | 0 |
| `scp` | the scratchpad read data, 256-bit chunks alternating A and B: 62 switches an op (floats), 32 (int8), the order the bench's FSM issues | switches x 8 x HD(A word, B word): 0 when v = w |
| `launch` | once a launch: op 0's multiply overwriting the previous launch's end state, the launch-end stores, TensorLoad's bus | below 0.01 an op |

So in a one-number kind the flips are those of the accumulator's walk (HD) and of two return-to-zero buses, where a value's
**popcount** becomes flips: the floats' write-back bus (the accumulator's own popcount) and int8's TenB bypass (the B word's
popcount). That is why int8 `neg_ones` (B word `0xFFFFFFFF`, popcount 32) costs more than `ones` (popcount 4) although both
accumulators count by 4 (+7,168 flips an op), and why fp32 2.0 flips slightly more than 1.0 (its accumulator's exponent field
holds one more 1). The model reports each class at the bench's three launch points and as the launch mean (`forecast-e178.json`
`kinds.*.flips_i`), with the run mean (the calibration launch weighted in) beside it.

**(i) against (ii), and which one the registration scores.** Over each format's kinds, (i) at the bench's points tracks (ii)'s
nets: int8 Pearson 1.000, 1.003 nets per flip (the TenC path and TenB's bypass are what (ii) counts as `lane_operand_bus`, the
classifier's stage for `.lane_tima.`, so M10's int8 `bus` term carries the accumulator); fp32 0.992 (1.38 nets per flip); fp16
0.946 (1.76). They disagree in three places: (1) **units**: (ii) counts merged RTL nets, so it also sees the accumulator's value
carried through the FMA's pipeline (some 25 named stages in the VCDs), the adder's internal nets and the result flags; (i) counts
each architectural register and bus once. (2) **the kind whose accumulator never moves** (`fp32.e178_uf`): (i) 0 flips, (ii)
6,350 nets above zeros (live lanes' control, and the underflow and inexact flags that rise and fall with the write-back bus).
(3) **the accumulator's time average**: (ii) and M10 sample three launch points (and M10's `c_end` one op at 2^19), while (i)
takes the whole launch. M10's `c_end` reads 7,936 for 4s, 5s and pi alike, and also for `e178_uf`, because make_blind5's
software accumulator does not flush denormal products; (i), with the silicon's FTZ, gives 8,192 / 11,351 / 0. **The
registration scores P (M10 frozen on (ii)) as the primary forecast of every rule; F is scored beside it as a named arm where it
differs**, and (i) is the explanation of where the flips are.

## 6. The noise model and the power (`power_e178.py`, `power-e178.json`, `noise/`)

From committed reductions only (`noise/resolution_q146.py` over Q139(u)'s footing reductions E78-E142 and E153, E157, E163,
E164, E171, E172; written by scout-q146 at 19:47 PST, re-run here at 20:44:57 on the same files, output `noise/resolution-q146.json`,
differing from the scout's only in the last digit of three floats): **the per-pass sd of a same-session difference of two
one-number kinds is 0.125 W** (median of 20 named pairs, 0.044-0.240; every same-type pair 0.10 W over 10,290); **identical
bytes in one session read 0.066 W rms** against a median se of 0.052 W, so a session term of sqrt(0.066^2 - 0.052^2) = 0.041 W
per kind (0.02 W left for an adjacent ABBA couple). A core couple's per-pass value is the mean of three couples (0.125/sqrt 3); a
kind's single read 0.125/sqrt 2. Each rule: 20,000 draws from `random.Random(17800 + rule index)` at n = 4, 3 and 2 and sigma1
0.10, 0.125 and 0.20, with truth = P, = F and = null, and a kind-specific model error of 0 or 0.03 W for the ranking and slope.
`power-e178.txt` lists the FULL-plan figures quoted in section 5.

## 11. What the rules cannot say

The float pairs at a few hundredths of a watt are below this protocol's resolution (95% half-width about 0.10 W at four passes);
a null there is not evidence of equality at the 0.01 W level. The rules test M10's prices on one-number kinds, not its absolute
level: about 8.08 W of a float one-number kind's 8.23 W is M10's live-lane and clocked-register terms, common to every nonzero
one-number kind. (i) counts architectural state once; the energy of one flip of the silicon's full-custom register file is
unknown, and E178-W measures an energy per flip in int8 only. Glitches, wires, the clock tree and any data dependence of the
scratchpad's read energy are outside (i) and (ii). One card, one session: nothing here separates a card's level from a gain.
