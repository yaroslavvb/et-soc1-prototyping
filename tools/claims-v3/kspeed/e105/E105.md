# E105: does the gap come back when the tiles fit the L2? (Q132)

Sun 1 Nov 2026 (week 44). Registered before the claim and the first launch. Lane e101-real-matmul. Follows E101 (`docs/reports/data/2026-11-01-e101-real-matmul/`), whose verifier found that E101's `gemm1` is one port of the vendor's GEMM pattern whose packed tiles miss the L2 on most lines, and named this as the first open item.

## The question

E101's `gemm1` runs T = C/f + M with C about 344 cycles and M about 0.84 µs, so the clock gap between zeros and random (×1.06, ×1.27) reaches the rate as only ×1.03, ×1.09. A desk model of the shire's L2 (one shire, 4 ways, true LRU, address bits 16:6 picking one of 2,048 sets; `desk/l2sim.py`, `desk/l2sim2.py`, the verifier's) has 75.0% of that port's lines missing in steady state, against 41.9% with the vendor's strides and 0.0% with B packed by column. If that is why, then the same GEMM pattern with the tiles in the L2 should lose M and get its gap back. If M stays, the reading is wrong.

## What runs

aifoundry2 card 0, under its own governor: nothing is set on the card, every start is cool, there is no mm16 and no hot start. Binary: `tools/claims-v3/rgm105/`, a copy of E101's `rgm` with one new field, `SpArgs.layout`; `rgm` itself is unchanged. Same partition, same op order, same cooperative loads, same tile contents from the same seed (the host computes its reference on the packed pools and copies the bytes to each layout); only the addresses differ.

| arm | layout | desk miss share, steady / one pass cold |
|---|---|---|
| `gemm1` | E101's: A by row then k, B by k then column | 75.0% / 100% |
| `gemmv1` | the vendor's strides: A and C row-major at a pitch of 2,048 B, B at 2,112 B | 41.9% / 80.2% |
| `gemmc1` | `gemm1` with B packed by column, the one edit | 0.0% / 66.7% |
| `gemmh1` | by column with 16 k tiles instead of 32, a block with room | 0.0% / 33.3% |
| `gemmc16`, `gemmv16` | 16 blocks, each touched once per pass: 48 MB against a 32 MB L3, so one pass from cold, fed from DRAM | as cold |

Kinds: zeros, ones (the constant), sparse50, a new `sparse25` (as sparse50 with a quarter of the elements zero, pair 0 the randn tile), randn or int8's full, where the power rule admits them. Design: E80's, E101 session 2's parameters: a burst is one 6 s process from a die reading at or below 55 °C, sustained is nine 8 s processes (about 76 s) from at or below 57 °C with the last 40 s read; the trace ring and `ettelem config` after every run; 85 °C and 97 W on three samples still end the session.

The order is one list, most important first, and the cap (52 card-minutes) cuts the tail: `protocol.txt`. A: fp32 in the three layouts. B: int8. C: the cold variant. D: the half-K control, half the chip on by-column random, the constant's sustained runs, replicates. E: fp16.

## The power limit

Tiles that fit raise the rate, and at 800 MHz a cool start draws its power before the governor answers. E101's relation puts by-column random fp32 at about 100 to 106 W at 800 MHz, over the 97 W stop, so that cell cannot honestly be planned. So a rule decides, before each high-power run (`run_e105.py`, frozen here):

estimate = B_800 + s_800 × c_ref × rho, where c_ref is mm16's c of that kind as E101 measured it, and rho is the session's own measure on a lower-power probe of the same layout and format: (the probe's measured c) / (the probe's c_ref). The probe's c is the upper quartile of the board readings at or above 790 MHz between 0.3 s and 3.5 s after its first launch began, at least 3 samples, less B_800, over s_800. On E101's real bursts this reads within 0.5 W of the reducer's c on mm16's sparse50 cells, in all three formats.

A cell runs only if its estimate is at or below **88 W**, E101's rule. A probe with no measure refuses the cell. After any run whose board reading reached **93 W**, no cell with an estimate at or above that run's goes on. A refused cell is recorded in `skipped.jsonl`, and that is a result: by this rule, full-chip random fp32 by column does not start on this card.

Because the rule will probably refuse that cell, the primary kind is `sparse25`, run in all three layouts so the comparison is the same kind each time. Half the chip (`gemmc1-fp32-randn-half`, mask 0x0000ffff) puts by-column random on silicon at half the c, to read its cycle count and its measured c; its rate is not a full-chip gap, and is labelled so.

## Rules, which can fail

The rules' full text and bars are in `predictions-e105.json`; `selftest_e105.py` shows each failing on made-up rows.

- **R1**, by column removes M: fp32 `gemmc1` fit. PASS at M ≤ 0.15 µs and C in 470–590. FAIL at M ≥ 0.50 µs: the desk reading of the fixed term is wrong. Between, PARTIAL. No verdict under 60 MHz of span.
- **R2**, the strides: M(`gemmv1`) ÷ M(`gemm1`), this session, against the desk's 0.559. PASS in 0.35–0.78, else FAIL.
- **R3**, the tie: `gemm1` here against E101's fit: C within 30 cycles of 344.3, M within 0.08 µs of 0.838. A FAIL means the rebuilt arm is not the old one, and everything else is labelled.
- **R4**, the gap, fp32 by column: zeros ÷ sparse25, sustained rate. COMES BACK at ≥ 1.15 and within 0.03 of the clock ratio. STAYS GONE under 1.10. Else PARTIAL.
- **R5**, the gap, int8 by column: zeros ÷ full, sustained rate (sparse50 if the limit refused full, labelled). COMES BACK at ≥ 1.15, STAYS GONE under 1.05, else PARTIAL.
- **R6**, the strides sit between: `gemmv1`'s zeros ÷ sparse25 strictly between `gemm1`'s and `gemmc1`'s, by more than 0.01 each side.
- **R7**, T at 800 MHz, zeros, per layout, within 12% of frozen, and the order `gemmc1` < `gemmv1` < `gemm1`.
- **R8**, cold: `gemmc16` and `gemmv16` T at 800 MHz within 25% of frozen (1.701, 1.959 µs); zeros ÷ randn at or below ×1.06: in a cold block the clock does not set the pace.
- **R9**, half K: T at 800 MHz within 5% of 529/800. Read only if R1 or R7 miss on `gemmc1`: `gemmc1` fills all 4 ways of every set, so a stray line could matter, and `gemmh1` tells an exact fit from a wrong model.

The fit is over launches of at least 0.1 s between 590 and 810 MHz, T = wall ÷ ops per minion, f = cycles_max ÷ wall, the verifier's method.

## What this does not do

No vendor binary: `libautogen/matmul_f32_1024_512_512` lasts about 58 µs per launch against about 2 ms of launch overhead and reads no cycle counter, so timing it needs an instrumented build, which does not fit this time-box. It stays open. `gemmv1` is its strides in this kernel, not its code: no prefetch threads, a repeated block. One card, one session.
