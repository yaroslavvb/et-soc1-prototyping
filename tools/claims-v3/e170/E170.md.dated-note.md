## A1-1. The scorer of record is `score_e170_a1.py`

**The fault**, found by the kit's dry pass on the Intel ([`logs/drypass-intel.txt`](logs/drypass-intel.txt)) before this commit: `run_hpl.py` gives a
run the index it has in **its own protocol**. `score_e170.py`'s `main()` reads every session against the full registered order, so a session of the
registered subset (`protocol-subset.json`, passes 1, 2, 4 and 5) or of the dry twin (`protocol-dry.json`, passes 1 and 4) is refused at its first run of
a later pass ("is not the registered order's run at that idx"). The registered subset could not have been scored.

**The fix** is a new file, [`score_e170_a1.py`](score_e170_a1.py): it imports `score_e170.py` unchanged (its reduction, card term, rules and report)
and reads each session against the registered order filtered to the passes of the protocol that session ran (its `e170.json` names it) and re-indexed,
which is exactly how [`make_protocol_e170.py`](make_protocol_e170.py) lists them. A protocol other than the three is refused. The kit's self-test
([`logs/selftest-kit.txt`](logs/selftest-kit.txt) § 3) shows every BURST run of the three protocols at its filtered index, `score_e170.py`'s mapping
refusing the subset's first pass-4 run, and A1's mapping it to pass 4; the dry pass scores the dry `s1` through A1 (45 runs, passes 1 and 4: one pass
of half A, so every rule but C and S reads NOT SCORED, as registered). `collect_e170.sh` runs A1.
