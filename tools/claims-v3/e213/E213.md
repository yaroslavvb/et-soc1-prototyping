## 1. The question, and the answer frozen here

**Do MM1-v3's frozen forecasts of a matmul's clock cycles, seconds and joules hold on points that never ran on their card, and does MM1-v3 beat
MM1-v2 there?** "Hold" is registered as: per case (time) or per point (joules), the measured value within a band of twice MM1-v3's own held-out
error, at 90% of cases, per arm, scored twice for energy - **given the measured seconds** and **with the seconds forecast** - over idle and with
the card at rest (section 7). "Beat" is registered as: closer to the measurement than MM1-v2 by the exact sign test with the arm's ties, **per
arm (H1-H5) and pooled over both arms (HP1-HP5)**.

**The models.** **MM1-v3** (`tools/matmul/mm1/v3/`, landed `b1493082`) is MM1-v2 with four changes: the kernel's operations as an input (the
census's code path from the tensor wait's return to each operand's request, relative to rgm136's gemmL loop, at zero fitted constants), the int8
factor on the L3-served stream (d8 = +4.8%), the one-K-block share r2 = 0.64 of the shire cache's hits at KB <= 2 with MB x NB >= 2, the L3
retention with two edges (37.4 MB, then a plateau of half the stream to 72.3 MB), and energy by the switch route (above an ungated base, energy
follows the counted signal fin edges at one constant); held out against MM1-v2's form refitted on the same folds: wall cycles LOEO 4.53% (5.04%
without configuration twins) against 5.20%, LOCO 4.18% against 5.05%; W over idle given the seconds LOEO 4.85% (4.94% without twins) against
6.14%, LOCO 7.66% (with AMENDMENT-A2's pin) against 11.59% (its README, sections 0 and 3). **MM1-v2** (`tools/matmul/mm1/v2/`, landed
`3ca4b717`), as frozen: blind on E207 4.80% cycles and 4.82% J over idle given t, on E209 7.22% and 5.42%, on E211's rgm211 8.93% and 3.21%.
MM1-v2 has no term for a kernel's code path: it prices rgm211's points and rgm136's gemmLnc points as their rgm136 gemmL twin, literally.
His question - how well the timing simulation matches reality - is read beside every point (section 7, reading g).

**What each arm tests.** Arm A is MM1-v3's own blind day, its six families as frozen: I (int8 / fp32 pairs inside the L3 and at the knee: the
int8 L3 factor), R (the two-edge retention, 44-104 MB), C (one K block at large C, 12-336 MiB: r2 against the C store), M (fp32 zeros at 11
never-run shire masks with each shape's full chip: v3 has no layout model, so these test the gap), P (rgm136's gemmL, rgm211's gemmLn, rgm211's own
gemmL and rgm136's gemmLnc at four shapes, and gemmLnc at 1x1x1 int8: the census path term blind), E (deep K in zeros and random; the anchor at two
die points). Arm B tests MM1-v3's new terms on a second card: BI the int8 L3 factor (int8 / fp32 pairs at 14-48 MB; the KB = 2 pairs read r2
too), BR the two-edge retention past the L3 (44-104 MB), BK one and two K blocks with MB x NB >= 2 (r2 and the C store), BE deep K in zeros and
random and BS zeros, ones and sparse50 beside random at one shape (the switch route's energy), BP the census path (rgm136's gemmLnc beside each
one's gemmL twin; MM1-v2 prices each as its twin).
