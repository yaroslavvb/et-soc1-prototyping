## 2. The kinds, by a mechanical rule ([`make_e183_kinds.py`](make_e183_kinds.py), [`kinds-e183.json`](kinds-e183.json), [`images/`](images/))

Fixed before any forecast or measurement of these kinds; no model's error, measured or held out, enters.

- **The words:** word(s, e, j) = s << 31 | e << 23 | the top j of the 23 mantissa bits set. For such a v (e + 1 <= 0xFE), 2v = word(s,
  e + 1, j) exactly, so each source operand's popcount is pop_O = s + pop(e) + j (A's and B's alike) and the result's is pop_R = s +
  pop(e + 1) + j. **pop_R - pop_O = pop(e + 1) - pop(e)**: +1 at e = 0x80 (2.0's), -6 at 0x7F (1.0's), +1 at 0x7E (0.5's). Two exponents set
  the result-against-operand difference; the fill j sets the popcounts.
- **R(j), results differ, operands match:** first word(0, 0x80, j + 6), second word(0, 0x7F, j): dO = 0, dR = +7. **T1 = R(0)**, **T3 =
  R(16)**.
- **O(j), results match, operands differ:** first word(0, 0x7F, j + 1), second word(0, 0x80, j): dO = +7, dR = 0. **T2 = O(0)** (E180's
  1.5s against 2s), **T4 = O(16)**.
- **X, the exponent control:** 0.5 against 1.0 (E180's 0.5s and `const`): every bit that changes is in the exponent field, and the two
  differences have opposite signs, dR = +6, dO = -1. **T6.** It separates the mechanisms by sign (section 4).
- **H, E180's own pair again:** 5s against 4s, dR = dO = +1 (E180-H). **T5.** It repeats E180's own reading of the owner's pair.
- **j = 0 and 16:** j = 0 is each form's sparsest word (T1's second and T2's pair are E180's const, 1.5s and 2s, so T2 repeats an E180
  pair); j = 16 holds 16 more mantissa bits in all four words, a test that a bit's price does not depend on the bits already held.
- **Not taken:** a sign control. -v against v moves the result's and the operands' sign bit together (dO = dR = +1), as T5 moves one bit of
  each, so it separates no mechanism T5 does not; the result's sign bit returns to zero on the write-back with the others. Every other
  pair of this form is a sum of R, O and X.
- **Anchors** before and after every train: fadd.ps zeros and fadd.ps random, the catalogue's own fills under their own names (E180's).
- **Labels and bytes:** a word E180 ran keeps E180's label and committed image (f_0p5, f_1p5, f_2, f_4, f_5); 1.0 keeps the label `const`,

## 3. How a bit can cost, and what a train reads

In the open RTL a one-number fadd.ps kind switches nothing but the write-back's return to zero: at each cycle without a result the
write-back bus and its bypass copies fall to zero and rise again, so each 1-bit of the result costs a fall and a rise (E180.md section 3,
24.995 named flips a result bit). The register file's read ports hold their data between reads, so **the operands' 1-bits cost nothing
there**. The silicon's full-custom register file is not in the open RTL, and three costs it could carry are not in the bench:
- **result-side:** the write-back (the RTL's path); the result's bits written into f8..f15 every instruction; the result's bits stored
  there (a stored-value leakage);
- **operand-side:** a read port that discharges per 1 read, every read, of each source operand; the operands' bits stored in f0..f7;
- a cost common to every stored bit, which would show on both sides at one price (f0..f7 hold v, f8..f15 hold 2v).

E180's 21 kinds moved pop_R and pop_O together, so a result-side and an operand-side price fit them equally (section 4). E183's pairs
hold one side fixed and move the other by 7 bits (T1-T4), or move them apart in sign (T6). **Four outcomes, told apart by the design:**
**RESULT** (b > 0, c = 0: the write-back's or another result-side cost; E180's price is the result's), **OPERAND** (b = 0, c > 0: a read-port
or stored-operand cost the open RTL lacks), **BOTH** (both: with b about c, a cost per stored bit), **NEITHER** (both 0: E180's slope was
something else that tracked popcount in its ladder, or chance).

**The host-only change** (the kit's; nothing committed is edited): a copy of the catalogue's host (`workloads/enercat/host/main.cpp`, in the
build tree that E180's generator writes, `gen_acc_e180.py`, unchanged) gains `--operands-b file:<S's image>`, `--abba SFFS|FSSF` and
`--launches N`. Both images are copied to the card once (the host's own runtime copy, `dev.put`), and each launch's kernel argument
`sources` points at one of them: launch 0 (the settle) takes the orientation's first letter, launch n >= 1 takes letter (n - 1) mod 4; F is
the `--operands` kind, S the `--operands-b` kind. Each launch's ENERCAT record names its kind. The kernel, the mode, the launch, its timing
(`--window 240000000`, 0.4 s) and every device call are E180's; a train process runs 13 launches, about 5.2 s, under `timeout 10`. The
kit compiles the change at the desk against a mock runtime and on the box as its build step; **if that build fails after one re-run, E183
does not run that day** (section 7, the registered fallback).

### 5.3 Reported beside, never a verdict

- **Each train against every arm** (inside, above, below its interval), with each train's two halves, its orientations' means (the
  curvature), its carry-over reading (the mean of L3 - L2 in SFFS quads, where L2 follows an S launch and L3 an F launch, against the same
  in FSSF, over the train's estimate: a first-order carry-over c attenuates every contrast by 1 - c) and its process times.
- **The anchors:** fadd.ps random - fadd.ps zeros in each train block (E180's catalogue reduction of the anchor bursts, unchanged), against
  E180's RA5 board reading +8.4546 W [+8.3417, +8.5675]: the card's state, a repetition of E180's anchor.
- **Each launch's rate** (ops_per_s) by kind: the timing must not depend on the number; a rate that does is reported first.
- **The minion rail's level** over each train (its filter of about 1 s smears a 0.4 s alternation, so it reads no quad).
- **The joint fit:** weighted least squares of the six trains on (dR, dO), b and c with their covariance, beside the registered pairs.

### 5.4 What each outcome means

- **MECH RESULT with E183-B REPEAT AGREES:** the vector add's power follows the result's 1-bits at E180's price; the operands' held bits cost
  nothing the trains can see (c inside the +-0.004 W band, its own interval about +-0.001 W a bit at BASE). The write-back's return to zero, the RTL's own path, or another result-side cost, carries it. With E183-B's
  SIZE PASS against BLIND, E180's measured price is the add's per-bit price, and P is about 17 times too high (its SIZE FAIL HIGH).
- **MECH OPERAND:** the operands' held 1-bits carry E180's slope, not the result's: a read-port or stored-operand cost of the full-custom
  register file, which the open RTL cannot show; the next model of the add needs a per-held-operand-bit term and drops the write-back's.
- **MECH BOTH:** each side pays; with b and c alike, one price per stored 1-bit in the register file (f0..f7 and f8..f15) is the simplest
  reading; E180's slope was the two together.
- **MECH NEITHER:** neither side's bits cost to +-0.004 W a bit; E180-L's slope came from something else that tracked popcount in E180's
  ladder, or from chance.
- **E183-X INCONSISTENT:** the exponent field's bits do not cost as the mantissa's do (T6 moves only exponent bits); a bit's price depends
  on its field. **E183-J INCONSISTENT:** a bit's price depends on the bits already held.
- **E183-HT POSITIVE with SIZE PASS against BLIND:** a matrix of 5s costs more to add than a matrix of 4s, by about the one result bit's
  price: the owner's sentence, measured for the add, at about 0.01 W. NOT RESOLVED: his pair is below this train's resolution.
- **E183-TR-N AS FORECAST and TR-H AS PLANNED or BETTER:** the trains read the vector add to the registered precision; E180's burst form read
  his pair to +-0.077 W at six passes, the train to about +-0.005 W.

## 6. The noise model and the power ([`noise/noise_e183.py`](noise/noise_e183.py), [`power_e183.py`](power_e183.py))

- **The trains' noise, from E180's committed telemetry** (`noise/noise-e183.json`, post hoc, the registration's input): each of E180's 282
  bursts in its six passes (8 launches of 0.4004 s, launches 1 ms apart) read under section 5.1's rule, launches (1, 2, 3, 4) as a quad, all
  four of one kind, so the quad's mean is 0 and its scatter is the noise. **The 144 one-number fadd.ps bursts: quad sd sigma_q = 0.06249 W**
  (robust 0.0704; se of the sd 0.0037), mean +0.0141 W (a curvature: launches 2 and 3 above 1 and 4 in a burst that warms after its 4.5 s
  gap; the orientation pairs cancel it). Every launch's window held 2 samples (1,597 launches) or 3 (659), none fewer. The settle launch 0
  read 0.251 W under the later launches, 2.2% of the step from the gap before the burst: an upper bound on a launch's carry-over from the
  one before (it includes the burst's warming). Launch readings within a burst correlate +0.47 at lag one (the warming, which a quad's
  weights cancel to first order).
- **The sizing rule** ([`plan_e183.py`](plan_e183.py)): a train's quads n_q = ceil((1.96 sigma_q / h)^2), its processes the next even
  number at or above n_q / 3. **h = 0.010 W for T1-T4 and T6** (each moves 6 or 7 one-bits: 0.010 W is about 0.0014 W a bit, a seventh of the
  blind price, and b and c each pool two trains), giving 151 quads, **52 processes**, a forecast half-width of 0.0098 W (BASE) and 0.0196 W
  (DOUBLED); **h = 0.005 W for T5** (his pair, one bit of each: E182's target), 601 quads, **202 processes**, 0.0050 / 0.0100 W.
- **The simulation** (`power_e183.py`, seeds random.Random(18300 + 100 level + case), 4,000 sessions a case): every train of the FULL plan,
  each process three quads, each quad lost with chance 0.01; a quad reads N(x_t + o kappa, sigma^2), x_t = b dR_t + c dO_t (or the case's
  override), o = +1 in SFFS and -1 in FSSF, kappa = +0.0141 W, sigma = sigma_q (BASE) or twice it (DOUBLED, E182's allowance for
  switching transients); then section 5.1's statistics exactly. The truths: BLIND, E180-L's two 95% ends, OPER, BOTH, NONE, P, BLIND with c
  at the NULL band's edge (+0.004) and at half of it (+0.002), OFM (T5 at +0.0042 W), and BLIND with T6 or T3 0.020 W off the sum rule.

**The design's figures** (`power-e183.txt` has every case): b's and c's median 95% half-widths are 0.0010 W a bit at BASE and 0.0020 W at DOUBLED, about a tenth and a fifth of the blind price, and the NULL band (+-0.004 W a bit) is twice the DOUBLED half-width, as E182's band was twice its own. Each 52-process train reads its pair to 0.0098 W (BASE) and 0.0196 W (DOUBLED); T5 to 0.0051 W and 0.0101 W. The mechanism word comes out right in 0.86-1.00 of the sessions for every arm at either noise, but for E180-L's lower end under DOUBLED noise (RESULT 0.69, NEITHER 0.17); a wrong mechanism word comes at most 0.03 of the time elsewhere. His pair reads POSITIVE 0.96 at BASE if BLIND is true, and 0.48 at DOUBLED: T5 holds four times a price train's processes for that reason. Every SIZE word against the true line reads PASS 0.95 (its 95% interval), and P reads FAIL HIGH in every rule under every other truth.

## 11. What the rules cannot say

- A train reads a difference, never a level. The kinds' levels above zeros are tabled, not read; BOTH's intercept is not tested.
- b joins every result-side cost (the write-back's return to zero, the result's written and stored bits), and c every operand-side cost:
  no one-number design splits a side's paths, since each is linear in that side's popcount. A and B carry one word, so their split is not
  asked (E182 asked it for the TensorFMA).
- The separation takes a bit's price to be the same in every field: R and O move bits between the exponent and the mantissa. E183-X and
  E183-J test that at one pair each, at the trains' resolution.
- A first-order carry-over from one launch to the next attenuates every contrast by (1 - c); the bound from E180's settle launch is about
  2%, and the trains report their own, but no rule corrects for it.
- One card, one session; card 1 is not run.
