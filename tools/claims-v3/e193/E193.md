## 1. The question (quoted from the proposal, section 1)

**What does an awake core cost per core-cycle, by the part of its clock a user program can switch on?** In five parts:

1. **A waiting hart against the firmware's idle park.** A user-mode hart cannot run `wfi`. Does a hart blocked on a fast credit
   (`csrw fcc`; the firmware's own post-launch wait) cost what the idle park costs, or what a stalled, awake core costs (E23: 1,024
   minions stalled on a contended atomic, 1.19 W over idle)? Read as the slope of `tk` over launched shires, by the duty ladder's
   intercept, and by the train `tk | spin1`.
2. **The awake core per cycle, independent of duty:** a duty ladder of no-op spin against credit wait (awake fraction about 0,
   1/3, 2/3, 1, measured in cycles); slope = the awake price per core-cycle, intercept = the waiting price; the same duty at 16 and
   64 times shorter periods prices or bounds a wake.
3. **Per core or per neighbourhood:** the worker ladder's slope and its last step (the one that wakes no further neighbourhood),
   and 8 workers packed against spread.
4. **Each instruction class per cycle, at equal issue:** one instruction of a class in every P issue slots of a no-op stream
   (P = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16), on zeros: `fsgnj.s`, `fadd.ps`, and `fadd.s` at P = 16; `fadd.ps` with `m0` = 1, 2, 4 lanes; `fadd.ps` at
   1/8 density spread against in a row.
5. **One hart against two**, and a dependent divide chain.

What is new (the proposal's words, shortened): the awake core (spin, one and two harts), the no-issue floor (E109, E129, E148,
E155) and the full-rate catalogue are priced; a launched-shire ladder of the firmware's wait, a wait-against-spin duty ladder, a
worker ladder by neighbourhood and an instruction-class density ladder are not. `spin2` repeats the energy catalogue's two-hart
**no-op** row on this card (`nop`, 2.496 W over idle) as the session's anchor; `faddps_p1` and `fsgnjs_p1` repeat two catalogue
rows (10.212 and 6.122 W) on 992 worker minions: a check of the meter's scale. *Desk check:* those three values are the energy
catalogue's aifoundry3 rows on zeros with two harts (`docs/reports/data/2026-09-23-energy-manual/catalogue.json`: 2.496014,
10.21188 and 6.122364 W over idle).

**34 arms plus the anchor; six of them trains** (A B B A: segments 0 and 3 are A, 1 and 2 are B; three 0.4 s launches a segment;
12 launches in one device process). **The ladders** (quoted): `tk` over shires (4 points), `tk` over launch length (3: a bound),
duty (`wait2`, `duty33`, `duty67`, `duty100`), workers (`w7`, `w15`, `w23`, `spin2`), `fadd.ps` density (`spin2` and P = 16 .. 1),
`fsgnj.s` density (`spin2` and P = 8 .. 1), mask lanes (1, 2, 4, 8). Class instructions read `f0..f7` (zeros, loaded once from a host
buffer) and write `f8..f15`, which nothing reads; every timed body is built with compressed instructions off, so every slot is a
four-byte instruction (quoted; *desk check:* section 16, item 4).

**No hart can stay blocked** (quoted, request.md section 9; *desk check of code:* `kernel/x04clock.c` does the kernel's part):
every credit wait has its partner in its own shire; each waiting thread empties its counter and reports ready (a local atomic in a
host buffer, tagged with the launch's epoch); the timekeeper starts its periods only when every waiting thread of its shire has
reported (or after 6,000,000 cycles), then writes the go word; a thread that finds the go word already written skips its waits and
flags itself (the host then exits 3: nothing blocked, the launch not counted); the timekeeper sends three spare credits at the end
and waits 4,096 cycles; the firmware empties the counters after the kernel, and its barrier tolerates a stray credit.

## 7. The reduction (registered: [`proposal/src/reduce_x04.py`](proposal/src/reduce_x04.py), sha256 `98bbc174db714667748c8d27436c7205e7d52be9d83f2614b4346aafc9630511`, run unchanged)

Per session, after the data commit: `python3 reduce_x04.py OUT [--telemetry FILE] --out x04.json` (no `--pvt`, section 4; no
`--keep-warmup`). Its rules, fixed before any card data (quoted from its own head, checked against its code in section 16):

- **Board:** a burst's value is the mean of the 10 Hz `board_w` samples from 0.5 s after its first launch to its last launch's end;
  idle is the mean of the two bracketing gaps (from 2.5 s after the previous burst, or 3.5 s before this one, to 0.3 s before it;
  from 2.5 s after this burst to 0.3 s before the next or 6 s after it). **Dip rule:** a board sample more than 4 W below its
  window's median (busy or idle) is dropped. A run whose busy window or idle-before window holds fewer than 5 samples is left out.
- **Leakage:** on the board, the lab's idle law, 23.257/36 x exp((T - 80)/36) W per C (E17), times the busy-minus-idle die
  difference; each rail gets a slope fitted to the session's own idle windows (rail idle W against die temperature, one line per rail
  over the session, used when more than 5 points span a range).
- **Rails** (`sp.minion_w`, `sram_w`, `noc_w`): each run's rail is fitted, not windowed: the input is taken as constant on each
  interval (the idle gap before, the burst or each train segment, the idle gap after), passed through the meter's first-order filter
  (tau 1.05 s) read 1.5 meter passes late (0.395 s), and the levels and the filter's starting value fitted by least squares; a burst's
  rail value is its level minus the mean of the two idle levels; rail current = rail W / rail V.
- **Clock:** a burst whose busy samples lack `mhz.minion`, or show anything but 600, is dropped. A run with a launch not `ok` is
  dropped.
- **Trains:** segments 0 and 3 are A, 1 and 2 are B; each segment's value is its mean from 0.5 s after its first launch to its last
  launch's end; the contrast is mean(B) - mean(A), leakage-corrected per segment; no idle subtraction; a train is kept only if all
  four segments are present, each has at least 2 board samples at 600 MHz, and every launch is `ok`.
- **Per arm, across the measured passes:** board W over idle (and its SE), the anchor-detrended value beside it, the minion, SRAM and
  NoC rails' W over idle, worker minion-cycles a second, pJ and electrons per worker minion-cycle (board and minion rail), slots a
  hart-cycle, the awake fraction and wakes a second (duty arms).
- **Fits:** `tk` over launched shires; `tk` over launches a second; the duty line through `wait2`, `duty33`, `duty67`, `duty100`
  against the measured awake fraction, with `duty33f` and `duty33k` as residuals off it and their pJ a wake; the worker line (W over
  idle against workers a shire); the `fadd.ps` and `fsgnj.s` density lines (W against class instructions a second, with the per-op
  pJ over `spin2` on the board and the minion rail); the mask-lane line.
- **Interpreter:** desk-checked under `/usr/bin/python3` 3.12.3 with numpy 1.26.4 on aifoundry2; the kit names the interpreter it
  runs on, and the data README records it.
- **What E193 publishes:** these reduced prices, per arm, per fit and per train, with their SEs and the checks of section 8: the

## 9. Stops, commands and what must not be done (registered; request.md section 9 and `run_x04.py`)

- **Stops, in the runner** (each touches `OUT/STOPPED` and the stop file with the reason and ends the session): a device process that

  nothing blocked); on the telemetry since the last check: **board above 65 W on three samples in a row, a die reading at or above
  70 C, a minion clock other than 600 MHz (or missing) on three samples in a row**; the telemetry file not growing for 5 s (a stalled
  sampler); the card not free for 10 minutes before a block; the stop file (`OUT/STOP`) or the deadline, checked before every run. A
  train whose segments did not all get their three launches is kept with `"ok": false` and dropped by the reduction.
- **A launch that did not finish** (exit 1 with "kernel did not finish", or a `timeout` kill) also means a hart may be hung, which
  needs a card reset (quoted). **A card reset is never this run's to make:** the session stops, keeps its records, and the lane
  reports to the owner and the lab admin.
- **Stopped by a signal**, the runner ignores further signals, ends a running device process (SIGTERM to its `timeout`, which kills it
  1 s later), waits for it before releasing the lock, then stops its own sampler with SIGTERM (quoted; *desk check of code*: the code
  does this). Nothing else ever kills a device process.
- **The arms sit far below the stops:** the `fadd.ps` P = 1 arms put the board about 10 W over idle for 4 s; there is no heater
  (quoted).
- **Commands: reads only.** The host opens the ops node only (the device layer created for the ops node; the runtime's launches,
  copies and allocations); the sampler without `--reset-ms`; the holder check `et-who --check`. **No statistics reset, no set, no
  reset, no power cycle, no firmware-revisions query, no PMIC command.** The kit runs the lab's device-command audit
  (`dmcmd_audit`, GO) on its file set.
- **What must not be done** (quoted, with the lab's rules): no voltage, frequency, firmware, governor, TDP, threshold or fan change;
  never on two cards of one host at once (the runner and the host refuse a host with more than one card node); no host compute
  beside the run; the build in a folder of ours on the box, never in another session's trees; never two sessions of ours on aifoundry3
  at once; no change to an arm, a value, the order, a window, a threshold or the reduction except by a dated amendment before the data.
