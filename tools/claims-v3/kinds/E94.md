# E94: tile-to-tile spread — what a kind of number costs (Q132, aifoundry1 card 1, 600 MHz)

*Pre-registered Sun 1 Nov 2026 (week 44), on the Intel, before the Discord claim of card 1 and the first launch, in Q132
(lane `a1c1-e94-spread`). It closes item 11 of the kinds page's section 11 ("Tile-to-tile spread. Fresh draws of two sparse
kinds moved by 0.3–1.2 W; whether E78's tiles sit off their kinds' means needs more draws per kind"). The frozen numbers are
in [`predictions-e94.json`](predictions-e94.json), written by [`make_predictions_e94.py`](make_predictions_e94.py) from the
committed sources it names (every number read or computed from its file, never retyped; each source recorded with its
sha256) and committed with this file. Any change after this commit goes in the Amendments section at the end, dated, before
the data it touches; no band moves. Card: **aifoundry1 card 1 only** (`V3_DEVICE=1` → `ET_DEVICES=1`, `aifoundry1-c1`,
`/run/lock/etsoc-shire1.lock`), 600 MHz in every sample since 25 Sep, ~499 mV, TDP 65 W and threshold 65 °C as read (never
set), firmware release 1.4.0 (E82 A1). E94 writes no knob: no governor move, no TDP, threshold or clock set, no reset, no
PCIe, driver, firmware or PMIC work, no `--reset-ms`.*

**The question.** Every interval on the kinds page is one fixed tile's repeatability between passes. A reader wants to know
what a *kind* costs. E90 re-drew three rules on aifoundry3 and moved the cost by up to 1.2 W, but three draws gave its rule
means ±0.74–0.81 W with the draw as the unit, so its R4 (over passes, for three fixed draws) could not say whether E78's
tiles sit off their kinds' means (E90 C2). kinds.json's 400 re-draws say E78's `fp16.nn_like` and `fp16.uniform01` tiles lie
outside their rule's 2.5–97.5% band (E78 C5), by gate share and by bus bits, but those are statistics of the tile, not watts.
E94 measures: for each of eight kinds, **the kind's mean and its between-draw SD, as numbers the page can print**; **whether
E78's committed tile lies inside the draws' spread**; **whether its row needs a correction**; and **whether a number computed
from the tile alone says where a draw lands**.

**What was dropped to fit about 40 card-minutes, decided before any data:**

- **Four draws, not six, on the three random rules.** Their committed tile is an anchor, read three times a pass, and their
  claim is a pooled one (R4a has 14 degrees of freedom with `fp16.uniform01`, R4b 8 with `int8.sparse50`). This is the one place
  the brief's "about 6" is cut.
- **`int8.gauss_q` and a truncated fp32 kind.** Of the brief's either-ors: `int8.sparse50` over `gauss_q`, because its gate moves
  with the draw and Q130(n) expects 0.04 W of spread from `gauss_q`; `fp32.nn_like` over `bf16trunc`, because it has the largest
  expected spread of any rule on the page and `bf16trunc` about 0.07 W. **`fp16.sparse50` is not run** though both laws expect
  0.6 W of spread and a +0.5 W offset: the desk table below freezes its numbers, and R6 and R9 decide if they may be quoted.
- **A fourth pass.** Each pass is about 700 s, so four would take 47 minutes; the draws, not the passes, carry the spread
  (below). Three passes at 56 items fit; four passes would fit only 36 items, leaving under four draws a rule.

**Why three passes, and why two still score.** E91 and E88 lost passes to registered gates on this card, so plan on losing one.
But here the unit of replication is the draw. A tile's mean carries σ²/n of pass noise against the tile component τ²: with
τ = 0.5 W and σ = 0.15 W the SD of a draw's mean is 0.507 W at n = 3 and 0.511 W at n = 2. Pass noise is estimated from the
residual of a tile × pass fit over all of a rule's tiles (≥ 8 degrees of freedom at n = 3, ≥ 4 at n = 2).

## What each law expects, frozen

Three numbers computed from the tile alone, each with a frozen delta for every draw (`predictions-e94.json` › `tiles`):

   proportional to the live share of lane-cycles. It has no fitted number. *It was noticed on E90's published draws at this
   desk, so E90 cannot test it*: on E90's six gated draws its rms against the measured offsets is **0.108 W**, against Q130(n)'s
   0.289 W and 0.831 W for predicting no move (`retrospective_e90`: offset (gate law, Q130(n)): `fp32.sparse50 d1` +0.90 (+0.95, +0.78); `fp32.sparse50 d2` +0.88 (+1.08, +0.88); `fp32.sparse50 d3` +0.33 (+0.35, +0.19); `fp16.nn_like d1` -0.60 (-0.77, -1.07); `fp16.nn_like d2` -0.82 (-0.85, -1.19); `fp16.nn_like d3` -1.19 (-1.19, -1.52)). The fresh draws are its first test.
   The committed D it scales is E82's dips-removed value on this card.
2. **Q130(n)'s predictor** (`real/make_real.py`, fitted on E78's 51 kinds, unchanged; per-type models u,N_sig,A,B,Vs_sig and u,N_pop,A,B,Vp_pop), its delta against the committed tile,
   divided by E82's per-type card scale (1.0300 / 1.0332 / 1.0271). E90 C1 stands: it is a guide to a re-draw's offset from a measured tile, not a price.

## The statistics: the draw is the unit

Per rule, every tile's per-pass D goes into an additive fit `y(tile, pass) = tile + pass + e`, which keeps each tile on the D scale and takes the shared pass shift out of the comparison. With k draws in n kept passes:

| | |
|---|---|
| s | the SD of the k draws' means |
| MS_res, N² | the fit's residual mean square (one tile's between-pass variance with the shared shift removed), and N² = MS_res / n, the noise of one tile's mean |
| **τ** | √max(0, s² − N²), **the tile variance component**: the kind's between-draw SD. Its 95% interval: χ²(k−1) on s², each end less N², floored at 0 |
| **kind's mean** | the draws' mean ± t(0.975, k−1) × s / √k |
| **offset** | D(committed) − the draws' mean; ± hw = t(0.975, Satterthwaite df) × √(s²/k + N²) |
| PI | t(0.975, k−1) × s × √(1 + 1/k): where one more draw of the rule would fall, by 95% |
| m | t(0.975, df_res) × √N², the committed tile's own repeatability |

E90's form of "one tile's between-pass SD" (the mean over tiles of each tile's SD over passes, the shared shift included) is reported beside.

## Decision rules

All on the primary reduction (dips removed, 0.2414 W/°C). Every verdict is also computed at the arms (dips kept; E78's pass gates opened; `auto`, 0.1994, 0.3803, 0.545, 0), and a verdict that moves is marked arm-sensitive. **Fewer than 2 kept passes: every rule NOT SCORED.** A tile with fewer than 2 passes leaves its rule and is named; a rule with fewer than 3 draws left is UNRESOLVED. No pass is added after a reduction is read, and none is chosen after the fact. Failures are reported as failures.

The tile component is in s, not pass noise for fixed draws: this is E90 R4's flaw, corrected (E90 C2). **Reported, never a verdict:** per rule, the same statistics on the fresh draws alone, S (over idle) and pJ per op for the kind beside the committed tile's, the kind's share of random data; per draw, its offset, gate share, bus bits and the three frozen deltas; per rule, Pearson r and slope of the offset on gate share, on bus bits and on each law's delta; each law's rms on the gated and on the dense draws, plain and centred; R1's p values; E90's own draws on aifoundry3 beside each translated row; E78-R5's checks (idle consistency, rail lag, duty, carry-over); every gate failure; each block's two `ettelem config` reads.

**What the rules cannot say.** One card, one clock, one session. Six to nine draws know an SD to a factor of about 0.6–2.0 (four draws: 0.57–3.7), which is why the dense claim is pooled. A kind's mean here is on card 1; the page's table is aifoundry3's, and the card scale (E82: 1.031, sd 1.2%) holds for kinds, not necessarily for offsets, which R8 tests on three rules only. E78's rule is a synthetic rule, so nothing here says what a real tensor's "kind" is. The gate law was noticed on E90's data, so R6 and R7 are its first test; it is a law of offsets from a measured tile, not of absolute prices. int8's bus counts are provisional (kinds README). The intervals cover draws and passes, not the leakage arms, which move D on the high-power kinds by about ±1 W on this card (E82 C6) but move a difference between tiles of one kind far less: the arms report it.

## Card budget

**About 40 card-minutes, from the claim post to its release edit**, including the smoke and every heating gate. `queue_e94.sh` runs with `E94_BUDGET_S=2400`, `E94_FIRST_S=730`, its budget in seconds: a pass starts only if its projected end (the longest measured pass; 730 s before the first ends) is inside 2,400 s of the queue's start, and before 07:00 PST; a pass in flight is never cut. E93 on this card ran 42 launches in 361–386 s, so a 78-launch pass takes about 680–720 s and three take 34–36 minutes; with the smoke and the release that is about 39. A pass over about 790 s (E91's 10.2 s a run would be 796 s) would refuse the third, leaving two passes, which still score. The config reads are the blocks' own, under the lock. The claim is posted for 1 h (40 min plus a margin, rounded up to the half hour). Every run ends by 07:15 PST at the latest.
