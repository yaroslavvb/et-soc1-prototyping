## The rule on trial

**One 32-bit raw-word compare with zero, taken when the operand register is written; a lane gates by
`A_zero | (tenB_zero[raddr] & tenB_zero[raddr|1])`.** Sources, all public:
`external/core-et/rtl/shire/minion/vpu/vpu_ctrl.v:1229-1234` (the compare), `:737-755` (one flag a lane latched per entry),
`:802-819` (the mask), `:827-839` (it clears `ex_tima_valid` and `ex_txfma_valid`). It was read off the RTL
(`docs/reports/data/2026-10-31-q130-zero-gate-correction/README.md`, `kinds/README.md` §1.3) and matched E78's sparse tiles,
but **these five predictions have never been tested on silicon**:

1. **A raw-word compare means negative zero does not gate.** fp32 `0x80000000` and fp16 `0x80008000` are value-zero and
   raw-nonzero: the lane stays on, where all-zero words read about 0 W.
2. **Denormal words do not gate either, though the float unit treats them as zero.** *The prediction, before data:* the
   multiplier never sees a denormal's fraction. PRM §3.4: "Input denormals are converted to zero with the sign of the
   original operand", setting the sticky InputDenorm flag, no trap. *Minion VPU Specification* §2.2.3.9: "treats all
   denormalized inputs as zeros". RTL: `txfma_7s/txfma_f0.v:312-318` (`opa_ps_dnclr_f0a_l = |opa[30:23]`, with no mode bit)
   and `:375-377`, `:392`, `:457-459`, `:475` clear the fraction and the hidden bit before the Booth generator, per half in
   fp16. No CSR switches this and the kernel (`workloads/sparsity/kernel/sparsity.c`) writes neither `fcsr` nor `frm`; the
   stream is `FMUL/FMADD.PS` (fp32) and `TMUL/TFMA.PH.PS` (fp16). So a denormal is **a zero the gate cannot see**.
3. **int8's eight-byte B rule.** A word of zero bytes alone does not gate; the pair must be zero.
4. **int8's column pairing.** The RTL says column `l` pairs with `l+8`: unit `(l, t)` reads TenB row `raddr + t`, word `l`
   (`vpu_lane.v:404-406`, `:438`; `vpu_top.v:348`), and the mask ANDs bit `l` of the same two rows (`vpu_ctrl.v:808-818`). E79's
   amendment A4, inferred from power alone, states `(2l, 2l+1)`; `kinds.json` carries `stats.gated_lane_frac_alt_grouping` for
   it and `kinds/README.md` §1.3 left the question open, because no tile E78 ran could tell them apart (largest
   disagreement 0.007). **If the card sides with the alternative, every int8 gate figure on the page changes.**
5. **int8's A rule.** All four bytes of an A word zero gate the whole micro-op; three of four do not. (An int8 A word
   sometimes reaches the lane through the temporal register, `vpu_ctrl.v:804`, `:1222-1231`: the same 32-bit compare on the
   same word, so the rule is the same.)

Beyond the brief's list, one thing the same reading exposes cheaply: **a recoder on one side.** `txfma_f1.v:113-114` makes
`fa` (A) the Booth multiplier and `fb` (B) the multiplicand. With `fa = 0` every digit is zero and no partial product moves,
whatever B does. With `fb = 0` a negative digit still gives `~0`, all ones (`txfma_booth_ppg_32r4_norm.v`), so the partial
products swing between all-0 and all-1 on a product that is exactly zero. So a live zero should cost more on B than on A.
And one tile a reader will recognise: `x * mask` leaves `-0.0` wherever `x` was negative, so a pruned matrix keeps only half
its zeros visible to the gate.

## The tiles

**Seventeen new tiles** (`e93/tiles/`, `e93/e93.json`), built by [`make_e93.py`](make_e93.py) with `make_kinds.py`'s own
`encode`, `from_words`, `issue_streams`, `tile_stats`; `make_e93.py --check` passes. **Three E78 kinds run beside them**:
`fp32.a_zeros_b_randn` (the +0 mirror whose B the −0 and denormal A-side tiles reuse byte for byte), `int8.a_zeros_b_full` (the
four-of-four tile whose B `int8.a_3of4_b_full` reuses byte for byte) and `fp32.sparse50` (the clean tile of `sparse50_mask`).
The tie `anchor_pi`, a constant, runs twice a pass, so fp32's "constant" is in every pass. The other +0 mirrors are E82's fixed
numbers on this card: `fp32.a_randn_b_zeros` 0.6306, `fp16.a_zeros_b_randn` 0.7611, `fp16.a_randn_b_zeros` 0.6244 W.

**int8's three B tiles are the same numbers in different columns.** One A tile (no zero byte) and, line by line, the same
eight nonzero B words (no zero byte) in ascending column order; only which eight columns hold zero differs, and odd lines take
the complement of even lines' set, so every column slot is live half the time in all three and no physical lane or unit is
favoured:

| tile | zero columns (even lines; odd lines the complement) | gated, RTL's `(l, l+8)` | gated, A4's `(2l, 2l+1)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `int8.b_pairs_rtl` | 0 2 4 6 8 10 12 14 | **0.500** | 0 |
| `int8.b_pairs_alt` | 0 1 4 5 10 11 14 15 | 0 | **0.500** |
| `int8.b_unpaired` | 0 2 4 6 9 11 13 15 | 0 | 0 |

Their operand bus bits are identical (69,980 an op, asserted), as is each line's multiset of words (asserted). So re-drawing
cannot move one against another (the item-11 spread does not apply), and a difference between them is the pairing. Under the
RTL's reading `b_pairs_alt` reads like the control and `b_pairs_rtl` sits below; under A4's, `b_pairs_rtl` reads like the control
and `b_pairs_alt` sits below; if the arrangement does not matter, all three read alike. `b_unpaired` against `b_pairs_rtl` is item
3 of the brief: the same zero bytes, one word of each pair against whole pairs.

**Departures from the brief's list, decided before any data.** (i) The B-side denormal runs in fp32 only (`a_randn_b_dnr`), and
fp16's constant-denormal twin `0x00010001` is left out: on the B side the flush moves the multiplicand either way and both readings
leave the partial products swinging, so it cannot tell a flush from no flush, and `dnr_both` already carries fp16's raw-compare test.
(ii) Only the fp32 A-side +0 mirror runs in the pass; the others are E82's numbers, because they sit 7–10 W below the live prediction, and
each run costs 10 s. (iii) Together these keep twenty items a pass, so that five passes fit the budget rather than four.

## Decision rules

All on D at the primary reduction (dips removed, 0.2414 W/°C), per pass, in **pass units**: a contrast is computed inside each pass (or
against a fixed E82 number), and its 95% interval is t(0.975, n−1) × SE over kept passes. **Three-way:** *above*: PASS if the interval's low end
is above the pass line, FAIL if its high end is below the fail line, else INCONCLUSIVE. *below*: PASS if the high end is below the line, FAIL if the low
end is above it. *within*: PASS if the interval lies inside ±w, FAIL if wholly outside. Every verdict is also computed at the arms (dips kept; `auto`,
0.1994, 0.3803, 0.545, 0), and a verdict that moves is marked arm-sensitive. Failures are reported as failures, with the value beside the line.

**If passes fall out.** E91 lost a pass to a registered gate and, needing four, scored nothing. Here: **at least 3 kept passes score; a cell with fewer
than 3 is UNRESOLVED and never counts as PASS; with 2 or fewer every rule is NOT SCORED and the session is reported as exploratory.** No pass is added
after any reduction is read; no pass is chosen after the fact; the arms are not a fallback. At 3 passes the intervals widen by ×1.56 over 4 and INCONCLUSIVE
gets likelier, which the power table states.

`score_e93.py` also names the int8 reading from R6's three contrasts: `rtl`, `alt`, `neither` (the three alike within the margins, so power cannot pick a

**Each tile's prediction, three-way:** PASS if D's 95% interval lies inside the tile's band above, FAIL if wholly outside, else INCONCLUSIVE. **The bands are wide on purpose**: they
test the gate law and E88's prices on a new corner, and a miss says the prices do not carry there (a live lane on a zero operand may not be priced by these terms), not that the gate
rule is wrong. The gate rule is decided by R1–R7's contrasts.

**The session tie (T), a separate verdict.** Each cell's mean D within its band of E82's on this card, with E91's bands: `fp32.randn`, `fp16.randn`, `anchor_pi` ±0.50 W; `int8.full` ±0.25 W; and for E93's
references, `fp32.a_zeros_b_randn`, `int8.a_zeros_b_full` ±0.25 W, `fp32.sparse50` ±0.75 W (its E82 interval is ±0.46 W and the dips move it 0.27 W). Beside it, the four anchors against E91's registered 0.1994 W/°C arm, the arm that kept
its four passes (E91's own primary slope, 0.42 W/°C, is not comparable). **A failure means the session moved, not that the gate rule did**: the same-pass contrasts stand, and any rule against a fixed E82 number is flagged.

**Reported, never a verdict:** every tile's S, pJ per op, MAC and flop (÷ 4,096 / 8,192 / 16,384, as E78), ΔT, D32 and the rail; the gate law's residual per tile; −0's premium over +0; denormal against −0 on each side; the sign's cost read off `a_nz_b_randn` against the bus-only arm
(this touches item 2 of the page: a product whose magnitude is exactly zero and whose sign flips; it is an open question whether that costs anything); fp16's recoder asymmetry; int8's cost per unit, gated and live-on-a-zero-word; cycles per op per tile (a stall on denormals would show here and in E78's cycle gate);
E78-R5's checks (idle consistency, rail lag, duty, carry-over); every gate failure; each block's two `ettelem config` reads.

**What the rules cannot say.** Power is the only reading: E93 does not read the micro-op counters and so does not count gated lanes directly. Int8's multiplier is a synthesized `$signed * $signed` (`tima_top.v`), so what a live unit costs on a zero B word is not predictable
from the RTL, and R6's power test needs it to differ from a gated unit's. If the two cost alike, the reading is `neither`, and the pairing stays open: power could not pick it, which is not evidence for either. One card, one A tile, one fixed set of B words.
`sparse50_mask` is synthetic: half of E78's zeros, with a chosen sign, is what `x * mask` leaves if `x` is symmetric. It is not a measured pruned network.

## Card budget

**At most about 40 card-minutes, from the claim post to its release edit**, including the smoke and every heating gate. `queue_e93.sh` runs with `E93_BUDGET_S=2280`, `E93_FIRST_S=450`, its budget in **seconds** (E91's spare pass was refused by 1 s because its budget had been floored to whole minutes): a pass starts only if its projected end (the longest measured pass; 450 s before the first ends) is inside 2,280 s of the queue's start, and before 07:00 PST; a pass in flight is never cut. At E88's and E91's 10.2 s a run, a 42-run pass takes about 430 s, so five passes fit, four if a run takes more than about 10.8 s. The config reads are the blocks' own, under the lock. The claim is posted for 1 h (40 min plus a margin, rounded up to the half hour). This session starts well before the 02:00 PDT clock change and ends before it.
