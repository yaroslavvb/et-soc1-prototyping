sample since 25 Sep, ~499 mV, TDP 65 W and threshold 65 °C as read (never set), firmware release 1.4.0 (E82 A1). E102 writes no knob: no governor move, no TDP, threshold or clock set, no reset, no PCIe, driver, firmware or PMIC work, no `--reset-ms`.*

**The question.** E79 ran one flip two ways. In E65's construction (`jg`) fp32 B bit 22 costs 1,052 fJ per flipped bit; in a layout that keeps a lane's two accumulators together (`jgp`) it costs 150 fJ, 7.0×; fp16 bit 9, 5.6×. It left open whether
"the other 30 positions" are the same composite, ran no balanced layout on A at all, and left int8's B level unestablished, because its list moved with the leakage slope. So: **does the ratio all-in / balanced stay near 5–7× across the mantissa, as the page
implies, or does it depend on position?** What are the exponent's and the sign's prices in a balanced layout? What is "balanced" on A? What does int8's B side cost, to what resolution, and is E79's odd/even shape real?

## What "balanced" means, and where it stops being clean

**On B** (E79's `jgp`): the two words one bit apart go to every column equally. `flip = hi(j) XOR (g & 1)`, where g is the line group. A lane still alternates at nearly full rate, but every `C[i][j]` accumulates the same mix, so the two accumulators a lane alternates between stay within one product of each other. 3,968 flips an op (fp32).

**On A** (new; the brief asks for it stated): the A word is broadcast to all eight lanes and held across both halves of a row, so it can change only every second micro-op. E67's layout alternates it by row (`flip = i & 1`), so even and odd rows' accumulators diverge, and a lane reads `C[i][l]`, `C[i][8+l]`, `C[i+1][l]`, … swinging between them every two micro-ops. **Balanced: `flip = (i XOR g) & 1`.** The word still alternates at the only rate it can inside a line group, and each row gets each word in half the line groups, so every `C[i][j]` accumulates the same mix (checked by `make_e102.py`: one term sum over all rows). The word does not change across a group boundary: 1,920 flips an op against 2,048, each item divided by its own count. (A layout on the group parity alone, E79's `kg`, flips only 128 an op, about 0.02 W at 150 fJ. Not run.)

**Balance removes the accumulators' swing, not every other effect, and the frozen counts say it inverts the exponent and the sign.** In `jg` an exponent flip moves a product and the accumulator it lands on together, so the aligned operand does not change (make_e92's flop reading on the real accumulators, over the zero: 0 at bit 23, 0 at bit 27). In `jgp` the product's exponent alternates against an accumulator that does not, so the aligner shifts every micro-op (155,708 flop-bit changes an op at bit 23; 165,528 at bit 27, where half the products are then absorbed). A balanced **sign** makes the sum cancel and the adder alternate, E92's 1,100 fJ mechanism (2,048 alternations an op; every accumulator walks 0, ±b, 0, ±b). So on those positions a balanced price is not "the flip alone": it is a different composite, and the registered rules say which way it should go. A flip with **neither** swing would need frozen sums (E88, E92's `h_add`), which E92 has already priced for the sign: 122 fJ.

### int8's B side, and the resolution this design can reach

The B line is held for sixteen micro-ops, and only line boundaries change it: 16 transitions × 8 lanes × 2 units × 4 elements = **1,024 flips an op per bit, the most a fixed tile allows** (E79 A4, A6). At 1.92 × 10⁹ minion-ops/s, 1 fJ per flip is 1.97 mW. So:

- **More flips per position cannot be had; several positions at once can.** `bk_ff` flips all eight bits (the level, 8×), `bk_15` against `bk_2a` tests E79's odd/even shape at 3×, `bk_0f` against `bk_f0` tests a rise toward the high bits at 4×. (0x55 and 0xAA would be the natural pair, but E79's lane-4 base byte *is* 0x55: its word would XOR to zero and the gate would act.)
- **The bracket that pins the leakage.** E79's B level moved with the slope because of the reading, not the card ([`desk_noise_e102.txt`](desk_noise_e102.txt) [1]). The reading is whole degrees, and at int8's low power a window's mean sits anywhere from 58.0 to 59.0 on a step's edge, so `P − k (T_win − T_launch)` adds up to k × 1 °C to one run and nothing to the next. With no temperature term E79's own per-pass steps have an sd of 0.02–0.07 W at every magnitude position; at E71's slope, 0.12–0.29 W. **In a train the pair is in one thermal state**: two runs apart, with own zeros either side, differing by 0.1 W, which is 0.013 °C at the window's measured 0.13 °C/W (E93). So the trains read **no temperature at all**: `p_raw` and `rail_raw` over the interpolated own zero. Leakage's feedback on such a pair is at most k × 0.13 = 7% at card 1's idle slope of 0.545 W/°C, stated, not corrected. E79's way (the run's own reading at each slope) is kept as labelled arms, to show what it does.
- **The primary arm for int8 is the minion rail**, because the board cannot reach it on this card: ±0.10 W a run against a 0.1 W step. Its window is `rail_raw`, the mean over [t0+2, t0+4). The board arm is printed beside it. A rail price is lower than a board price by about 0.81 (E79).

**What the design can reach, before data** (`predictions-e102.json` › `analysis.resolution_half_width_fJ`; t(0.975, n−1) × σ √(1 + 1/own zero reads) / √n, σ from E93): a single int8 position ±29 rail-fJ (±70 board), a 3-bit mask ±10, a 4-bit mask ±7, `bk_ff` ±3.6 rail-fJ per flipped bit. **So the bench's single-bit list (about 10 fJ, or 20 at its int8 fit) is below what a single position can resolve, and if int8 B's single bits are there, the result is a bound, and it is reported as one**; the level and the two contrasts still resolve. E79's list, if real, mostly resolves (29–96 fJ on the board at its magnitude positions).

**The bench's int8 list is a floor.** The public RTL writes int8's multiplier behaviourally, `$signed(f2_in1_part) * $signed(f2_in2_part)` on 9-bit operands (`tima/tima_top.v:83-90`), so the bench sees its inputs and its product, and no partial product inside it, where the float multiplier is structural. That is why its k and a fitted on E78's int8 kinds are twice the float pair (3.74 and 2.13 fJ, rms 0.29 W). The same line says what to expect of the MSB: with A = 1 a flip of B's bit 7 flips the operand's sign extension and the product's top bits, so the bench prices bit 7 at 4.7× bit 6.

## Analysis (`score_e102.py`, committed with this file)

Per run: board = `p80` in block G (frozen slope, launch gate); `p_raw` in a train (above). Rail = `rail_raw` in every block (E79's rail arm, uncorrected). Per pass, each item minus its **own** zero interpolated linearly in time between that zero's reads (settling runs never count; flat beyond them, and the bracket makes that rare). Price = step / (designed flips per op × the run's rate), each item on its own count. Over passes: mean, SE from the between-pass scatter, 95% interval t(0.975, n−1) × SE. **The pass is the unit of replication.** A price whose interval contains zero is a **bound**. A rule needs three kept passes of each block it reads, or it is **not scored**.

A ratio all-in / balanced is per flipped bit, with its 95% Fieller interval: paired by pass in one block, independent across blocks (df = min n − 1). A ratio whose denominator's interval contains zero is unbounded and decides nothing.

Gates: E78's, per run (600 MHz, ±3 mV, records ok, cycles per op within 0.5%, window samples and duty, tile dump equal to its sha256; in block G also the launch reading and the quiet time), and its pass gates, unchanged, where they have reads to act on. In a train: **the trains' own gate**, a zero's interior read more than 0.25 W off the straight line through its neighbours on the rail (about 5σ) sets the pass aside.

**Primary arm:** the rail for every rule except where named (R5 and R7 read the board, because E79's numbers and E92's flop price are in board watts). Both arms are always printed. The prices the page would quote are the board's, with the rail's beside them.

## Predictions, frozen before data

Every step is over its own zero, in board watts on this card. **The bench** is Q132(b)'s `vpu_top` with `-fno-dfg`, run on E102's frozen tiles on the mini at 01:45–01:50 PDT ([`counts-e102.json`](../../../docs/reports/data/2026-11-01-e102-balanced-bit-prices/bench/counts-e102.json), 0 functional mismatches over 72 tiles), priced with E92's frozen `nets_map` coefficients (1.871 fJ per merged-net toggle + 1.092 fJ per clocked register bit, fitted on E78's float kinds alone), over E82's card scale. **Its out-of-sample record on the lists this builds on** ([`backtest-hl-hlp.json`](../../../docs/reports/data/2026-11-01-e102-balanced-bit-prices/bench/backtest-hl-hlp.json); the bench lane's counts of E65's, E67's and E79's tiles, none of them in its fit): rms 0.122 W over E65's 31 B positions, 0.229 W over E67's 31 A positions (whose own intervals are about that wide), 0.136 and 0.229 W over E79's fp16 lists, 0.104 W over E79's `b32_22`, `b32p_22` and `b16p_9`. So it is the prediction to beat.

**The bench says the ratio depends on position, and it does so for a simple reason: a balanced price is nearly flat over the mantissa and E65's low floor is nearly all flip.** At bit 22 the excess is the adder and normaliser (2.30 M merged-net toggles an op over the zero: adder_norm 1.34 M, multiplier 0.22 M, c_regfile 0.15 M, exponent 0.15 M); at bit 6 the two layouts count the same (0.21 M merged-net toggles an op over the zero: multiplier 0.09 M, adder_norm 0.08 M, operand 0.03 M, lane_operand_bus 0.00 M against 0.20 M merged-net toggles an op over the zero: multiplier 0.09 M, adder_norm 0.08 M, operand 0.03 M, lane_operand_bus 0.00 M).

On A the balanced list is not flat: A is the Booth multiplier (`txfma_f1.v:113-114`), so a flip that changes a recoded digit moves partial products (at bit 22, 0.83 M merged-net toggles an op over the zero: multiplier 0.71 M, adder_norm 0.09 M, operand 0.01 M, vpu_sequencer 0.01 M), and the bench's A-side ratio is non-monotonic with position (4.3 at 16, 2.0 at 22). It depends on the base word's neighbouring bits, and E102 runs one base.

## What a composite price means now

E92 priced the adder's flop bits at 6–7 fJ per bit, and showed one flip of its operation moves all of them. So an all-in price at position j is three things, each of which E102 can now see:

1. **the flip's own path**: operand, multiplier, a few adder bits. A balanced mantissa flip is close to this alone (bench: 90–190 fJ).
2. **the accumulators' swing**: when a lane alternates between two sums that differ, the C read, the adder's inputs and flops and the result bus all toggle in the bits where they differ. A low-bit difference never builds up: once a sum is 2^m times a product, each addition keeps only the product's top bits, so two addends that differ below that round to the same increment and the sums stay equal. Hence the near-zero swing at the low bits, on the real accumulators (352 C-read bits an op at bit 6, against 50,260 at bit 22). (`make_e88`'s and `make_e92`'s C = fl((n−1) S), exact for the integer and absorbed designs they were written for, misses this on a random significand: they put `bj_6` at 20,347 C-read bits and 2.68 W, where E65 measured 0.38 W. E102 counts both ways, and reads the real one.)
3. **for exponent and sign, a term the balanced layout adds rather than removes**: the aligner, or the adder's alternation. So a balanced price there is dearer than `jg`, and "all-in / balanced" stops being a discount.

R7 tests E92's flop price against term 2: at 6.68 fJ per bit the flops carry 32% of the excess at bit 22, 27% at 20, 27% at 16, by the frozen counts on the real accumulators and the bench's steps. The rest is the C path, the multiplier and the normaliser's other nets, which the bench's stages split.
