# E101: the same kinds of numbers in a matmul that streams its operands (Q132; aifoundry3 card 0 at 600 MHz, aifoundry2 under its own governor)

*Pre-registered Sun 1 Nov 2026 (week 44), on the Intel, before the Discord claim and the first launch. The frozen numbers are [`predictions-e101.json`](predictions-e101.json) (table: [`predictions-e101.txt`](predictions-e101.txt)), written by [`make_predictions_e101.py`](make_predictions_e101.py) from committed inputs, with the rules in [`rules-e101.json`](rules-e101.json), all committed with this file. Any change after this commit goes in the Amendments section at the end, dated, before the data it touches. No band moves. No governor, TDP, threshold, clock, reset, PCIe, driver, firmware, OTA or PMIC work anywhere in E101: `ettelem config` reads only, the card's own clock.*

**The gap.** Every per-kind number on the [kinds page](https://spacesheep.dev/@yaroslavvb/horace-kinds-of-numbers) comes from one 16×16 TensorFMA repeating on tiles resident in the minions' L1 scratchpad (`sparsity_host --test fma`). A real matmul also streams its tiles, and Horace He timed a real GEMM. In int8 at a pinned 600 MHz, E37's matmul benchmark draws about 26–28 W over idle against the loop's 9.65 W on random int8: +18 W by E39's registered kernel clause (`docs/findings/05-claims.md:481`). So "int8 hardly slows", int8's ×1.00 and every speed gap on the page belong to the loop until someone measures otherwise. This is the top item of the previous round's final critic.

**The question.** In a real matmul that streams its operands, how much do the same kinds of numbers change power, heat and clock, in each format, and do the page's headline gaps survive?

## Which real matmul this is

Three things were found at the desk that change the plan the critic sketched:

1. **The benchmark's source is in this repo**, `kernels/mmbench/mmbench.cc`, `launchers/mmbench/mmbench.cpp` (sha256 equal to the copies on aifoundry2 and aifoundry3). It runs the inner loop of gp-sdk's generated matmul, on a pool of tile pairs in the shire's L2. It fills them with ±1 and ±2 and has no option for other operands.
2. **The vendor's real GEMM is in the SDK:** `external/et-platform/gp-sdk/device/sdk/libautogen/matmul_f32_1024_512_512`, compute thread `MatmulCmd_compute.cc`, host `gp-sdk/host/sdk/src/autogen_matmul/autogen_matmul.cpp`. It computes C (1024 × 512) += A (1024 × 512) × B (512 × 512) in fp32: each minion owns two row tiles and one column tile of C, runs 32 TensorFMAs per output tile, and stores C with a TensorStore. **Its loads are cooperative:** the 8 minions of a neighbourhood load one A tile together, and the minions at one position in the 4 neighbourhoods load one B tile together. So one shire reads 256 KB of A and 256 KB of B, the size of its 512 KB L2: that is its cache block. Nothing in this lab has run it or anything cooperative. mmbench's 26 W has every minion loading its own 2 KB per op from the L2, which the vendor's loop does not do.
3. **A launch of that GEMM lasts 58 µs at 600 MHz**, against about 2 ms of launch overhead, so it cannot be read on a board meter one launch at a time. It has to loop inside a launch.

So E101 runs a ladder, every rung on the same kinds and the same frame:

| arm | what runs | memory traffic per minion-op | relation to the page and the records |
|---|---|---|---|
| `res` | E78's loop: one tile pair resident in the L1 scratchpad | none | the page's numbers, re-read in this session |
| `tenb` | E39's mode: A resident, B streamed through TenB | 1 KB from L2 | E39's rung |
| `mm16` | **the matmul benchmark's loop**, op for op: A into two L1 buffers, B through TenB, the next op's loads issued while the FMA runs; 16 tile pairs in the L2 | 2 KB from L2 | E37's workloads `*-tensor-L2`, with per-kind operands |
| `gemm1` | **a real GEMM**: the vendor kernel's partition, its cooperative loads and TensorStore, in its order, for all three formats; one block, C (64 × 32 tiles) = A (64 × 32 tiles) × B (32 × 32 tiles), repeated inside a launch | 2 KB delivered, 1/8 of A's and 1/4 of B's read from L2; 1 KB of C stored per 32 ops | the most honest arm: this is the production pattern |
| `gemm16` | the same, walking 16 different blocks | as `gemm1`, with 48 MB of operands, past the 32 MB L3, so refills come from DRAM | what a matrix bigger than one block does |
| `gemmnc1` | `gemm1` with every minion loading for itself | 2 KB from L2 | isolates the cooperative loads |
| `mmp64` | `mm16` with 64 private tile pairs per minion | 2 KB from DRAM | E37's `fp32-tensor-DRAM` |

Kernel and host: [`tools/claims-v3/rgm/`](../rgm/), a copy of `workloads/sparsity` with two new kernel modes (`SP_STREAM`, `SP_GEMM`) and one new host path. The repo's `workloads/sparsity` and `kernels/mmbench` are not edited, and nothing is built over another lane's binaries: it builds to `build/e101/` on each box. The grammar sits inside `--values` (`mm16:randn:<tile>`, `gemm1:tiles:<tile>`), so E78's and E64's runners run byte-unchanged.

**What stays different from a production GEMM, plainly.**
- *fp16 and int8 have no vendor GEMM.* Only `matmul_f32_1024_512_512` exists. `gemm1` in fp16 and int8 is that kernel's pattern with the TensorFMA type changed and K scaled (the same bytes, 32 ops per output tile), written here, not the vendor's.
- *fp32's `gemm1` is a port, not the vendor's binary:* packed tiles of 16 contiguous lines where the vendor strides a row-major matrix; a loop inside the launch; no helper threads (the vendor's prefetch threads are empty in this kernel, and thread 1 only hands out a start credit).
- *`gemm1` repeats one block*, so after the first pass its operands sit in the L2 and the L3. *`gemm16` cycles 16 blocks and never accumulates C across blocks:* each block's C is that block's product, stored and overwritten. A true 8192³ walk (Horace He's size, in bf16) would also reload C between K blocks; E101 does not do that.
- *`mm16` is a benchmark, not a GEMM:* every minion computes the same product, C is stored once a launch, no cooperative loads.
- The tile sets of `mm16`, `gemm1` and `gemm16`: pair 0 is E78's tile, the rest fresh draws of its rule, new each pass. An `int8` real tile set is a quantised one, as on the page.

**Checks at the desk.** `mm16`, `tenb`, `gemm1`, `gemmnc1` and a private pool ran in `sys_emu`, one shire, all 32 minions, across fp32, fp16 and int8: no error, and C against the host's own reference inside 1e-4 of the sum of the absolute products on every checked word, exact on zeros, ones and every int8 case ([`../rgm/sim-check.txt`](../rgm/sim-check.txt)). `gemm16`'s walk over blocks had not run there when this was frozen: the simulator's machine stopped answering; it goes through the simulator on a box's idle window if it can, and its smoke run otherwise. The simulator shows the loops and the cooperative groups compute the right thing. It says nothing about silicon: **no cooperative load has ever run on these cards in this lab**, hence the step below.

## Session 1: power at 600 MHz, aifoundry3 card 0

E78's frame: every number is board power over idle, one 4 s process per run, launched at a 58 °C reading after heating to 59 °C and 2.0 s quiet; `S = p80 − idle80` over `[t0+1, t0+3)`, E78's leakage correction; anchors and tie in every pass; **the pass is the unit of replication**. It is E78's protocol and code: [`run_e101.py`](run_e101.py) checks `run_e78.py`'s sha256 and runs it unchanged with E101's items and order installed; [`reduce_e101.py`](reduce_e101.py) does the same with `reduce_e78.py`. Only the items differ:

- **Kinds:** zeros, ones, random (normal, int8 uniform −128..127), sparse50 (half the elements zero: the gate acts on 77 / 41 / 7% of lane-cycles, kinds.json) and **real**, a set of four Q132(r) tiles with no zero word per format (GPT-2, BERT, ViT; int8 at the 99.9th-percentile and other calibrations), all from committed files, in `fp32`, `fp16` and `int8`. Plus **e37**, E37's own ±1 / ±2 fill (`rgm/tiles/*/e37.bin`, `make_e37_tiles.py`), resident and in `mm16`: the one fill that ties to E37 and splits its +18 W clean.
- **A pass** ([`e101_common.py`](e101_common.py)): the tie, three segments, the tie. 78 runs: E78's six anchors three times each, E78's ones and sparse50 per format, resident e37 and real; `mm16` × 6 kinds × 3; `gemm1` × 5 × 3; `gemm16` zeros and random × 3; `gemmnc1` random × 3; `tenb` random in fp32 and int8; `mmp64` zeros and random in fp32. Order shuffled per pass with `Random("E101|aifoundry3|<pass>")`, three independent passes, so arms and kinds interleave. Tier 2, what a short pass drops (`real`, `tenb`, `gemmnc1`, `mmp64`), leaves 62 runs.
- **Gates:** E78's, per run and per pass, unchanged, with one difference by construction: the new arms have no cycle gate and no window-duty gate, because whether the data changes their cycle count is a finding to report (R9b), not a run to drop. A launch whose check reads wrong fails its run's `ok` gate, and every such run is reported.
- **What is checked in every process:** the host compares C of the last launch with its own reference on 64 sampled minions: `exact`, `approx` or `wrong`.

**Card time.** Build (about 2 min, no device node) after E95's release, then the claim, then `coop1` and the smoke (about 3 min), then three passes of about 12–14 min each: **about 45 card-minutes, more than the 25 the brief guessed**, because the ladder is the point. The queue ([`queue_e101.sh`](queue_e101.sh)) starts a full pass only if its projected end fits the budget (44 card-minutes) and the deadline set at launch; with fewer than three passes done it falls back to a tier-1 pass; a pass in flight is never cut. Fewer than three kept passes: NOT SCORED.

**If cooperative loads fail on this card** (`run_e101.sh coop1`: first `gemmnc1` on one shire, then `gemm1` on one shire for 0.2 s, then on every shire for 1 s, each `timeout 10` under the card lock; the host aborts a launch after 6 s):
- `gemm1` fails or computes wrong while `gemmnc1` works and the card answers: the queue runs with `E101_GEMM=gemmnc` (the items keep their names; the README says so on every number; R8 is NOT SCORED).
- `gemmnc1` itself computes wrong (TensorStore, not coop): `E101_DROP_ARMS=gemm1,gemm16,gemmnc1`: `mm16` is the real matmul of this experiment, and that is reported as a failure of the arm.
- The card stops answering: stop, keep the claim, write it up, alert (`notify.py --unblock`). Never a reset. The lanes after this one are told at once.

## Session 2: speed under the governor, aifoundry2

E80's design ([`../kspeed/`](../kspeed/README.md)): E64's runner unchanged, 10 Hz sampler, **no `--reset-ms`**, trace ring and `ettelem config` after every run, 65 W / 65 °C as read, stops at 85 °C and at 97 W on three samples. In the slot E98's lane gave it: **start epoch 1793535600 (04:20 PST), end 1793540100 (05:35 PST)**, under claim `1566355908076765257`, no post of its own. Its runner, queue, reducer and protocol are committed before the slot (Amendments), with `gemm1` first: `gemm1` burst and sustained on all five kinds in all three formats, `mm16` on zeros and random at least, E80's resident anchors for the tie, replicates of fp32 random and int8 full and zeros.

**Why the plan depends on session 1, frozen here.** A burst starts at 800 MHz, and `P_800 = B_800 + 1.905 c`. Frozen, random fp32 in `mm16` predicts **98.8 W** and in `gemm1` 92.5 W, at or past the 97 W stop that ends the session, on a card that read 88.88 W at most so far. So:
- a cell runs a **cool burst** (≤ 55 °C, E80's) or a **cool sustained start** (≤ 57 °C) only if session 1's measured S gives `P_800 ≤ 88 W` (E80's own random fp32 peaks about 6 W over this formula);
- any other cell runs a **warm burst**: E64's `hot` start at 60 °C with 2 s resident random fp32 launches, so the governor's power loop has already taken the clock off 800 MHz when the work starts, labelled in every table, and a **hot sustained start**: heated to 66 °C, in the thermal loop;
- every cell's mode is written into the protocol from session 1's committed `e101.json`, by rule, before the slot. The session still ends on 97 W × 3, whatever the plan said.

## The frozen predictions

`S(arm, t, kind) = r · S_res(t, kind) + [E_base(arm) + E_data(arm, t, kind)] · rate(arm, t)`: the resident watts from E78, carried to the arm's op rate, plus the memory path: its cost with nothing changing, and E75's prices per changed bit on each tile's bits between consecutive lines (L2 0.274 pJ on both operands, L1 0.0492 pJ on A; a cooperative load pays the L2 once per group). `E_base` for `mm16` comes from E37's watts less all of that, 4.67 nJ per op [3.03, 5.94]; for `tenb` from E39's int8 ones; for `gemm1` it is a guess with a wide interval, 3.3 nJ [1.5, 6.1], half of `mm16`'s falling to 3/16 plus one TensorStore, because nothing has measured it. **Clock:** E83's frozen relation on `c = c_zeros + (S − S_zeros)/xfer`. The table:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fp32 `res` (E78) | 1.99 | 10.29 | 26.70 | 9.24 | ×1.13 | ×1.33 | 10.9 / 9.3 TFLOPS |
| fp32 `mm16` | 7.45 | 16.02 | 35.38 | 17.14 | ×1.25 | ×1.33 | 10.1 / 9.5 |
| fp32 `gemm1` | 5.87 | 14.44 | 31.99 | 13.92 | ×1.21 | ×1.33 | 10.5 / 9.5 |
| fp16 `res` | 1.99 | 10.38 | 24.10 | 15.22 | ×1.09 | ×1.31 | 22.5 / 18.8 |
| fp16 `mm16` | 7.45 | 16.11 | 32.66 | 23.23 | ×1.22 | ×1.33 | 20.9 / 19.0 |
| fp16 `gemm1` | 5.87 | 14.53 | 29.30 | 20.07 | ×1.17 | ×1.33 | 21.8 / 19.0 |

(W over idle at 600 MHz, aifoundry3; intervals in the JSON.) Written down plainly: **the prediction is that streaming narrows the power gaps, raises the zeros' share from 7% of random to about 20% in fp32 and 50% in int8, widens the fp32 burst gap to about ×1.2, keeps the sustained clamp at ×1.33, and takes "int8 hardly slows" away**.

## Rules that can fail, and what each outcome means for the post

Thresholds machine-readable in `rules-e101.json`; scored by [`score_e101.py`](score_e101.py) (self-test: [`selftest_e101.py`](selftest_e101.py), [`selftest_e101.txt`](selftest_e101.txt)). Paired per pass, 95% t over passes; fewer than three kept passes: NOT SCORED. Every rule also at the leakage arms 0.1994, 0.3803 and 0 W/°C, a verdict that moves marked slope-sensitive.

| rule | what | PASS says | FAIL says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | `mm16` on E37's fill reads E37's watts over idle (less 0.8 W of leakage) within ±1.5 W, and E37's 529 / 529 / 280.35 cycles within 0.5% | the ported loop is E37's benchmark | every `mm16` number carries the port's difference |
| **R2** | resident cells read E78's S within ±0.60 W, on the new binary | the session sits in the page's frame | read the arms only against this session's resident cells |
| **R3** | `S(mm16) − r·S(res)` > 1 W at 95% in all 12 cells of four kinds × three formats | every per-kind watt on the page is low for a matmul that streams | in that cell the page's number stands |
| **R3b** | rms of measured − frozen S over those 12 cells: ≤ 3 W `mm16`, ≤ 4 W `gemm1` | loop + path cost + E75's prices predicts a streamed cell | they do not add up, and the residuals say which term is off |
| **R4** | int8: `S(mm16, e37) − S(res, e37)` against 17.3 W [14.8, 19.8], three-way | the register's +18 W is the kernel | part of the +18 W was the fill |
| **R5** | memory path's data part, `[S(random) − S(zeros)] − r[S_res(random) − S_res(zeros)]` in `mm16` against E75's prices × bits (2.4 W [1.7, 3.2] fp32 and fp16, 5.3 [3.7, 7.0] int8), three-way | it is the size E75's prices say | prices measured on bare loads do not carry over |
| **R5b** | the same with ones: inside ±1 W | a constant costs the path what zeros do | the path pays for the constant itself |
| **R6** | Spearman ρ ≥ 0.9 between resident and arm over five kinds, each format, `mm16`, `gemm1` | the kinds rank as on the page | streaming reorders them |
| **R7** | zeros' share of random: a) above the resident share at 95%; b) below 0.75 at 95% | a) "5–7% of random" is the loop's; b) the gate still saves at least a quarter | a) the loop's figure stands; b) zeros hardly save once the operands stream |
| **R8** | `S(mm16, random) − S(gemm1, random)` and `S(gemmnc1, random) − S(gemm1, random)` > 0 at 95% | the production pattern draws less: E37's watts overstate a cooperative GEMM | cooperative loads do not save board power |
| **R9** | no check reads wrong; zeros and all int8 exact | the kernels compute what they should on every kind | **a kernel takes a different path on some data: a finding** |
| **R9b** | within an arm and format, cycles per op of every kind within 1% | the data never changes the cycle count, streaming or not | the page's first sentence is the loop's |
| **R10** | OLS die rise on S over cells ≥ 12 W in [0.10, 0.14] °C/W, as E78's 0.113–0.129 | a watt on the memory path heats the die sensor as a watt on the lanes does | the heat line must carry the arm |
| **R11** | `mmp64` fp32: `S(random) − S(zeros)` against 4.42 W [3.31, 5.52], three-way (E75's chord at E37's 82 GB/s plus the multiply-adds) | the DRAM path's data dependence is the size E75 and E81 say | it is not |
| **R12** | `gemm16`: `S(random) − S(zeros)` > 0 at 95%, each format; its cycles reported with no band | the kinds still set the power of a GEMM past the L3 | past the L3 the stalls hide the kinds |
| **S1** | aifoundry2, fp32, zeros ÷ random TFLOPS in the burst, `gemm1` (and `mm16` where a cool burst is allowed): GROWS ≥ 1.20 (predicted), SURVIVES [1.10, 1.20), SHRINKS < 1.10 | GROWS: the page's ×1.13–1.15 understates a real matmul; SURVIVES: it stands | SHRINKS: the burst gap belongs to the loop |
| **S2** | the same, sustained: SURVIVES in [1.20, 1.40] (predicted 1.33) | the page's ×1.29–1.33 stands | the sustained gap belongs to the loop; which way it moved |
| **S3** | int8 sustained, `gemm1` and `mm16`: SLOWS ≥ 1.05 (predicted 1.19, 1.22), HARDLY < 1.03 | SLOWS: **"int8 hardly slows" belongs to the loop** | HARDLY: int8 hardly slows in a real matmul too |
| **S4** | rms of measured − frozen clock, burst and sustained, ≤ 40 MHz | the chain kind → watts → clock carries over | not on the frozen S; the relation on session 1's measured S is scored beside it, labelled |
| **S5** | real set against random, `gemm1`, within 25 MHz, each format | real float tensors stream at random data's speed | they part once the operands stream |

Failures are reported as failures, with the measured value beside the band.

## How to run it

On the Intel: this commit on origin/main. `rsync` E101's files, `real2/` and `tools/claims-v3/rgm/` into `~/aifoundry` on aifoundry3 after E95's release. There, from that tree:

```bash
bash tools/claims-v3/kinds/run_e101.sh build         # no device node: build/e101, nice 19
bash tools/claims-v3/kinds/run_e101.sh setup         # no device node: the files against the freeze, a V3_DRY pass
# -> the claim in #community-lab, one line, THEN:
bash tools/claims-v3/kinds/run_e101.sh coop1         # the first cooperative loads on this card
bash tools/claims-v3/kinds/run_e101.sh smoke         # 13 runs of 2 s; cycles, ok
bash tools/claims-v3/kinds/run_e101.sh go 10110 3    # detached; the budget rule decides
```

Then, on the Intel, over a copy of the raw passes: `PY=/usr/bin/python3 nice -n 10 bash tools/claims-v3/kinds/collect_e101.sh 'p101[1-9][0-9]' <raw root>`.
