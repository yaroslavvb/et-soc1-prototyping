# E92: where a random sign's cost goes (Q132, aifoundry3 card 0, 600 MHz)

*Pre-registered Sun 1 Nov 2026 (week 44), on the Intel, before the Discord claim and the first launch, in Q132's run
(item 2 of the kinds page's section 11). The frozen numbers are in [`predictions-e92.json`](predictions-e92.json),
written by [`make_predictions_e92.py`](make_predictions_e92.py) from the committed sources it names (every number read
from its file, never retyped), and the tiles and counts in [`e92/e92.json`](e92/e92.json), written by
[`make_e92.py`](make_e92.py); all are committed with this file. Any change after this commit goes in the Amendments
section at the end, dated, before the data it touches; no band moves. Card: **aifoundry3 card 0 only**, 600 MHz,
~522 mV, TDP 0 and threshold 65 °C as read (never set). E92 writes no knob: no governor move, no TDP, threshold or
clock set, no reset, no `--reset-ms`.*

**The question.** Random ±1 costs about 5 W over all +1 in fp32 and fp16, 8–16× its sign bits (E90). E65's `bit_31`
flips B's sign at every read and costs only 0.56 W. Half of fp32's sign cost survives a frozen result (E88 `s_abs` −
`c_abs`, 2.52 W). E88 named a guess before its data and left it untested: a product's sign decides whether the adder
adds or subtracts. E92 tests it: **it holds the operands fixed and changes only the sign the running sum holds**, so
one operand stream can always add, always subtract, or alternate. It says where a random sign's ~5 W goes: the sign
bits, the add/subtract switch, the moving sums.

**A design point, checked.** The kinds page's section 11 suggests "a design whose operand signs vary while every
product keeps one sign". On the fp32 stream, a product's sign is `a[i][k] b[k][j]`. If it is one sign over the whole
op, then `a[i][k] b[k][j] = s` for all i, j, k, which forces `a[i][k] = s γ[k]` and `b[k][j] = γ[k]`: the signs depend
on k alone and flip only at k boundaries, at most 128 + 128 flips an op (`k_only`), too few to test anything, as this
run's brief says. But **if each accumulator may hold its own sign, the same equation allows `a[i][k] = α[i] γ[k]` and
`b[k][j] = γ[k] β[j]`, with s[i][j] = α[i] β[j]**, and those flip at full rate: `h_add` (β alternates by half, 4,096 B
flips an op) and `i_add` (α alternates by row, 2,048 A flips). That is E65's `bit_31` layout, each accumulator there
only ever adding, which may be why that bit was cheap (R1 tests it). The price of the design is that the product's,
the C read's and the result's signs alternate too, so the suggestion cannot separate those from the operand bits. The sharper test is the brief's: the
same operands with a different sum sign (`h_add`, `h_sub`, `h_allsub`), and operands whose signs do not move at all
(`c_abs`, `c_neg`, `c_alt`). `k_only` stays as the low-flip control.

**The reading from the public RTL (a reading, labelled; the RTL bench still returns no correct multiply-add, Q130(b)).**
The fused multiply-add's effective operation is `eff_sub1 = sign(P) ^ sign(C)`
(`external/core-et/rtl/shire/minion/vpu/txfma_7s/txfma_c2.v:144`, `:161`). The smaller operand goes through the aligner
(`txfma_ediff_opdorder_logic.v:167-179`), and an effective subtract **inverts all 52 bits of the aligned operand** into
the carry-save adder (`txfma_c2.v:303`, `:364`; `txfma_f3.v:384-421`, `:428-443`), whose sum and carry are flopped
(`:120-121`) and fed to a 52-bit carry-propagate adder (`txfma_f4.v:61-71`), a leading-digit anticipator and a
trailing-zero counter (`:91-119`). A product shifted out of range is zapped to 0 (`txfma_c2.v:389`, 49 places in fp32;
`:398`, 26 in fp16), so even when the sum cannot change, the bus into the adder is all 0s when adding and all 1s when
subtracting, and every one of those nets moves when the operation flips, and none when it holds. So the reading says:
**the alternation is dear, a steady subtract is not.** fp16 has two aligned operands with their own `eff_sub`
(`txfma_c2.v:150`, `txfma_ediff_opdorder_logic.v:208-224`): the high product into the 52-bit `csa_in3`, the low one into
the 29-bit `csa_in2` (`txfma_f3.v:241-242`). int8 runs on the TIMA integer unit, with no effective subtract, so E92
keeps int8 as E78's anchors only.

**Two things the emulation changed from the brief.** (1) **The walking seed is 16, not 2^10.** With k ≥ 1 products of 1,
a k = 0 product of 2^10 takes C past 2^25 within about 3% of a launch, after which a product of 1 is below half an ulp:
only 6.25% of results then move, at every one of the 16 sampled op indices (`e92.json` › `seed_2p10_note`). With 16,
the sum is an exact integer through a launch and every result moves. (2) **`w_h_add` is C[l] = +31n, C[l+8] = −16n, not
±31n.** With equal magnitudes the two accumulators a lane alternates between would share their mantissa and E88's bus
swing (R32bus) would land entirely on `w_h_sub`'s side. 16n has n's mantissa, so `w_c`, `w_h_add` and `w_h_sub` carry
99,306, 107,498 and 109,025 result bits an op. `wr_h_add`'s seed is chosen the same way: result bits 121,679 against
112,984, result-change rate 0.659 against 0.652.

**A launch restarts C.** The first op of each ~0.49 s launch is a MUL pass for its k = 0 group, so each launch re-seeds
each accumulator's sign from the k = 0 product (`vpu_tensorfma.v:704`, `:726`; `workloads/sparsity/kernel/sparsity.c:168`).

## The frozen predictions

Five models, all computed before any E92 data exist. **The card scale** is E78's random tile on aifoundry3 over E82's
on aifoundry1 card 1, where E88 ran: 1.0308 (fp32), 1.0336 (fp16).

- **bits** (E90's model, unchanged): the price of the sign bits a contrast flips. fp32 B: E65 `bit_31` 0.5629 W at 4,096 flips an op; fp32 A: E67 `abit_31` 0.5607 W at 2,048; fp16 B: E79 row 15, 0.313 W at 8,192 (a bound); fp16 A: E79 row 15, 0.327 W at 4,096.
- **alt, the adder-alternation reading.** E88's frozen random-sign cost M32s = 2.524 W, less `s_abs`'s sign bits 0.569 W, over `s_abs`'s 2,080 alternations an op: 835 fJ per alternation on card 1 (at the 600 MHz chip's 1.125e+09 ops/s). Times the card scale and a tile's alternations. **`h_sub` alternates 3,840 times an op against `s_abs`'s 2,080, so linearity puts `h_sub` − `h_add` at 3.72 W in fp32.** fp16 multiplies by E90's fp16 step over its fp32 step, each beyond its bits (1.079): **4.03 W**. In `s_abs` the alternation is about 50% of lane-micro-ops and in `h_sub` 94% (none at k = 0).
- **e88**: E88's four-price fit on E82, frozen (`rpath/e88.json` › `fit`), which has no term for the effective operation. It says `h_sub` − `h_add` is **−0.41 W**, because `h_add`'s sum signs move and `h_sub`'s do not.
- **e88+flops**: e88 plus one price per flop bit of the adder, calibrated on the same one datum: f = (M32s − e88's 0.242 W) / the 303,668 flop bits `s_abs` adds = **6.68 fJ per flop bit**, times the card scale. This is the only model with a term for alternation on moving sums: the flop counts say a walking sum already moves `comb_s` and `comb_add`, so alternation adds less there (343,288 flop bits an op at magnitude 1, 155,761 on random magnitudes, against 560,592 frozen).
- **q130n**: Q130(n)'s predictor, fitted on E78's 51 kinds, unchanged (`predictions-e92.json` › `tiles.*.q130n_predictor`).

## Decision rules

All on D at the primary leakage slope, paired per pass; each verdict at the three arms is reported beside, and a verdict
that moves is marked slope-sensitive. 95% intervals are t(0.975, n−1) × SE over kept passes. Three-way per contrast:
CONFIRMED, REFUTED, INCONCLUSIVE; a rule combines its contrasts: REFUTED if any is, CONFIRMED if all are, else
INCONCLUSIVE. **Fewer than 3 kept passes in the primary reduction: every rule is scored on the registered dips-removed
arm (E86's posthoc_dips method: board-reading samples 5–20 W low just after a launch boundary removed, then E78's
reduction), labelled as that arm; fewer than 3 there too: NOT SCORED.**

| | rule | threshold | why this value, before the data |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E92-R1** the sign bits | `h_add` − `c_abs`, fp32, fp16: CONFIRMED if the 95% upper bound ≤ 2 × bits, REFUTED if the lower bound > 2 × bits | 1.126 W, 0.626 W | E90-R1's factor 2 for the price lists' own spread (E67's A price 0.30–0.56 W by pass set; fp16's B price a bound). **Reported, never a verdict:** for every tile, D, S, pJ per op / MAC, ΔT, the rail. **The factorial:** with `c_abs` as the
origin, a B-and-product sign path α, a sum-sign path β, the add/subtract switch γ and a steady subtract δ,
`h_add` = α + β, `c_alt` = β + γ + δ/2, `h_sub` = α + γ + δ/2, `h_allsub` = α + β + δ, solved per pass, in W and in fJ
per lane alternation, in fp32 and fp16. **The budget:** `signs` − `ones` (E90's step, this session) split into the frozen
part (`s_abs` − `c_abs`) and the moving-sum part, with the bits and γ at `signs`' alternation rate beside. The ladder's
slope; `hh_sub` + `hl_sub` − `h_sub`'s additivity (the flops say +227,512 bits an op: a both-alternate op's `comb_s` flips only 88,328 bits because the two inverted buses cancel in the sum); `r_h_add` − `r_abs`,
`wr_h_add` − `r_walk`, `w_h_add` − `w_c`, each against E65's 0.563 W; E88's M32, R32, W32 on this card. **Failures are
reported as failures.** No band moves after this commit.

**What the rules cannot say.** A confirmed R2 shows that the sign the sum holds, with the operands fixed, sets the power;
it does not separate the flops of the reading from any other net that moves with `eff_sub`, and the flop counts are a
reading, not a gate-level count: f is one price calibrated on one datum, on another card. The card's result is not read
back: with `--values file:` the host skips its check (E88 C8), so frozen and walking rest on the desk emulation and on the
power agreeing with it. All these tiles are designed: their C is at ±(n+1)·2^100 (or a small integer), not where a real
tensor's lands. 1 µop in 16 (k = 0) moves the sum even in the frozen designs. Four passes is a small sample
(t = 3.18). One card, one clock. int8 is not tested: it has no effective subtract.

## Card budget

**At most 45 card-minutes, claim to release**, including the smoke, the configuration reads and the heating gates.
Expected about 43: the smoke ~1 min, then passes of ~10 min (56 runs at E90's measured ~9.8 s a run plus the block's
~50 s) with 15 s between them. `queue_e92.sh` starts a pass only if its projected end (the longest measured pass; 620 s
before the first one ends) is inside **41** card-minutes of the queue's start and before **06:45 PST**; a pass in flight
is never cut. Every run ends by 07:15 PST. The clocks go back at 02:00 PDT, after this run's planned end (about 01:10 PDT); the
blocks' records carry epochs.
