## 1. The question (quoted from the proposal, section 1)

**What does one 64 B line of memory traffic cost, with the data's ones and its toggles held apart, written as well as read?** Per line:
the price of a data pin held high for a beat (termination at the receiving end), of a pin toggle (the line's charge, the serialisers),
of a raw one carried behind the pins (the mesh, the memory path's raw words), of a bit changing on the memory path's 64-bit and 256-bit
words and between the lines one channel carries; whether write data-bus inversion is live (decided by toggles, not by termination);
what share of each no metered rail sees; whether each is a price per line (the same at half the line rate); and what a line of zeros,
and a line that is not all zeros, costs against controls whose cores do exactly the same thing. In pJ and in electrons per line, at the
12 V input and on each metered rail.

The lab has priced the read side on this card with designed images (E75, E81, E99, E110, E132, E139; E156 on a second card); nothing
with designed data has run on the write side, only the catalogue's tensor store of zeros, one constant and random floats. The request
(i) re-runs E99's tent, lane, toggle, one-bit and random images on reads in the same session (the tie, and same-session read prices for
every write/read comparison), (ii) mirrors them for writes, with the write side's width and channel images and E99's lane/beat pair as
the write-inversion test, and (iii) runs zeros, 0x0F and random lines at half the line rate in both directions, with three no-traffic
controls for the zero line. A line of zeros is a special case: the shire cache keeps a zero state per line (the lab's public research
page `docs/reports/data/2026-09-27-chip-diagram/research/research-inside.md`, from the open shire-cache specification), so every zeros
arm skips the L2/L3 data arrays, and a line that is not all zeros pays a floor that E99 measured at about 0.1 W on reads; the request
reports the zero line and "any line" = the zero line plus that floor.

## 2. The arms (registered; request.md section 2, the host's own table and the counts from its bytes, checked cell by cell in section 17)

- **All arms** (quoted): hart 0 of all 1,024 minions; each hart on its own slice of DRAM (reads 128 KB, writes 256 KB a hart: 128 and
  256 MB in all). **Flat out** = the catalogue's access loops: reads one 32 B `flw.ps` a line at a stride of 64 through the L1
  (`flw_pat`), writes one 512 B tensor store of f0..f15, eight stores then a wait (`tstore_raw`). **Paced** = one access, then the cycle
  counter is read until the next slot: reads one line per 1,024 cycles a hart, writes one 512 B store (8 lines) per 8,192 cycles a hart:
  0.600e9 lines a second either way at 600 MHz, about half of flat out (*desk check of arithmetic:* 1,024 x 600e6 / 1,024 and
  1,024 x 8 x 600e6 / 8,192 are both 6.00e8). Minion m starts its slot grid (m x period)/1024 cycles into the launch, so the chip's
  accesses are spread evenly over each period. Within a direction and a rate every arm runs the same instructions on the same
  addresses; only the data differ.
- **Designed counts per line** (quoted; `make_x09.py`, recomputed from the host's own bytes by `--check`; the lab's E99 conventions):

  then the 64-bit words, then the 256-bit words; `c44` is the channel step; the tent `w1, u2, u4, u7, u8, l08` separates ones from high

  `a44 - u4` is 512 toggles either way, so `Q = (a08 - l08) - (a44 - u4)/2` is -192 toggle prices if inversion is live and +256 if
  not, whatever a written pin or toggle costs. With inversion live, `u4 - l08` is 224 high pins at equal ones with no toggle anywhere.

## 9. The registered sanity checks (quoted from request.md section 7; computed by `reduce_x09.py`)

Each PASS, FAIL with the arms it names, or NO DATA:

- **C1** thread-0 instructions an access equal across the flat arms of a direction (within 0.5 % of its zeros arm);
- **C2** polls a slot equal across the paced arms of a direction, controls included (within 2 %: a core that waited on a miss would poll
  less);
- **C3** late slots at most 1 % of a paced arm's slots;
- **C4** the line rate of every memory arm within 0.5 % of its zeros arm (a slower arm is named);
- **C5** L1 misses one a load on the read arms (0.95-1.05), none on the writes and on `rdh.l1` (at most 0.01 an access);
- **C6** the memory shires' counts: reads 0.9-1.1 a line and writes at most 0.05 a line on the read arms, writes 0.9-1.1 a line and
  reads at most 0.05 a line on the write arms (no read before a full-line store). If the counters are not read (`--no-ms`), C6 is NO
  DATA and every write price is labelled as assuming that a full-line store reaches memory once, unread.

A FAIL does not remove data; the verdicts it touches are read with it.

## 15. What this cannot say (stated before the data)

- **The designed counts rest on a model of the pins** (quoted, `make_x09.py`'s head: 16 channels of 16 data pins and 2 inversion pins,
  a 64 B line as two 16-beat bursts on one channel, bytes in order on two lanes, every 16th line of the physical address on one channel,
  the public standard's DC inversion rule, assumed live in both directions). R1 tests write inversion; the other counts assume the lane,
  beat and channel model. If the card maps lines to channels otherwise, k_chan and the edge counts differ.
- **k_chan = 256 is the independent-draws count over minions** (R7's own text): the real count depends on how the memory path
  interleaves different minions' lines, and the channel price carries that factor.
- **The zero line's skip** is the open specification's zero state, as the lab's research page reads it; whether the scratchpad also skips
  zero rows is open (quoted). `wrh.z - wrh.scp` and `wrh.z - wrh.nst` bracket the write zero line; they do not split it.
- **If the memory shires' counters are not read** (section 4), every write price assumes a full-line store reaches memory once, unread.
- **The paced store loop and its no-store control are equal instruction for instruction but do not sit at the same address modulo 32**
  (*desk check of code* on this box's build: loop heads at 0 and 16 mod 32, their poll loops at 8 and 24; the no-store loop is the store
  loop with its access replaced, 0x710 bytes later). The lab's trap of E61: a loop's issue rate can depend on its head's address mod 32.
  C2 (polls a slot within 2 %) reads it; if C2 fails on `wrh.nst`, `wrh.z - wrh.nst` and `wrh.scp - wrh.nst` carry a poll-rate
  difference, and `wrh.z - wrh.scp` (one loop) does not. The kit reads the heads of aifoundry3's build.
- **`hpmcounter3` reads 128 short** when its low 7 bits are 0-10 (E2; the four-read form does not fix it): at most 128 cycles in a
  launch's deadline and count; in a paced loop one short read can count a late slot as on time, or lengthen one wait by at most 128
  cycles.
- **The floor's missing terms** and **the rails' fixed volts** (section 7): properties of the registered code, reported beside.
- **aifoundry3 is the lab's card for switching power over idle, never absolute watts;** one card, one session; the board meter's fixed
  gain sits in every absolute W.
- **The kernel built here differs, as a file, from the one aifoundry3 builds:** request.md states no ELF sha256; this box's RISC-V gcc
