## 1. The question

**Does the ledger turn a program's counts into its joules?** Four programs, six configurations, forecast before the card
by the load ledger v2 as frozen: a copy through DRAM (zeros and random bytes), SHA-256 of 2,048 messages, a merge sort
(sorted and shuffled input) and a sparse-parity instance that has never run on a card. Every blind success so far was one
instruction, in a tile loop or a matmul pattern; this is the first blind test of a whole program that is not a matmul,
and the first fair test of the ledger's awake rule (post hoc for E37 and E101's DRAM arm, below).

Each configuration is scored twice (section 8):
- **given its measured seconds** (score 1): tests the prices and the counts;
- **with its seconds forecast too** (score 2): tests the whole ledger.

Both numbers are always reported: the joules over idle, and the joules with the card at rest.

## 8. Scoring (REGISTERED; the scorer is `score_e189.py`, self-tested before any card)

### 8.1 The data's form (the kit's runner writes it; the scorer reads only this)

`docs/reports/data/<day>-e189-.../passes/<configuration>/p<k>/`: `energy.json` (the reducer's output for that pass),
`host.json` (what the reducer read: sparse parity's own host line; for the ledger programs the runner's adapter of the
ledger host's line into the reducer's fields), `host-raw.json` (the ledger host's own line), `pass.json` (the runner's
marks: configuration, pass, order index, launches requested, set aside or not). Measured, per pass:
- **J over idle a launch** = `board.j_per_solve`: the SP's board average over the long window above the before bracket,
  less the leakage law on the measured die, over the launches (energy.sh's reduction, E59's headline; the same reader in
  the smoke's gate);
- **J with the card at rest a launch** = `board.j_per_solve_total`;
- t_k, t_w and T0 as in 5.2.

### 8.2 A pass counts if

the reducer's hard checks pass (every busy sample at 600 MHz, its windows filled, no foreign device process); the
program's own checks pass (the ledger host: every record good, no mismatch, every `--expect` digest equal; sparse parity:
its host's status, the solve found); and the runner did not set it aside. A burst the host's budget cut short is scored
on the launches it ran and flagged SHORT: never read as the program failing. A configuration with fewer than 2 good passes
reads **NO DATA**: never a failed prediction.

### 8.3 The rules, with their words and thresholds

For each configuration c, its error is e_c = (the mean over its passes of the forecast) / (the mean over its passes of
the measured value) - 1; the pass is the unit of replication.

| rule | words | threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **E189-S1** | score 1, given the measured seconds: J over idle a launch | within 10% on at least 5 of the 6 configurations, **and** the rms of the six errors at most 10% |
| **E189-S1T** | score 1, given the measured seconds: J with the card at rest a launch | within 5% on at least 5 of 6 |
| **E189-S2** | score 2, with the seconds forecast: the measured kernel seconds (the mean of the passes' t_k) | inside the frozen two-sided band **and** within 15% of the point (0.85 <= mean t_k / point <= 1.15), on at least 5 of 6 |
| **E189-S2T** | score 2, with the seconds forecast: J with the card at rest a launch, at the forecast seconds and the forecast die (the frozen point) | within 15% on at least 5 of 6 |

- **Verdicts.** PASS if at least 5 configurations meet the rule (and, for S1, the rms over the configurations scored is
  at most 10%); FAIL if 2 or more miss it (so 5 cannot), or, for S1, if at least 5 are scored and their rms exceeds 10%;
  otherwise NOT DECIDED. A configuration with NO DATA counts neither way. A FAIL is reported as FAIL.
- **The day's verdict is the primary's.** The arms v2.1 and v2c are scored by the same rules and reported beside it.
- **S2's ratio** (named in the re-made freeze, before any datum). S1, S1T and S2T decide on e_c above. S2 alone takes its
  deviation as a share of the point, 0.85 <= mean t_k / point <= 1.15, as the frozen scorer tests it (`score_e189.py`,
  `ok_S2`). The `e_t` the scorer reports beside it is e_c's form, point / mean t_k - 1, and does not decide S2. The two
  disagree only when the mean t_k lies at 0.850-0.870 of the point (S2 passes, e_t reads +15.0% to +17.6%) or at
  1.150-1.176 of it (S2 fails, e_t reads -13.0% to -15.0%).

## 9. Replication and order

Four independent passes a configuration, one host process each: 24 scored passes. The order is four rounds, each holding
every configuration once, shuffled by `random.Random(189)` and written out in `plan-e189.json` (the runner reads it and
never re-draws it):
1. sparse parity, copy zeros, sort shuffled, copy random, SHA-256, sort sorted;
2. copy random, sparse parity, SHA-256, sort shuffled, copy zeros, sort sorted;
3. copy zeros, SHA-256, sort sorted, sparse parity, copy random, sort shuffled;
4. copy random, sort shuffled, SHA-256, sparse parity, sort sorted, copy zeros.

Each pass is energy.sh's form: 8 s of idle, ONE host process (a burst of K launches), 10 s of idle, the sampler at
10 Hz (`ettelem sample --every-ms 100`, no statistics reset), then the die gate before the next pass. K is in section 4:
the most launches the host's own budget rule lets start at the band's slow end, capped at energy.sh's 6 s target at the
point (`plan-e189.json` shows the arithmetic; every ledger-host process ends inside 10 s at the slow end, 8.6-9.3 s by
the same arithmetic; sparse parity's host keeps energy.sh's 9 s budget and its own start rule).

the closing reads, release.

## 11. Stops and commands

- **Stops** (live samples): board power at or above **65 W**, or `minshire[0]` at or above **70 C**, ends the queue: the
  running process finishes (a device process is never killed), nothing further starts, the closing reads, release.
  Predicted peaks are about 40 W.
- **The clock:** every sample must read 600 MHz. The first sample off it sets its pass aside; a second one, in any pass,
  stops the session.
- **The die gate:** a pass starts only when `minshire[0]` reads 60 C or less (it waits up to 10 minutes, else that pass is
  skipped: no data). A die below 52 C does not block: the pass is scored given its own T0 and labelled so.
- **Every device command is a READ**, listed and classified from the firmware source by the kit (`dmcmd_audit.py` GO on
  its file set): the two hosts open the ops node only (enercat's Session); `ettelem sample` without `--reset-ms`;
  `ettelem config` at the start and the end. **No statistics reset, no set, no reset, no power cycle, no firmware-revisions
  query.** The host's package counter is **not read** unless his go allows it (default no; the host's share then stays
  assumed and labelled so).

## 12. Fallbacks, and what each means

| what happens | what it means |
|---|---|
| a build fails on the box (the kit's behaviour check of a host, or a kernel's section hash) | that program is SKIPPED: no data, never a failed prediction; at most two build attempts a Pacific day (E183's NO-THIRD-BUILD) |
| a stop (board, die, clock) | the passes done are scored; the rest are NO DATA |
| a gate: the look finds the card or a device node held, `load_guard` hot, the 40-minute rest rule unmet, the smoke fails, the window closed | no session (or no further pass): no data, never a failed prediction |
| a program's own check fails on the card | that pass is a FAILED RUN, reported; not a datum and not a failed prediction |
| a burst cut short by the host's budget | scored on the launches it ran, flagged SHORT |
| the counters arm's desk test fails | the arm is dropped, said so in a dated kit note before any datum |
| the cap is reached | the queue ends after the current pass |

## 14. What this cannot say (stated before the data)

- **The meter's absolute scale is one fixed gain** (1.0787) for every board, never set beside an outside meter (E184's arm S
  is the open test). The scores are of the ledger against this meter.
- **The reducer reads whole degrees.** Its leakage correction can miss part of a low-power burst's own rise: on the stub
  card (not a measurement) it read SHA-256's and the sorts' J over idle about 6% above the injected energy, the copies 3%
  and sparse parity 2%. Section 8.4 reports the uncorrected reading beside the forecast plus its own rise term.
- **The copy's write path inside a copy** is the fsw.ps write-back price measured on a store-only stream; its loads are
  priced by analogy with tensor loads. The day decides which path a plain copy pays (0.49 J a GiB over idle on random
  bytes forecast, against 0.29 at the tensor paths' prices).
- **Seconds of SHA-256 and the sorts** rest on the catalogue's issue rates; the bands are two-sided by rule, not by
  measurement. Sparse parity's seconds are the fitted cycle model's.
- **Caches:** the simulator does not say which level served each access; the bytes past the L1 are bracketed.
- **The ledger programs' card path has never run;** the kit's smoke is its first.
