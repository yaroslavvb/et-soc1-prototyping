New to the ET-SoC-1 lab? Start now

30 September 2026 (PDT) · for new users of the AI Foundry lab and their coding agents · part of the ET-SoC-1 measurement reports

The AI Foundry lab has three machines with four ET-SoC-1 cards, and several people, CI jobs and long measurement queues share them. This page gets you in, puts the rules on one screen, and gives you a brief to paste into your coding agent (Claude Code or similar), so that the agent can set you up without spoiling anyone else's work. The brief holds what a week of running experiments on these cards taught us (23–30 September).

Log in once

What the lab is

Three x86_64 Ubuntu 24.04 machines on AI Foundry's Tailscale network: aifoundry1 has two cards, aifoundry2 and aifoundry3 one each. Each card is an ET-SoC-1 on PCIe: 1,088 small RISC-V cores (“minions”) with vector and tensor units, 32 of its 34 shires running kernels, and 32 GB of LPDDR4X. The four cards run three firmware releases and behave differently, so a number measured on one card needs its card named.

Other users of the cards include CI runners on aifoundry1 and aifoundry2, a demo service on aifoundry3, and measurement queues that hold a card for hours. Nothing on these cards limits the die temperature, so stop any run yourself at a 90 °C die mean.

What you need

  1. An account and a Tailscale invite. Ask the lab lead for an account on the machines and an invite to the lab's Tailscale network. Ask also which machine and card to use, and for an invite to #community-lab on the AI Foundry Discord, where people say which machine they are using.
  2. Tailscale on your computer. Accept the invite and install Tailscale.
  3. The first login. ssh <you>@aifoundryN prints a login.tailscale.com link and waits. Open it in a browser signed in to the lab's network and approve it. Tailscale asks again after its check period (12 hours by default). If the link answers 404, sign out of login.tailscale.com, sign back in and run ssh again. “Policy does not permit” can mean that your account does not exist on that machine yet: ask the lab lead.
  4. Read the login banner. It lists the machine's cards, who is using them now, and the rules. It is older than the brief: where they differ, follow the brief.
  5. Hand the brief to your agent. The agent cannot approve a Tailscale login, push to GitHub or post on Discord for you. When it hands you a link or a question, it waits for you.

The rules on one screen

  1. Ask before you use a card.The lab lead says which machine and card. Post “using aifoundryN” in #community-lab on the AI Foundry Discord, and “released” when done.
  2. Look first with et-who.It lists who holds each card and never opens one. A card opens in one process at a time; two users corrupt both runs.
  3. Lock, and cap at 10 s.ET_DEVICES=<N> flock -n /run/lock/etsoc-shire<N>.lock timeout 10 <cmd>. On aifoundry1, ET_DEVICES=1 selects card 1; without it a program opens card 0. Long holds block everyone, and only the lab admin can recover a hung card.
  4. Stop tools gently.Ctrl-C or a plain kill, never kill -9. The telemetry sampler finishes its request in flight on either; a request cut off mid-way leaves a stale reply that kills the next program to open the card.
  5. Never reset a card or change its settings.Resets, clocks, TDP, thresholds, firmware and driver change other people's runs silently, and a reset can hang a card. Ask the lab admin.
  6. Stop at a 90 °C die mean.Nothing on the cards limits their temperature; one ran at 90–103 °C and nothing tripped.
  7. Work in your home directory./tmp is cleared at every boot, each machine has its own /home, and aifoundry1's disk is nearly full. Build with nice and -j4.
  8. Leave nothing behind.No locks, samplers or stray jobs; log out when done. Some queues wait while another user is logged in.
