The lab's matmul model, MM1: a GEMM's cycles, joules and transistor switches on the ET-SoC-1, and how it held up blind

Tue 17 Nov 2026 (week 47), Pacific time. Written by the round's lane report-final (Q215) at the desk on aifoundry2: no card, no device command, no simulator run. It finishes the draft of round Q209 with every finding of that round's critic applied and E209, E210 and E211 filled in. Every number is traced to its committed source and label in the section that gives it; readme_numbers.py finds each one in its committed file and writes readme-numbers.txt. On one screen

In short. The lab built MM1, a model that predicts how many clock cycles and joules a matrix multiplication (a GEMM) takes on an ET-SoC-1 card, and how many transistors switch, from the matrix sizes, the number format, the data and the card; on the way it checked the lab's timing simulation and energy models against the cards (section 1 has the owner's question). Frozen before each blind test and scored on GEMMs mostly new to the card, MM1's errors were well below those of the lab's earlier routes, but still several times larger than the card's own run-to-run scatter. E206 to E212 are the lab's numbered experiments (E208 and E212 are a separate modelling session's sweeps); MM1-v0, MM1-v1 and MM1-v2 are successive versions of the model, each frozen before a test; F1 is the lab's earlier GEMM route and F2 the lab's energy ledger.

  • The model (section 2): MM1 takes M, N and K on the lab's GEMM kernel's grid, the number format, the data kind, the shire mask, the card and its clock, and gives cycles, seconds, joules over idle and with the card at rest, and transistor switches. It prices each tile op by where its 192 cache lines come from: the L2, the L3, or fresh from DRAM.
  • The timing simulation (section 3): the lab's instruction-level simulator has no clock: on 265 blind GEMM points the card took 8 to 34 times the simulator's step count (median 24; the counts come from a law fitted to 11 simulator runs). The RTL bench of the tensor unit is exact for the tensor op with its operands resident (12 micro-loop configurations, 18 registered readings within 2 cycles, one card). MM1 reads the gap as the memory path: the tensor unit's own op filled 17 to 99% of each op (median 39%); but E211's controls show the compiled code alone moves the cycles by 3-6%.
  • Blind (section 4): five tests on three cards, every forecast committed before the test's first card command. The newest MM1 frozen before each test read 4.7 to 9.5% rms in cycles and 3.2 to 11.9% in joules over idle (given the measured seconds, at the points of 3 W and above), against 17.1 to 23.0% and 18.4 to 31.0% for F1, the lab's earlier GEMM route, and 52.7 to 82.0% for F2, the energy ledger, on the three tests where F2 has a given-seconds figure (E206's arms and E207; the note under figure 3 gives E209 and E211). The low end in cycles, 4.7%, is arm B, a card-transfer test; on E207, the one test whose every point was new to every card, MM1-v2 read 5.0% and MM1-v1 5.9%, and MM1-v1 passed every registered rule and beat F1 and F2 on all four head-to-head rules. On aifoundry2's governed card (E209) MM1-v2 passed every band and beat F1 and F2; its registered win over MM1-v1 rests on the mean and is not confirmed case by case. On a variant of the kernel (E211: the same kernel with its C-block walk swapped, recompiled) MM1-v1 passed its absolute bands, the ratio rule could not be read (its controls failed), and MM1's cycles error rose from 3-4% on the old kernel to 8-9% on the variant.
  • The switch count, through energy (section 5): E210 changed only the data (nine fp32 operand patterns at two shapes on one card) and found the card's energy follows the signal-net part of MM1's counted switches above an ungated base, with one constant per shape, to 1.9% of the data-dependent power (REPORTED), against 16 to 47% for the bit-flip prices and 20% for the nearest measured kind; against MM1-v1's own kind term the registered comparison was UNDECIDED (5 cases).
  • The card repeats itself to about 0.4% in cycles and 1.2% in joules over idle, so model error, not noise, dominates.
  • What the blind tests do not show (section 6): MM1 has no operations input: it covers one kernel (rgm136) on its grid, with no epilogue; every measured GEMM is warm and repeated; its energy needs a measured GEMM anchor on each card; MM1-v2 prices no energy for a tile kind without a resident price on that card (MM1-v1 borrowed frozen per-tile predictions for E207's four such points and passed, -6.0% to +7.1%); nothing measures transistor switches (E210 tests their signal-net part through energy only).
  • Still wrong (section 6): all-zero data on aifoundry1 card 1, narrow shire masks, the stride step at NB 8, int8 at one K block, and the kernel variant's code path.

Labels. Measured: a card's committed reading. Registered: a forecast or a rule committed before its data existed. Blind: scored on data that did not exist when the forecast was committed and pushed. Shape-blind: blind, and no point had run on any card before the freeze. Card-transfer: blind at shapes the model was fitted on at another card, run on this card for the first time. Held out: left out of a fit (one experiment or one card at a time); held out is not blind. Unread: scored on data that existed before the forecast but that the model never read. Post hoc: read or fitted after the data. Desk: arithmetic on committed files (this folder's scripts, or the lane named). REPORTED: a figure beside the registered rules that decides nothing. NOT RUN: a check nobody ran. Error = forecast / measured - 1; "rms (bias)" is the quadratic mean and the mean of that error, in %.

Words used here. A minion is one of the chip's small cores; it runs two harts (hardware threads). A shire is a group of 32 minions with its own L2 cache. The tile buffer (TenB) is the tensor unit's operand buffer, through which the GEMM streams B. The knee is the band of footprints just past the 32 MB L3 (33-70 MB), where some lines still come back from it. J over idle is what a GEMM adds to the card's idle draw; J with the card at rest is the card's whole draw over the GEMM, its rest plus what the GEMM adds. Each energy forecast is scored twice: given the measured seconds (the power model alone, on the card's own seconds) and with the seconds forecast (the model's own seconds and watts). On a governed card the governed base is the base power the governor's higher clock adds.

The meter. Every joule here is the card's own board-power reading, whose absolute scale is one fixed gain for every board, never set beside an outside meter (the lab's data READMEs, e.g. arm B's note). The joules compare models and cards with each other, not with another lab's meter.

1. The question

The owner's words that started the matmul model (Mon 16 Nov 2026, at or before 09:48:24 PST; a voice transcription, where "matte mold" is the matmul):

"I want you to focus on the matte mold most of all, so I want to have a model which predicts matte mold energy and number of transistor switches and clock cycles. From based on what I know about the size of a matrix and which operations are contained. See check how well the timing simulation for the matte mold matches reality and the energy."

So there are three asks: one model (sizes and operations in; cycles, joules and transistor switches out), a check of the timing simulation against the card, and a check of the energy against the card. The quote is as committed in tools/matmul/mm1/v0/README.md (2d1413f5).

2. What MM1 takes in and gives out, and how

The chip, in one paragraph. The ET-SoC-1 has 1,088 minions in 34 shires (32 compute shires, a master and a spare); the lab's kernels use 1,024 of them, in 32 shires of 32 (docs/et-soc1-notes.md; 05-claims.md). Each minion has a tensor unit that multiplies tiles of matrices. Each shire has its own L2 cache; the chip shares a 32 MB L3; behind them is DRAM. The lab's cards run at a fixed 600 MHz (aifoundry3 card 0 and aifoundry1 card 1 in the tests below) or under the card's own power governor (aifoundry2's card, about 800 MHz in E208's GEMMs).

The kernel. MM1 covers the lab's GEMM kernel, rgm136, on its grid (v0/README.md section 2): gemmL<MB>x<NB>x<KB> computes M = 1,024·MB, N = 512·NB, K = 512·KB·w (w = 1, 2, 4 for fp32, fp16, int8), and each minion does 64·MB·NB·KB tile ops a GEMM. One tile op is 4,096 multiply-adds in fp32, 8,192 in fp16 and 16,384 in int8 (desk: E206's registered points, points-e206.json, multiply-adds a GEMM over minions and ops). An off-grid size is padded to the next grid point; another kernel needs its own map of its tile order (E211 tested that, section 4).

What goes in and what comes out (MM1-v2, frozen in 3ca4b717, v2/README.md):

How, in four steps (figure 1; the equations are in v1/README.md section 2 and v2/README.md section 2):

  1. The memory path's line map. A tile op of one shire asks for 192 cache lines, 64 of A and 128 of B. A closed-form model of rgm136's tile order (its reuse distances) and a logistic L3 retention, r(d) = 1 / (1 + exp((d - x50) / s)) with x50 = 39.7 MB and s = 4.53 MB in MM1-v2, say how many of them the L2 serves, how many the L3, and how many come fresh from DRAM. No constant is fitted to a shape.
  2. Cycles. An op takes the slowest of three: the tensor unit's own op, A's tile stream and B's tile stream, each stream priced by where its lines come from. The tensor op's time is a law measured on the card (E119's: 527.48 cycles + 0.0176 µs in fp32 and fp16, and 283.03 cycles + 0.0146 µs in int8), never under the floor measured with B streamed through the tile buffer, 529 and 270 cycles (v0/README.md section 2). Seconds = ops a minion × the wall time of an op.
  3. Joules. Watts over idle = an awake power for the active minions + an awake power for the memory path + the governed base on a governed card + the op rate × (a price per op for the tensor work + a price per cache line by where it came from). The awake powers are constant watts; only the per-op and per-line prices scale with (V / 0.517 V)² (v2/README.md). The price per op is the energy ledger's frozen resident price for the format and data kind; MM1-v2's line prices are 58.9, 245.1 and 794.7 pJ a line (L2, L3, fresh) for zeros, plus 501.2, 1,150.2 and 1,298.6 pJ for random data. Joules = watts × seconds.
  4. Transistor switches. The lab's unit is the fin edge: "one transistor gate, one fin, charged or discharged by a net transition" (v0/README.md section 5), so a transistor with several fins counts once per fin. MM1 counts the tensor unit's fin edges on an open stand-in netlist of its public RTL (fp32 at gate level; fp16 scaled from it; int8 the vector unit only): an estimate for the silicon, not a count of its gates. It estimates every other class on the die (clock tree, memory path, issue, int8 arithmetic) from its share of the joules over idle, with the fresh-line energy set apart, in a low, a central and a high estimate (v1/README.md section 7). Every constant comes from the lab's public files. No card counter measures switches; section 5 tests the counted difference through energy.

Their own pools differ in size (769, 888 and 1,072 points), so MM1-v0's 5.54% (v0 section 4) does not compare with the others.

3. How well the timing simulation matches the card, and how well today's energy models did

The simulator is not a clock. The lab's instruction-level simulator steps every active hart through its instructions with no timing. At E206's and E207's points nobody ran it: the step counts there come from a law fitted to 11 simulator runs of rgm136 (post hoc, registered in E206 before its data: "a post hoc desk fit on 11 sys_emu runs of rgm136 ... the simulator has no timing, its steps are instructions", E206.md section 7, eb2f7ac2), 46.625 steps an op + 24.25 a C-tile row, with + 92 a launch in MM1's column (v1/README.md section 2). A step is close to, not exactly, an instruction: E211's kit found about 5 steps for each stack load (rgm136's loop runs 36 instructions an op and 47.742 steps; KIT.md). Over every blind point that has a step count, 265 points of E206's two arms and E207 (161 distinct configurations: arm B reran most of arm A's on another card), the card's cycles a GEMM were 8.2 to 33.9 times the steps, median 24.0 (quartiles 19.4 and 29.4; desk, from the frozen scorers' registered readings in 50476f31, f155c8ca and f5c53145). The medians describe this test design's mix of shapes, not GEMMs in general. The low end is int8 at one K block (gemmL1x1x1), the high end the long-K, DRAM-fed gemmL1x1x32. The step law has no term for the format or the data, while the card's cycles have both: gemmL1x1x1 on all 32 shires took 537.98 cycles an op in fp32 and 388.02 in int8 on aifoundry3 (E206 arm A's registered reading, measured, 50476f31). The simulator did see one thing the card then showed: rgm211's compiled loop takes 32.762 steps an op against rgm136's 47.742 (31 instructions against 36), and E211's controls, where only the compiled code differs, ran 3-6% fast on the card (section 4).

The RTL bench is exact for the tensor op with its operands resident. A register-transfer simulation of the tensor unit, with A and B already resident, plus one constant of 7.75 cycles set on the card in E109, gave the card's cycles an op in the three experiments that registered it before the card ran: 18 of 18 registered readings within 2 cycles (E129, E148 and E155, rule R6; 12 micro-loop configurations, all with zeros data on aifoundry1 card 1; for example 544.0 forecast against 544.001 measured in fp32, 318.0 against 318.001 in int8, 62.0 against 62.0 for an op with one row of A; measured). A GEMM is not that loop. It streams B through the tile buffer, and its op runs at E119's law: 527.48 cycles + 0.0176 µs is 538 cycles in fp32 at 600 MHz, and the card measured 537.98 at gemmL1x1x1 above. So four numbers describe the fp32 tensor op: 546 (E101's resident loop, the RTL bench's reach, which E206 registered as its floor) and 544 (the bench's m0 loop); 538 (E119's law with B streamed, at 600 MHz); and 529, the floor MM1 never goes under, a post hoc closed form read from E101's loop with B through the tile buffer (measured on the card, not the RTL bench; v0/README.md section 6). In int8 the same roles are taken by 318, 292 and 270.

MM1 reads the gap as the memory path. Over the floor with B through the tile buffer (529 or 270 cycles), the tensor unit's own op filled 17 to 99% of each op on the card, median 39%, over 285 blind points (desk, from the same committed points). Over E206's registered floor (546 or 318 cycles, the scorers' registered readings) it filled 21 to 101%, median 40%, over 265 points; 12 of them ran faster than that resident floor. Near 99% sit the ops whose tiles stay in the L2; the lowest are int8 GEMMs with a long K, fed from DRAM. No simulator the lab has models the memory path (a counted simulator run "gives instructions and bytes, never the level that served a line", v0/README.md section 3); that is the part MM1's line map and stream terms fit. It is MM1's reading, not a measurement of the memory path: the code path moves the cycles too (E211's controls, above).

Today's energy models, before MM1 (blind, section 4's table). F1, the lab's earlier GEMM route (E190's, widened: seconds from a line-cost law at the point's lines, watts from E136's power law), read joules over idle 18.4 to 31.0% rms at the points of 3 W and above. Its miss is a scale, not a shape: about a quarter low in every family on E207 (v2/score-e207.md section 6, 030ffa42), and it reads a shire subset's lines from E206's masked map, which lost to MM1-v1's full-chip lines on every fold (v1/README.md section 4.1). F2, the lab's energy ledger (ledger v3's tensor terms with F1's seconds), read 52.7 to 82.0% rms given the measured seconds, on E206's arms and E207 (the tests that give F2 that figure), and on E207 its error grew with the lines fetched from DRAM: "+94% to +160% at the long-K points ... against -19% to -42% at one K block"; part of that growth is its byte convention, which counts every shire's request (same section). With the card at rest, where the idle draw dominates, the same models read 6.5 to 10.1% (F1) and 16.4 to 18.3% (F2).

5. The switch count, through energy (E210)

The question: MM1 reports switches; nothing on a card counts them. At one GEMM shape the kernel runs the same instructions on the same lines in the same cycles whatever the data, so the energy difference between an operand pattern and random data is data switching. E210 ran nine fp32 operand patterns never run in a GEMM, at two shapes (gemmL1x1x1 in the L2 and gemmL1x1x4 inside the L3) on aifoundry3 card 0, each in chained blocks against random, and asked whether the difference follows MM1's counted switches better than four other routes (E210.md, 7b59f719; data 6689d68f; scores d203e577). The switch route (R1) is MM1-v1's counted switches times one constant per shape, calibrated on committed ungated kinds (ones and real tiles: every lane valid, so the clock part of the count is random's and cancels), plus MM1-v1's line term. The registered result: R0 PASS; the time equality PASS in all 20 cases; B5 (R1 within 5% of D, the data-dependent power, random minus zeros: 29.78 W and 20.53 W) PASS 13 of 13; R1 BETTER than the nearest measured kind (H1, 10 to 1), the raw bit-flip price (H2, 12 to 0) and the bit-flip price with one scale (H3, 10 to 0); R1 against MM1-v1's kind term UNDECIDED (4 to 0 with 1 tie; 5 cases, too few to decide, as registered). REPORTED: R1's error is 0.481 W rms, 1.87% of D, against 3.849 W (16.19%) for the scaled bit flips, 5.128 W (19.71%) for the nearest kind, 5.575 W (21.22%, 5 cases) for MM1-v1's kind term and 12.165 W (47.48%) for the raw bit flips; the card's own constant, fitted post hoc, sits within 0.8% of the calibrated one (S1 1.6767 × 10^16 switches a J against 1.6733 × 10^16; S2 1.6235 against 1.6367 × 10^16). With the pattern as the unit (REPORTED), H1 is UNDECIDED (6 to 1, 1 tie).

  • The run was cut, as registered: 142 of 171 runs, when the card-time rule stopped new runs at 85.01 card-minutes (the die gate made each run about 35 s, not the planned 26); the registered subset (the opening and passes 1-4) is complete, and 9 of the 13 test cases are scored on 2 of 3 blocks.
  • What it tests, and what it does not: the signal-net part of the count, not its clock part (the clock part cancels in every scored difference); fp32, one card, two shapes; ungated patterns only. Switches themselves are never measured.
  • The known miss it was built around: MM1's fin-edge count misprices register clocking: committed data put ones at about 0.31 of D where the count says about 0.05 (E210.md section 1). That is why R1 is calibrated on ungated kinds, and why its success says nothing yet about the clock nets or about gated data (zeros, sparse tiles).

6. What is still wrong, and the limits of the claim

The scope limits (each stated in the registrations before the data):

  • No operations input. MM1 models one kernel, rgm136, on its grid (M a multiple of 1,024, N of 512, K of a block), with no epilogue: no bias, activation or requantization. "From the sizes and operations" holds for that kernel's tile order only; E211 shows a recompiled kernel's cycles drift 8-9% even with its tile order mapped.
  • Warm, repeated GEMMs. Every measured GEMM repeats the same A and B, so an L3-sized footprint is served from the L3 after the first pass; sizes below the grid come only from shire subsets.
  • A GEMM anchor on every card. The awake power does not carry across cards: held out a card at a time, MM1-v2's energy reads 10.13% against 9.15% for MM1-v1's form (v2/README.md section 0), and MM1-v1's 14.2-42.1% (v1/README.md section 5).
  • An unseen tile kind's energy only from a borrowed price. MM1-v2 prices random, zeros, ones and the real tiles it was fitted on; it prices no tile kind that lacks a resident price on that card: E207's real and designed tiles b49-b52 were NOT FORECAST in energy by MM1-v2 (v2/score-e207.md section 0), and on E209 its desk transfer of real tiles from another card missed the int8 saving by 6 points. MM1-v1, the registered model on E207, took those four from committed per-tile frozen predictions ("blind4: M6; blind3: M5", v1/README.md section 2) and passed: blind, its joules over idle given the measured seconds read -5.4%, -6.0%, +4.2% and +7.1% at b49-b52, with E1 and E2 PASS at all four (f5c53145).
  • Switches are estimates. Counted on a stand-in netlist for the tensor unit, estimated for the rest; E210 checked the counted signal-net difference through energy on one card in fp32.

The misses the blind days named (post hoc readings of blind data):

  • All-zero data on card 1. On arm B's 14 all-zero points MM1-v1 read joules over idle 27.8% rms (+25.7% bias) and MM1-v0 33.7% (+31.4%), where F1 read 13.5% and was closer at 13 of the 14 (f155c8ca). MM1-v1's card-1 awake power came from one shape; on E208's card-1 zeros it read watts over idle +21.2% (42 runs, unread, v2/README.md section 1). MM1-v2 refitted card 1's awake power on E208 (2.406 W at 1,024 minions against MM1-v1's 3.257 W); no blind test of MM1-v2 on card 1 has run.
  • Narrow shire masks. On arm B the subset points of 3 W and above read 7.0% for MM1-v1; the 7 under 3 W (1, 4 or 8 shires, 0.31 to 2.78 W over idle) read -0.05 to +0.39 W off, MM1-v1 high at 6 of them, and its four one-shire points sit +0.18 to +0.22 W high, which a relative error shows as +62% and +43% at the two smallest (f155c8ca). On E207, MM1-v1's two 1x2x2 subset cases read -14.0% (mask 0x3) and -10.2% (0xfff) in wall cycles; MM1-v2's contention on the DRAM stream alone moved them to -0.5% and -0.9%, and its worst subset case on E207 was 3x6x4 fp32 on 12 shires (0xfff), +13.3%: on that mask the shape ran faster than on 2 or on 24 shires, which no model forecast (v2/score-e207.md section 6, 030ffa42). On E209 the one shire 0x80000000 ran 24% over MM1-v2 (above), while MM1-v2 was closer than MM1-v1 at 6 of the 9 subset points.
  • The stride step and one K block. E209's step at NB 8 measured 1.297 against MM1-v2's 1.187; int8 at one K block ran 1.158 times MM1-v2's wall cycles on E209 (e07) and -14.0% on E207 (n01); joules over idle at one K block read -18.4% (E207's n02, int8) and -13.6% (n03, fp32); int8 inside the L3 ran 6.3 to 6.5% more cycles than fp32 at the same shapes on E207 (wall cycles at its four such shapes; f5c53145), which both MM1 versions forecast equal.
  • The kernel variant's code path (E211, above): rgm211's controls ran 3-6% fast, and MM1's cycles error there was 8-9%.
  • The governor's clock: K1's one miss (e44, 788.9 MHz measured against 764.1 forecast) and two of 55 runs whose capped state MM1-v2 read wrong (note 15).

The lab's residual analysis of every blind and unread miss (a514caf0, post hoc) names candidate terms for each of these.
