Power and temperature on the ET-SoC-1 card

20 September 2026 · aifoundry2's card · firmware source read at et-platform 353f20e (the cards' own log strings point to an older build; see §6) · tools/ettelem, a telemetry client on the management library · part of the ET-SoC-1 measurement reports

The card has one power sensor path: a PMIC that reports 12 V board power and three of its rails (minion cores, SRAM, mesh), polled by the service processor every 133 ms. Everything else is voltage or temperature. This report lists every way to read them, what each resolves, and what a small client on the management library adds over the stock tool: the per-rail snapshot the CLI refuses, up to 45 samples a second, and a 34-shire on-die voltage map. Then what the numbers show. The main surprise is temperature: under load, the same work costs about 0.8 W more for every °C the die warms, the die idles at 72 °C, and idle power after a load is 5 W higher than before it, so every energy comparison on this card has to control for heat.

Later measurements. This was the first look at the meters. Later reports refine three things.
  • Leakage. Idle power is now a fitted law, Pidle = 12.6 W + 23.3 W·e(T−80)/36, with T the die temperature in °C (The DVFS loop and its leakage; the energy manual, §1). Its slope is 0.52 W/°C at 72 °C, 0.65 at 80 °C and 0.78 at 87 °C, and it reproduces this page's two idle readings to 0.1 W. Busy power climbs faster per degree of the die reading: about 0.8 W/°C in this page's matmul, and 0.81 W/°C for the drift within the Horace experiment's hot 7 s runs (80–86 °C).
  • The rail readings are the PMIC's own running average, which the service processor copies every 133 ms pass. It behaves like a first-order filter with τ ≈ 1 s. This page first called it a 2 s moving average (Limits of observability, §4.1).
  • The unmetered remainder. What a workload adds to the remainder in §2 is now attributed by regression: about a fifth of the minion rail's power is lost in delivery, plus a cost per DRAM byte. The 15 W at idle is still not split (Limits of observability, §4.2). The hub's improvement ladder supersedes §5.

Terms. The ET-SoC-1's compute cores are minions: small in-order RISC-V cores, each with two hardware threads (harts). Thirty-two minions form a shire, the chip's tile. Of the 34 minion shires, 32 run kernels (1,024 minions), shire 32 is the master shire, which runs firmware, and shire 33 is a spare. Memory shires hold the DRAM controllers, and the shires talk over a mesh network-on-chip (the NoC, "mesh" on this page). The four Maxions are larger out-of-order RISC-V cores on the same die. The service processor (SP) is the on-die management core: it reads the sensors, runs the clock and voltage governor and answers the host's management commands. Its SPST trace is a log of its stats, one record per pass; the MMST trace is the master shire's log of bandwidth and utilisation. The PMIC is the board's power-management controller, which meters the 12 V input and three regulators (the minion, SRAM and mesh rails). Moortec PVT (process, voltage and temperature) monitors, read by five PVT controllers, measure temperature and voltage on the die. MDI, the Minion Debug Interface, reads the registers and CSRs (control and status registers) of a halted hart. More in the hub's glossary.

2. A load step, seen through every sensor

Twenty seconds idle, 58 s of fp32 matmul on all 1,024 minions, 40 s idle, 34 s of DRAM-bound loads, idle. Sampled at 10 Hz with ettelem; every load process held the card for under 10 s.

Power (W): board total and the three measured rails
Die temperature (°C, mean of the 34 minion-shire sensors)

  • Power climbs while the work stays constant. The matmul draws 56 W two seconds after it starts and 65 W at its end, 56 s later, while the die warms from about 75 to 87 °C. Same kernel, same clock, same rate: the extra 9 W is leakage. A straight line through board power against die temperature, from 5 s after launch to the end, has a slope of W/°C, W/°C of it on the minion rail. The first, uncontrolled session of the Horace experiment (20 September, 22 runs at 79–90 °C, since superseded) fitted nearly the same: 0.78 W/°C on the board and 0.38 on the minion rail.
  • Idle is not one number. Before the load: 31.2 W at 72 °C. Forty seconds after it: 36.5 W at 81 °C. Both sit on the idle law measured later (31.3 and 36.5 W at those temperatures). The die sheds heat slowly (6 °C in 40 s), so a baseline taken before a run understates the idle power during and after it. Energy measurements here should take their baseline at the same temperature, or fit a temperature term.
  • The three rails are not the whole card. Board minus rails is 15.0 W at idle, 20–22 W under matmul and 21 W under DRAM load. That remainder is the DDR and PCIe rails, the Maxions, the IO shire, and regulator losses, none of which has a sensor. A DRAM-bound load moves it by 5 W and the minion rail by 0.3 W; a matmul moves the minion rail by 16–20 W.
  • Why the remainder sometimes looks too small, or the rails seem to exceed the step. Board power is a fresh reading every 133 ms. The rail figures are the PMIC's own running averages, which the service processor copies each pass (read later from the firmware: Limits of observability, §4.1). They behave like a first-order filter with τ ≈ 1 s: a step reaches 61% after 1 s and 88% after 2 s. So when the matmul starts, the remainder jumps to 39 W and decays to about 22 W within 2–3 s as the rails catch up. When it stops, the remainder goes briefly negative (−3.5 W: the rails' averages still include the load) and climbs back through 14 W over about 3 s. In steady state the board reading and the PMIC's own board average agree to about 0.5 W. The same averaging, and how the trace was lined up in time, account for a gap in the memory-anatomy report: there the rise above idle in the service processor's trace (the PMIC's board average) is 14–20% smaller than in the host log. Mid-run the two agree to within 0.3 W. As aligned, the trace starts to rise about 0.7 s before each load process starts and to fall before the run ends, so its baseline reads high and its run average low.
  • Voltage barely droops at the die. The minion rail's on-die monitor reads 518 mV idle and 517 mV with 18 W more load (about 35 A more at 0.52 V): the regulator senses at the die. The DDR domain reads 768 mV on die against an 800 mV set-point, and sags 3 mV more under DRAM load.
  • The clock stayed at 600 MHz because the die was hot. The power-management governor is on, with a 65 W power limit and a 65 °C software temperature threshold. The thermal branch wins: above 65 °C it holds the lowest operating point, 600 MHz at 0.52 V, and every run in this report had the die above 70 °C. That is why the clock did not move even at 65 W here and 70 W in the first Horace session. From a cool die (62 °C after a night idle) the governor raises the same kernel from 600 to 800 MHz and 0.62 V within 0.4–1 s of launch, and steps it down as the die passes 65 °C: see §6 of the Horace experiment, and the DVFS report, which reads the governor's source and classifies every clock change.

3. The per-shire voltage map

With its log level raised to DEBUG, the service processor writes one line per shire per pass with the on-die voltage of each rail and the hardware's low/high capture. ettelem loglevel debug then ettelem sptrace reads them; the level is restored afterwards. This is the finest spatial view of the power system the card gives: below is the minion-rail voltage of all 34 minion shires at idle, with each shire's lowest captured value. The 32 compute shires sit where marty1885's logical shire map (etTopoScan) puts them on the mesh, as in the on-chip communication report. The four dashed cells hold the master, spare, I/O and PCIe shires in an order not measured here, so shires 32 (the master) and 33 (the spare) are drawn below the grid.

Stronger colour = higher voltage, from 516 mV (faintest) to 521 mV. Hover a shire for its SRAM and mesh rails. Current values span 517–521 mV across the die; the lowest captured minimum is 513 mV (shires 0–2 and 18). The low/high registers hold extremes since the last stats reset, so they catch droops that the 133 ms polling misses. Per-shire temperature is sampled by the same controllers but never logged; that needs firmware (the spatial temperature brief sets out the options).

4. What a debug-interface client would and would not add

The Minion Debug Interface reaches minion state only: registers, CSRs, memory of a halted hart. It cannot read the PMIC, the on-die sensors or anything in the service processor's address space, so it adds no power or temperature sensor. Its value for energy work is indirect: it can write the counter event selectors without a reflash, and chosen event counts (L1 misses, tensor ops, cache hits per level) are what turn rail power into energy per event by regression. The telemetry side of the management library, which this report uses, was the larger gap, and it needs no halting.

6. Reproduce

cmake -S tools/ettelem -B build/ettelem -DCMAKE_PREFIX_PATH=/opt/et -Wno-dev && cmake --build build/ettelem
export LD_LIBRARY_PATH=/opt/et/lib
build/ettelem/ettelem sample --seconds 10 --every-ms 100      # JSON lines; quit et-powertop first (one opener)
tools/ettelem/run_thermal.sh build/thermal1                   # the load step, about 3 minutes
build/ettelem/ettelem loglevel debug; build/ettelem/ettelem sptrace sp.bin; build/ettelem/ettelem loglevel info
strings -n 8 sp.bin | grep 'Voltage'

Firmware. What this page says about the service processor's code (the 133 ms pass and its 1 ms I2C waits, what each management command returns, how the governor chooses a clock) was read from et-platform at commit 353f20e. Both cards report firmware release 1.3.1, and aifoundry3's trace holds log lines that exist only in et-platform before commit 60b40c10f (24 September 2024), which rewrote the governor. So aifoundry3, and very likely this card with the same release, runs an older build than the source read here, and details of the code may differ. The measurements do not depend on it.
