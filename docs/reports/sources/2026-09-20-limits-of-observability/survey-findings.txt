
=====  =====
SUMMARY: The open RTL in external/core-et (erbium branch, one commit "Initial drop of the Erbium original RTL", Apache-2.0, 115,774 lines of Verilog in 433 files / 453 modules) is the CPU subsystem only: one neighbourhood of 8 dual-thread minions (frontend, intpipe, 4 KB dcache, 8-lane VPU with TXFMA/TIMA/transcendental units), the shared L1 I-cache, PLIC/CLINT/FLB/FCC and an ET-Link-to-AXI bridge. Everything the lab card has beyond that is absent: the shire cache (L2/L3/scratchpad), memshire/DDR controller/PHY, mesh NoC, PCIe, Maxions, service processor and IO shire survive only as ESR register slaves, include files and DV stubs (dv/ip_stub), and all SRAMs/register files/PLLs are behavioural models, so there is no gate-level or hard-macro view. Within that scope the answer to "can I track individual bits flipping" is literally yes: the standalone bench dumps every net, flop and behavioural SRAM bit to an FST/VPD waveform at 1-cycle (1 ns at the 1 GHz tb clock) resolution, toggle coverage per signal is a build flag (VCS -cm tgl, Verilator --coverage), and an architectural monitor probes ~380 internal signals per minion to check every retired instruction in lockstep against sw-sysemu (the same sys_emu the user runs) on PC, instruction bits, integer/FP/mask register writes, memory writes and trap state. The hard limits are practical: the Verilator make fragment the Makefile includes (dv/standalone/common/verilator.mk) is missing from the drop, Verilator is not installed on this machine, the primary flow is Synopsys VCS/Verdi with UltraSoC and Zebu pieces that are not shipped, no SAIF/power flow or standard-cell/liberty data exists, and the repo records no measured simulation rate (expect order 10^3-10^4 cycles/s single-threaded Verilator for 8 minions, orders of magnitude slower than the card's 600 MHz, so seconds of silicon time are infeasible). The origin/main branch (ETASP) is a Verilator-first, coverage-enabled re-implementation that already runs the translated minion_top under Verilator with 235k cycle-by-cycle comparisons against the original, and is the fastest path to an open, working bit-level simulation of the core. On silicon, the RTL shows the machinery that would allow a snapshot of every flop (full scan chains and a DFT scan-dump mode) and a per-neighbourhood UltraSoC status monitor / instruction-trace encoder, but none of it is reachable from a user account and continuous per-cycle bit tracking of the fabricated chip is impossible. Micro-architecture documentation beyond the PRM is substantial: 13 PDFs (541 pages: minion, frontend/intpipe stages and stall conditions, dcache pipeline/miss handler/FSMs, 99-page VPU spec with per-stage TXFMA pipeline and instruction latencies, neighbourhood MAS with PMU implementation, 131-page shire-cache spec, ET-Link protocol, cooperative tensor load), a 4,103-line generated Erbium ESR register map, and the PMU event encodings and CSR/ESR generator CSVs in the RTL itself.

SUMMARY: The power/thermal layer of the ET-SoC-1 dev card is three PMBus rails (VDD_MNN, VDD_NOC, VDD_SRAM: 1 mW LSB, avg/min/max only) plus board input power (10 mW LSB), all funnelled through a PMIC MCU whose firmware is closed, and read by the service processor once per loop pass, which is measured at 133 ms, not the 10 ms delay constant suggests (the ~100 I2C transactions per pass cost ~1.2 ms each). On-die there are 36 temperature sensors, 36 ring-oscillator process detectors and 128 voltage-sense points (12-bit/0.06 °C and 14-bit/89 µV), one TS+PD+3 VM channels per minion shire, but they are SP-only, the host gets 34-shire averages truncated to 1 °C/1 mV, and none measures current. The finest energy attribution demonstrated on the card is pJ-per-operation-class by regression (e.g. 46 pJ per L1 load, 3.8 vs 0.5 pJ per active vs zero-gated FMA slot), which needs ≥1e9–1e10 identical events per second across 1,024 minions for a few seconds; one record's LSB is 133 µJ on a rail and 1.3 mJ on the board, about 12 orders of magnitude above a single ~0.1 fJ bit flip, so individual toggles are impossible to see on silicon through power. The only switching-activity proxy on-chip is the shire-cache perfmon cycle counter, which (erratum 4.3) stops while a bank's clock is gated and therefore reports per-bank clock-active duty. Firmware-only changes would give: instantaneous per-rail and board power in the SP stats trace (the values are already read and discarded), a ~10-50x faster trace loop by dropping the 84-read PMB snapshot, per-shire PVT maps, and per-shire frequency via each shire's own LVDPLL, which would isolate one shire's dynamic power on the single minion rail. Externally, all card power enters through one 1 mΩ shunt on the 12 V edge feed (or ATX AUX via jumper), whose LTC4218 IMON pin gives 82.7 mV/A and can be scoped, but the IMON RC network (12.4 kΩ, ~0.1 µF as read from the schematic image) and the input bulk capacitors limit it to roughly 100 Hz–kHz and to board total. The per-minion-group voltage registers exist in the PMIC map but the PCIe V3 card has a single 3-phase minion regulator, so they cannot map to shires. Bit-level toggles exist only in RTL simulation (Erbium cpu_subsystem FST dump), and sys_emu models neither power nor real PVT values.

SUMMARY: This layer is the software-visible surface: what a U-mode kernel on the lab card (et-platform 353f20e) can read, what the open toolchain decodes, and what the open firmware/RTL let you add. Everything an instruction does is documented at the bit level: PRM Table 2-2 gives the 32-bit encodings of every Esperanto extension, the assembler/objdump in /opt/et name every custom CSR (evict_va, tensor_fma, tensor_wait, hartid, flb), and the kernel is a plain relocatable ELF, so there is no SASS-style hidden layer; the RTL csr.csv lists about 38 implemented custom CSRs, of which at least 8 (stall, hartid shadow, matp, menable_shadows, mbusaddr/mbuspc, dcache_debug, ddata0) are absent from the PRM. The finest timing instrument available today from U-mode is hpmcounter3 at 1-cycle resolution (with the bit-7 carry bug fixed in software, ~10 cycles per read pair), plus five fixed neighbourhood-summed event counters that reset per launch and a software trace buffer stamped with the same counter; the event selectors, the one-per-thread hardware trigger, the instruction-pattern trap (minstmask/minstmatch), excl_mode, and the debug CSRs are all M-mode, so any per-instruction or per-hart-isolated view on silicon needs a few lines of firmware the card would have to be flashed with. Per-instruction architectural state (every register bit written, all 8 VPU lanes, every memory byte read or written, every CSR write) is available today in sys_emu with -l/-lm/-lt/-ltrigger_insn, but without cycle timing because the emulator is functional only. The literal answer to "can I watch individual bits flip" is: yes in RTL simulation (the Verilator standalone testbench dumps every signal every clock to trace.fst with +dump=1, and DPI monitors export per-retire cycle/pc/instruction/writeback/store events), but that flow covers the Erbium single-neighbourhood CPU subsystem, not the 34-shire ET-SoC-1, and has not been built here. On silicon the hard limit is that microarchitectural state is reachable only through the neighbourhood PMU (no overflow interrupt, no sampling), a 3-4-bit-per-submodule dcache_debug CSR, and the JTAG debug module (single-step and program-buffer injection, with errata), all of which observe architectural state at instruction granularity, never gate-level bits; physical bit flips are impossible to observe from software on the fabricated chip.

SUMMARY: On silicon, the hardware-counter layer of the ET-SoC-1 is three families of event counters plus a software-driven trace sink, and none of them observes state: they count events (up to 8 per cycle per neighbourhood PMU, 1 per cycle per shire-cache bank or memshire monitor) and a U-mode kernel can read cycle-stamped 64-bit values at 1-minion-cycle resolution. The finest thing a user-mode kernel can log today is one 64-bit hpmcounter3 timestamp per software event (about 10 cycles per raw read pair, roughly 20-40 cycles per 32-64 B trace entry, estimate not yet measured), at a DVFS-varying clock with no U-mode wall clock, and subject to the known bit-7 carry bug. The RTL confirms the PRM's minion and neighbourhood event tables bit for bit (events = {vpu[10:0], dcache[11:0], branch[1:0], instret[1:0], cycle, 0}), and the Erbium RTL exposes four extra TBOX neighbourhood events (19, 20, 24, 25) the ET-SoC-1 PRM lists as reserved. The shire-cache perfmon's resource modes (queue occupancy, outstanding to_l3/to_sys requests, coalescing buffers, with acc/cnt/max over a min/max window) and the memshire monitor's five qualifier formats (MCOP with activate/precharge/refresh per bank, MCDATA, MCMISC hazards, address-filtered MESHCNT, MESHOUT) are all in core-et defines, but every one of them is an M-mode ESR: at firmware 353f20e a kernel can only sample the boot-time defaults through syscalls 9 and 10, and the firmware itself (statw) stops and restarts all 132 bank counters and 8 memshire counters every 1 ms. Event selection needs about a dozen lines across four firmware files, but the images live in SPI flash, so it means a flash update through dev_mngt_service and a card reset, not a runtime change; the MDI host debug path cannot touch PMU CSRs either, and the SP's stall/latency/utilisation commands are stubs returning 0. The profiler_converter in /opt/et/bin converts easy_profiler .prof files from the host runtime to JSON and never sees device data; a device-side flame graph would need a small converter from et-trace user_profile_event entries plus addr2line. sys_emu models only CYCLES and RETIRED_INST, so counter experiments cannot be rehearsed offline. The hard limit of this layer: no counter, trace buffer or debug command on silicon returns the value of an arbitrary flop or SRAM bit; the closest things are the memshire status monitor's 200 selectable debug signals over the JTAG/USB debug network (undocumented tooling, SP-only registers) and RTL simulation, so tracking individual bit flips is not possible from the card, and observing them at all belongs to the simulation layer.

=====  =====
SUMMARY: On an A100/H100 the observable surface is an API surface, not a hardware surface: PTX is a documented virtual ISA, SASS is published only as opcode-name tables, there is no RTL, no microarchitecture manual, the GSP firmware is a signed binary, and the L2-slice hash and HBM/GDDR bank-row mapping are proprietary. What a user can see today is aggregate: Nsight/CUPTI hardware counters rolled up per SM, per SMSP, per L2 slice and per FBPA (with per-instance values on request), a per-SM statistical PC sampler at 2^5..2^31 cycles, PM-sampling timelines, a per-SM cycle counter (clock64) and a "nanosecond" %globaltimer that in practice steps by 32 ns and updates about every microsecond, one board/module power number that on A100/H100 is a 25 ms average refreshed every 100 ms (plus a mJ energy counter, which the ET card lacks), and ECC/row-remap counts with no syndrome, bank, row or bit position. The finest GPU granularity is therefore one SASS instruction: cuda-gdb single-steps a warp and reads $R/$P registers, and NVBit/CUPTI SASS patching can trace every dynamic instruction, but device watchpoints are unsupported and there is no trace port. Everything below the architectural state (register-file, cache and DRAM cell bits, control logic) is invisible: microbenchmark papers (Jia 2018, Ampere 2022, Hopper 2024, GPUHammer 2025, L40 2026) recover latencies, encodings, control bits and bank/row classes by timing, Accel-Sim reproduces behaviour with a tuned model, and NVBitFI can only corrupt destination registers. The hard limit on NVIDIA silicon is that bit flips are observed only by reading memory back or by counting ECC events. The open ET-SoC-1 design removes exactly that ceiling: 115,774 lines of RTL with a Verilator/VCS testbench that dumps every signal every cycle ($dumpvars into trace.fst), full firmware source with 11 documented U-mode syscalls and a DM firmware-update command, and a functional simulator with per-hart instruction logging and a GDB stub; the caveats on the ET side are that the Verilator include file referenced by the standalone Makefile is missing from the drop, and BL1/BL2 authenticate firmware against an OTP public-key hash, so loading rebuilt firmware depends on the signing chain (to be settled by the firmware layer).

SUMMARY: On-silicon debug goes through one door: the service processor (SP). The 1088 minions carry an Esperanto-specific debug module (not a RISC-V DTM/DMI): a chip-wide DMCTRL mirror in SPIO-MISC, per-neighborhood HACTRL/HASTATUS0/1 ESRs and per-hart ABSCMD/NXDATA0/1 registers in the debug-only (PP=10) ESR space that only the SP in M-mode may touch. A halted hart executes two-instruction pairs from ABSCMD and hands data back through the ddata0 CSR (0x7b8); the lab card's BL2 firmware (et-platform 353f20e, unchanged at HEAD) wraps this as DM commands 128-144 (MDI) that any user can send over /dev/etN_mgmt (mode 0666) with a ~100-line libDM client, since dev_mngt_service rejects them as "currently unsupported". Today that yields: non-intrusive per-hart halted/running/exception status; halt at neighborhood granularity (16 harts, chip-wide haltreq); GPRs x1-x31, dpc and seven other CSRs; any CSR write (raw address, so dcsr.step, tdata1/2 and mhpmevent are reachable); one PC-or-address hardware trigger per hart with an async breakpoint event; and 8-byte memory reads restricted to host DRAM, U-mode stacks and trace buffers (DRAM reads are done by the SP's own memcpy, so they do not see dirty minion L1/L2 lines). Finest granularity on silicon is therefore one architectural snapshot per DM round trip (milliseconds) of a halted hart, and, by writing dcsr.step and resuming, one instruction at a time on hart 0/1 of a neighborhood (errata 1.18 makes other harts unsafe to resume alone); PMU counters and 256-bit vector registers need a one-line firmware change and a reflash. The hard limits: no gdb server or OpenOCD support for silicon exists in the open tree (the JTAG/USB debug port speaks UltraSoC, and the internal gdb-over-MDI debug_server was never released); the UltraSoC fabric that the RTL wires up (a per-minion instruction trace encoder with retired instruction + PC every cycle, per-neighborhood status monitors with 200 filter/64 match/128 data signals and 3 counters, shire bus monitors, memshire status monitors, debug-APB reads of L1 cache SRAMs) is unreachable without the unpublished message-engine protocol and Data Book, is never configured by firmware, and the SP clock-gates the debug logic at the end of boot. Halting a hart mid-kernel wedges the kernel (RESET_HART is unimplemented, the firmware abort cannot recover it), so on the shared card MDI must be used on idle shires or with a reset plan. Watching individual bits flip is possible only off-silicon: sys_emu gives per-instruction traces and a full gdb stub (with 256-bit vector registers) but no cycles, and the core-et Verilator flow can dump every flop of one Erbium neighborhood per cycle.

SUMMARY: The literal question has a clean answer per route. On the lab card, nothing observes an individual bit flipping in real time; the only bit-level detectors are ECC checkers, and they exist on exactly three arrays: the shire-cache SRAM (L2/L3/scratchpad: tag-state, tag RAM and data RAM, SECDED with an 8-bit error_bits field and a 32-bit index in the per-bank error log), the neighbourhood I-cache (8 ECC bits per 64-bit dword, set/way logged), and the PCIe controller's internal RAMs (RASDP counters, never read by firmware). The L1 data cache, L1 scratchpad, GPRs and the 256-bit VPU register file have no ECC and no parity, so a flip there is invisible to hardware. DRAM ECC is compiled off in the service-processor bootloader (ecc=false, ECCCFG0 ecc_mode=0), so DM_CMD_GET_MODULE_DDR_ECC_UECC and DM_CMD_GET_MAX_MEMORY_ERROR can only ever return 0 on this card; what does work today is the SRAM/I-cache path: SP interrupt -> CE/UCE event with the raw error-log ESRs as syndrome -> driver dmesg line and /sys/bus/pci/devices/<BDF>/err_stats/{ce_count,uce_count}, plus SP software counters via dev_mngt_service -m DM_CMD_GET_MODULE_SRAM_ECC_UECC. The SET_*_ECC_COUNT commands set the correctable-error report threshold (default 32), not a count, and DM_CMD_GET_DEVICE_ERROR_EVENTS is a no-op outside a TEST_EVENT_GEN build. The finest architectural read on silicon is the SP-owned Minion Debug Interface (DM commands 128-144): it halts harts and injects two-instruction abstract commands through per-hart ABSCMD/NXDATA ESRs at the SP-only privilege, returning any GPR, CSR or 8-byte memory word per round trip; it is in the lab firmware but needs a small libDM client, halts the harts, and has known data-breakpoint errata. Raw SRAM rows including their ECC check bits can be read and written (fault-injected) through the M-mode sc_idx_cop_sm Dbg_Read/Dbg_Write state machine, which no firmware exposes to U-mode. The UltraSoC debug fabric (JTAG/USB2, memshire status monitor with 200 signals, shire-cache address trace) and the DFT scan chains are the only paths to signal- or flop-level state on silicon, and both need external hardware plus proprietary tooling that is not in the repo. In simulation the answer is yes: sys_emu -l prints, for every instruction on every selected hart, the fetched PC and encoding, the disassembly, and every architectural write (x-regs, 8-lane f-regs, TenC, fflags, memory reads/writes up to 512 bits, ESR writes, traps), so successive lines give the exact bits that changed; it is functional, not cycle-accurate, and models no cache tags. RTL simulation of the core-et drop gives every flop and net every cycle as an FST/VPD waveform ($dumpvars in the standalone testbench), which is the true bit-flip record and the source for toggle counts and SAIF-based power, but the drop covers the minion/neighbourhood/Erbium subsystem only (no shire cache, NoC, memshire, PCIe or SP RTL) and its Verilator makefile include is missing. Esperanto's ZeBu emulation flow is visible only as ifdefs, a ZeBu AXI memory model, hex-image helpers and changelog entries; no emulator or full-chip RTL is available here.
