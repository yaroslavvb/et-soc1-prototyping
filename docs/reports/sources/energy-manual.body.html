The ET-SoC-1 energy manual

Two ET-SoC-1 cards, 18–23 September 2026 · third edition, with confidence bars · every number from a measurement on the card, every table traceable to its data file · docs/energy-manual/ is the same manual as markdown · what the instruments can and cannot see: the limits of observability

A workload's energy on this card is the time it takes, times what the card draws doing nothing at the temperature the workload leaves it at, plus a sum over everything it does of a per-event cost. This page is the table of per-event costs, and the two things it needs alongside them. The card at rest is 12.6 W plus leakage that doubles every 25 °C. An awake minion is 2 mW. An integer add is 6 pJ, a float add 23, an eight-lane multiply-add 27 on zeros and 56 on random data. A byte from the shire's own scratchpad is 2–8 pJ; from DRAM, 90–140; written back through the L1 to DRAM, 250–350. A second card gives the same numbers to 5%. Every entry now carries a confidence bar: the mean over every pass on every card, and in brackets the range those passes spanned — three shuffled passes on each of two cards for the instructions and bytes, repeated warm passes on both cards for the relay, the hot line, the rings and the levels.

9. Method, and what the manual cannot tell you

  • Board power is the PMIC's reading, 10 mW resolution, refreshed every 133 ms, sampled at 10 Hz. Each measurement is a burst of back-to-back launches with six seconds of idle either side; its idle is the mean of the two bracketing stretches, and the extra leakage of a burst that runs slightly warmer than its brackets is taken out with the section 1 slope — a correction of . Rates come from the device's cycle counter, not the wall clock.
  • "Per instruction" includes the awake core that issued it (about 7 pJ per slot with both harts). Only the tensor unit is resolved below the instruction, and a "flip" there is an event in a simulation of the design, not a transistor.
  • Everything is at 600 MHz and 0.517 V. fdiv.ps and fsqrt.ps trap.
  • The unsensed 15 W — the largest single component of idle — cannot be split with any instrument here. What could be done about that, and every other limit of the card's instruments, is the subject of the limits of observability.
  • Confidence bars. Every entry is the mean over every pass on every card, with the full range those passes spanned in brackets. The catalogue (sections 2, 3, 4.1, 4.3, 8) is three passes in shuffled order on each of two cards, n = 6; the relay, the hot line, the rings and the levels (4.2, 5, 6) are their first measurement plus three passes on aifoundry2 with the die held above 76 °C and three on aifoundry3; the tensor rows (3.2) are the ablation's two runs and the card transfer's runs on both cards. The range is used rather than a standard error because with n = 6 the bar is dominated by the systematic difference between the two cards, about 5%, which a standard error of the pooled sample would understate; pass-to-pass scatter on one card is 1–2% for most entries. Two things the bars do not hold: a change of operating point (bursts in which the governor lifted the clock off 600 MHz are excluded, as the first cool-card attempt at the reruns was in its entirety), and the idle law's own ±0.2 W, which every "over idle" figure inherits and which is why the small-signal entries — the awake core, the hot line, the DRAM relay — carry the widest bars.
