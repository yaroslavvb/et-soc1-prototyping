Tracking heat down to transistor flips

, all times Pacific · built from the lab repository’s request log, experiment register and published pages, and from the project’s own hub · data and tools in yaroslavvb/et-soc1-prototyping · part of the ET-SoC-1 measurement reports

The result this thread was chasingIt is now one page: From matmul speed to one transistor switch — the whole chain, from the speed gap down to 108 electrons on one transistor’s gate. This page is how it was reached: every ask, session, card measurement and write-up behind it, on one axis.

Horace He found that a matrix multiply runs faster on predictable data. This is seven weeks of following that result down into an ET-SoC-1: from the speed, to the watts, to the transistors that flip, to the heat you can film coming off the back of the board. The chart below puts four things on one time axis. The questions are asks of mine, each with its own words. The sessions are agent sessions on machines, in lanes. The experiments are measurements holding an ET-SoC-1 card, in all. The documents are write-ups, of them public.

What’s next

  • Move the power cap. Every step above the governor says the cap is what makes zeros faster — but the cap has never actually been moved. About half an hour of card time would turn the best-supported link in the chain from an inference into a measurement.
  • Count the flips that happen at once. The single-bit prices do not add, and nothing yet says by how much. A ladder in the number of positions flipping together is written and never run: 20 card-minutes, and it turns the bottom-up range ×1.06–1.19 into one number.
  • Price the other half of the capacitance. Half the design’s farads — 23,331 fF of 47,093 — sit on nets made after the netlist was simulated, so the bottom-up total is a floor three times over. Writing the netlist out later and re-simulating needs no card.

The first two need a card; the third does not.
