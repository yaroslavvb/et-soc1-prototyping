The Horace experiment on the ET-SoC-1

aifoundry2's card, 20 September 2026 · fp32 TensorFMA on all 1,024 minions at a fixed 600 MHz · workloads/sparsity --values, tools/ettelem

Horace He's post "Strangely, Matrix Multiplications on GPUs Run Faster When Given 'Predictable' Data!" (April 2024) found that an A100 multiplies matrices of zeros about 15% faster than random ones (295 against 257 TFLOPS). His explanation: predictable data flips fewer transistors, draws less power, and a power-limited GPU can then clock higher. (The post's direction is that predictable data uses less power, not random data.) On this chip the same physics shows up much more directly. The clock is fixed and the card has power headroom, so speed does not move at all: 546 cycles per operation for every pattern. Power does: 38 W for zeros, 48 W for any constant, 67 W for random values, with every run started at the same 80 °C die temperature. All of the difference is on the minion-core rail.

1. Result

Board power by operand pattern, every run started at 80 °C (mean of two rounds; split: minion-core rail, everything else)
  • Zeros are cheapest, as on the GPU. The tensor unit gates a multiply whose operand is zero (the sparsity report measured that), so this is clock gating more than quiet wires.
  • Every constant costs the same. Ones, twos, π (a dense mantissa) and a float with a single set bit all land within 0.4 W. So it is not how many bits are set that costs energy. What costs is bits that change from one operand to the next: with a constant, the multiplier inputs stay the same every cycle and only the accumulator moves.
  • Random values cost the most: +20 W over a constant, +29 W over zeros. Uniform [0,1) is 2.8 W below random normal: no sign flips and a narrower exponent range. About 1.5 W of the random figure is its own heating: within 6 s it takes the die from 80 to 85.5 °C, and the first seconds of the run read 65.8 W.
  • Half zeros is not half way. The checkerboard (every other element zero) and the 75%-zeros random matrix sit at 40.9 and 41.3 W, close to zeros. A product is gated when either operand is zero, so with both A and B half zero only a quarter of the products run; with 25% dense random in both, about 6%.
  • Only the core rail moves. SRAM and mesh power are the same to 0.1 W for every pattern: the data travels the same wires either way, and the arithmetic is where the switching is.

2. Why no speed-up here

The A100 result needs a power-limited part: lower power lets the GPU hold higher clocks. This card ran all patterns at a fixed 600 MHz, and the clock stayed put even as random data took it to 67 W against a 65 W TDP setting (and to 70 W at 90 °C in the earlier back-to-back run). So the ET-SoC-1 shows the cause (data-dependent switching power) without the GPU's effect (data-dependent speed). At iso-power the conclusion flips into Horace's: a DVFS governor pinned at a power limit would clock the zeros case higher.

3. Controlling temperature

Power on this card rises with die temperature at the same work, about 0.78 W per °C on the board (0.38 on the minion rail), so heat left over from one run contaminates the next. In this run every pattern starts from the same state: after a warm-up with random data, each run waits until the mean of the 34 minion-shire sensors has cooled to 80 °C, and only then launches. All 20 runs started at exactly 80 °C. Waits were 1–10 s after the cool patterns and 60–90 s after the random ones. The two rounds, in different orders, agree to within 1.1 W for every pattern and within 0.25 W for five of them, with no correction applied.

Die temperature over the run: each run launches when the die has cooled to the 80 °C line

What cannot be held constant is the heating a pattern causes during its own 6 s: zeros stay at 80–81 °C, constants reach 82 °C and random data 85.5 °C. The table gives both the first seconds of each run (closest to a common temperature) and the last three. An earlier, uncontrolled run (three rounds back to back, the die drifting from 79 to 90 °C) needed a fitted temperature term; corrected to 82 °C it gave the same ordering and values within 1.5 W of these (zeros 39.3, constants 47.4–47.9, random normal 64.8).

4. Method

  • Kernel: hart 0 of each of 1,024 minions runs back-to-back TensorFMA ops on one 16×16×16 fp32 tile pair held in the L1 scratchpad, 6 s per pattern (each process under 10 s of card time). The host fills both A and B with the pattern.
  • Power: tools/ettelem samples board power and the service processor's rail averages at 10 Hz. Each run uses the mean over its last 3 s, because the rail averages lag by about 2 s; board power, which does not lag, is also given for seconds 1–3.
  • Temperature control: run_horace.sh <out> 80 2 waits for the die to cool to the target before each run. The target has to sit above the idle equilibrium (72 °C) by enough that the wait is short: from 81 °C the die loses only about a degree a minute.
  • Energy per multiply-add: 1,024 minions × 600 MHz / 546 cycles × 4,096 multiply-adds per op = 4.6 × 10¹² per second; 27.7 W (seconds 1–3) / that = 6 pJ between random and zeros, and 2 pJ between a constant and zeros.
  • Results are not checked for these patterns (random sums are not exact); the same kernel is checked exactly in its integer mode.
