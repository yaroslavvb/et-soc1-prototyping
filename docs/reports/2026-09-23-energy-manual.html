The ET-SoC-1 energy manual

Two ET-SoC-1 cards, 18–23 September 2026 · every number from a measurement on the card, every table traceable to its data file · docs/energy-manual/ is the same manual as markdown

A workload's energy on this card is the time it takes, times what the card draws doing nothing at the temperature the workload leaves it at, plus a sum over everything it does of a per-event cost. This page is the table of per-event costs, and the two things it needs alongside them. The card at rest is 12.6 W plus leakage that doubles every 25 °C. An awake minion is 2 mW. An integer add is 7 pJ, a float add 23, an eight-lane multiply-add 28 on zeros and 59 on random data. A byte from the shire's own scratchpad is 2–8 pJ; from DRAM, 90–140; written back through the L1 to DRAM, 250–350. A second card gives the same numbers to 5%.

9. Method, and what the manual cannot tell you

  • Board power is the PMIC's reading, 10 mW resolution, refreshed every 133 ms, sampled at 10 Hz. Each measurement is a burst of back-to-back launches with six seconds of idle either side; its idle is the mean of the two bracketing stretches, and the extra leakage of a burst that runs slightly warmer than its brackets is taken out with the section 1 slope — a correction of . Rates come from the device's cycle counter, not the wall clock.
  • "Per instruction" includes the awake core that issued it (about 7 pJ per slot with both harts). Only the tensor unit is resolved below the instruction, and a "flip" there is an event in a simulation of the design, not a transistor.
  • Everything is at 600 MHz and 0.517 V. The 18 September read-by-level table was taken with the governor free to move the clock, and says so per row. fdiv.ps and fsqrt.ps trap.
  • The unsensed 15 W — the largest single component of idle — cannot be split with any instrument here.
  • Uncertainty: ±3% for instructions and scratchpad bytes, ±5% for DRAM bytes, ±2% for the tensor unit at 80 °C, and the two cards agree to 5% with one common scale factor.
