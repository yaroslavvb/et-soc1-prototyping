L2 mainline starvation: superseded by One hot line stops a shire

22 September 2026 · no card: an argument from a reported result, the shire-cache spec and the errata · part of the ET-SoC-1 measurement reports

This brief argued from a result Ivan reported in the AI Foundry Discord (shire 0 got 6% of its fair share of a contended global atomic) and from the shire-cache spec, by which requests arriving from other shires outrank the owner shire's own, that the owner of a hot line is starved. It called the l3_yield priority the lever, advised homing hot structures in a shire that does no compute, and warned that a 2D TensorSend array can hang a hart. The reproduction that afternoon, on two cards, kept the aggregate (60 million atomics a second) and the even split among the other shires, but not the 6%: the owner's share of the atomic is 1.004. What stops, with the clock steady at 600 MHz, is the owner shire's other memory traffic, to 0.02–0.05% of normal in a 10 ms window, once more than about 20 remote requesters saturate its bank (24 stopped it, 20 did not). Two errata describe the mechanism and limit what l3_yield could do. The placement advice holds, and so does the TensorSend warning for cells that take two partners at once, which rests on the RTL and on one hang seen on aifoundry2.

Related reports: where its points now live

  • One hot line stops a shire — the measurement: the atomic is fair, the owner shire's own loads stop (at a steady 600 MHz), and a bank rule that predicts when. Its advice is in What to do instead (section 7): make the owner a shire that does no compute, not the master shire; pace the requesters; spread instead of sharing; and read the victim's bank counters from user mode. Section 8 says why l3_yield was left alone.
  • On-chip communication, “A trap: one ready flag per minion” — why a 2D TensorSend array whose cells take readies from two partners at once can hang a hart until the chip is reset (read from the RTL; one such hang was seen, on aifoundry2), and the layouts that are safe: 1D rings, 2D in barrier-separated phases, and the hardware trees.
