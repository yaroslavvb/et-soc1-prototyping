Power and temperature on the ET-SoC-1 card

aifoundry2's card, 20 September 2026 · et-platform 353f20e firmware · tools/ettelem, a telemetry client on the management library

The card has one power sensor path: a PMIC that reports 12 V board power and three of its rails (minion cores, SRAM, mesh), polled by the service processor every 133 ms. Everything else is voltage or temperature. This report lists every way to read them, what each resolves, and what a small client on the management library adds over the stock tool: the per-rail snapshot the CLI refuses, 45 samples a second, and a 34-shire on-die voltage map. Then what the numbers show. The main surprise is temperature: the same work costs 0.8 W more per °C, the die idles at 72 °C, and idle power after a load is 5 W higher than before it, so every energy comparison on this card has to control for heat.

1. Every way to measure, by granularity

Space, time and resolution for each instrument. works now means from a user account with the tools in this repo; firmware means a service-processor change and a signed image; hardware means physical access.

2. A load step, seen through every sensor

Twenty seconds idle, 58 s of fp32 matmul on all 1,024 minions, 40 s idle, 34 s of DRAM-bound loads, idle. Sampled at 10 Hz with ettelem; every load process held the card for under 10 s.

Power (W): board total and the three measured rails
Die temperature (°C, mean of the 34 minion-shire sensors)
  • Power climbs while the work stays constant. The matmul starts at 57 W and ends at 65 W 56 s later, with the die going from 74 to 87 °C. Same kernel, same clock, same rate: the extra 8 W is leakage. The Horace experiment's fit over 22 runs gives 0.78 W/°C on the board and 0.38 W/°C on the minion rail.
  • Idle is not one number. Before the load: 31.2 W at 72 °C. Forty seconds after it: 36.5 W at 81 °C. The die sheds heat slowly (6 °C in 40 s), so a baseline taken before a run understates the idle power during and after it. Energy measurements here should take their baseline at the same temperature, or fit a temperature term.
  • The three rails are not the whole card. Board minus rails is 15.0 W at idle, 20–22 W under matmul and 21 W under DRAM load. That remainder is the DDR and PCIe rails, the Maxions, the IO shire, and regulator losses, none of which has a sensor. A DRAM-bound load moves it by 5 W and the minion rail by 0.3 W; a matmul moves the minion rail by 16–20 W.
  • Why the remainder sometimes looks too small, or the rails seem to exceed the step. The rail figures are the PMIC's moving averages and lag a step by about 2 s; board power is near-instantaneous. For 2 s after a load starts the remainder reads 36 W (rails have not caught up), and for 2 s after it stops it reads 14 W, below idle. In steady state the two board readings agree to about 0.5 W. This also explains the 15–20% gap between the host log and the trace average in the memory-anatomy report: its 6 s windows were short against the settling time.
  • Voltage barely droops at the die. The minion rail's on-die monitor reads 518 mV idle and 517 mV with 18 W more load (about 35 A more at 0.52 V): the regulator senses at the die. The DDR domain reads 768 mV on die against an 800 mV set-point, and sags 3 mV more under DRAM load.
  • The clock never moved. The power-management governor is on with a 65 W limit, the board touched 65 W here and 70 W in the Horace run at 90 °C, and the minion clock stayed at 600 MHz throughout.

3. The per-shire voltage map

With its log level raised to DEBUG, the service processor writes one line per shire per pass with the on-die voltage of each rail and the hardware's low/high capture. ettelem loglevel debug then ettelem sptrace reads them; the level is restored afterwards. This is the finest spatial view of the power system the card gives: below is the minion-rail voltage at idle on the shire mesh, with each shire's lowest captured value.

Current values span 517–521 mV across the die; the lowest captured minimum is 513 mV (shires 0–2 and 18). The low/high registers hold extremes since the last stats reset, so they catch droops that the 133 ms polling misses. Per-shire temperature is sampled by the same controllers but never logged; that needs firmware.

4. What a debug-interface client would and would not add

The Minion Debug Interface reaches minion state only: registers, CSRs, memory of a halted hart. It cannot read the PMIC, the on-die sensors or anything in the service processor's address space, so it adds no power or temperature sensor. Its value for energy work is indirect: it can write the counter event selectors without a reflash, and chosen event counts (L1 misses, tensor ops, cache hits per level) are what turn rail power into energy per event by regression. The telemetry side of the management library, which this report uses, was the larger gap, and it needs no halting.
