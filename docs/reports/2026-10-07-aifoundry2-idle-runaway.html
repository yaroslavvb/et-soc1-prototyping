aifoundry2's card ran away again, a day after the fan fix

Covers Tue 6 Oct 2026, 16:05 PDT, to Wed 7 Oct 2026, 17:01 PDT: aifoundry2 in the AI Foundry lab at Studio 45. Written on Wed 7 Oct from the lab's live collector (one record every 5 s) and the machine's kernel log.

⚠ Out of service since Wed 7 Oct, 16:46 PDT

The card is out of service again. Idle, with nobody using it, it heated from 64 °C to a 138 °C mean (hottest sensor 144 °C) at 133 W, and fell off the PCIe bus at 16:46, 24.7 hours after the 6 October fan fix brought it back. It failed exactly as on 2 October (138 °C, hottest 144 °C, 134 W). It is still off the bus; the machine itself is up.

What changed was the cooling at the card, not the card. At 03:55 on Wednesday, with the host idle and its own temperatures unchanged, the card started heating and settled about 11 °C warmer. In the afternoon it got worse again and crossed the tipping point. All day the card drew exactly the power that its temperature calls for, on the same curve as on 2 October. Something at the card stopped carrying heat away: its own fan, or a fan near it, that stopped, slowed or moved would do this. Fan speeds are not visible from the machine, so someone has to look on site.

Only the cooling at the card changed

Three things rule out everything but the card's own cooling.

  • Nothing ran on the card. No process held it after the 6 October tests ended at 16:35, and at 03:55 the host's CPU was 0.5% busy.
  • The card drew what its temperature calls for, and nothing more. On 2 October the card's power fitted 13.6 W plus a part that doubles every 23 °C (the chip's leakage). On Wednesday every reading sat on that same curve, 0.25 W rms over the day and within 0.4 W at every temperature the card held for a minute or more (the second chart). Power only ever followed temperature: had work started, or had the firmware changed the clock or the voltage, the readings would have left the curve.
  • The rest of the machine did not warm up. Across 03:55 the host's NVMe drive read 45.9 then 46.3 °C, its network chip 54.1 then 53.8 °C, and its CPU 39 then 38 °C. The room and the case air stayed as they were.

The 2 October heat model puts a number on it: the air temperature the card's heat balance needs (the model's single "air" number, so a loss of airflow also shows up as warmer air). Overnight that was about 39 °C, as just after the fix. After 03:55 it was about 46 °C; after 14:25 about 51 °C, which is where it was before the fix. The model checks itself: with air at 39, 46 and 51 °C it says the idle card settles at 63, 76 and 91 °C, which is what it did. Above 51.5 °C it has no steady temperature at all.

Why the card could not save itself

From 65 °C to 138 °C nothing on the card slowed it down, today as on 2 October. The firmware's thermal throttle does not look at an idle card: in the upstream firmware source (et-platform 836a4ab, check_power_throttle_conditions()) the temperature check runs only while the card's master minion is busy, and its default threshold is 65 °C. An idle card whose cooling falls short is never throttled, however hot it gets. This card runs firmware 1.3.1, and the check above is the current upstream code, not that release's own source, which has not been read.

So the two problems meet. Hotter silicon leaks more, which heats it further; a fan keeps that loop from closing, and the firmware does nothing once it does. Until the firmware throttles idle cards too, the cooling at the card is the only thing that keeps it out of the loop.

What is not known: the fan

The machine cannot see its fans: no fan sensor driver is loaded for this board, and loading one needs root. Two causes can still be ruled out. The BIOS setting cannot have reverted, because the machine has not rebooted since the fix, and Linux never switched a fan (the kernel's ACPI fans show no change of state since boot). That leaves the hardware: the card's own fan, if it has one, or a case fan by it, that stopped, slowed or was knocked out of line, or something in the way of its air. The change came at 03:55 at night, all at once, while nobody was there.

On site: look at the card's own fan (follow its cable, if it has one) and at the case fans by it, then at the BIOS's Smart Fan 6 page, which shows each fan's speed (System 5 read 2,368 RPM at full speed on 6 October).

What changes for people using the lab

  • Run nothing on aifoundry2's card. The lab's docs, the new-user brief and the dashboard mark it out of service; new users set up on aifoundry3 or aifoundry1. The machine stays up.
  • To bring it back: first fix the cooling at the card, then the full reset as root, the cold reboot that power-cycles the slot. A warm reboot or a slot reset leaves it off the bus, as the 17:01 reset showed.
  • Then watch it. After a reset the card looks healthy for about an hour while it heats. This time it ran 25 hours, and the trouble started 12 hours in.

Sources and method

  • Readings. The lab's live collector on aifoundry2 reads the card once a second and keeps one record every 5 s: the mean of the 34 minion-shire temperature sensors ("die"), the highest reading since the card started ("hottest"), board power, and whether anyone holds the card; and the host's own sensors. 18,047 records, 17,678 with a card reading.
  • The model. The 2 October fit, recomputed from that day's records by the same method: power P(T) = 13.56 + 2.025·e0.0300·T W; heat balance C·dT/dt = P − (T − Tair)/R with C = 296 J/°C and R = 0.897 °C/W. Each minute's air is T − R·(P − C·dT/dt), with dT/dt a least-squares slope over 6 minutes. These are model numbers, not measurements, and R and the air are correlated in a one-run fit.
  • The kernel log. The card's lines, 16:46:32 to 17:01:20. The 16:46 lines come from a read at 17:16: the ring buffer, flooded with AppArmor audit lines, had dropped them by 17:40.
  • Earlier pages. 2 October, the first runaway and its fit; 6 October, the fan fix.
