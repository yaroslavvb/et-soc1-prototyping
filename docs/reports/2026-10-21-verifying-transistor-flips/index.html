Names on this page. A minion is one of the chip's 1,088 small RISC-V cores in 34 shires (these kernels use 1,024); each has an 8-lane vector unit, so one 256-bit operation is 8 lanes × 32 bits. The minion rail is the supply that feeds them, metered on the card separately from the board's 12 V input. Energies are board energies (the card's 12 V input, regulator loss included) unless marked rail; the charge-equivalent capacitance and electron count of every price we quote is tabulated at the foot of this page, in both conventions, ½CV² and CV². fxor.pi, fand.pi, for.pi, fadd.pi and fnot.pi are the vector unit's bitwise and integer operations. h is the designed number of bits that flip per lane from one instruction to the next.

E-D1 · The toggle dial, the inverter and the register-file cell

What the page proposed. Alternate two source registers so that exactly k bit-slices flip per core per clock cycle, and read the price of one flip off the slope. It added that in the generated loop the eight destination registers are rewritten with the value they already hold, so the cells never flip while the result bus does — a real and useful observation.

What we ran, and why it differs. The dial itself, as a fnot.pi ladder (mode 202) at h = 8, 16 and 32, in two arms: d1n, whose operand set contains the all-zero word, and d1nz, which never holds one. The cell question needed a new shape: the page's 1A/1B pair cannot work as written, because the two arms differ in opcode and in rate as well as in cells. We rebuilt it as a 16-operation pair (modes 304 and 305) in which the two arms share their port and result counts exactly and differ only in how many register-file cell bits are rewritten, and we take the difference of the two slopes against the shared regressor, so each arm's own intercept drops out.

The two arms lie on top of each other: the zero word is worth -134 ± 72 fJ/op over the whole operation (-0.0478 ± 0.0257 W), which the reduction calls under-resolved — SE 0.0257 W exceeds one third of the rule’s separation (2% of the 2.569 W matched delta) (§9.4). The sum of an input and an output bit through the inverter is 50.681 ± 0.520 fJ on the never-zero arm and 50.687 ± 0.592 fJ on the arm with zeros.

The plan registered four replications of that zero-word term, and they do not agree, so the term is not pooled into one number. Two of the six pairs are more than the 3.85-standard-error window apart (N primary (d1n - d1nz) against miller (m_solid_z - m_phase0) at |t| = 4.53, fadd (d3p - d3) against miller (m_solid_z - m_phase0) at |t| = 4.47), and Cochran's Q over the four is 28.7 on 3 degrees of freedom. So the set is reported as opcode-dependent — §9's registered open arm — and never collapsed: the replications disagree by more than their own SEs allow, so there is no one zero-word term to apply across opcodes. The four are gathered across cards and compared there, each in femtojoules per operation at its own item's measured rate, which is the only comparison §8.6 allows; they are not averaged. An inverse-variance mean of -285.3 fJ/op exists in the file as the centre Cochran's Q is taken about and nothing reads it. What is applied, wherever the zero-word correction is used, is the form the §8.6 fit prefers — a constant per operation, -95.5 ± 53.0 fJ/op — not the single-rung watt difference this row's headline quotes.

E-D2 · The input-versus-output mask bridge

What the page proposed. Run fand.pi with the second operand held at zero, so the inputs flip and the output cannot: “zero gate outputs flip, zero pipeline latches flip, zero writeback wires flip”. Subtract that from the same operation with the operand held at all-ones, and what remains is the price of the output path alone — the page's 44.38 fJ per result bit.

What we ran, and why it differs. Two arms are not enough: the held word and the opcode both move with the mask. We ran it as a 2 × 2 — fand.pi with the operand held at 0 and at all-ones, for.pi with it held at all-ones and at 0 — at h = 0, 16 and 32, so that the opcode's own intercept cancels by construction. The page's Run A is our d2A, its Run B our d2B, its Run C our d2C.

The read-port leg. d2A reads 26.11 ± 0.13 fJ per port bit and d2C, the same shape under another opcode, 26.08 fJ — the agreement the bridge's output leg depends on. The reduction marks that row not scored against E60's 26.00–30.20 fJ interval and reports it as AGREEMENT, because §5.2 of the plan says d2A cannot both supply e_in(fand) and be tested against it — and in this build it does supply it: it is the subtrahend inside the registered estimator for e_out(add) and e_carry. §9's table nonetheless registers a confirmed arm, so the plan contradicts itself; the first builds of this reduction resolved the contradiction silently in favour of scoring, and this one drops the arm as a decision and keeps the direction. Read as agreement, the two numbers agree. Three caveats the row's own blocks carry. The value sits 0.112 fJ above the interval's lower edge, 0.85 standard errors. The four fit forms the reduction prints for it span 25.01–26.33 fJ. And where the answer lands moves with the leakage slope: the fitted slope 0.033 W/°C (PRIMARY, §8.1) → 26.112 ± 0.132 fJ, inside the strict interval; k = 0 → 26.217 ± 0.144 fJ, inside the strict interval; E60’s 0.203 W/°C → 25.567 ± 0.230 fJ, OUTSIDE the strict interval — so at E60's own 0.203 W/°C the point value is below the interval and inside it only after the ±0.885 fJ widening §5.0 rule 2 allows. That reading survives on the widening alone.

The output leg does not land where the page put it, and the routes to it disagree with each other. E-D5 · The address decoder — the night's largest effect, and the one we cannot price

What the page proposed. Hold every data register at zero so nothing flips in the datapath, and change only which registers the instruction names. What is left is the 5-bit index decoder and its wordline drivers, which the page priced at 32.0 fJ per address bit across three ports.

What we ran, and why it differs. A ladder of register-index Hamming distances (new modes 306–309) in two data regimes: gated, every data register zero, as the page specified; and live, every data register holding the same non-zero word, so that the data path is awake while the index still moves. The 5-bit rung retires at a different instruction rate from the rest of the ladder, so the amendment keeps it out of the slope and reports it beside — it is drawn hollow below.

Something very large is there. The gated ladder's slope is 368.9 ± 21.9 fJ per toggled index bit if it is fitted with a free intercept, and 452.1 ± 12.2 fJ if it is forced through the origin — two forms the plan registers, differing by 83.2 fJ a bit, far more than either standard error. It is the only one of the confirmatory five that survives Holm correction (p = 3.4 × 10⁻⁹), it is 11.5 to 14.1 times the page's 32.0 fJ, and it sits 184.5 times our own registered ceiling of 2.0 fJ.

And it is under-resolved. Not refuted: the registered arm is a ceiling test — the page claimed 32 fJ a bit and the question was whether silicon is cheaper — so excluding 32 fJ from above does not answer it, and the exclusion is read one-sided downward only. SE 21.928 fJ exceeds one third of the 32 fJ separation (§9.4). The ladder is reported descriptively and no verdict rests on the number.

Four reasons the slope is not “energy per address bit”. First, the two fit forms disagree by more than their errors, which is itself why the row cannot be scored. Second, the three rungs are three separately emitted kernels, and a per-kernel constant that a single free intercept cannot remove is measurable elsewhere on this card: the five zero rungs of other families that share the A ladder's own issue rate (d1n_h0, d1nz_h0, d2A_h0, d2B_h0, d3z_h0) span 0.0895 W, which on this ladder is 25.1 fJ a bit — 6.8% of the slope itself. A per-kernel constant of the size measured elsewhere accounts for part of this slope, not all of it, and it cannot be subtracted, because the A rungs' own spread is the ladder. Third, the cost is not one slope: a fixed step is paid the moment the source index stops being the same register twice in a row, and only the remainder grows with distance, so a single number per bit is the wrong functional form. Fourth, the design is exactly degenerate in its own regressor — we chose f1, f7 and f31, so Hamming distance equals log₂(1 + physical row distance) rung for rung (d5_a1: 2.0 index bits, row distance 1, log₂(1+distance) 1.0; d5_a3: 6.0 index bits, row distance 7, log₂(1+distance) 3.0; d5_a5: 10 index bits, row distance 31, log₂(1+distance) 5.0). A per-address-bit law and a per-decoder-tree-level law are the same arithmetic here, and the index travels in the instruction word as well as into the register file's decoder, so instruction-fetch toggles and read-port address toggles move together by construction.

What is solid: changing which register an instruction names, with every data bit held still, moves real power, and it is the most expensive bit on this page — about 19.0 times a read-port data bit (19.38 fJ). The gated and live ladders differ by -128.7 ± 22.6 fJ per bit — the registered test for data-path gating reaching the address path, and it fires — with the live arm reading 497.64 ± 8.46 fJ. The page's 32 fJ across three ports is refuted and nothing is confirmed in its place. Two rungs break the degeneracy for E62: one with high Hamming distance and small row distance, one with the reverse, and one with the two source index fields different so that the units stop being ambiguous by a factor of two.

One consequence for E-D1, which we owe the reader. The pre-registration budgeted family V's address-count difference as “0.05% of the signal”. At a price of this order it is nearer 19% of the cell signal, and in the direction that makes the cell term larger. The A2.3 slope-difference form E-D1's headline uses is immune — the address count is constant inside each arm's own ladder — so 0.76 ± 0.32 fJ stands. The cross-mode per-rung step figures are not immune. The B bridge, family C, family M, family Z and the X ladder all hold their address count constant across rungs, so they are clean.

The operand law, and what two harts do to it

The page's split of a flip's cost into input and output — 6.81 to 1 — comes from assuming uniform random operand bits. They are not uniform, and the catalogue's own numbers admit two readings depending on whether two harts interleave and destroy operand sharing. The registered statistic is a rate-free ratio ρ of the per-operation cost at two harts to one.

ρ = 1.1169 ± 0.0242 on aifoundry1-c0 over 8 passes: interleaving destroys sharing, so the catalogue cannot settle the split at all, and the page's 6.81:1 is the full-interleave reading arrived at by coincidence. Verdict: REFUTED. This one survived every attack we could make on it. The obvious objection — that two harts change the per-operation overhead the baseline removes — does not hold, because the baseline is removed inside each hart setting before the ratio is taken. To pull ρ to 1.000 the two-hart arm would have to shed 865 fJ an operation, 10% of its 8378.5 ± 149.0 fJ/op, and the only mechanism left is temperature — where the measured differential heating between the two arms is the same at one hart as at two, so it cannot be the cause. The leakage arms all read above 1: the board leg is 1.1169 ± 0.0242 and the minion-rail leg 1.292 ± 0.142 in the independent reduction. A phase-locking alternative pushes the wrong way: locked harts would give ρ below 1.

Two honest limits, and one correction against our own plan. The row is thinner than it looks: 1.000 is excluded at |t| = 4.82 against a 4.53 window at 8 passes — 106.5% of the threshold, so one more lost pass would reopen it — and the statistic cannot tell complete destruction of sharing from near-complete (90.3% ± 17% destroyed is consistent with all of it). It also rests on one card, one opcode. The correction: our own §0.2 argued that the catalogue's forward agreement proves sharing survives interleaving. Redone with §0.2's own interleaved counts, the five ratios move from a uniformly low 0.951 (sd 0.030) to a centred 1.002 (sd 0.045), so neither reading is excluded and the forward check discriminates nothing. ρ is the only evidence here, and the claim that the catalogue settles it is withdrawn. The model-free leg reads 61.856 ± 0.936 fJ against the 68.12–69.56 fJ that E60's measurement predicts for this card once scaled by this campaign's own measured per-bit tie (1.310, against E60's 1.216) — outside, even widened, so the operand law is not confirmed on this leg. The tie itself is 1.077× E60's, outside §8.7's registered 0.88–0.99 card-to-card band, so the scaling is a reported method choice and not a check that passed.

What the page got right

  • Its arithmetic on real data — twelve claims, all holding. The largest single group in the desk check is the page's mining of a corpus that does exist, and it survives in full: 13.148 / 13.158 / 13.661 ✓; +7.353 / +5.995 / +5.859, gap 24% ✓; six rows to ±0.01 pJ ✓; fsll.pi +4.657, fswizz.ps +5.311, fbcx.ps +5.237 ✓; fadd.pi const − zeros +0.054 ✓ (the one real const value on the page); every E-D6 bit pattern decodes, 23/24 = 95.8% ✓. C39’s 2.09× is arithmetic but not a width-only comparison (the flip counts also differ… That is the page's whole data layer, and it is correct.
  • The sum, to 0.43%. Its input and output prices add to 50.90 fJ per flipped bit-slice. Measured through the inverter at matched counts, an input bit plus an output bit costs 50.6810 ± 0.5203 fJ. The page split that sum wrongly — 6.81:1 against a measured ratio of 1.37:1 — but the quantity its arithmetic actually fixed, the sum, is the quantity it got nearly exactly.
  • One operation retires per cycle per hart. Its “1.0 IPC” is right about the vector unit's issue rate, which we did not expect and first scored against it. What is wrong is the conversion to Gops/s, because 41 to 46 cycles an iteration go on the loop's own scalar work.
  • The flip counts are deterministic. 1437 runs over 111 items passed the within-mode instruction-rate, ok and participant gates on every card, with no subtraction voided — verdict CONFIRMED. One rung is excluded by name as rate-confounded and reported beside its ladder; the on-silicon register dump that would close the last gap is not in tonight's build, which the row says in full.
  • The destination cells really do stay still. The page's observation that the generated loop rewrites each destination with the value it already holds is correct, and it is what makes the cell term separable at all: the rebuilt 1A/1B pair finds a cell bit at 0.76 ± 0.32 fJ, far below a result bit.
  • The address path costs energy. Changing which registers an instruction names, with every data bit held still, moves real power — the one effect tonight that survives Holm correction across the confirmatory five (e_addr, p = 3.4 × 10⁻⁹). The page was right that this is worth measuring; it was wrong about the size, and so were we, and neither of us measured it.
  • The halves move heat. Giving one half of the mesh flipping result bits and the other half none does move heat across the board, it does so without changing total card power to within the camera's reading of it, and it reverses when the masks swap — exactly the qualitative claim E-D7 makes, at a size the camera cannot convert into millikelvin on an uncalibrated card, and with the halves it names replaced by halves that exist.

How this was run

Pre-registered. The design, the eleven families, every prediction as an interval, the analysis and the decision rules were committed before the first measured pass: tools/claims-v3/dtf/README.md (the pre-registration commit), with two amendments, each committed before the data it touches — A1, more passes inside the claim window, decided on elapsed time alone, and A2, which fixes the issue rate by mode and so reduces families V and A within mode. 68 manifest items, seed 20261021; the analysis manifest A2 introduces, and which the reduction actually reads, is manifest-a2.json. The reduction's own record of what it departed from: the on-silicon register dump (§7 proof 6) is not implemented; modes are 300-309; anchor_pi runs first, middle and last, so the drift statistic is a fitted W/min.

Protocol. E60's: each run launched as the die's reading fell to a fixed value after a heater burst, the board's power read over a fixed window once the clock had settled, every item differenced against its own zero rung inside the same pass, every error bar the scatter between passes, and a three-sigma window read as the two-sided t quantile at p = 0.0027 on that scatter. The confirmatory five (e_cell, e_carry, e_addr, phase_term, zero_word_term) are Holm-corrected at α = 0.050; everything else is exploratory and says so. Leakage is corrected with each card's own fitted slope, and because tonight's fit and E60's differ by an order of magnitude (0.0330 against 0.203 W/°C on aifoundry3, with only 23.0% of runs qualifying for the within-run fit), every quantity is also reported at a zero slope and at E60's.
