## What the page may now say

**§5.4,** replacing E106's sentences from "Like for like, on zero products" through "the adder alternating at its lower price on moving sums":

> Like for like, on zero products and in one session (E108; aifoundry1 card 1, 600 MHz, four passes, designed tiles, sums read back as held), fp32 keeps 0.41 ± 0.04 of its 4.8 W step and fp16 0.43 ± 0.04 of its 5.0 W step: alike (the difference is 0.01 ± 0.02). fp32's absorbed design keeps 0.10 ± 0.04 more. The rest is not the sums changing. A held tile whose accumulators sit at exactly the walking tile's sums at every op's end costs 0.97 ± 0.01 of that tile's rest on zero products, and the walk itself adds 0.09 ± 0.04 W; held by absorption, a tile whose accumulators differ as the walking ones do costs at least the whole rest (1.13 ± 0.09; about 1.0 with its own extra operand bits taken off). So the rest goes with the 256 accumulators holding different values: a lane alternates between two of them and moves row to row, and reads, adds and writes back a different word every micro-op. About 35 fJ per bit of the lane's C and result words that differ. It costs more as more of those bits differ: sign alone, nothing resolved; exponent, about 0.6 W more; mantissa, about 1.7 W more. On these tiles a random sign's step is therefore two things: the held part (its sign bits and the adder alternating while the sums hold) and the rest (the accumulators it leaves holding different values).

The rest of E106's §5.4 text stands. **May not say:** "the sums changing cost nothing" (0.09 ± 0.04 W on zero products; and against an absorbed held tile the walk reads −0.30 ± 0.20 W, because an absorbed subtraction has a swing of its own); "the result bus" or "the register file" as the measured place (it is the bench's reading); "every random tensor" or "real data" (designed tiles, rank-one accumulator patterns matched to the walking tiles' counts or sums); "the same split in both formats" (the location is fp32's; fp16's held shares are alike, its rest was not split here); "A's signs cost nothing" (alike per alternation, nothing measurable on top of B's walking, inconclusive held); anything about other cards; the moving-sum ratio as decided (R10 INCONCLUSIVE).

**Item 5 narrows again; its last open part is located.** **Keep one line:** "Where a random sign's ~5 W goes: on one card, designed tiles, the held part is 0.41–0.43 on zero products in both formats (alike, one session) and 0.52 absorbed in fp32; the rest goes with the accumulators holding different values (0.97 ± 0.01 of it in a held tile at the walking sums), not with the sums changing (0.09 ± 0.04 W) (E108). ## Caveats

Four passes, one card, one clock, designed tiles: every accumulator pattern here is a rank-one seed (`c_mx_f` matched to `s_walk`'s bit count, `b_B_z` to `b_B_w`'s sums) beside random ±1 operands, not a real tensor. The location is a statement about watts on the board, paired within passes: "goes with the accumulators differing" names what the tiles change, and the bench's model, not a measurement, says which nets. `b_B_z` has 468 fewer alternations an op than `z_s` and 216 fewer than `b_B_w`; priced, that makes R12 read low (1.13–1.16 post hoc), not high. `c_mx_f`'s k = 0 row carries mantissa bits on the B bus that `s_abs`'s does not (4,076 against 3,364 designed bus bits an op): M4 prices it at 0.29 W, and with it off R7's share is 1.00 ± 0.09. R7's tiles hold by absorption, whose subtraction swing `s_walk` lacks, so a share above 1 there is not a negative walk on its own. The board's dips widen every interval 5–10× in the dips-kept arm, which moves R3, R4, R5, R7 and R9 to INCONCLUSIVE; their means hold within 0.1 (R7 1.052), and the rail, which the dips do not touch, gives R12 1.005 ± 0.122 and R7 1.31 ± 0.69 (one pass's `c_mx_f` rail reads high). The watts are the board's; the rail figures are about 0.8–0.9 of them. The fp16 side of the rest was not split here: the location is fp32's, on B's signs (R12) and both sides' (R7).

## What this changes

- **For the blog (Horace follow-up, §5):** a random sign's ~5 W has a two-part account on one card, both parts measured in one session on designed tiles: about 0.4–0.5 of it is the adder alternating between adding and subtracting while the sums hold, alike in fp32 and fp16 on zero products; the rest is the running sums it scatters, because a lane alternates between accumulators that now hold different values and reads, adds and writes back a different word every micro-op, about 35 fJ per differing bit.
