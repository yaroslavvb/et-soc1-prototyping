Four readings of that table:
- **TenC is read every cycle**, while the FSM works and through the core's 8-cycle loop, by all 16 TIMA units, whether or not anything issues. That runs at the same rate in every int8 arm (9.80–9.81 × 10¹² a second in `int8.zeros`, `m0`, `k8`, `r8` and `m0k8`), so it is a per-time quantity, the shape E109's and E124's R3 and R0 read on the card.
- **The TIMA controls** are about 107 register bits clocked on every working cycle (33,167 an op over 310 working cycles), at 6.4 × 10¹³ a second, also per-time. They are not new to the count: they sat inside the first bench's "rest". They exceed the whole +5.7 × 10¹³ register bits a second by which int8's loop out-clocks fp32's. The rest of that difference, at E124's rates ([`corrections-check.txt`](corrections-check.txt) F6): fp32-only registers, the float FSM's (`vpu_tensorfma.v:628`'s 32-bit instruction register, `:132`'s TXFMA clock delay, `:622` and `:624`), take −2.49 × 10¹³. int8-only registers outside the TIMA group add +1.58 × 10¹³: half of it is TenTMP's control (`vpu_tensorfma.v:940-946`, priced in the scratchpad row), and half is TenTMP's 64-bit zero mask (`vpu_ctrl.v:738`), which no candidate row prices (0.008–0.017 W at the bench's constants). Registers clocked in both formats add +0.21 × 10¹³. *(Corrected after verification, F6: it said "fp32-only registers, such as `vpu_tensorfma.v:628` and the lane register file's, net −0.7 × 10¹³", which is the net of every register outside the TIMA group.)*
- **TenB is where B goes in both formats.** It is the only TenB storage there is, and both TIMA units' write-data latches open on every B write. Per op the two formats write it identically; int8 writes it 1.71 times as often a second. So fp32's own no-issue level bounds it, as it bounds the scratchpad's reads (result 5).
- **The two formats request the same 16,384 scratchpad bits an op.** int8 reads A in 64-bit halves into TenTMP (`vpu_tensorfma.v:338-366`), so it makes 224 reads an op against fp32's 288.

**3. TenC's read enable carries over: on a card, fp32 reads TenC too, at one frozen address.** TenC's read enable is `f2_tima_ren3`, a register on `tima_clk_en = working && req_int8` (`vpu_tensorfma.v:841`, `:917`). It loads `~first_pass` (`:865`, through `:885`) on every int8 working cycle. So an int8 op with first_pass set clears it during its first pass (`f1.m0k1.int8.zeros`: high for 14.25 of its 34.25 cycles), and outside int8 work nothing enables it, so only a reset clears it ([`corrections-check.txt`](corrections-check.txt) F7). *(Corrected after verification, F7: it said "and only a reset clears it".)* TenC is clocked by the lane's ungated clock (`vpu_lane_tima.v`, `tenc_rf`). So once an int8 op has run past its first pass, the enable stays high through every later fp32 op, until an int8 op's first pass or a reset. E109's and E124's passes interleave int8 and fp32 runs on cards that had run int8 since their last reset. Run as the card runs them, after two int8 ops (`+pre=2`), **`pre.m0.fp32.zeros` reads TenC 8,708 times an op (16 × 544.25), none at a new address**. That is 9.80 × 10¹² a second, as int8's ([`priced.md`](priced.md) §2). In the card's order, then, TenC's reads are not int8's alone. What stays int8's is the walk of its read address: 7.89 × 10¹² reads a second at a new address, 1.48 × 10¹³ address-bit toggles. Whether silicon does the same is not known here: this is the later revision's logic. Whether a card's minions are reset between launches is not known either.

At the cards' issue price, the TIMA controls alone carry about two thirds of N. Their break-even, 24.5 / 27.2 fJ a clocked bit, is only 1.4–1.5 times that price's mean, closer than TenC's read port comes to the bench's price (2.1–7.1 times). - **It is not one price for every bit.** At it, int8's no-issue loop's enabled bits alone would cost 12.7 ± 6.3 W on card 1 (13.2 ± 2.5 W on aifoundry3), against that loop's measured 1.77 ± 0.20 W (2.11 ± 0.08 W): it can hold for at most about a sixth of them. From reset it would put TenC's output-register reads alone at 5.4 W (5.6 W), more than three times N.
- **It cannot be TenB's or TenA's price.** At it, fp32's own TenB and TenA bits would cost 0.79 ± 0.39 W (0.83 ± 0.16 W), against fp32's whole no-issue level of 0.20 ± 0.04 W (0.38 ± 0.09 W). So the 0.34–0.35 W it would give TenB is excluded, and fp32's level bounds TenB instead (result 5).
- **What it leaves to int8's own registers.** At the issue price, the registers int8 alone clocks come to 1.37 ± 0.68 W on card 1 and 1.43 ± 0.27 W on aifoundry3, most of N (0.89 ± 0.53 and 0.82 ± 0.19 of it). TenTMP's latch bits are left out: they sit in the latch module that holds TenA and TenB, whose bits the issue price cannot price. This is a post hoc reading on one price, not a fit.

## What this changes

**Item 11 (critic 5) narrows; it does not close.** The bench now counts every candidate the critic named except the core, per stream, for E109's and E124's design. At the bench's own constants the four price at **0.47–0.96 W of N from reset (1.57 ± 0.21 W on card 1, registered; 1.74 ± 0.16 W on aifoundry3, post hoc, three passes), and 0.15–0.29 W in the card's order**. So the bench, as fitted, does not explain int8's no-issue excess. Whether its counts can depends on the price of a clocked register bit, which nothing here fixes. *(Corrected after verification, F1.)* Its breakdown:

## What this cannot say

- **RTL, not silicon, and a later revision.** The carry-over of TenC's read enable is core-et's logic; the ET-SoC-1's own RTL is not public here.
- **A macro's own read energy is not in the bench**, nor is the dcache's SRAM, nor the core. The prices above are the bench's for the bits it sees: TenC's output register, address and data lines.
- **The price of a clocked register bit.** The bench's constants (1.07–2.13 fJ), the cards' issue price for int8's enabled bits on zeros (17–18 fJ) and the same cost per clock edge (about 0.5 fJ, which prices the TIMA controls at nothing, since they see an edge every cycle in both formats) disagree. Which one holds for the TIMA controls decides whether they carry 5% of N or two thirds (result 5b). *(Added after verification, F1.)*
- **The constants are extrapolated.** They were fitted as D over each format's zeros on E78's kinds. Here they price what runs on zeros, which no fit has tested (Q132(b) §14). M2 int8's has no committed spread.
- **The idle bracket is not modelled.** If a card's minions clock the VPU at idle with TenC's read enable high, those reads are in the idle level too. Either way, they cancel in N in the card's order.
- **The scratchpad is the bench's array.** The read requests (number, size, address) are the RTL's; the data bus's toggles are the bench model's.
