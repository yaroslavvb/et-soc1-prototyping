  The measured price per flipped bit barely moves — 87 to 129 fJ over m = 1 to 23 — while the
  single-bit price list says it should run from 106 to 382 fJ per bit over the same masks.
  Along this ladder the position premium does not add when bits flip together. It is NOT
  gone in general: at a fixed m = 2 it is paid in full (position.py), so the compression is
  a function of m, and this ladder always takes the lowest bits.

  two defensible flat prices from this same ladder:

      its intercept is -0.420 W, 71% of the m=1 rung, which is the whole difference)

  three candidate laws, FITTED ON AND SCORED ON these same seven rungs (in-sample, not a test):
    flat, 107 fJ per flipped bit, position ignored rms  14.4%   worst  22.7%

  >>> the finding is the free exponent, 0.993, which is 1.00 within its scatter; the two

      Along THIS ladder, board power counts flipped bits and all but ignores which ones.
      The out-of-sample test is nrd_a / nrd_b in part 4, and both laws miss there.
