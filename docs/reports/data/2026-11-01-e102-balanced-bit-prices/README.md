## Results

**In plain words.** A low mantissa bit costs the same whether or not the accumulators are balanced: E65's and E67's low floor is the flip itself, not a composite. The composite appears between bits 11 and 16 and reaches about 6× at the top of the B-side mantissa. By the frozen counts on real accumulators, that is because a low-bit difference is rounded away inside a large sum and never makes two accumulators differ. On the B side a balanced mantissa flip costs about the same at every position run (87–176 fJ, one base word); on A it does not (83–636 fJ). At the exponent and the sign the order reverses: the balanced layout is 2–6× dearer, by the frozen counts because the aligner or the adder's add/subtract now moves. int8's B list resolves on the rail: with A = 1, on one set of base bytes, odd bits cost 2–3× even bits (1.97× [1.69, 2.33] by the registered masks, 2.85× by the singles, post hoc), E79's shape, on a second card. **The fixed RTL bench, frozen before the data, predicted the float prices to 0.15–0.26 W rms and their order, though not every point (4.3× at bit 16, where B reads 5.8× and A 7.1×); it undercounts int8's single bits, as a behavioural `*` would (a reading).** Along the way E102 found a fault in two lab tools that other lanes' models rest on: about 4 W on E92's fp16 tiles at prices fitted with both terms, whose "frozen" sums, by the public RTL, are not frozen; C has been read back on no card (§ A fault).

## A fault in two lab tools: `make_e88` and `make_e92` start an op from C = fl((n−1) S)

*Rewritten Sun 1 Nov 2026 after verification (§ Corrections after verification, C1–C4).*

**The fault.** `make_e88.simulate` and `make_e92.sign_counts` count an op from C = fl((n−1) S), S being one op's exact sum: the accumulator a launch would hold if it rounded only once, at the end. Their docstrings call that exact for integer and absorbed designs. It is not exact for fp16's absorbed designs, and not wherever two sums are equal or differ only by how they got there, because a real accumulation rounds at every addition. `make_e92.self_check` could not see it: it proved fp16's tiles frozen with `make_e88.emulate_launch`, which rounds to nearest, over four ops. **Why they walk: not rounding toward zero alone.** fp16's accumulate is set to round toward zero (`vpu_tensorfma.v:691-693`), and the manual says how: "The three single-precision values, that is the two products and the accumulator from the previous iteration, are added with no intermediate rounding. The final sum is calculated using round towards zero. This 3-way addition is not IEEE754 compatible" (ET Programmer's Reference Manual, §9.3.3, TensorFMA16A32). Read literally, that walks `h_sub`, `c_alt` and `h_allsub`, whose products subtract on net, and it leaves `hh_sub` and `hl_sub` frozen: each has one product adding and one subtracting, ±1, exact sum 0. They walk in the RTL because its window truncates each aligned term separately: a negated term that lost bits gets no carry-in (`txfma_7s/txfma_c2.v:309-310`), and the window is cleared below B24 (`txfma_7s/txfma_f4.v:82-85`). So a product that subtracts takes one ulp off a large sum even if its partner adds. `fp16_model.py`, `accum.c` and the RTL's `txfma_top` agree on that: 0x58000000 goes to 0x57ffffff with both, the high or the low product subtracting, and stays with both adding (`fault_e92_fp16_e102.txt` part A). My own emulator of the manual's sentence holds one word on `hh_sub` and `hl_sub` at n = 2, 16 and 1,000, where the RTL has two; `s_abs` has 10 words against 9, `r_abs` 5 against 1. The RTL bench's README makes the same point: 33,793 of its 40,000 hard vectors equal the exact sum rounded toward zero. **So there are three readings, not two: frozen, the manual's, the RTL's.** `corrections-check.txt` [C2] gives the words each holds. ## Interpretation (labelled: a reading, not measured)

- **Rounding.** A real fp32 accumulation adds a product to a sum about 2²³ times its size late in a launch, so only the product's top bits reach the sum. Two addends that differ below that round to the same increment, so the sums stay identical. This is `accum.c`'s behaviour, the bench's and the desk's, and it fits the card: ratio 1 where the count says the sums are equal, and rising where it says they part. E102 did not read C back.
- **The int8 list's shape looks like a radix-4 recoder on B.** An odd bit enters two digits and an even bit one, which predicts about 2×; a mask's cost then follows the digits it touches rather than its bits: 0x15 3 of 3 (measured 1.05 of its singles), 0x2A 4 of 6 (0.73). It predicts too little for the nibble masks (0.50 against 0.69, 0.72) and for `bk_ff` (0.42 against 0.63), and bit 6 is above its even neighbours ([F]; written after the data). The public RTL says only `*`; which gates the netlist has is not something E102 can see, and no public source was found to settle it.

## Caveats

One card, one clock, 499 mV. **One base word per side**: on A a ratio depends on the word's neighbouring bits, so A's non-monotonic list is this word's. **int8 has A = 1 and one set of eight base bytes on every tile**, E79's, so its second card is not a second tile. Ten of 32 fp32 positions, six of fp16's on two sides, and nothing is interpolated here. Three kept passes in T (t = 4.30), four or five in G, so a board interval in T is wide. A ratio across protocols (bits 16 and 20) inherits R6's unresolved ±0.2 W. The train's own-zero interpolation under-reads the first quarter of a float train, which raises T's steps by a few hundredths of a watt on average (compare `primary` with [M]). A rail price is about 0.80 of a board price here (23 steps of 1 W or more, 0.71–0.87, [K]). The counts are the desk's and the bench's: C was not read back.
