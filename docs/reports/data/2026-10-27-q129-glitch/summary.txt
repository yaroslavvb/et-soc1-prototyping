A FUNCTIONAL CHECK ON THE TIMED RUN (positive evidence, and a caveat)
  the timed run's own SETTLED count is 2,118,201 against the zero run's
  2,115,833: +0.112% over 32 nets of 17,122. So the two passes compute the same
  function to 1 part in 900, which they must for the ratio to mean anything - worth
  stating because the netlist misses its SDC by about 2x. Using the timed run's own
  settled as the denominator instead gives x1.5699 at the whole design against
  x1.5716: a 0.1% effect on the headline, and the published convention (the
  zero-delay pass is the denominator) is the one used.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
  - one tile per pattern and one netlist pair; no repeat, so no repeat-variance
    interval. The LADDER fits do carry a regression interval and it is printed above
    (4-9% on the slopes); the headline ratios carry no interval at all.
  - the design misses its 1250 ps SDC by about 2x, so these delays belong to a netlist
    that would not run at 800 MHz; and the SDF is ASAP7 TT at 0.700 V while the energy
    is priced at the 0.517 V rail, where every delay would be longer.
  - inertial delay with full pulse rejection is the conservative model; a transport
    model would report more glitches.
  - 4.6% of the 'timed' netlist ran at ZERO delay in gsim too: sdf_bind
    iopath_missing = 1,974 of 42,486 input-pin paths, essentially every flip-flop's and
    clock gate's CLK->Q, because the behavioural stand-in asap7_seq_behav.v carries no
    specify block. gsim therefore launches every flip-flop simultaneously, removing the
    launch skew that would deepen these glitches: x1.572 is conservative on that axis
    too. The probe faults Icarus for exactly this; it is only fair to fault gsim for it.
  - WHICH FLOOR-REASON EACH RUN RETIRED, of Q126(e)'s three: Q129(a) retired the
    coupling/SPEF one (detailed route closed, coupling extracted, and the wire turned
    out not to be a floor at all); THIS run retires the coverage one (the routed
    netlist is what is simulated, so no extracted net goes unpriced). The third - the
    ledger covers one lane's arithmetic while the board also pays for the register-file
    read ports, the operand bus, the result path, pipeline registers and leakage -
    stands untouched by either.
