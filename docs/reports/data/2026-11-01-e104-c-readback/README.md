## Results

**In plain words.** On one card, 110 reads on 44 designed file tiles, all 1,024 minions on one value each, equal the RTL's arithmetic word for word. 41 of those reads could tell a frozen sum from a moving one, and 21 could tell the manual's sentence from the RTL's. (The host has read C on its own `--values small` tiles since September and skipped file tiles, so these are the first raw reads on designed file tiles.) E92's fp16 "frozen" tiles are not frozen. fp16 truncates each aligned product separately, so an absorbed product that subtracts takes one unit off a large sum on that micro-op, even if its partner adds. `h_sub`'s two halves read 4f800000 and 4f7fffe9 after 2 ops and are 12% apart by op 199,874 and 29% apart by op 541,999. The manual's sentence (exact sum, then toward zero) fails on silicon for `hh_sub`, `hl_sub`, `s_abs` and E88's `r_abs`, `r_walk`; E92's frozen reading fails on all 11 tiles that can tell. fp32's frozen sums hold. **E92's fp16 watts stand and its reading goes. 6.71 W for `h_sub` − `h_add` splits, as run, into 3.55 ± 0.29 W with the adder alternating and the halves together, about what fp32 pays (3.76 ± 1.48 W), and 3.16 ± 0.51 W more that goes with the two halves parting; at `h_sub`'s own alternations that is about 3.8–3.9 W and 2.8–2.9 W. The bench's model said 3.72 and 3.27 W before the data. "1.55× per alternation" goes for absorbed products: with the halves together the ratio is 0.95 ± 0.30. "About 92% remains frozen" goes: in a sum that truly holds, on zero products, random signs cost 2.15 ± 0.33 W, about 45% of fp16's ~4.8 W step carried from another card.** On sums that move alike the premium is 3.27 ± 1.96 W in fp16 and 2.12 ± 2.46 W in fp32; that ratio is not resolved, and it centres near 1.55.

**§5.4,** in place of "Where the cost goes is not measured" and the guess it names:

> On designed tiles, with products absorbed and a lane's two running sums kept together (E104; aifoundry1 card 1, 600 MHz, two kept passes, C read back), making the adder alternate costs 3.76 ± 1.48 W in fp32 and 3.55 ± 0.29 W in fp16 over a tile that always adds, at 90% of `h_sub`'s alternations: about 900–1,050 fJ per lane alternation in both, by baseline. fp32's sum holds; fp16's walks 7 units an op in one word. A steady subtract costs nothing measurable in fp32 (E92, 0.04 ± 0.35 W). The price falls when sums move: 2.12 ± 2.46 W in fp32 and 3.27 ± 1.96 W in fp16, ratio not resolved. When fp16's two halves part, as in E92's `h_sub`, power rises a further 3.16 ± 0.51 W (about 2.9 W with the extra alternations taken out); the bench places about 70% of those toggles inside the FMA and about 30% in the lane, bypass and register file. Of the ~5 W random-sign step, about a third to a half is there with the sum read back as held; this is an estimate, across two cards and designed tiles. The rest, by subtraction, goes with the sums moving. E88's 2.52 ± 0.19 W of 4.78 in fp32 now sits on a sum read back as held. A reading: neither format's step is priced by its sign bits.

## Caveats

Two kept passes in the primary. One card, one clock, designed tiles. C sits at n × 2^31 or small integers, not where real tensors land. The reads are at the end of a launch of n ops, so they say nothing of C inside a launch beyond what the words imply. The power runs use the existing host and the read-back an added flag, with the same kernel; the reads ran on the same tiles, sha256-checked. The E92 values on this page are from another card, carried by a scale. `z_` measures a design with zero products, whose multiplier path does not move, and `z_s` holds A's signs still, so it is not `s_abs` with the walk removed. `u_sub`'s halves differ by 1 ulp inside an op (half 0 loses on even k, half 1 on odd k) and meet at its end, and fp16's walks 7 ulps an op; `u_sub` and `h_add` do not share operands: B's sign differs at every even k from 2 to 14, and at k = 0 and k = 15. The premium over `h_add` understates the adder by the C-sign term and a little, and is not what a real result does: products absorbed, aligned operand all 0s or all 1s, the maximum swing. The bit counts behind fJ per result bit move with n (E102's verifier: 58,584–142,936 at one index; 102,851 at 128). The watts are the board's; the rail figures are about 0.8 of them.
