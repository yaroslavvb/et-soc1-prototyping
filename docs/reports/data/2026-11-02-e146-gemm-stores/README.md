## The answer

**Where C stays in the L3, a store of C costs no time: two GEMMs with the same lines an op, one storing a C tile every 128 ops and one every 256, ran within 0.3% of each other in every format (T800(`L8`) ÷ T800(`L4`) = 0.997, 0.998, 0.999), where the frozen store term predicted 0.980, 0.979 and 0.967. R1: NO STORE TERM in fp32, fp16 and int8, and in each of the two passes.** Where the operands come from the L3, a store into DRAM costs no more than one into the L3: `D1` (64 MB of C streaming, a store every 32 ops) ran at 1.2667 µs an op in fp32 zeros against `L1`'s 1.2699 (8 MB of C held), both at E119's L3-served walk's pace (`c8s`, 1.2655); where the operands stream from DRAM too, C's DRAM traffic does cost time (E126's `c16` against `c16s`), and the one cadence contrast there, `D8` ÷ `D4` (1.034 in every format, against MS 1.037-1.048, MAB 1.053-1.066, M0 1.052-1.057), is confounded with A's fresh lines (8 against 21.9 an op), so it is printed, not scored. **What E136's law got wrong is where the lines come from, not C's stores**: the operand split (a B line, which streams into the multiplier, costs a fourth to a seventh of an A line, which the kernel waits for) predicts the L3-held rungs `L4` and `L8` out of sample to within 1.4% in every format, and both E136 GEMMs replicate to 0.4%; the misses that remain are shapes whose B panels come back after 36-44 MB of other traffic (`D2` 18% faster than every model, the fresh shape `F6` 9% faster), which a strict-LRU L3 model calls fresh and the card serves from the L3 a third to three fifths of the time. - **The store term is refuted for time.** Read straight from the pair that differs only in cadence, a store costs 0.71, 0.37 and 0.30 µs at 800 MHz in fp32, fp16 and int8 and −0.12, −0.27 and +0.02 µs at 600 MHz (`posthoc.txt` §2), against the 4.8-8.0 µs the frozen term needed to explain E136; the 0.3% differences are inside the 0.74% that any cell's T800 moved between the two passes (R8). So E136's post hoc reading ("pricing the C-store cadence moved fp32 to −1.5%") is not a cadence effect: that footing (E126's `c16s`) took away the cost of C's DRAM traffic beside fresh loads, which a store every 256 ops carries an eighth of.
- **MS has the lowest ladder rms, but not through its store term.** At a store every 32 ops MS is M0; at 64-256 its correction is (n_miss/128) σ (1/N − 1/32), nearly the same factor on every shape with KB ≥ 2, so it acts as a cheaper price for those shapes' extra B lines. Where the cadence is the only difference (`L4`, `L8`) the data side with MAB. MAB, which has no store term at all, gets `L2`, `L4`, `L8` and `D8` to within 1.4% (two of them out of sample) and fails on the KB = 1 shapes (`L1`, `D1`: the card ran 12-27% slower than MAB says; post hoc, the desk's L2 model gives A 16-28 L2 hits an op there that the card does not show, since both run at `c8s`'s pace, where A always misses) and on `D2`.
- **Where the lines are served sets the rest.** With MAB's frozen prices, the measured T800 needs the L3 to have served 57-59% of the B lines the desk's strict-LRU L3 calls fresh in `D2` (a B panel comes back after 36 MB, C included), 33-38% in `F6` (44 MB), and none in `D4` (56 MB) and `D8` (96 MB) (`posthoc.txt` §7, every format alike). The L3 keeps lines past its nominal 32 MB that LRU would have dropped: the time law's next term is an L3 retention curve by reuse distance, not a store term.
- **A post hoc refit on E146's own 24 ladder cells** (`posthoc.txt` §3) fits them to 6.6-8.9% rms and predicts `F6` 4.2-7.5% slow: refitting the prices cannot fix a line count the desk model gets wrong.
- **The stores' energy is not resolved by the registered reading.** R4's first-second power at 800 MHz scatters by up to 13 W between the two passes in the L3-held random cells, which run at the 66 W burst cap (`L8` fp32 random 69.84 and 56.49 W, `L1` 68.69 and 57.55). Post hoc, each burst's whole-window mean power at its own clock (the pairs ran at the same clock and rate) gives 238 ± 95 µJ for one store step of random C (every minion storing one 1 KB tile; 12 per-pass contrasts, ± one standard error) and 40 ± 44 µJ for zeros (`posthoc.txt` §8), against the fitted 288-618 and 251-371: the zeros' part of E136's power over-read was not a store energy, and random C's stores cost, if anything, about 0.23 nJ a byte (28 pJ a bit): 0.7 W at a store every 256 ops, about 6 W at every 32, both within 2.5 standard errors of nothing.
- **The heat law holds in bursts and misses the fresh shape's sustained die by 3.6-4.6 °C.** Every one of the 102 bursts' die maxima fell within 3 °C of E136's law (0.98 °C rms); `F6` fp32 random reached the 65 °C trip and the governor held it at 745 MHz where G2 said 800, its window die 65.3 °C against 60.7 frozen; int8 full ran at 794 MHz and 61.7 °C against 58.1. The rest reading (22.06 W, 46.0 °C) equals E136's at 15:50 (22.02 W, 46.0 °C), so it was not a warmer card at rest.

T = wall ÷ ops per minion over the launches whose kernel clock read at or above 790 MHz, every kind pooled (32-169 launches a shape and format; `per_shape`); the burst power is the mean of the two passes' first-second medians at 800 MHz (`cells.*.burst_p800_w_runs`). **So for the blog's kinds table, on aifoundry2 under its own governor:** a large GEMM's time per op does not depend on how often it writes its result: what sets it is how many of its operand lines miss the cores' caches, which operand they feed (the one the core waits for costs four to seven times the one it streams), and whether the 32 MB L3 still holds them. The kind of number changes the power (34-38 W for zeros in every format, 54-70 W for random fp32 in these bursts), not the speed.

## What E146 says about the page and the model

- **Section 11 item 1 narrows:** the large GEMM's 7-13% time error is not C's store cadence (R1, three formats, two passes) and not C's destination where the operands come from the L3 (`D1` = `L1`); it is the line classes: the operand a missing line feeds and the L3's retention past 32 MB. Still open: an L3 retention curve by reuse distance (`D2` 36 MB: 57-59% kept; `F6` 44 MB: 33-38%; `D4` 56 MB: none), the KB = 1 shapes' A lines, the vendor's own binary and a second card.
- **For the power model (Q138):** time per op on 24 out-of-sample ladder cells, E136's law 13.5% rms → the store-term model 8.3% / the operand split 10.9%; on the fresh shape, 18.2% → 9.1% / 9.9% (a post hoc refit on the ladder: 4-7%). Burst power: E136's law 2.69 W rms, with the store's energy 2.96 W (worse), fresh shape 1.73 → 2.50 W: drop the fitted store energy term (post hoc the stores' energy is 238 ± 95 µJ a store step for random C, 0.4-0.8 of the fit, and 40 ± 44 for zeros, about a tenth). The heat law: bursts 0.98 °C rms (102 of 102 within 3 °C); the fresh shape's sustained die 3.6-4.6 °C over.
- **For the governor:** G2 (E136's better footing) predicted `F6` fp32 random sustained at 800 MHz; it was held at the trip at 745 MHz, 56.15 W.

**For the post, in short sentences.** Each names its card and clock.
- On aifoundry2, writing each finished tile of a large matrix multiply's result every 128 or every 256 steps made no difference to its speed: with the result kept in the chip's L3, the two ran within 0.3% of each other in fp32, fp16 and int8 (`rules.R1`).
- Sending those results to DRAM instead of keeping them in the chip's 32 MB L3 cost no time either, while the inputs came from the L3 (`per_shape` `D1`, `L1`).
- What does set the speed is which input the missing data feeds: a line of A, which the core waits for, costs four to seven times a line of B, which it streams (`predictions-e146.json` `fitted`, `rules.R2`).
- The L3 kept data it "should" have dropped: when a block came back after 36 MB of other traffic, more than half of it was still there (`posthoc.txt` §7).
- Predicted before the run, a never-run 3,072 × 2,048 × 3,072 multiply came in 9% faster than the best model said; its power within 2 W and its burst temperature within 1.5 °C, but its sustained temperature 4 °C over (`rules.R5`).
