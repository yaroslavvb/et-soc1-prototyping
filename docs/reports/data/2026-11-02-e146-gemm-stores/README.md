- **The store term is refuted for time.** Read straight from the pair that differs only in cadence, a store costs 0.71, 0.37 and 0.30 µs at 800 MHz in fp32, fp16 and int8 (per pass −0.03 to 0.97 µs, `posthoc.txt` §10) and −0.12, −0.27 and +0.02 µs at 600 MHz (`posthoc.txt` §2), against the 4.8-8.0 µs the frozen term needed to explain E136; the 0.3% differences are inside the 0.74% that any cell's T800 moved between the two passes (R8). So E136's post hoc reading ("pricing the C-store cadence moved fp32 to −1.5%") is not a cadence effect: that footing (E126's `c16s`) took away the cost of C's DRAM traffic beside fresh loads, which a store every 256 ops carries an eighth of.
- **MS has the lowest ladder rms, but not through its store term.** At a store every 32 ops MS is M0; at 64-256 its correction is (n_miss/128) σ (1/N − 1/32), nearly the same factor on every shape with KB ≥ 2, so it acts as a cheaper price for those shapes' extra B lines. Where the cadence is the only difference (`L4`, `L8`) the data side with MAB. MAB, which has no store term at all, gets `L2`, `L4`, `L8` and `D8` to within 1.4% (two of them out of sample) and fails on the KB = 1 shapes (`L1`, `D1`: the card ran 12-27% slower than MAB says; post hoc, the desk's L2 model gives A 16-28 L2 hits an op there that the card does not show, since both run at `c8s`'s pace, where A always misses) and on `D2`.
- **Where the lines are served sets the rest.** With MAB's frozen prices, the measured T800 needs the L3 to have served 57-59% of the B lines the desk's strict-LRU L3 calls fresh in `D2` (a B panel comes back after 36 MB, C included), 33-38% in `F6` (44 MB), and none in `D4` (56 MB) and `D8` (96 MB) (`posthoc.txt` §7, every format alike; inferred through MAB's prices, not measured line by line). Either the L3 keeps lines past its nominal 32 MB that LRU would have dropped, or C's lines take less of it than the model gives them: the distances count C's (without them 20, 36, 40 and 80 MB), and whether C's stores occupy the L3 was not tested. *(Corrected after verification, C5: this said only the first.)* Either way the time law's next term is an L3 retention curve by reuse distance, not a store term.
- **A post hoc refit on E146's own 24 ladder cells** (`posthoc.txt` §3) fits them to 6.6-8.9% rms and predicts `F6` 4.2-7.5% slow: refitting the prices cannot fix a line count the desk model gets wrong.
- **The stores' energy is not resolved by the registered reading.** R4's first-second power at 800 MHz scatters by up to 13 W between the two passes in the L3-held random cells, which run at the 66 W burst cap (`L8` fp32 random 69.84 and 56.49 W, `L1` 68.69 and 57.55). Post hoc, each burst's whole-window mean power at its own clock, with each shape at its own time per op (e = (P − B) T, as §4 and R4 put it), gives **92 ± 86 µJ for one store step of random C** (every minion storing one 1 KB tile; 12 per-pass contrasts, ± one standard error; `L4/L8` alone 147 ± 140, `D4/D8` alone 38 ± 110) and **18 ± 45 µJ for zeros** (`posthoc.txt` §8b), against the fitted 288-618 and 251-371. Both are consistent with zero (random 1.1 standard errors from it), at 0.15-0.32 of the fit for random C and 0.05-0.07 for zeros. Two standard errors above the mean would be 0.25 nJ a byte of C, 0.8 W at a store every 256 ops and 6 W at every 32. *(Corrected after verification, C1: the first version multiplied each pair's power difference by the pair's mean time per op and said "the pairs ran at the same clock and rate", which is true of `L4/L8` only; `D8` runs 3.4% slower per op than `D4`, so its lower power at its slower rate read as a store saving. **So for the blog's kinds table, on aifoundry2 under its own governor:** a large GEMM's time per op does not depend on how often it writes its result: what sets it is how many of its operand lines miss the cores' caches, which operand they feed (in the operand-split model solved from four earlier runs, a line from the L3 that the core waits for is priced at four to seven times one it streams, at 800 MHz), and whether the 32 MB L3 still holds them. *(Corrected after verification, C5: this gave the model's price as a measured cost.)* The kind of number changes the power (34-38 W for zeros in every format, 54-70 W for random fp32 in these bursts), not the speed.

## What E146 says about the page and the model

- **Section 11 item 1 narrows:** the large GEMM's 7-13% time error is not C's store cadence (R1, three formats, two passes) and not C's destination where the operands come from the L3 (`D1` = `L1`); it is the line classes: the operand a missing line feeds and the L3's retention past 32 MB. **For the post, in short sentences.** Each names its card and clock.
- On aifoundry2, writing each finished tile of a large matrix multiply's result every 128 or every 256 steps made no difference to its speed: with the result kept in the chip's L3, the two ran within 0.3% of each other in fp32, fp16 and int8 (`rules.R1`).
