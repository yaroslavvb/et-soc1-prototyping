## The answer

**Reuse is the variable, and DRAM sets most of a fresh walk's extra time over a warm block: about two thirds in fp32 and fp16, 55% in int8 (C1).** At aifoundry3's pinned 600 MHz:

- **Passing over each of 16 fresh blocks R times before the walk moves on divides the walk's extra time per op by R.** T(R) = Tinf + D / R fits the five rungs to within 0.22% in fp32, 0.19% in fp16 and 0.49% in int8, with Tinf 0.8% under a repeated block's time in fp32 and fp16 and 3.0% under it in int8 (R3: REUSE IS THE VARIABLE, as frozen). A block's first pass costs what a pass of the cold walk costs, and every pass after it what a repeated block's pass costs: each fresh block adds 108.5 µs to every minion's work in fp32 (134 µs in int8).
- **With the 8 blocks' 24 MB of operands inside the L3, 31% of the cold walk's extra time stays** in fp32 and fp16, and 46% in int8 (R4: DRAM SETS MOST OF IT; frozen 27% and 39%). Post hoc, on the desk L2 model's 128 missing lines per shire-op and at the launch's pace, a line missing from the L2 that the L3 serves costs 4.15 ns of each op, and one in the walk that goes to DRAM costs 13.25 ns, ×3.2; on the mean minion they cost about 2.8 and 13.2 ns, ×4.8 (C2). The walk leaves DRAM: the DDR rail droops 0.60 and 0.79 mV on zeros and random, as a repeated block does (0.48, 1.00), against 1.90 and 3.07 mV in the 16-block walk (R4c PASS). 4 blocks (14 MB) run within 0.02% of 8 (26 MB), so 26 MB stays resident (R4b PASS).
- **The L3 has to hold C as well.** With each block's C beside it, 40 MB in all, the same 8-block walk keeps 95% of the cold excess (R5: C ALLOCATES). Sharing C in the 16-block walk removes 14% of the excess, not the 40% frozen (R6: PARTIAL).
- **In the walk the L3 serves, minions finish unevenly**: the mean minion takes 749 cycles an op and the slowest 855, and the launch waits for the slowest. Every other arm, the DRAM walk and the whole repeat ladder included, finishes together (slowest ÷ mean 1.000-1.013). On the mean minion the 8-block walk would keep 21% of the excess, not 31% (post hoc, `corrections-check.txt` §1-2).
- **So a large GEMM on this chip falls on two axes**: how many times each L2-sized block is reused before the walk moves on (the extra time divides by that number), and whether the walk's working set, its C included, fits the 32 MB L3 (post hoc, a missing line costs a third as much when it does at the launch's pace, a fifth on the mean minion, C2). **E107 still does not run a large GEMM** and does not place one: that needs a GEMM's own reuse and footprint, the speed half (what a clock gap buys in each arm, which needs a governed card: it is left for later), and the vendor's binary, which can be built instrumented from public sources (below) and was not run.

## Eight blocks inside the L3: the walk's limit

- **DRAM sets about 69% of the cold walk's extra time over a warm block in fp32 and fp16, 55% in int8**; the rest is the L2's misses served by the L3. Of the cold walk's whole time per op, DRAM's part is 45% in fp32 and fp16 and 44% in int8 (C1). Frozen: 73% and 61%, from E101's packed arm, whose misses the L3 serves at 3.58 ns a line in fp32 against the 4.15 ns this walk's pace gives. Both prices are post hoc, on desk-model line counts; on the mean minion this walk's is about 2.8 ns (C2).
- **Is it DRAM's bandwidth?** Not by the bytes alone: past the L3 the cold walk moves 18.9 GB/s of A + B and 12.6 GB/s of C's stores at 600 MHz, under the lab's 47.9-53.4 GB/s past the L3 (`docs/findings/05-claims.md`), and the L2-miss traffic, on the desk model's line count, is 101 GB/s in the DRAM walk against 183 GB/s in the L3 walk (`posthoc.txt` §3, labelled). What DRAM costs per line here, 13.25 ns against 4.15 at the launch's pace (13.2 against 2.8 on the mean minion; post hoc, C2), is its service time under this walk's traffic, latency and queueing together; E107 does not separate the two.
- **C counts twice**: as footprint (40 MB with C per block push the 8-block walk back to 95% of the excess, R5) and as traffic (sharing C in the 16-block walk takes 14% of the excess away, R6, against the 40% the bytes would give).

## The vendor's own binary: can an instrumented build be made from public sources?

*Desk only: nothing was built or run. Section 11 item 1's last sentence.*

**Yes, from public sources alone, with two libraries the boxes do not have installed.**

- **The kernel** is in et-platform (`external/et-platform` at `836a4ab60`, Apache-2.0): `gp-sdk/device/sdk/libautogen/matmul_f32_1024_512_512/`, a static library whose entry, `uberKernel_RAWKERNEL_entry_point`, is gp-sdk's test kernel `autogen_matmul.elf` (`gp-sdk/device/tests/CMakeLists.txt` lines 136-137, `tests/autogenMatmul/autogen_matmul.cpp`). **Its host** is `gp-sdk/host/sdk/src/autogen_matmul/autogen_matmul.cpp`, the `matmul_launcher` executable (`gp-sdk/host/sdk/CMakeLists.txt` lines 104-109; `-k` kernel, `-n` launches, `-d sysemu|fake|silicon`).
- **What the boxes lack:** `libautogen` links `dnnLibrary::dnn_lib`, and the launcher links gp-sdk's `etsoc_gpsdk`. aifoundry3's `/opt/et`, listed today, has et-common-libs, the runtime, the device layer, sw-sysemu and the RISC-V toolchain, and neither dnnLibrary nor gp-sdk. et-platform's own superbuild builds both from the same tree (its `CMakeLists.txt` lines 67-97: `DeviceProject(dnn-library ...)`, then gp-sdk's `HostProject(host ...)` and `DeviceProject(device ...)`).
- **The instrumentation** is the minion cycle counter the lab's kernels already read (`hpmcounter3`; et-common-libs `include/etsoc/drivers/pmu/pmu.h` has the read), at the entry and the exit of `startUberKernelComputes` on each compute hart (thread 0 of shires 0-31, `uberKernel.cc` line 42), written to one 64-byte line per hart in a buffer the launcher reads back, as this lab's `SpResult` does. That gives each launch's compute time with the 2 ms launch overhead out of it, and needs no loop inside the launch. The helper threads have nothing to time: `MatmulCmd_act_pref.cc` and `MatmulCmd_w_pref.cc` only send credits, as E101 found.
- **"About 58 µs a launch" is not a timing.** It is E101.md's desk figure, 64 ops a minion at the FMA's 546 cycles and 600 MHz (58.2 µs). Nobody here has timed the binary.
- **What E107 adds** (a desk prediction, labelled): the vendor's launch is one cold pass over one block, and the host's upload leaves A and B in the L3 (a host write allocates in its line's L3 home, `docs/findings/05-claims.md`, T34-A). That is E107's L3-served cold pass with the vendor's strides, which the desk L2 model has missing 80.2% of lines in a cold pass (154 of 192).
