# Inside the ET-SoC-1, down to the silicon: what each selectable part holds

Research for the chip diagram's deep zoom (worktree `et-soc1-chipzoom`, branch `chipzoom`, from 1980ebb), 30 September
2026. The owner asked that everything the diagram lets a reader select can also be double-clicked to zoom in, down to
individual circuit components, using this chip's own facts where the repository has them and known sources otherwise.
This file answers three questions for the builder: (1) which parts the page lets a reader select today, at every
scale; (2) what is inside each, as a tree down to circuit components, the FinFET, its fin and the silicon crystal, with
sizes and sources; (3) what the memory-levels page ("Anatomy of a memory access, interactively") already draws that can
be reused, and how.

Generated by `build_inside.py` beside this file, which also writes `inside.json` (the same tree, machine-readable).
Facts that already exist in the two pages' data are copied from there with their source and a tag: `[chip:<id>]`
(`docs/reports/data/2026-09-27-chip-diagram/facts.json`), `[chip-research:<id>]` (a row of that page's `research/`
files that did not reach its facts.json), `[ml:<level>:<id>]` (`docs/reports/data/2026-09-28-memory-levels/facts.json`).
Paths under `external/` are the gitignored clones, present in the main checkout. core-et's micro-architecture documents
are cited as spec, as the chip diagram already does; a fact read from core-et's RTL is marked "Erbium RTL": that branch
is the same Minion core lineage in a later MCU-class configuration (docs/findings/01-resources.md R2), and that the
ET-SoC-1's silicon matches it block for block is not confirmed.

## What the research found, in brief

- **Twenty-six kinds of selectable part (the minion is one at two layers), 107 selectable groups**: 52 on the die view, 46 in a shire, 9 in a minion (section 1). Double-click zooms
  today only from a compute shire and a minion. The shire view's edge labels ("↑ PCIe shire", "shire 12 ←",
  "→ shire 28", "↓ shire 29") are plain text, not parts; section 1 says where they are drawn, for the request to make
  them clickable.
- **Inside the minion the repository goes much deeper than the page does.** core-et's RTL and micro-architecture
  documents (the Erbium branch: the same Minion core lineage, flagged wherever used) give the integer register file (a
  latch array, 64 x 64, 2 read and 1 write ports), the iterative multiplier and divider (8 multiply iterations of 4
  radix-4 Booth partial products; 65 divide iterations), the front end (stages 0-7, a round-robin thread scheduler, no branch
  predictor described), the vector lane's register file (64 x 32, 3 read and 2 write ports, "ET-custom RF or
  sea-of-latches"), the fused multiply-add (7 stages: radix-4 Booth partial products, a Wallace tree of 4:2
  compressors, alignment and normalisation shifters, a leading-digit detector, a rounding adder), the int8 units and the
  transcendental unit's ROM tables. The energy manual's flip model prices the tensor unit per register bit clocked and
  per multiplier-tree net toggled, and shows the lane clock withheld on zero operands.
- **Two storage technologies, for a physical reason.** The minion's L1 (and its register files) are latch arrays on the
  ~0.52 V minion rail; the shire cache is compiled SRAM macros (4,096 x 144 data panels, 64 per shire) on the 0.705 V
  SRAM rail, where the I-cache's memory also had to be moved "because it has to be operated at high voltage". Esperanto:
  the SRAM banks "operate near the process-nominal supply voltage to allow higher density".
- **The process, from outside sources.** TSMC N7: 57 nm contacted gate pitch, 30 nm fin pitch, 40 nm minimum metal
  pitch, 0.027 µm² high-density 6T SRAM cell (TSMC, IEDM 2016), 240 nm high-density cell height, ~91 MTr/mm² for dense
  logic. The ET-SoC-1 averages 42 MTr/mm² (24 billion on 570 mm²). TSMC does not publish N7's fin width and height; by
  its 10 nm generation (6 nm, 44 nm) and Intel's 10 nm (7 nm, 46-53 nm), an N7 fin is about 6-7 nm wide and 45-50 nm
  tall, labelled as that inference. A 6 nm fin is about 31 planes of silicon atoms across (derived from a = 0.5431 nm).
- **Sizes below the shire are order-of-magnitude inferences.** No floorplan of a shire, neighbourhood or minion is
  published (L114, l1.u-floorplan, l2.floorplan). The tree gives each node a characteristic size with its reasoning
  (for example: a minion 0.22-0.29 mm², about 0.5 mm on a side, if the 32 minions take one half to two thirds of the
  13.9 mm² tile; a data panel about 117 x 137 µm of cells if the macro is 1,024 x 576 HD 6T cells). The only sizes
  below the die that any source supports come from the die plot: the tile pitch (3.72 mm) and the memory-shire strips
  (1.76 mm).
- **The memory-levels page already draws most of the memory side and several shared circuits** (section 3): the bank,
  sub-bank, data panel and 6T cell (shared by L2, L3 and scratchpad), the L1's latch-RAM chain down to a 10-transistor
  latch, the comparator, the crossing's level shifter, the mesh hop and its repeater, the memory shire, PHY, DQ driver
  and the DRAM from channel to 1T1C cell. Nothing in the repository draws the FMA's datapath, the register files, the
  clocking, the PCIe SerDes, the FinFET, the fin or the crystal: those are new drawings.

### Drawn, but not selectable today

- **The shire view's edge labels** (the owner's second request: "arrows like Shire 28 and shire 12 and shire 29 ...
  should be clickable"). `buildShire` draws them at `chip-diagram.script.js:323-334`: for each of N, S, W, E it looks up
  the die-orientation neighbour `BYDIE[(cell.r + dr) + ',' + (cell.c + dc)]`, names it with `cellName` (line 186: "shire
  N", "memory shire N", the master or spare, "PCIe shire", "I/O shire", or "die edge"), draws a `mlink` stub and a
  `T(...)` text ("↑ PCIe shire", "shire 12 ←", "→ shire 28", "↓ shire 29"; on a phone the side labels are rotated by
  `sideLab`). They are plain text inside the frame group `fr`, with no `comp` and no handler. Making them parts: wrap
  each in a `comp(fr, 'edge', {cell: c, dir: s}, ...)` (or a `<g role="link">`) when `c` is a compute shire and send
  `userNav({level: 1, sid: c.id})` on click and Enter; for a memory shire, the master, the PCIe or I/O shire, either
  zoom out to the die with that cell selected or (once the zoom tree covers them) zoom into that part. `P.edge.N/S/W/E`
  already holds each stub's end point.
- **The drawn sub-structures inside the parts**, all `pointer-events: none` today, which the deep zoom would make
  targets: in a shire, each bank's four sub-bank rectangles (`buildShire`, bank loop), the mesh stop's four lane boxes
  (`P.lane`), the neighbourhoods' fast-network tree edges (`fln-e`); in a minion, each hart's 32 register squares
  (`P.regs`: f0-f31, the vector registers), the vector unit's 8 lanes x 5 unit boxes (FMA, IMA, IMA, INT, TR; `P.units`), the tensor sequencer's
  TensorFMA box and TenB/TenC boxes, the L1's 16 set rectangles (sets 12-15 in `l1d`, sets 0-11 are the `l1scp` part).
  Their nodes in the tree: `shire.subbank`, `shire.meshstop.xing` (lanes), `fln`, `vpu.lane.vrf` (the register
  squares; the integer registers, `core.irf`, are not drawn), `vpu.lane`, `vpu.lane.fma`, `vpu.lane.tima`, `vpu.lane.trans`, `tensor.fsm`, `tensor.tenb`,
  `tensor.tenc`, `l1d.block`.
- **Chip-wide infrastructure no layer draws:** power delivery, clock distribution, the wiring stack (`die.power`,
  `die.clock`, `die.metal`), each shire's PLL, DLL and sensors (`shire.clock`, `shire.sensors`).

### The ET-SoC-1 die `die`
**Size:** 2.57 cm (inferred: about 25.6-25.8 mm east-west by 22.1-22.2 mm north-south, scaled from Esperanto's die plot to 570 mm2 (chip.die-dims))  
**Selectable today:** comp `chip`, layer 0; aria-label "The ET-SoC-1 die: details"

One slab of silicon about 26 by 22 mm, carrying over 24 billion transistors. It is a grid of 36 tiles called shires (32 compute shires, a master, a spare, a PCIe shire and an I/O shire), eight memory shires down the two sides, and an on-chip network joining them.

- Die area 570 mm². *(spec [chip:chip.die-area]; docs/findings/05-claims.md:513; docs/findings/01-resources.md:234 (R14: Hot Chips 33 slide 20; IEEE Micro 42(3) p.37))*
- Die about 25.6-25.8 mm east-west by 22.1-22.2 mm north-south (width/height 1.15-1.17), from the published die plot scaled to 570 mm²; the shire grid spans about 86% of the die's width, the rest being the memory shires and their DRAM PHYs down each side (about 1.76 mm per side). *(inferred [chip:chip.die-dims]; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/research/geometry/pitch.json, `scale_from_570mm2.A_black_layout_region` and `.B_symmetric_bottom` (`die_w_mm`, `die_h_mm`, ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*
- TSMC 7 nm, over 24 billion transistors, 89 mask layers (Esperanto, Hot Chips 33 and IEEE Micro 2022). *(spec [chip:chip.process]; docs/findings/05-claims.md:513; docs/findings/01-resources.md:91-104 (R6))*
- Average density: 24 billion transistors over 570 mm2 is about 42 million per mm2, less than half the ~91 MTr/mm2 of TSMC N7's dense logic cells; SRAM periphery, analog blocks (PLLs, PHYs), I/O and wiring take the rest. *(derived; arithmetic on chip.process and chip.die-area; the N7 figure: Wikipedia, "7 nm process", comparison table, row TSMC N7 (gate pitch 57 nm, fin pitch 30 nm, minimum metal pitch 40 nm, 91.2-96.5 MTr/mm2, SRAM cell 0.027 um2; fins SAQP, gates and M0-M4 SADP), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_nm_process (read 30 Sep 2026))*
- Package is 45.0 × 45.0 mm (lid 44.8) with 2,494 balls and over 30,000 bumps to the die; the package drawing shows no die outline. *(spec [chip-research:L12]; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/research/SYNTHESIS.md §1a row 'Package' (citing external/et-man/ET Preliminary Datasheet Rev 1.0.pdf p.33 Fig. 9-1 and HC33 ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*
- The ET-SoC-1 has 1,093 RISC-V cores: 1,088 ET-Minions, 4 ET-Maxions and 1 Minion-based service processor. *(spec [chip:chip.cores-total]; external/et-man/ET Preliminary Datasheet Rev 1.0.pdf, pdf p.4 (§1 Product Overview); external/et-man/ET Programmer's Reference Manual.pdf, pdf p.7 (§1))*

#### Compute shire `shire`
**Size:** 3.72 mm (inferred: one tile pitch: 3.73 mm east-west, 3.70 north-south, measured in pixels on the die plot (chip.hop-pitch))  
**Selectable today:** comp `cshire`, layer 0, 32 instances; aria-label "Shire N, compute shire. Space for details; Enter zooms in."

A tile of 32 small cores (minions) in four neighbourhoods, with 4 MB of shared on-chip memory, a crossbar joining them, and one stop on the on-chip network. All 34 minion shires are the same design; 32 of them run the programs.

- A minion shire is 4 neighbourhoods = 32 minions = 64 harts, a four-bank 4 MB shared L2/L3/scratchpad cache, a mesh stop, a crossbar between them, and an uncacheable (UC) block with fast local barriers, fast credit counters, inter-processor interrupts and global atomics. *(spec [chip:shire.composition]; external/et-man/ET Preliminary Datasheet Rev 1.0.pdf, pdf p.5 (§2.1); external/et-man/ET Minion Overview.pdf, pdf p.4 (slide 5))*
- A minion-shire cell is about 13.8–13.95 mm²; the 34 minion shires take about 468–474 mm² (82–83% of the die). *(derived [chip:L11]; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/research/SYNTHESIS.md §1a row 'Minion-shire cell area')*
- Shire top level: the main NoC interface (ports to_l3 ×4, to_sys, l3_slave ×4, uc_to_l3, uc_to_sys, sys_slave) sits above the shire channel (shire cache with 4 banks, UC block, RBOX, ESR, I$ memories), which feeds the four neighbourhoods; clock domains CLOCK_NOC, SHIRE_CLOCK, NEIGH_CLOCK, with the PLL/DLL, sensors and debug NoC bridge below. *(spec [chip:L114]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET Minion Shire Description.pdf p.5 Figure 1)*
- A shire spans several voltage domains separated by level shifters and FIFOs: minions on the low-voltage minion rail, the SRAM arrays (shire cache, and the I-cache data memory, placed in the shire channel because it must run at high voltage) on the SRAM rail, the mesh stop on the NoC rail. *(spec [chip-research:shire.voltage-domains]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET Minion Shire Description.pdf, pdf p.4, p.10 (§3.1) and p.18 (§5); docs/research/why-low-power.md:23-28 (R6))*
- Each minion shire has its own PLL; the shire cache and minions run on the shire clock (the neighbourhood clock is the same clock shifted 180°), outside the NoC's clock domain. Design value 1,000 MHz. *(spec [chip-research:shire.clock]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET Minion Shire Description.pdf, pdf p.4 and pdf p.12 (Table 2: o_clkpll_shire, o_clkpll_neigh))*
- Per-shire on-die voltage at idle (34 minion shires): minion rail 517–521 mV, lows 513 (shires 0, 1, 2, 18) to 520, highs to 522; SRAM 703–707 mV; mesh 483–486 mV. *(measured [chip:L123]; docs/findings/05-claims.md:41 (docs/reports/data/2026-09-20-power-aifoundry2/per-shire-voltage-idle.json))*

**Inside:** `shire.meshstop`, `shire.cache`, `shire.l2`, `shire.l3`, `shire.scp`, `shire.uc`, `shire.xbar`, `shire.neigh`, `shire.clock`, `shire.sensors`

##### Mesh stop `shire.meshstop`
**Size:** 500 µm (inferred: order of magnitude only: nine 512-bit routers and their crossings; no floorplan of the shire is published (L114, l2.floorplan))  
**Selectable today:** comp `meshstop`, layer 1; aria-label "Mesh stop: details"

Where the shire joins the on-chip network. Every request that leaves the shire, and every one that comes in, passes through its routers, which pass packets on to the four neighbouring stops.

- Each shire meets the mesh at a single point: minion 31 of each shire gives the same round trips as minion 0. *(measured [chip:mesh.single-attach]; on-chip communication page §'What the numbers say')*
- Each mesh stop has 9 main-NoC routers (layers 0–8) plus one debug-NoC router; routers have 8 ports (H, E, S, W, N, I, J, K) with 4 VC slots each; route info, packet, sideband and data are parity-protected (no ECC). *(spec [chip:L105]; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/research/SYNTHESIS.md §1b rows 'Main-NoC layers', 'Router ports and VCs', 'Error protection' (etsoc_shire_other_esr.h:3877-3981; ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*
- Every shire's NoC port is 512 bits wide: one 64 B line per beat, single-beat only. *(spec [chip:L102]; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/report.json inputs.port_bits (core-et rtl/inc/axi_defines.vh:43 SC_MESH_MASTER_AXI_DATA_SIZE 512))*

###### Router (one of nine main-NoC layers) `shire.meshstop.router`
**Size:** 200 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: an 8-port router with 512-bit datapaths; the NetSpeed configuration is not in the repository)

A small switch. Packets arrive on its input ports, wait in short queues, and an arbiter decides which one crosses the router's internal switch to which output each cycle. Each mesh stop has nine of them stacked as separate networks, plus one for debug.

- Each mesh stop has 9 main-NoC routers (layers 0–8) plus one debug-NoC router; routers have 8 ports (H, E, S, W, N, I, J, K) with 4 VC slots each; route info, packet, sideband and data are parity-protected (no ECC). *(spec [chip:L105]; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/research/SYNTHESIS.md §1b rows 'Main-NoC layers', 'Router ports and VCs', 'Error protection' (etsoc_shire_other_esr.h:3877-3981; ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*
- GENERIC. A mesh router stage: input virtual-channel buffers (flip-flop or register-file arrays), route computation, virtual-channel and switch allocators (arbiters), a crossbar of multiplexers, and output drivers onto the link; each link is a bundle of wires broken by repeaters (inverter pairs) every few hundred microns. *(generic [ml:l3:g.router]; textbook (Dally and Towles, Principles and Practices of Interconnection Networks))*
- UNKNOWN. The router pipeline per hop (4 NoC cycles each way is derived), which of the 9 main-NoC layers carries L3 requests and replies, and the flit width. The dimension order, asked here until 29 September, is measured (l3.route). *(unknown [ml:l3:u.noc-hop]; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/research/SYNTHESIS.md:66-68, 75-76; the order: docs/reports/data/2026-09-29-nocr (E56))*

###### Input buffers (virtual channels) `shire.meshstop.router.buf`
**Size:** 100 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: 8 ports x 4 VC slots of a flit (512 bits or more) each, if flip-flops)

Short queues at each input where packets wait their turn. Each port has four slots (virtual channels), so one blocked packet does not stop the others.

- Routers have 8 ports (H, E, S, W, N, I, J, K) with 4 VC slots each. *(spec [chip:L105]; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/research/SYNTHESIS.md §1b rows 'Main-NoC layers', 'Router ports and VCs', 'Error protection' (etsoc_shire_other_esr.h:3877-3981; ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*
- If every slot holds one 512-bit flit, one router buffers 8 x 4 x 512 = 16,384 bits, about 147,000 bits over the nine layers of one stop. *(derived; arithmetic on L105; the flit width is an estimate (SYNTHESIS.md row "Flit width", unknown))*
- Router input buffers are flip-flop or register-file arrays (textbook). *(generic [ml:l3:g.router]; textbook (Dally and Towles, Principles and Practices of Interconnection Networks))*

**Made of (see):** `lib.flipflop`

###### Route computation and allocators `shire.meshstop.router.alloc`
**Size:** 50 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: arbitration logic for 8 ports)

Logic that reads each packet's destination, picks the output (x first, then y, for a request), and arbitrates when two packets want the same output in the same cycle.

- The mesh routes in dimension order, and a request and its reply take opposite orders: a request goes x first, then y, and a reply y first, then x, on the logical map, so a reply retraces its request's route backwards. Measured on 29 September (E56) with sets of streams between shires chosen so that their streams share one directed link under one order and none under the other: 1 KB tensor loads, whose data travel as replies, fell to 0.54-0.77 of their bandwidth alone when they shared a link under y first and held at 1.00 when they shared one under x first; tensor stores, whose data travel as requests, showed the mirror (0.52-0.76 against 1.00). A shared link carried about 92 GB/s. ###### Voltage and clock crossing (VCFIFO) `shire.meshstop.xing`
**Size:** 50 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: a FIFO of a few 512-bit entries with level shifters on every bit)

###### Bank (one of four) `shire.bank`
**Size:** 700 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: a quarter of the shire cache)  
**Selectable today:** comp `banks`, layer 1, 4 instances; aria-label "Shire cache bank N: details"

Each bank is a complete cache of its own, with a queue of waiting requests, a pipeline and four sub-banks of memory. Consecutive 64-byte lines go to banks 0, 1, 2, 3 in turn, so the four work in parallel.

- The shire cache is four identical banks; each bank is an independent L2 cache with its own request queue, pipeline and next-level ports. A bank takes at most one request per cycle and returns at most one 512-bit line per cycle. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.banks]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.6 §1; pdf p.30 §2.1)*
- L2 and scratchpad requests go to the bank chosen by PA[7:6], so consecutive 64 B lines rotate through banks 0-3. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.bank-select]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.6 §1 and p.24 §1.4.1.1)*
- Per bank: a 64-entry request queue (up to 21 entries reserved for L3-slave requests), an 8-entry read buffer and a 32-entry write-around coalescing buffer; L3-slave requests take priority over local L2 requests. *(spec [chip:shire.bank-queues]; docs/research/counters-and-dram.md:458-465; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf, pdf p.9 (coalescing buffer, 32 entries per bank) and pdf p.36 ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*
- With 2-cycle RAMs a sub-bank accepts a new request only every other cycle (3-cycle RAMs: every third). Operations that need two RAM passes (a fill that may evict, an atomic) reserve bubble slots, because the pipeline never stalls once a request is scheduled. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.subbank-busy]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.35-36 §2.6, pdf p.35 §2.5)*

###### Request queue `shire.bank.reqq`
**Size:** 100 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: 64 entries of address and state)

A list of the requests the bank is working on, like a to-do list: 64 entries, each kept from its arrival until its data is back, so many misses can be outstanding at once.

- Each bank has a 64-entry request queue (reqq), the equivalent of MSHRs. An entry lives from allocation through miss, mesh read, fill and any victim write-back. Two entries can be allocated per cycle (one neighbourhood, one L3-slave request), and 21 of the 64 are reserved for L3-slave requests by default. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.reqq]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.34-35 §2.5-2.6; external/core-et/rtl/inc/shire_cache_defines.vh:69; ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*
- Choosing what enters the pipeline takes three steps: round-robin per sub-bank (neighbourhood and L3 separately), then L3 requests before neighbourhood requests, then masking out busy sub-banks and a round-robin across sub-banks. Errata RTLMIN-6207 and 6214 record that a neighbourhood request can starve behind L3-slave traffic. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.reqq.arb]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.36 §2.6 Figure 8; external/et-man/ET-SoC Errata.pdf, pdf p.80-81 §4.1-4.2)*

**Made of (see):** `lib.flipflop`, `lib.arbiter`

###### Read buffer `shire.bank.rbuf`
**Size:** 65 µm (inferred: 8 x 512 bits = 4,096 flip-flops at about 1 um2 each (an N7 flip-flop, inference) is about 0.004 mm2, 65 um on a side)

Eight recently read lines kept in registers next to the pipeline. A repeat read is served from here without waking the big memory panels: 36 cycles instead of 47.

- Read buffer (RBUF): an 8-entry fully associative mini-cache of clean lines per bank (2 KB per shire). Lines are installed on L2 and scratchpad read hits. Requests are checked against it when they are allocated a reqq entry, and hits are served from it without touching the RAM panels, 'as these memory panels are slow and power hungry'. Anything that will change the data RAM clears the entry, and a 4-entry FIFO holds hit responses. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.rbuf]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.9 §1.1, pdf p.40 §2.8; external/core-et/rtl/inc/shire_cache_defines.vh:273-275)*
- The RBUF is on by default (esr_sc_l2_rbuf_enable = esr_sc_scp_rbuf_enable = 1 in the RTL's ESR init), and the cards show it: a 36-cycle plateau for working sets of 768 B to 2 KB, then 47 cycles from 4 KB, on all three cards. *(derived [ml:l2:l2.rbuf.enabled]; external/core-et/rtl/shire/esr/esr_cache_bank.v:311-312; docs/reports/data/2026-09-25-claims-v3/results/lat.json items[LAT-M1] (knees between 512/768 B and 2/4 KB))*
- What the RBUF is built from on silicon is not documented. The Ainekko reimplementation keeps it as 8 x 512-bit registers (flip-flops) holding ECC-corrected data, with an LRU replacement arbiter and a 7-stage pending-install pipeline. *(unknown [ml:l2:l2.rbuf.storage]; external/core-et-main/hw/ip/shirecache/rtl/shirecache_pipe_rbuf.sv:1-20, 105, 416-419)*

###### Data queue `shire.bank.dataq`
**Size:** 100 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: 64 lines of 576 bits in a two-port register file)

A holding area for the data of requests in flight: one 64-byte line per request-queue entry.

###### Atomic unit `shire.bank.atomic`
**Size:** 50 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: a 256-bit ALU)

A small arithmetic unit beside the memory that performs read-modify-write operations (add, swap, min, max ...) on a word in one place, so that many cores can update a shared counter without racing.

- Each bank has an atomic unit (32-, 64- and 256-bit operands: swap, add, and, or, xor, min/max signed and unsigned, float min/max). ###### Bank pipeline `shire.bank.pipe`
**Size:** 100 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: the stage registers and control of one bank)

The assembly line a request moves through, one stage per clock: allocate, arbitrate, read the tags, check them, compare, read the data, check it, send it. Registers between the stages hold each request's state.

- Read-hit pipeline stages (2-cycle RAMs): ag (reqq allocate), ad (dependencies), rqa (reqq arbitration), tap, ta, ta0, ta1 (tag and tag-state RAM access), te (tag ECC), tc (tag compare: hit, victim), dap, da, da0, da1 (data RAM access), de (data ECC), dc (data complete: OR'd across the sub-banks, then to the response mux, or the dataq if the mux is busy). *(spec [ml:l2:l2.stages]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.44 figure, pdf p.45-46 §2.14.1)*
- If each named stage is one cycle, the 15 stages from reqq allocation to data complete account for 15 of the spec's 21-cycle L2 hit, leaving about 6 cycles for the request crossbar, response mux and response crossbar. The spec cites an 'L2 and L3 Pipeline stages Spreadsheet' that has the real budget, which we do not have. *(derived [ml:l2:l2.stages.budget]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.11 Table 1, pdf p.44-46, pdf p.131 ref. 3)*
- Module-level clock gaters (esr_sc_clk_gate_disable) stop the bank's clock when it has no outstanding requests (errata RTLMIN-6235, which is why the bank's cycle counter under-counts). The reimplementation gates each RAM's clock on only for the ram_delay cycles of an access. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.clock-gating]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.97 §4.1; external/et-man/ET-SoC Errata.pdf, pdf p.82 §4.3; ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*
- Each bank has a performance monitor: a cycle counter and two 40-bit event counters, which can count RBUF hits, tag hits and misses, victims and opcodes. They are M-mode registers. A U-mode kernel can only read them (syscall 9) with the firmware's default qualifier, which counts all read-type requests and does not separate hits from misses. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.perfmon]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.104-109 §4.9; docs/research/counters-and-dram.md:186-191, 252, 259)*

###### Sub-bank `shire.subbank`
**Size:** 350 µm (inferred: order of magnitude: four data panels of about 150 um plus a tag and a state macro)

A quarter of a bank: its own tag memory (which lines are here), state memory (valid, dirty, LRU), and four data panels that together hold one 64-byte line per row.

###### Tag-state RAM (1,024 x 40, two-port) `shire.subbank.state`
**Size:** 50 µm (inferred: 40,960 bits; a two-port cell is larger than a 6T cell (order of magnitude))

A memory read and written on every request: which lines are valid, dirty, locked or all zeros, and the order in which they were used. It has two ports so the read and the update happen in one pass.

- Tag-state RAM: one 40-bit row per set = 4 x {valid, locked, zero, qwen[3:0]} + 5-bit LRU code + 7-bit ECC. It is read and written on every L2 request, and it is two-ported, so the read and the LRU/dirty update happen in the same pass. *(spec [ml:l2:l2.tag-state-ram]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.47 §2.14.3 Table 12 and pdf p.45; external/core-et/rtl/inc/shire_cache_defines.vh:231-237)*
- The tag-state RAM is 'saculs0g4l2p1024x40m4b1w0c0p0d0s1rm0rw11', RAM type 2PUHDRF: 1,024 words x 40 bits. The spec says the state RAM is two-ported (a read and a write per request). *(spec [ml:l2:l2.macro.state]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf (v1.1), pdf p.51 §2.14.6.4, pdf p.45)*
- Zero lines skip the data array. When a fill or a full-line write brings an all-zero line (the bank computes the NOR of all 512 bits as the line arrives), the tag state's zero bit is set and the data macros are not written; a later read of that line does not read them either and returns zeros. Enabled by default (esr_sc_zero_state_enable = 1). *(spec [ml:l3:l3.zero-state]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET-Shire-Cache-Specification.pdf pdf p.47 Table 12 ('zero ... cache memory must not be read') and pdf p.101 (esr_sc_zero_state_enable); ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*

###### Precharge, column mux, sense amplifiers, write drivers `shire.panel.colio`
**Size:** 140 µm (inferred: a strip the width of the array (inference))

The circuits along the bottom edge. Before a read they charge every bitline pair high; a tiny difference then appears on the selected pair, and a sense amplifier turns it into a full 0 or 1. For a write, drivers force the bitlines.

- GENERIC. In a differential read exactly one bitline of each pair discharges whether the cell holds 0 or 1, so the array's read energy is to first order independent of the data; data dependence comes from what follows the sense amplifiers (outputs that toggle, buses, ECC) unless the macro uses a precharged single-ended global read line. *(generic [ml:scp:scp.g-read-data]; GENERIC (textbook single-port 6T SRAM; not from an ET source))*
- GENERIC. A write: write drivers pull one bitline of each selected pair to ground, the wordline rises, and the access transistor overpowers the cell's PMOS pull-up so the cell flips. Write assist (for example a negative bitline or a lowered cell supply) and a longer write pulse make the flip reliable at low voltage. *(generic [ml:l3:g.write]; textbook)*
- GENERIC. Read assist usually lowers the wordline voltage so a read cannot flip a weak cell; read-margin settings delay the sense-amplifier enable so more bitline swing develops, slower but safer at low voltage. *(generic [ml:l3:g.assist]; textbook; memory-compiler datasheets)*

###### Request path to the banks `shire.neigh.req`

###### Transcendental unit (exp2, log2, reciprocal) `vpu.lane.trans`
**Size:** 30 µm (inferred: order of magnitude)

###### Miss handlers and replay queue `l1d.mh`

###### DRAM cell and sense amplifier `dram.cell`
**Size:** 100 nm (inferred: DRAM cells are tens of nm apart, their capacitors far taller; order of magnitude only)

A DRAM bit is one transistor and one tiny capacitor: charged is 1, empty is 0. The charge leaks away, so every row is read and rewritten every few tens of milliseconds (refresh).

- A DRAM bit is one 1T1C cell: an NMOS access transistor whose gate is the wordline and whose drain is the bitline, in series with a storage capacitor; the stored charge is the bit, it leaks away (hence refresh), and reading it is destructive until the sense amplifier restores it. *(generic [ml:dram:gen.cell]; GENERIC (textbook DRAM; no ET source describes the die))*
- Each cell shares its charge with its bitline, nudging it slightly above or below the half-voltage; the bitline sense amplifier, a cross-coupled latch of two NMOS and two PMOS, is then fired (NMOS side pulled to ground, PMOS side to the array voltage) and drives the pair to full rail, which also rewrites the cell. The row of latched sense amplifiers is the open page ('row buffer'). *(generic [ml:dram:gen.sense]; GENERIC (textbook DRAM))*
- Between accesses each bitline pair is held at half the array voltage by a precharge/equalize circuit (typically three NMOS: two tie BL and /BL to the half-voltage, one shorts them together); the equalize signal turns off just before a wordline rises. *(generic [ml:dram:gen.equalize]; GENERIC (textbook DRAM))*
- All-bank refresh: the die's internal counter supplies the row addresses and each REFab activates and precharges a batch of rows in every bank at once; JEDEC LPDDR4 asks for 8,192 refresh commands per 32 ms retention window at normal temperature, which with 131,072 rows per bank means 16 rows of each bank per REFab. *(generic [ml:dram:gen.refresh]; GENERIC (JEDEC JESD209-4 tREFW 32 ms, 8,192 REF); rows per bank from dram.org.geometry)*

#### DRAM cell (1T1C) `lib.dram1t1c`
**Size:** 100 nm (inferred: order of magnitude (a DRAM process, not TSMC N7))

One access transistor and one capacitor. The memory chips on the card are made this way, in a DRAM process, not in the logic process of the ET-SoC-1.

- A DRAM bit is one 1T1C cell: an NMOS access transistor whose gate is the wordline and whose drain is the bitline, in series with a storage capacitor; the stored charge is the bit, it leaks away (hence refresh), and reading it is destructive until the sense amplifier restores it. *(generic [ml:dram:gen.cell]; GENERIC (textbook DRAM; no ET source describes the die))*

**Reuse:** memory-levels scene `dram`, scale `dcell`, `buildDCell`: direct

#### On-chip wire `lib.wire`
**Size:** 40 nm (outside: the minimum metal pitch of TSMC N7, 40 nm: the finest wires are about 20 nm wide)

A copper line embedded in insulator. Its capacitance, about 0.2 fF per micrometre, must be charged every time the signal changes; on long wires that costs more energy than the logic.

- TSMC N7 minimum metal pitch 40 nm. *(outside; Wikipedia, "7 nm process", comparison table, row TSMC N7 (gate pitch 57 nm, fin pitch 30 nm, minimum metal pitch 40 nm, 91.2-96.5 MTr/mm2, SRAM cell 0.027 um2; fins SAQP, gates and M0-M4 SADP), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_nm_process (read 30 Sep 2026))*
- Most wires have about 0.2 fF/um of capacitance, nearly independent of the process generation. *(generic; D. Harris, "Interconnect RC" lecture (https://pages.hmc.edu/harris/class/hal/lect4.pdf), as quoted in SYNTHESIS.md line 160)*
- "7-nm wires are relatively slow." *(spec; D. Ditzel et al., "Accelerating ML Recommendation With Over 1,000 RISC-V/Tensor Processors on Esperanto's ET-SoC-1 Chip", IEEE Micro 42(3), May/June 2022 (https://www.esperanto.ai/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Dave-IEEE-Micro.pdf), p. 34)*
- A random bit per mm on free links, mesh rail at 0.485 V: 37 fJ (25 of it data-dependent); on board power 47 fJ. Dally's rule of thumb is ~100 fJ/b-mm. *(measured [chip:mesh-bit-mm-free]; report.json: headline["uncontended/noc_rail"].random_bit_total / .random_bit_data, headline["uncontended/board"].random_bit_total; per card (V3) ... (full source in inside.json and the pages' data))*

**Made of (see):** `lib.repeater`

#### Repeater `lib.repeater`
**Size:** 1 µm (inferred: a pair of wide inverters (order of magnitude))

A pair of strong inverters placed along a long wire to re-drive the signal, so that the delay grows with the length instead of its square.

- GENERIC. A wire's switching energy is about C x V^2 per 0-to-1 transition (C about 0.2 pF/mm), so random data (a transition on about half the bits) costs several times zeros; repeaters add to it. *(generic [ml:l3:g.wire-energy]; textbook; docs/reports/data/2026-09-24-wire-energy/research/SYNTHESIS.md:135-147 (literature table))*
- Delay of an unrepeated wire grows with the square of its length; optimally spaced repeaters make it linear. *(generic; N. Weste and D. Harris, CMOS VLSI Design, 4th ed. (Addison-Wesley 2011), ch. 6 (interconnect))*

**Made of (see):** `lib.inverter`  
**Reuse:** memory-levels scene `l3`, scale `rep`, `buildWire`: direct

#### Clock tree `lib.clocktree`
**Size:** 3.72 mm (inferred: a shire's clock tree spans its tile)

A tree of buffers that brings the clock from the PLL to every flip-flop at nearly the same instant; a delay-locked loop corrects the delay of the branch that feeds the neighbourhoods.

- Each minion shire has its own PLL; the shire cache and minions run on the shire clock (the neighbourhood clock is the same clock shifted 180°), outside the NoC's clock domain. Design value 1,000 MHz. *(spec [chip-research:shire.clock]; external/core-et/docs/CORE-ET Minion Shire Description.pdf, pdf p.4 and pdf p.12 (Table 2: o_clkpll_shire, o_clkpll_neigh))*
- The DLL compensates the variable delays in the distribution network of shire_clock and neigh_clock (voltage, temperature, process). *(spec; external/core-et/docs/Minion Shire DLL Delay Control.pdf, pdf p.3 (§1))*
- Clock distribution by H-trees, grids or spines of buffers. *(generic; N. Weste and D. Harris, CMOS VLSI Design, 4th ed. (Addison-Wesley 2011), ch. 13 (clocks, PLLs and DLLs))*

**Made of (see):** `lib.inverter`, `lib.icg`

#### FinFET transistor `lib.finfet`
**Size:** 57 nm (outside: one contacted gate pitch of TSMC N7: 57 nm)

The switch everything is made of. A thin wall of silicon (the fin) runs from source to drain, and the gate wraps over it on three sides: a voltage on the gate lets current flow along the fin, or stops it.

- TSMC N7: FinFET, contacted gate pitch 57 nm, fin pitch 30 nm. *(outside; Wikipedia, "7 nm process", comparison table, row TSMC N7 (gate pitch 57 nm, fin pitch 30 nm, minimum metal pitch 40 nm, 91.2-96.5 MTr/mm2, SRAM cell 0.027 um2; fins SAQP, gates and M0-M4 SADP), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_nm_process (read 30 Sep 2026))*
- TSMC N7 is TSMC's 4th-generation FinFET. *(outside; S.-Y. Wu et al. (TSMC), "A 7nm CMOS platform technology featuring 4th generation FinFET transistors with a 0.027um2 high density 6-T SRAM cell for mobile SoC applications", IEDM 2016, paper 2.6)*
- N7 high-density cells are 240 nm tall; high-performance cells 300 nm. *(outside; WikiChip Fuse, "TSMC 7nm HD and HP Cells, 2nd Gen 7nm, And The Snapdragon 855 DTCO", https://fuse.wikichip.org/?p=2408 (HD cell 240 nm tall = 6 tracks of 40 nm, HP 300 nm; about 91.2 MTr/mm2 HD, 65 HP); values as the search summary reported them, the page itself refused the connection on 30 Sep 2026)*
- TSMC 7 nm, over 24 billion transistors, 89 mask layers (Esperanto, Hot Chips 33 and IEEE Micro 2022). *(spec [chip:chip.process]; docs/findings/05-claims.md:513; docs/findings/01-resources.md:91-104 (R6))*

**Inside:** `lib.gate`, `lib.fin`

##### Gate stack `lib.gate`
**Size:** 20 nm (outside: gate length about 20 nm for a 7 nm-class process (IRDS); TSMC does not publish N7's)

The gate: a metal electrode on an insulating layer only a few atoms thick (a high-k oxide), wrapped over the top and both sides of the fin.

- 7 nm-class gate length about 20 nm (IRDS 2021). *(outside; Wikipedia, "7 nm process" (quoting IRDS 2021, Lithography: 7 nm-class gate length 20 nm), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_nm_process)*
- Comparable generations: TSMC 10 nm minimum Lg about 25 nm; Intel 10 nm 18 nm. *(outside; D. James (Siliconics), "A Quick Look at 14-nm and 10-nm", AVS Joint Users Group, July 2018 (JTG718-4), slides 11 and 17-18: TSMC 10 nm (Apple A11) fin pitch ~33 nm, fin width ~6 nm, functional gate height ~44 nm, gate width ~95 nm; Intel 10 nm fin height 46-53 nm, width ~7 nm at half height, Lg 18 nm; https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/JTG2018/JTG718-4-James-Siliconics.pdf)*
- A high-k metal gate: a hafnium-based oxide on a thin silicon-oxide interlayer under work-function metals; TSMC does not publish its N7 stack. *(generic; N. Weste and D. Harris, CMOS VLSI Design, 4th ed. (Addison-Wesley 2011), ch. 3 (processing))*

##### The fin (cross-section) `lib.fin`
**Size:** 50 nm (inferred: about 6-7 nm wide and 45-50 nm tall, by analogy with TSMC 10 nm (6 nm, 44 nm) and Intel 10 nm (7 nm, 46-53 nm); TSMC does not publish N7's)

A wall of crystalline silicon about 6 nm thick and some 50 nm tall, standing up from the wafer; fins sit 30 nm apart. Current flows along it under the gate.

- TSMC N7 fin pitch 30 nm (self-aligned quadruple patterning). *(outside; Wikipedia, "7 nm process", comparison table, row TSMC N7 (gate pitch 57 nm, fin pitch 30 nm, minimum metal pitch 40 nm, 91.2-96.5 MTr/mm2, SRAM cell 0.027 um2; fins SAQP, gates and M0-M4 SADP), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_nm_process (read 30 Sep 2026))*
- TSMC 10 nm (Apple A11): fin width ~6 nm, functional gate height ~44 nm; Intel 10 nm: fin height 46-53 nm, width ~7 nm at half height. *(outside; D. James (Siliconics), "A Quick Look at 14-nm and 10-nm", AVS Joint Users Group, July 2018 (JTG718-4), slides 11 and 17-18: TSMC 10 nm (Apple A11) fin pitch ~33 nm, fin width ~6 nm, functional gate height ~44 nm, gate width ~95 nm; Intel 10 nm fin height 46-53 nm, width ~7 nm at half height, Lg 18 nm; https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/JTG2018/JTG718-4-James-Siliconics.pdf)*
- For N7, a fin about 6-7 nm wide and 45-50 nm tall, carrying about 2 x 45 + 6 = ~100 nm of effective channel width. *(inferred; inference from the two outside rows above)*

**Inside:** `lib.channel`

###### Channel (the silicon under the gate) `lib.channel`
**Size:** 6 nm (inferred: the fin's width, about 6 nm (inference))

The strip of fin under the gate, about 6 by 20 by 45 nm, where the current flows: a few hundred thousand silicon atoms.

- 6 nm x 20 nm x 45 nm = 5,400 nm3 at 50 atoms per nm3 is about 270,000 silicon atoms. *(derived; arithmetic on the fin and gate estimates above and the atomic density below)*

**Inside:** `lib.si`

###### Silicon crystal `lib.si`
**Size:** 0.543 nm (outside: one cubic unit cell: 0.543 nm (NIST CODATA))

Silicon atoms in a diamond lattice: each bonded to four neighbours at the corners of a tetrahedron. A 6 nm fin is only about 30 planes of atoms across.

- Silicon lattice parameter a = 0.5431 nm. *(outside; NIST CODATA 2018: lattice parameter of silicon a = 543.1020511 pm (at 22.5 C in vacuum), https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?asil)*
- Diamond cubic: 8 atoms per cubic cell gives 8 / 0.5431^3 nm3 = 50 atoms per nm3 (5.0 x 10^22 per cm3); nearest neighbours are sqrt(3)/4 x a = 0.235 nm apart. *(derived; arithmetic on the lattice parameter)*
- Across a 6 nm fin whose sidewalls are (110) planes (the usual orientation on a (100) wafer, an assumption), the (220) planes are a / sqrt(8) = 0.192 nm apart: about 31 planes of atoms. *(derived; arithmetic on the lattice parameter)*

## 5. Zoom ladders, with each step's magnification

Using the sizes in the tree (most below the shire are order-of-magnitude inferences). A step under about 2x is better
drawn as one scene; a step over about 30x wants an intermediate scale.

**Compute, to the atom.** The ET-SoC-1 die (2.57 cm) → Compute shire (3.72 mm) → Neighbourhood (1.5 mm) → Minion (500 µm) → Vector unit (350 µm) → Lane (120 µm) → Fused multiply-add unit (60 µm) → Compressor tree (40 µm) → 4:2 compressor (1.5 µm) → Full adder (800 nm) → XOR / XNOR gate (300 nm) → FinFET transistor (57 nm) → The fin (50 nm) → Channel (6 nm) → Silicon crystal (0.543 nm)
Steps: 6.91×, 2.48×, 3×, 1.43×, 2.92×, 2×, 1.5×, 26.7×, 1.88×, 2.67×, 5.26×, 1.14×, 8.33×, 11×; overall 4.73e+07× (14 steps).

**Shire memory, to the atom.** The ET-SoC-1 die (2.57 cm) → Compute shire (3.72 mm) → Shire cache (1.5 mm) → Bank (700 µm) → Sub-bank (350 µm) → Data panel (150 µm) → Bitcell array (137 µm) → 6T SRAM cell (240 nm) → FinFET transistor (57 nm) → The fin (50 nm) → Channel (6 nm) → Silicon crystal (0.543 nm)
Steps: 6.91×, 2.48×, 2.14×, 2×, 2.33×, 1.09×, 571×, 4.21×, 1.14×, 8.33×, 11×; overall 4.73e+07× (11 steps).

**The L1, to the latch.** Minion (500 µm) → L1 data cache (120 µm) → Data array (90 µm) → LRAM block (45 µm) → Row and read tree (32 µm) → Latch (500 nm) → Inverter (240 nm) → FinFET transistor (57 nm)
Steps: 4.17×, 1.33×, 2×, 1.41×, 64×, 2.08×, 4.21×; overall 8.77e+03× (7 steps).

**A mesh hop, to the repeater.** The ET-SoC-1 die (2.57 cm) → The mesh (2.22 cm) → One hop's link (3.72 mm) → A wire segment between repeaters (200 µm) → Repeater (1 µm) → Inverter (240 nm) → FinFET transistor (57 nm)
Steps: 1.16×, 5.97×, 18.6×, 200×, 4.17×, 4.21×; overall 4.51e+05× (6 steps).


Suggested fixes from the ladders. Four steps are large: the data panel's array (137 µm) to one 6T cell (0.24 µm),
about 570x (memory-levels bridges it with the panel's "representative grid" of 8 x 12 cells, which can be its own
scale: a 16 x 16 patch of cells, about 4 µm); a wire segment (200 µm) to its repeater (1 µm), 200x (an intermediate
"repeater with its wire stubs", about 10-20 µm); an L1 row (32 µm) to one latch (0.5 µm), 64x (a few latches of the
row with their clock gate, about 3 µm); the compressor tree (40 µm) to one 4:2 compressor (1.5 µm), 27x (a column slice
of the tree, about 10 µm). The steps from a gate (0.3 µm) to the FinFET (57 nm), about 5x, and from the channel (6 nm)
to the crystal (0.54 nm), about 11x, are fine.
