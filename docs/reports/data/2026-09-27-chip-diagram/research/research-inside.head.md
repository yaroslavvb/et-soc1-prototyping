# Inside the ET-SoC-1, down to the silicon: what each selectable part holds

Research for the chip diagram's deep zoom (worktree `et-soc1-chipzoom`, branch `chipzoom`, from 1980ebb), 30 September
2026. The owner asked that everything the diagram lets a reader select can also be double-clicked to zoom in, down to
individual circuit components, using this chip's own facts where the repository has them and known sources otherwise.
This file answers three questions for the builder: (1) which parts the page lets a reader select today, at every
scale; (2) what is inside each, as a tree down to circuit components, the FinFET, its fin and the silicon crystal, with
sizes and sources; (3) what the memory-levels page ("Anatomy of a memory access, interactively") already draws that can
be reused, and how.

Generated by `build_inside.py` beside this file, which also writes `inside.json` (the same tree, machine-readable).
Facts that already exist in the two pages' data are copied from there with their source and a tag: `[chip:<id>]`
(`docs/reports/data/2026-09-27-chip-diagram/facts.json`), `[chip-research:<id>]` (a row of that page's `research/`
files that did not reach its facts.json), `[ml:<level>:<id>]` (`docs/reports/data/2026-09-28-memory-levels/facts.json`).
Paths under `external/` are the gitignored clones, present in the main checkout. core-et's micro-architecture documents
are cited as spec, as the chip diagram already does; a fact read from core-et's RTL is marked "Erbium RTL": that branch
is the same Minion core lineage in a later MCU-class configuration (docs/findings/01-resources.md R2), and that the
ET-SoC-1's silicon matches it block for block is not confirmed.

## What the research found, in brief

- **Twenty-six kinds of selectable part (the minion is one at two layers), 107 selectable groups**: 52 on the die view, 46 in a shire, 9 in a minion (section 1). Double-click zooms
  today only from a compute shire and a minion. The shire view's edge labels ("↑ PCIe shire", "shire 12 ←",
  "→ shire 28", "↓ shire 29") are plain text, not parts; section 1 says where they are drawn, for the request to make
  them clickable.
- **Inside the minion the repository goes much deeper than the page does.** core-et's RTL and micro-architecture
  documents (the Erbium branch: the same Minion core lineage, flagged wherever used) give the integer register file (a
  latch array, 64 x 64, 2 read and 1 write ports), the iterative multiplier and divider (8 multiply iterations of 4
  radix-4 Booth partial products; 65 divide iterations), the front end (stages 0-7, a round-robin thread scheduler, no branch
  predictor described), the vector lane's register file (64 x 32, 3 read and 2 write ports, "ET-custom RF or
  sea-of-latches"), the fused multiply-add (7 stages: radix-4 Booth partial products, a Wallace tree of 4:2
  compressors, alignment and normalisation shifters, a leading-digit detector, a rounding adder), the int8 units and the
  transcendental unit's ROM tables. The energy manual's flip model prices the tensor unit per register bit clocked and
  per multiplier-tree net toggled, and shows the lane clock withheld on zero operands.
- **Two storage technologies, for a physical reason.** The minion's L1 (and its register files) are latch arrays on the
  ~0.52 V minion rail; the shire cache is compiled SRAM macros (4,096 x 144 data panels, 64 per shire) on the 0.705 V
  SRAM rail, where the I-cache's memory also had to be moved "because it has to be operated at high voltage". Esperanto:
  the SRAM banks "operate near the process-nominal supply voltage to allow higher density".
- **The process, from outside sources.** TSMC N7: 57 nm contacted gate pitch, 30 nm fin pitch, 40 nm minimum metal
  pitch, 0.027 µm² high-density 6T SRAM cell (TSMC, IEDM 2016), 240 nm high-density cell height, ~91 MTr/mm² for dense
  logic. The ET-SoC-1 averages 42 MTr/mm² (24 billion on 570 mm²). TSMC does not publish N7's fin width and height; by
  its 10 nm generation (6 nm, 44 nm) and Intel's 10 nm (7 nm, 46-53 nm), an N7 fin is about 6-7 nm wide and 45-50 nm
  tall, labelled as that inference. A 6 nm fin is about 31 planes of silicon atoms across (derived from a = 0.5431 nm).
- **Sizes below the shire are order-of-magnitude inferences.** No floorplan of a shire, neighbourhood or minion is
  published (L114, l1.u-floorplan, l2.floorplan). The tree gives each node a characteristic size with its reasoning
  (for example: a minion 0.22-0.29 mm², about 0.5 mm on a side, if the 32 minions take one half to two thirds of the
  13.9 mm² tile; a data panel about 117 x 137 µm of cells if the macro is 1,024 x 576 HD 6T cells). The only sizes
  below the die that any source supports come from the die plot: the tile pitch (3.72 mm) and the memory-shire strips
  (1.76 mm).
- **The memory-levels page already draws most of the memory side and several shared circuits** (section 3): the bank,
  sub-bank, data panel and 6T cell (shared by L2, L3 and scratchpad), the L1's latch-RAM chain down to a 10-transistor
  latch, the comparator, the crossing's level shifter, the mesh hop and its repeater, the memory shire, PHY, DQ driver
  and the DRAM from channel to 1T1C cell. Nothing in the repository draws the FMA's datapath, the register files, the
  clocking, the PCIe SerDes, the FinFET, the fin or the crystal: those are new drawings.

## How the facts are labelled

| Label | Meaning | Page style |
|---|---|---|
| spec | an Esperanto/ET document, manual, firmware or RTL says so | documented |
| measured | measured on the lab's cards | measured |
| derived | arithmetic on spec or measured values | model |
| inferred | our reading or estimate; no source states it | inference |
| outside | a published outside source about the process or this class of circuit | outside source |
| generic | a textbook circuit or rule, not specific to this chip | outside source (generic) |
| unknown | an open question: no source settles it | unknown, asked |
