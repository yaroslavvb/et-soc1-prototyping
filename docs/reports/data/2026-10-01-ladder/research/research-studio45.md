# Studio 45, Bernal Heights and 29th Street: research for the ladder

For the owner's request of 1 Oct 2026 (OWNER-REQUEST.md): "Studio 45 is located on 29th Street, in Bernal Heights".
This file gives what Studio 45 is in public, the facts about Bernal Heights and 29th Street, and what the zoom from
San Francisco down to the studio should show. Written 1 Oct 2026. Its working files (the DataSF and USGS downloads) stayed in the session's work directory; the scripts that read them are beside this file.

**Privacy, as applied here and as it should stay on the page.** The owner's words give the street and the
neighbourhood, and those may be shown. Nothing here gives, and the page must not show, a house number, a point, a
block or coordinates for the studio. Every public listing of the venue prints its full street address, including
the event pages and press releases cited below. Cite them in the page text by publisher, title and date. Do not
print their URLs where the URL itself carries the address (the CoworkingCafe listing does, so it is left out). No
people's names: the venue's hosts and organisers are named on its event pages but are not needed (AGENT.md §10).
The coordinates in the scripts are the city's and USGS's public outlines and the hill's public summit point,
never the studio's.

## 3. 29th Street

- **The whole street is 1.29 km long** and runs west to east. It starts at Diamond St, at the foot of Diamond Heights, runs
  through Noe Valley (1,022 m) and a corner of Glen Park (115 m), and ends at its east end in Bernal Heights
  (156 m, by the Analysis Neighborhoods). Its blocks, from the west, end at Zircon Pl, Castro, Noe, Sanchez, Church,
  Dolores and San Jose Ave. East of San Jose Ave come its last two short blocks, the 156 m in Bernal Heights
  (DataSF Streets, Public Works centrelines; `studio45/st29.json`). Kind: derived from outside data. Do not confuse it with 29th *Avenue* in the
  Richmond and Sunset, which the same street file also lists under "29TH".
- **It falls about 95 m:** from 126 m at its west end to about 30 m at its east end. The steepest part is the top:
  34 m of drop in its first 140 m, about 24%. These are from the USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM, sampled every 40 m by
  `studio45/epqs_29th.py`; the output in `epqs_29th.out` gives distances and heights only. The hill's summit
  stands about 110 m above the street's east end.
- **Privacy note for the drawing.** The owner's two facts, "29th Street" and "Bernal Heights", already narrow the
  place to that 156 m stretch at the street's east end. Do not narrow it further. Draw the whole street, put no
  dot, block highlight or arrow on it, and keep the studio as an inset beside the drawing. Never put it at a
  point. This is the rule the SF level already follows ("the Studio 45 inset sits beside the city's outline").
  The camera should never zoom onto a position on the street.
- Light along the whole street takes 4.31 µs, or 2,588 ticks of the chip's 600 MHz clock. In fibre (group index
  1.4682, the SF level's Corning number) it takes 6.33 µs, or 3,799 ticks. Kind: derived.

## 5. Electronics tie-ins (the owner's goal: learn how electronics works)

1. **The hill and the chip are made of the same stuff.** Bernal Hill's chert is microcrystalline quartz, SiO₂
   (Wikipedia "Chert"; NPS). USGS: "The source of the silicon is silica in various natural forms, such as
   quartzite." Silicon metal "may be further processed into ultra-high-purity semiconductor- or solar-grades,
   commonly referred to as polysilicon" (USGS *Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025*, Silicon; the PDF is in `studio45/`).
   SiO₂ is also the insulator grown on the silicon of a chip. The TSMC N7 research file has this chip's gate stack,
   which puts SiO₂ under a high-k layer. A line for the page: *"The hill is quartz. Quartz is silicon and
   oxygen. Refined, the same silicon is the die."* If the page adds a purity figure for electronic-grade
   silicon, it needs its own source: this file has none.
2. **Scale against the die.** A radiolarian shell is 0.5-1.5 mm across (NPS), and the die is 25.6 x 22.2 mm (the
   repository's facts.json). So 17 to 51 shells would span the die's width. Each shell is a silica skeleton made by
   a single cell.
3. **The tower on the summit.** A 50-ft telecommunications tower (SF Planning, via Wikipedia) carries radio links
   over the city: the same electromagnetism as the chip, at metre wavelengths. Optional.
4. **The light-and-ticks line** (above) carries the chip's 600 MHz clock out to the neighbourhood, as the
   existing levels do.
5. **The building is a hardware shop.** A 4 x 8 ft CNC, laser cutter and 3D printers make the step from atoms to
   products concrete. The ET-SoC-1's open platform was presented there (the inference above).
