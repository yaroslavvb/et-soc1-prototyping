On one screen

In short. The lab's matmul model, MM1, predicts how many clock cycles and joules a matrix multiplication (a GEMM) takes on an ET-SoC-1 card, and how many transistors switch, from the matrix sizes, the number format, the data, the card and, since its fourth version, the operations the kernel's loop contains. Since the first version of this report, MM1-v3 was frozen and tested blind on two cards (E213): it kept the wall cycles to 4.1% rms and beat its predecessor on all five registered head-to-head rules, but it under-priced one loop's energy by up to 1.8 times. Its declared refit, MM1-v3.1, came out no better held out, and on its own blind day (E215) it failed its registered time bands on both cards (27 of 32 and 15 of 18 cases within 10%, against 90% needed) and, on aifoundry3, its energy bands at that loop's four points. E206 to E215 are the lab's numbered experiments (E208, E212 and E214 are a separate modelling session's sweeps); MM1-v0 to MM1-v3.1 are successive versions of the model, each frozen before a test; F1 is the lab's earlier GEMM route and F2 its energy ledger.

  • The timing simulation (section 3): it counts a kernel's instructions and memory requests exactly (its traces matched MM1-v3.1's masked walk at 352 of 352 working minions at each of two masks), but it has no clock and no caches: over 451 blind points the card took 8.2 to 35.1 cycles for each simulated instruction.
  • The scorecard (section 4): frozen before each blind test, the newest MM1 read 2.7 to 9.0% rms in wall cycles and 3.2 to 11.9% in joules over idle given the measured seconds, against 14.5 to 19.7% and 18.4 to 31.0% for F1 on the first version's five tests.
  • E213 (section 4.3), MM1-v3's blind day on two cards: every time rule and all but one energy rule passed, and MM1-v3 beat MM1-v2 on all five pooled head-to-head rules (wall cycles closer at 86 cases, farther at 13).
  • E215 (section 4.5), MM1-v3.1's blind day: both arms ran full (aifoundry3 card 0, 119 of 119 runs; aifoundry1 card 1, 71 of 71 runs after a second booking); pooled against MM1-v3, MM1-v3.1 was NOT DECIDED in time, as registered, and on the post hoc layouts MM1-v3 was better.
  • The two candidate terms, on E215 (sections 4.5 and 6.5; REPORTED): the gemmLnc term cut that loop's energy miss from 24.53% to 9.18% rms at its five points but overshot on arm A; the layout term moved almost no case, and a shire-count-only control came closest.
  • The switch count, through energy (section 5): blind on E210, energy followed MM1's counted switches to 1.9% of the data-dependent power; unread, on data patterns MM1-v3 could not price before, its switch route with 179 new bench counts read 6.8% against 25.0% for a null (closer at 35 cases, farther at 5), and on E214's 20 new kinds 7.76% against 28.05%.
  • E214 (section 4.4), the separate session's third sweep (622 runs on aifoundry1's two cards, committed and verified), scored unread: MM1-v3.1 read 6.66% in wall cycles and MM1-v2 8.06%, and MM1-v3.1's declared time bands failed on both cards.
  • MM1-v3.1 (section 6), declared before E213's data and fitted after it, kept MM1-v3's time form by its own rules (4.72% against 4.72% in wall cycles held out) and failed two of its acceptance rules: it is frozen as built.
  • The card repeats itself to about 0.4% in cycles and 1.2% in joules over idle, so model error, not noise, dominates.
  • Still wrong (section 7): the non-cooperative loop's energy (1.15 to 1.80 times the forecast at all seven E213 points; on E215 the frozen term overshot), the time of sparse shire subsets (10 of E215's layouts ran 10.1% to 16.6% faster than MM1-v3.1 forecast) and of one or two K blocks, and a held-out card's energy scale.

"rms (bias)" is the quadratic mean and the mean of that error, in %.

Words used here. A minion is one of the chip's small cores; it runs two harts (hardware threads). A shire is a group of 32 minions with its own L2 cache. J over idle is what a GEMM adds to the card's idle draw; J with the card at rest is the card's whole draw over the GEMM. Each energy forecast is scored given the measured seconds (the power model alone) or with the seconds forecast (the model's own seconds and watts). The lab's GEMM kernel is rgm136; its default loop gemmL issues its operand loads in cooperation groups, and gemmLnc is its non-cooperative branch; rgm211 is rgm136 with its C-block walk swapped and recompiled (E211). A configuration twin is a scored point whose card, kernel, arm, format, shire mask and clock are also in the training set that priced it. LOEO, LOCO and LOLO leave one experiment, one card or one mask layout out of a fit.

The meter. Every joule here is the card's own board-power reading, whose absolute scale is one fixed gain for every board, never set beside an outside meter. The joules compare models and cards with each other, not with another lab's meter.

1. The question

The owner's words that started the matmul model (Mon 16 Nov 2026, at or before 09:48:24 PST; a voice transcription, where "matte mold" is the matmul):

"I want you to focus on the matte mold most of all, so I want to have a model which predicts matte mold energy and number of transistor switches and clock cycles. From based on what I know about the size of a matrix and which operations are contained. See check how well the timing simulation for the matte mold matches reality and the energy."

So there are three asks: one model (sizes and operations in; cycles, joules and transistor switches out), a check of the timing simulation against the card, and a check of the energy against the card. The quote is as committed in tools/matmul/mm1/v0/README.md (2d1413f5).

2. What MM1 takes in and gives out, and how

The chip, in one paragraph. The ET-SoC-1 has 1,088 minions in 34 shires (32 compute shires, a master and a spare); the lab's kernels use 1,024 of them, in 32 shires of 32 (docs/et-soc1-notes.md; 05-claims.md). Each minion has a tensor unit that multiplies tiles of matrices. Each shire has its own L2 cache; the chip shares a 32 MB L3; behind them is DRAM. The lab's cards run at a fixed 600 MHz (aifoundry3 card 0 and aifoundry1 card 1) or under the card's own power governor (aifoundry2's card and aifoundry1 card 0).

The kernel and its operations. MM1 covers the lab's GEMM kernel rgm136 on its grid: gemmL<MB>x<NB>x<KB> computes M = 1,024·MB, N = 512·NB, K = 512·KB·w (w = 1, 2, 4 for fp32, fp16, int8) (v0/README.md section 2). Since MM1-v3 it also takes the operations the kernel's loop contains, as a counted census of each loop's instructions: rgm136's cooperative gemmL and its non-cooperative gemmLnc, and rgm211's gemmLn and gemmL (v3/README.md section 2). A loop outside these needs its own census and its own map of the lines it fetches first.

How, in four steps (MM1-v3; v3/README.md section 2):

  1. The memory path's line map. A tile op of one shire asks for 192 cache lines. A closed-form model of rgm136's tile order (E211's exact walk map for rgm211's gemmLn) and an L3 retention with two edges say how many the L2 serves, how many the L3, and how many come fresh from DRAM: the B streams' lines are kept up to a first edge at 37.4 MB, then half of them on a plateau that ends at 72.3 MB.
  2. Cycles. An op takes the slowest of the tensor unit's own op and the two operand streams, each stream priced by where its lines come from and by its code path: the instructions between the tensor unit's wait and the operand's request, relative to rgm136's gemmL loop, at one cycle an instruction plus the L1's load-to-use stalls, with no fitted constant. MM1-v3 adds an int8 factor on the L3-served stream (+4.8%) and, at one or two K blocks, a shire-cache share (0.64) of the desk L2 model's hits.
  3. Joules. Watts over idle are awake powers (for the active minions and the memory path), the governed base on a governed card, and the op rate times an ungated base an op, the data's share and a price per cache line by level. The data's share follows the counted switches: above the ungated base, energy follows the counted signal fin edges at one constant (1.58e16 fin edges a joule), the switch route E210 registered. A data kind with no committed count is NOT FORECAST in energy, never guessed.
  4. Transistor switches. The lab's unit is the fin edge: "one transistor gate, one fin, charged or discharged by a net transition" (v0/README.md section 5). MM1 counts the tensor unit's fin edges on an open stand-in netlist of its public RTL (fp32 at gate level; fp16 scaled from it; int8 around the arithmetic only) and estimates every other class. No card counter measures switches; section 5 tests the counted difference through energy.

3. How well the timing simulation matches the card

The simulator counts operations, not time. The lab's instruction-level simulator steps every active hart through its instructions with no clock and no caches. One step is one retired instruction: in a counted run, "every one of the 64 harts retired exactly as many instructions as its cycles_span, the simulator's steps" (E211's dated correction 1, ee0356d9). This corrects the first version, which read a step as "close to, not exactly, an instruction" from a count of another routine's loop: rgm136's gemmL loop runs 46 instructions an op, not 36; rgm211's gemmL walk 40, its gemmLn 31, and gemmLnc 39 (E211.md.A1-correction-1.md). At the blind points nobody ran the simulator: the instruction counts there come from a law fitted to 11 simulator runs of rgm136's gemmL (46.625 an op, 24.25 a C-tile row and 92 a launch; post hoc, registered in E206 before its data), so figure 1 holds the points of that loop only. Over 451 blind points (E206's two arms and E207, 265; E213's two arms, 110; E215's two arms, 76) the card took 8.2 to 35.1 cycles a GEMM (the slowest minion) for each simulated instruction a minion, median 24.0 (desk, from the frozen scorers' committed readings). The low end is int8 at one K block, the high end the long-K, DRAM-fed shapes. That ratio is how far a GEMM's time is from any instruction count; MM1 reads it as the tensor unit's op and the memory path, which no simulator the lab has models in time.

The simulator traces a kernel's requests exactly. MM1-v3.1's masked walk (T7, desk code with no fitted constant: which minions work, each minion's tile order, every fetch and store and the shires that share it) was checked against traced simulator runs of E212's layout pair, four shires at mask 0xf (compact) and 0x1111 (spread), with its predictions frozen before any trace. The registered trace check reads PASS: at each mask, 352 of 352 traced working minions issued exactly the frozen sequence of tensor operations (704 of 704 over both), and 900 traced off-mask minions issued none, as the walk says (walk/README.md, 5a75e212; 10 simulator runs). For the "which operations are contained" half of the question, that is exact. What the simulator cannot give is which level serves a line and how long it takes: post hoc, the walk's fresh-line count ordered only 3 of 8 to 3 of 11 of E212's measured layout pairs by time.

The RTL bench is exact for the tensor op with its operands resident (the first version's section 3): 18 of 18 registered readings within 2 cycles over 12 micro-loop configurations, all with zeros data on one card. A GEMM is not that loop: it streams B through the tile buffer, and MM1 reads the rest of each op as the memory path and the code path. On E213 the counter's mean ran 1.33 to 5.33 times the tensor unit's floor (desk, from its scorers' committed readings).

6.5 Two candidate terms, frozen before E215's data. Its verifier found that no L3 slice or memory controller runs hot (every mask loads them evenly, max/mean 1.000), and that F1 is a correction for shire count and shape, not a placement or contention term: permuting its feature within a shire count and shape does as well in 92.4% of draws, a count-only control (N1+N2) would have won, and the gain is within noise (an interval of -0.171 to +0.032 points). 7. What is still wrong, and the limits of the claim

The misses the blind days named (post hoc readings of blind data):

  • The non-cooperative loop's energy. At all seven E213 points of rgm136's gemmLnc, the card drew 1.15 to 1.80 times the J over idle MM1-v3 forecast given the measured seconds, while their cooperative gemmL twins read 0.97 to 1.04 (figure 5) and their cycles held. Without those seven points MM1-v3's J over idle reads 4.08% on both arms, against 9.92% with them. MM1-v3 priced each gemmLnc point at about its twin's watts. On E215, MM1-v3.1 still under-priced its five gemmLnc points (the card drew 1.14 to 1.52 times its J over idle given the seconds), and the term frozen for them (section 6.5) overshot at arm A's four (0.88 to 0.95 times its forecast) while reading 1.01 at arm B's one: the term's NB form does not hold yet. Their time was missed too: at NB 12 and 15 the card ran gemmLnc 14.8% and 15.5% faster than MM1-v3.1 forecast.
  • The layout of shire subsets. At an equal shire count the layout moves the time by up to 13.2% (E213, family M); MM1-v3 and MM1-v3.1 price a subset by its shire count alone, so every E213 time miss and the three largest E212 S3 misses are subset masks. The masked walk is exact in its requests but its fresh-line count does not order the measured layouts (section 3). On E215, MM1-v3.1 forecast family L's 28 layouts too slow by 8.52% on average (this report's footing), 10 of them past its band (the card 10.1% to 16.6% faster); neither the frozen layout term nor the masked walk ("v3.1 with T7") fixed it (REPORTED).
  • A held-out card's energy. With a card left out, MM1-v3.1's energy reads 10.92% by the declared anchor route against MM1-v3 refitted's 8.85%, and 9.64% against 9.11% by the fallback: each card still needs its own energy anchor.
  • The one-K-block form. T5r and tC split E213's family C 10 to 10; T5r won on pooled error, while tC reads E146's never-trained store rungs better (3.5% against 5.1%, REPORTED). On E215, one of family C's one-block points on each card and family R's gemmL2x1x7 on both cards ran 10.9% to 14.9% slower than MM1-v3.1 forecast.
  • E213 arm A's E4 (J at rest with the seconds forecast) failed by one point.

The scope limits (each stated in the registrations before the data):

  • Four loops of two kernels. The operations input is a census of rgm136's gemmL and gemmLnc and rgm211's gemmLn and gemmL; another kernel needs its own census and line map first. No epilogue (bias, activation, requantization) is modelled.
  • Warm, repeated GEMMs on the kernel's grid: every measured GEMM repeats the same A and B.
  • An energy anchor on every card (above), and two fixed-clock cards and two governed ones in every fit.
  • Switches are estimates. Counted on a stand-in netlist for the tensor unit, estimated for the rest; E210 and the unread test check the counted signal-net difference through energy only.

Better since the first version: all-zero data on aifoundry1 card 1 (the first version's first named miss: MM1-v3's card-1 zeros held out read 5.32% against 8.50% for MM1-v2's form); unseen tile kinds (now counted, and priced to 6.8% unread); the kernel variant's code path (with rgm211 held out MM1-v3 reads 7.78% against 9.31%).
