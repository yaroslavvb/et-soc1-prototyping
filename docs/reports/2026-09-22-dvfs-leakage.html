The ET-SoC-1's DVFS loop, and the leakage it never switches off

21–24 September 2026 · aifoundry2 and aifoundry3 (aifoundry1 unavailable) · firmware source read at et-platform 353f20e (the cards' own log strings show an older build; see §8), open RTL core-et b38a1a3 (Erbium branch) · checks six claims from a conversation with David Kanter (MLCommons) on 20 September 2026, in private notes · part of the ET-SoC-1 measurement reports

The ET-SoC-1 manages its power more simply than a mature chip, and leakage is where it pays. Its governor reads a power meter instead of estimating power, and its 65 °C threshold has no dead band, so near it aifoundry2's clock hunts. Its open RTL has per-minion sleep controls that are tied off, and the card shows no sign of array power gating. What is left is leakage. On aifoundry2, idle power climbs W for every degree at 80 °C, and the leakage behind that slope is W, of an idle card, well above the 5–30% David Kanter called typical, and of a card running a random-data matmul, at or above its top. And the second card, aifoundry3, has a firmware power limit of 0 W (aifoundry2's is 65 W), which pins it at 600 MHz while the driver still reports 65 W.

The yardstick is how Kanter described a mature chip managing its own power. It runs a DVFS loop (dynamic voltage and frequency scaling) that knows and, because voltage and frequency are already known, computes its own power estimate from activity counters. It folds thermal sensors into the same loop, because leakage depends on temperature. And it puts cache data arrays behind leakage-suppression transistors, so that only the ~10% of an array in use is awake. He hedged that this might not hold for Esperanto's part, only for anything from a more mature company. The hedge was right. The table below checks six of his statements, paraphrased, against the firmware source, the open RTL and the card.

Terms used on this page

DVFS (dynamic voltage and frequency scaling) moves the clock and the supply voltage together: here the service processor (SP), the on-die management core, steps among three operating points, 600, 700 and 800 MHz, each one row of the firmware's frequency-to-voltage table (the VMIN-LUT). It reads board power from the PMIC, the board's power-management controller, and die temperature from the on-die PVT sensors, and compares them with 65 °C and with TDP, the firmware's power limit of 65 W. The compute cores are minions, 32 to a shire, and kernels run on 32 shires (1,024 minions); the master minion dispatches them and tells the loop whether the card is busy. The flip-counting model is the Horace experiment's model of power from the bits the multiply-add unit clocks and toggles; more in the hub's glossary.

1. The loop as built

In the et-platform firmware source at 353f20e, the governor's decisions are one function, check_power_throttle_conditions() in ServiceProcessorBL2/services/thermal_pwr_mgmt.c, called once per pass of the device-management task. (The cards' own log lines show that they run an older build of this governor; §8 says what differs.) Reduced to its logic:

once per device-management pass, about every 133 ms on aifoundry2 (about 250 ms on aifoundry3)
(each I2C sensor read waits 1 ms, then the pass sleeps DM_TASK_DELAY_MS = 10 ms):
    T   = pvt_get_minion_avg_temperature()          // on-die thermal sensor, whole degrees
    P   = pmic_read_average_soc_power()             // MEASURED board power, from the PMIC over I2C
    if  active_power_management and master minion is BUSY:
        if T > 65 °C:
            if f > f_min:  step DOWN one VMIN-LUT point   // at the floor: hold; the power tests are not reached
        elif P > 65 W and f > f_min:  step DOWN one VMIN-LUT point
        elif P < 65 W and f < f_max:  step UP   one VMIN-LUT point
    on master minion going IDLE:
        set frequency back to the boot point

Four things follow, and all four show up in aifoundry2's measurements below (aifoundry3's clock cannot move, §3), the last one with a delay.

  • The input is a measurement, not an estimate. pmic_read_average_soc_power() is a read of the power-management controller's own averaged wattage. Nothing in the loop counts bus bits, instructions or activity factors, in this source or in the older one. Kanter's is not evaluated anywhere in this firmware, because the chip does not need to infer the left-hand side: it has a meter.
  • Thermal has priority. The temperature test comes first, and the frequency check sits inside it, so when the die reads above 65 °C the power tests are never reached, not even at the lowest operating point, where the thermal test itself does nothing. aifoundry2 often rests above 65 °C (73 °C on 22 September, about hours after our last recorded workload, and 73–74 °C after a night of no recorded work on each of the next two days; aifoundry3 rests at 50–55 °C), so a kernel launched on aifoundry2 starts above the threshold and runs at the lowest operating point however little power it draws: every launch of the strict protocol, at 80 °C, ran at 600 MHz.
  • There is no hysteresis. The 353f20e source defines UPPER_POWER_THRESHOLD_GUARDBAND and LOWER_POWER_THRESHOLD_GUARDBAND, a ±5% band around the TDP level, and then never uses them. Both tests compare against the raw thresholds, and the temperature test has no band of any kind: it fires at a whole-degree reading of 66 °C and stops firing at 65 °C. The older governor the cards' logs point to (§8) uses one of the two power guardbands, but neither would damp hunting at the thermal threshold (§2); that needs a temperature dead band.
  • The clock returns to the boot point when the master minion reports idle. The service processor then sets the boot frequency (go_to_idle_state_and_update_pwr_status()). On aifoundry2 that came about s after the host saw the last launch end ( s, at the of block ends on 21 and 23 September that found the clock above 600 MHz), and at of those the loop first stepped the clock up. Back-to-back launches rarely trigger it, and a short pause between launches more often: .

3. The same firmware on three cards


So far the loop has been measured on one card. AI Foundry has three machines, and the loop's behaviour turned out to depend on a
single byte that differs between them.



aifoundry1 could not be measured when we checked it, once, on 22 September. It holds two ET-SoC-1 cards, both enumerated on the PCIe bus, with
their device nodes present. Its kernel module is built from different sources than the other two machines
(srcversion 1383B256EB24A0A53F04CC7 against 47D26A305A0428B29FB7FC4) while carrying
the same libDM.so, and the library's compatibility check refuses to open the card:
Error unable to evaluate compatibility!. Its management service is not running. Reinstalling a
kernel driver on a shared lab machine is not ours to do, so those two cards are reported here as unavailable
and the lab admin has what they need to fix it. (Only the driver's configuration readout is stored; the rest was read
by hand.)



aifoundry2 and aifoundry3 look identical and are not. Same firmware release, same PMIC firmware, same
static device configuration over the driver's ioctl: 65 W TDP, 600 MHz boot clock, 32 compute shires, 32 MB of
L3. But asked the same question over the management interface, the two service processors give different
answers.






The governor compares measured SoC power against that TDP level. With the level at zero, the comparison in
check_power_throttle_conditions() can only come out one way:






So on aifoundry3 the loop is not disabled. Each kernel start logs a power throttle-down request and each return
to idle an idle event, and the step-up request, the only way above 600 MHz, can never fire: the clock read 600 MHz
in all 7,745 samples of the session. The service processor's own log, read back out of the card, shows the
pattern (six consecutive lines, verbatim):



Power idle state event, current pwr 26220  tdp level 0
Power throttle down event, current pwr 35380  tdp level: 0
Power idle state event, current pwr 26100  tdp level 0
Power throttle down event, current pwr 40200  tdp level: 0
Power idle state event, current pwr 26060  tdp level 0
Power throttle down event, current pwr 48470  tdp level: 0






A second, independent reading agrees. get_power_state() classifies the card as
MAX_POWER whenever power exceeds the TDP level and MANAGED_POWER when it is merely
above the 30 W safe threshold. aifoundry3 reports max_power while sitting at 23 W and 600 MHz,
which is only consistent with a TDP level below 23 W; aifoundry2, drawing more, reports
managed_power. Two different code paths, one cause.



The host cannot see this. The driver's ETSOC1_IOCTL_GET_DEVICE_CONFIGURATION reports a
65 W TDP on all three machines, including aifoundry3. Anything that checks a card's configuration from the host
side gets the nameplate number, not the one the governor is using. The card works. It is simply held at 600 MHz
for as long as its flashed TDP stays at zero, which on a compute-bound workload that would otherwise run at 800 MHz
gives up a quarter of the throughput (800 MHz is a third faster than 600), and nothing reports an error.



The contrast is not that aifoundry2 is always faster. Launched warm, it too runs at 600 MHz: the thermal
branch holds it at the floor, and every one of the strict protocol's launches at 80 °C ran at 600 MHz (§1). The
difference is the remedy. aifoundry2 reaches 700 and 800 MHz when it starts cool (§2). aifoundry3 cannot at any
temperature, because the branch that would raise it is unreachable.



This also settles what §2 could not: whether the power branch works at all. It never fired on its own
in any aifoundry2 run. On aifoundry3 it is the only branch that fires, at every kernel start, on a die well
below the thermal threshold, and it does what the comparison says it will.



4. The leakage-suppression transistors


Kanter's mechanism is a tag check that wakes only the part of an array a lookup needs, at a small wake-up latency;
the same trick applies to whole cores and execution units.


The design has the ports. The open RTL of the neighbourhood (a group of eight minions) carries the full
interface: a per-minion sleep control and its acknowledge (pwr_ctrl_min_nsleepin /
nsleepout, one bit per minion), a global pair, and per-minion isolation
(pwr_ctrl_min_isolate) with level shifters and a power stub that forces the outputs safe while a
domain is down. That is a textbook power-gating structure, sized for the granularity he described.


That RTL is the Erbium configuration (one neighbourhood, no shire cache), so it says nothing about the L2 and L3
arrays; for those, the wake-up probe below is the only evidence.


Nothing drives it. In the open configuration the whole interface is tied off, with a comment saying
so:


// Power Ctrl - power control handled outside cpu subsystem and this ifce is not used
.pwr_ctrl_glb_nsleepin ('1),   // '1 = never asleep
.pwr_ctrl_min_nsleepin ('1),
.pwr_ctrl_min_isolate  ('0),


And the service-processor firmware, searched both at 353f20e and in the older governor, contains no
power-gating control of any kind: its only PWR_CTRL hits are an eMMC controller's bus voltage, and
elsewhere in the tree the name appears only in eMMC and PCIe PHY register headers. Whatever drives those pins on
the taped-out part, it is not this firmware, and the chip-level netlist is not in the open release.


And the card shows no sign of it. If an array were suppressed once idle, the first access afterwards
would pay the wake-up. The probe: put one line at a chosen level, let the minion spin on its cycle counter for a
swept interval from zero to 27 ms (0, 1.7 µs, 17 µs … 27 ms) without touching that line, then time a single load
of it: same line, same address, only the idle time varying, 20 repeats. (The probe counts the idle in cycles; the times
assume 600 MHz. The clock during the probe was not recorded; aifoundry2 was resting at about 73 °C that morning, and in
26 sessions on five days it never ran above 600 MHz at a die reading of 68 °C or more.)

  • L2: −11 cycles, and this comes from the baseline, not the idle. With no idle at all the load reads 60 or 61

from 1.7 µs to 27 ms it reads 49 or 50 cycles, a normal L2 hit (139 of those 140 loads; the other is a counter

    • 

  • DRAM: +10.5 cycles, and that one has a known cause: the line's DRAM row has closed, so the next load
pays an activate, 11 cycles (tRCD, the activate-to-read delay).
Anatomy
of a memory access measured that cost on aifoundry2 (§5), and found there that an idle row stays open until a
refresh closes it (every 3.87 µs), as the memory controller's open-page policy implies; the +40 cycles in that report are for a load
issued right behind another to a different row of the same bank, a different case. Here
. The shift is there in full after the shortest idle, 1.7 µs, and does not grow out
to 27 ms, which is not how a wake-up would behave.
    • 



So on this card, in this firmware, no leakage suppression is in use. The open design has the ports and ties them
off, no firmware line drives them, and the card shows no wake-up. Whether the taped-out chip could gate its arrays is
not established (§8). The gating the open RTL does use is clock gating, and aggressively (per lane, per functional
unit, with a seven-cycle tail). That is the likely reason a powered core that is not computing costs little
dynamic power (Why is the ET-SoC-1
low power?, section 4) and still leaks.

7. What this means for the equation, and for measuring power


    

  • The equation is right; it is just not what the loop uses. Its activity term holds well on aifoundry2.
Power over idle is close to linear in active minions: 25.6 mW per minion at 256 and 512 active, 26.2 at
768 and 27.0 at 1,024; a line through zero fits 26.5 mW per minion (one session, two runs per count). And
a model that counts four
classes of switching event inside the multiply-add unit predicts aifoundry2's board power across fourteen operand
patterns to 0.5 W rms, each pattern predicted by a fit that left it out, and carries to aifoundry3 with one scale factor
(§6). The  term is only weakly testable, and only on aifoundry2 (aifoundry3 cannot leave 600 MHz),
because the governor will not hold the high operating point long on a heavy workload. For constant data it predicts
 for the switching-power ratio between the two points; the
cool-start runs give about 2.1 for ones, a loose check: the idle levels it subtracts are uncertain by about 5% (the
600 MHz idle alone moves about  W per degree at  °C),
the runs are seven short, uncontrolled ones on one morning, and zeros are too small to test it at all
(Why is the ET-SoC-1 low power?,
Voltage and clock). A clean test needs a die below 65 °C and a runner that pins the work rate.
    • 

  • Kanter's argument that measured power is too expensive to mandate is weaker for this card, though it does
not vanish. His reason MLPerf cannot require measured power is that most submitters will not pay the time and
energy to instrument a system. This chip is already instrumented, as a load-bearing part of its own safety loop: a
PMIC that meters board power, a service processor that reads it on every pass of its management loop, and a
management interface that hands the number to the host. The cost of reporting the card's own power here is one
query. But MLPerf's measured power is the whole system (host, power supply, cooling), which this meter does not
see, and the meter gives one new board reading per ~133 ms pass on aifoundry2 (about 250 ms on aifoundry3), with the
rail readings behind the PMIC's own running average (a time constant of about one second).
    • 

  • But the loop's hunting is exactly the kind of thing a provisioned-power metric cannot see. A
normalisation by provisioned kilowatts scores a card by its nameplate. On aifoundry2 a governor with no dead band
moves the clock, and so the throughput, by up to a third within a single 7-second run, invisibly.

decided on readings between our samples, which is the likeliest reading but untested (sampling at 25 ms on a cool die
would settle it); how the idle law's power divides between fixed and leakage beyond the range of §5; and why two relay
launches on aifoundry2 returned wrong elements (1,118 and 1,000) on 23 September, in one pass of the discarded first
attempt at a cool-card rerun. Both ran while the governor dropped the clock from 800 to 600 MHz inside the launch, and
the relay's other launches on both cards were correct. An error at the clock transition and a race in the relay's own
completion flags when the clock ratio changes would both fit; one pass on one card cannot tell them apart.
