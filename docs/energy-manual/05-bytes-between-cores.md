# 5. Bytes between cores and shires

Register file to register file over the tensor network (`TensorSend`/`TensorRecv`), 1 KB messages unless said otherwise, hart 0 of every minion sending and receiving in rings, at 600 MHz. Shire IDs do not follow the mesh, so each ring between shires is given with its mean distance in mesh hops, the Manhattan distance between shire s and shire s + k averaged over all 32 compute shires on the shire map of [On-chip communication](https://spacesheep.dev/@yaroslavvb/et-soc1-on-chip-communication) (checked against latency on aifoundry2).

Every entry is **mean** [lo–hi]: the mean over every pass on every card, and the full range those passes spanned. "a2", "a3" and "a1c1" are aifoundry2, aifoundry3 and aifoundry1's card 1, each as its own mean ± its pass-to-pass standard error. The rings were re-measured in the version-3 check (26 September) with the manual's own sampler, six passes on each of three cards, the die held warm on the governor-free cards; they replace three passes on each of two cards of 23 September. The pair of 18 September runs on aifoundry2, sampled without the die temperature and so without a leakage correction, is not pooled; against aifoundry2's own new passes the values On-chip communication publishes from it read +4% to +16% (median +7%). **The s ↔ s+16 ring starves the service processor's own management path** — the sampler's latency rises from 22 ms to 63–141 ms and the board reading takes a new value about twice a second instead of six times — in every version-3 pass on aifoundry2, aifoundry3 and aifoundry1 card 1, so those bursts were dropped and that row keeps aifoundry3's three passes of 23 September, when its sampler stayed at 22 ms (On-chip communication gives 14.3 pJ/B for it on aifoundry2 on 18 September).

## Handing a slab to the next shire through its scratchpad (E25)
The relay was re-measured in the version-3 check (26 September), six passes on each of three cards (n = 18); it replaces the 22 September session and the 23 September passes. A stage reads a slab, adds 1 and writes it where the next stage reads it; the next shire is shire s − 1 by ID, 3.5 (1–10) mesh hops away. GB/s: the 22 September session's, on aifoundry2.

|---|---|---|---|
| Write to DRAM, read back | **116.2** [104.5–135.3] | a2: 111.3 ± 1.6 · a3: 107.5 ± 1.0 · a1c1: 129.9 ± 1.4 | 50 |

**What the tables say.**
- **Between the two minions of a pair a byte costs under a picojoule** (0.69 pJ); around a neighbourhood or a shire about 2.2 pJ; across the mesh 12–18 pJ. A straight line through the 1 KB rings between shires against their mean distances (the five that every card measured, 1.6–4.7 hops) gives 9.1 pJ to leave the shire plus 1.8 pJ per mesh hop on aifoundry2; 8.0 plus 1.7 on aifoundry3; and 10.4 plus 1.7 on aifoundry1 card 1; the version-3 check finds no card's per-hop cost different from another's, 1.75 pJ/B per hop pooled. [Heat per millimetre](https://spacesheep.dev/@yaroslavvb/et-soc1-heat-per-mm) measures 1.5 pJ/B per hop on the mesh rail and 2.2 on board power directly. **For messages, leaving the shire is the biggest step**, mostly because the same busy cores move 7–34× fewer bytes across the mesh than around a shire ([On-chip communication](https://spacesheep.dev/@yaroslavvb/et-soc1-on-chip-communication)); for tensor loads it costs about one hop ([4.3](04a-fine-grain.md)). The step out of the shire beyond one hop is 5.2 [3.3, 7.0] pJ/B on aifoundry2 and 6.2 [2.7, 9.8] pJ/B on aifoundry1 card 1, not resolved on aifoundry3 (4.2 [−1.0, 9.3]) (99% intervals over six passes).
- **Small messages cost more per byte**: the 128 B rows are dearer than the 1 KB ones on every card, by 1.3–1.5 pJ/B around a shire and 4.9–6.4 pJ/B between neighbouring shire IDs (resolved from zero at 99% on each card); the per-message overhead is 40–224 cycles of the sending and receiving harts ([On-chip communication](https://spacesheep.dev/@yaroslavvb/et-soc1-on-chip-communication), one session on aifoundry2).
- **Handing a slab to the next shire through its scratchpad costs 12.9×, 13.0× and 13.1× less than the DRAM round trip** on aifoundry2, aifoundry3 and aifoundry1 card 1 (the same on every card at 99%, 13.0× pooled); the hand-off itself costs 8.6, 8.3 and 9.9 pJ/B in that order for the write and the read together, and sits between the in-shire and cross-mesh tensor-network figures.
- The three relay bars are ±13–15%, most of it aifoundry1 card 1 reading 17–21% above the others through DRAM, a difference the version-3 check resolves; within a card the passes agree to about 1% (standard error).
